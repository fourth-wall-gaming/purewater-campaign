**The last image of the second ending.** It fires after the Prince is summoned
to the room and comes out wearing Thuban; after a quiet old-rite audience
nobody knows to shoot, so it fires only if Temerach has learned what the
champion is.

The Prince on the temple steps, siding with the Baron above a city that is
still burning. The Baron beside him, smoke on his coat and the locket back
on his chain. Four thousand people on the slopes and the stands, and **the calculation
inverts for Temerach** -- a bound spirit in a stolen boy, and now in the
Crown's own nephew, is desecration and the goddess's own business, and a Sword
may act on that without asking anybody.

She **names him**, across the whole width of the arena, in front of the city
-- the King's officer's last lawful act -- and then she draws.

**It is a shot nobody else alive could make.** Distance, crosswind, smoke,
the angle of the steps, a crowd between. Roll it, and **it hits**, because she
is who she is. **And it is not enough.** The arrow takes him high, through the
shoulder or the side; he goes down on the steps, and gets up again, and he is
alive.

Then the Knights are coming across the arena for her, and the crowd breaks.

## The flight

She is an outlaw from the instant the arrow leaves the string, and she knew
that before she drew. **She flees with the party** -- or the party flee with
her. Over the water, through the smoke, out of the city by whatever route is
left: Crowbill's canals, a Dredger's boat out through the Mouth, the open road.
It costs them something to get out, and it should.

She will not thank them and will not explain.

**The end.** The Baron is wounded and alive. The Prince is a vessel. The
Lullwater is ash. The party is on the road out of Purewater with a King's
officer turned outlaw, and the only way left to undo any of it is four days
away, in a tower on the border estate, in a box of bones.
