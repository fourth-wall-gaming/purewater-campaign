KAG'S OPENING, and it is a **sums** problem, not a violence problem.

First light on the Shoals, cold, the fish coming in. A round of eleven calls
that she has made every week for four years and could do asleep.

Three of them are short. Not skimming -- **short in the open**, with the money
simply not there and the person saying so to her face, which nobody does.

**The pattern is the beat.** All three are on or near a bridge the advance
detachment has been standing on for a week. All three have had something taken
off them -- a barrel, a morning's catch, the use of their own landing -- by men
who then walked away, and nobody made those men pay for it.

So they have reached the reasonable conclusion. **If the Knights do not have to
pay, why does anybody?** They are not defying Crowbill. They are asking a
question about arithmetic and they would like an answer.

**Play it warm and ordinary.** These are people who know her, who are not
frightened of her, and who are slightly embarrassed. Nobody threatens anybody.
The old woman with the smoker gives her a fish.

**What it costs to notice:** nothing but reading the book. Commerce to see that
three shortfalls in one week on one bridge is not coincidence; Insight to see
that they are telling the truth.

**What it costs to act:** she can lean on them and get the money today, and the
same three will be short again next week and four more with them. Or she can
carry it back to Crowbill as a number, which is not what muscle is for.

**Do not resolve which.** The point of the opening is that she is the only
person in this organisation who can see it, and that seeing it is her job in a
way nobody has ever said out loud.

---

**AS AN NPC.** This fires whether anybody is watching or not, and it is where
Kag's whole track starts. If a PC is anywhere on the Shoals that morning, the
scene is a very large half-orc at a rope stall doing sums out loud and arguing
with a man about eleven shillings, and she will explain the whole of it to
anybody who asks — because the explanation is an accusation and she wants it
repeated. If nobody asks, she takes it to Crowbill and the party hears it third
hand on `d-1` with the numbers wrong.
