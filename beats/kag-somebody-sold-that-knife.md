THE LINE FROM THE UNDERWORLD INTO THE BARON, and it is walkable only by somebody
who understands the trade rather than the crime.

By the morning after the carving, the Pearl is describing a knife: eleven
inches, barbed down one edge like fish-hooks, a mark on the pommel -- a ring
with a line through it and three strokes off the line.

**What everybody else hears:** a horrible detail in a horrible story.

**What Kag hears:** an inventory item. Objects like that are commissioned, not
bought off a stall. Somebody in this city either made it, moved it, valued it or
was offered it, and every one of those is a conversation she is qualified to
have and most people are not.

**The trail, and it takes a day:**

- **A fence** who was offered a marked piece months ago and would not touch it,
  because a thing with a name on it is unsellable and everybody in the trade
  knows that.
- **A goldsmith in the Forge Quarter** who cut the pommel mark to a drawing
  brought in by a servant, was paid properly and asked nothing -- and who kept
  the drawing, because he keeps all of them, because that is how a workshop
  works.
- **The drawing is in the same hand as the scroll.** She will not know that
  until somebody puts the two side by side, and the person holding the scroll is
  whoever searched that room.

**This is the beat that makes her indispensable to the main plot.** Everyone
else has evidence that names the son. This names the father's *hand*, through
the trade, with a witness who has no stake and no reason to lie.

**And the cost:** a goldsmith who talks to Crowbill's about a di Teufel
commission is a goldsmith who has just put himself in front of it. Whatever she
promises him, she will be held to.

---

**AS AN NPC.** She gets to Orsk & Daughter on `d-1/dawn` by taking a charcoal
rubbing of the pommel mark rather than lifting the knife — which means **the
knife stays where it was dropped** and is still there for the party to find.
Hild will not discuss the same mark with two sets of people without saying so,
so whoever gets there second is told, plainly, that a big half-orc was here
first. That is a door, not an obstacle: Hild liked her.
