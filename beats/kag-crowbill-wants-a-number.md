He is on a roof or in a cookshop or on the back of somebody's boat, because he
is never in an office, and he already knows two of the three names before she
says them.

**He does not want a grievance.** He wants it in money, by the day, with the
bridges named. He says so once, without heat, and goes back to what he is doing.

> "Don't tell me they're unhappy. Tell me what it's costing and how fast it's
> getting worse, and then I'll know whether I'm buying him off or burying him."

Then the part that matters, and it should land quietly enough to be missed:
**he gives the job to her.** Not to a clerk, not to one of the two men standing
behind him who are what people think of when they think of Crowbill's. Her.

He does not explain why and he does not make a speech about it. He says the
thing about the number and then he says *you do it* and turns away, and the two
men behind him look at each other.

**This is the beat that runs her campaign.** From here she has a standing
commission that takes her into every quarter, every faction's business and every
account in the city, and a legitimate reason to be asking questions in rooms
where a half-orc has no business standing.

**What he will not say, and never will:** he gave it to her because she is the
only one who can do it. She will have to work that out from the fact that he
keeps giving her things.

**If she does not bring him the ledger,** he sends for her by the end of the day
anyway -- he has heard about the three from somebody else -- and it goes the
same way with less credit.
