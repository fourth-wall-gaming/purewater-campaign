Caravan Square, mid-morning, the column coming through the gate, and every eye in
the square on the litter. Randall is in the middle of robbing a cart.

A guard at the Baron's stirrup turns his head and sees him, and **goes white.**
He breaks step. He says **"My lord --"** in a voice that is not respect but
fear: the fear of a servant who has just seen the heir standing in the street
where the heir is not supposed to be, in a thief's clothes, with his hand in
somebody's cargo -- and who will be the one blamed for it. He offers a hand down
off the cart, because he cannot think what else to do.

Then the column moves and the guard has to move with it, looking back twice.

**Deceit to play it, Stealth to be gone.** Playing it well is worth more than
the escape: a name, a piece of the household's business, and on a critical,
where his lordship is expected this evening. Failing it puts him in front of
somebody who has seen Santo close up. Running clean costs nothing and teaches
nothing. **That is a legitimate outcome and should be allowed to be dull.**

**What the guard thinks he saw:** Santo, slipped out of the household again, in
borrowed clothes, among the crowd. He does not report it, because reporting the
young master's absences to the Baron is how a guard stops being a guard. **This
does not wake *A di Teufel who is not his*.** Nobody in that column has seen a
second face; they have seen the first one in the wrong place.

**What Randall gets:** a stranger who was terrified of him and called him a
lord, with the Baron three feet away. Nothing to act on, and something he cannot
stop turning over. He knows this city well enough to vanish -- and to start
asking, quietly, whose face he has.

This is the first thread of the twins, and the reason Randall walks into the
catacombs already half-knowing the answer.
