## How religion works here

Purewater is not a devout city; it is a **transactional** one. People give to the
god whose business they are in, at the season it matters, and expect a return. A
boatman pours a little water back before a long pull. A stallholder in the
Merchant's Quarter keeps a craft-god's token by the scale. Nobody thinks these
are exclusive commitments, and only two traditions in the city ask for exclusivity
at all.

Worship is mostly **public, wet, and civic**: processions on the water, offerings
into it, the reflecting pools, and a calendar of festivals of which the Lake
Lady's Tourney is by far the largest.

---

## The Lake Lady (Nimue) — the establishment faith

Purewater's patron: goddess of the sacred waters, of purification and abundance,
healing and prophecy-in-reflection, and protection against corruption. Hers is the
city's official religion, its landlord, and its festival calendar.

**What she asks:** that water be kept clean, that the drowned be named, that
guests be given water before questions, and that nothing sworn over still water be
broken.

**Two houses serve her, and they do not command each other** -- the priesthood
([[The Order of the Lake Lady]]) and the knights ([[The Swords of the Lake]]).
This distinction matters enormously in play: see *[[The Order and the Swords: one goddess, two houses]]*.

**As the city's waters foul, her power is felt to wane.** Healing works less well
than it did. The reflecting pools give poorer answers. Whether this is the
goddess weakening or the medium fouling is the argument dividing her own clergy,
and some of the higher clergy are quietly studying the corruption rather than
merely praying about it.

## The older water — the Mermaid's Court

Beneath the Lady's faith, and considerably older than it, are the **pre-human
water powers**: not gods with temples but presences with places, honoured by the
merfolk and by [[The Mermaid's Court]] in hidden chambers under the Northern
Isles. [[Ravella]] and the city's concealed waterfolk keep these observances
quietly.

**The Order finds this awkward** and always has. The water-faith arrived, put
temples on the pools, and named the goddess -- and something was already in the
water when it got here. Officially the Court is a superstition. Unofficially
Lilura Deepcurrent is rumoured to carry merfolk ancestry and nobody in the Order
raises the subject.

## The woodland old ways

Not a church, not a priesthood, and not written down: the observances of the
forest peoples of the borderlands, carried by whoever is left to carry them.
Burial rites, hospitality rites, the naming of springs, and the old obligation to
lay the unquiet dead to rest properly rather than bind, banish or use them.

**This is [[Gardwen]]'s tradition** -- what found her in the grove after the raid
and what her woodland magic comes out of. *Unseat* is a burial rite before it is
anything else, which is exactly why it will not move a spirit that is holding on
because of what was done to it until somebody deals with the wrong first.

The city regards it as peasant superstition. The city is wrong.

## Vacarus — the soldier's god

God of war, invoked constantly in oaths and exclamations (*"Vacarus knows"*) by
people with no religious feeling whatsoever. He has a square in the old city and
no great temple here. Soldiers, watchmen and duellists keep his day; so, nominally
and without much sincerity, do the Dragon Knights, whose dragon heraldry is the
thing they actually revere.

## Hel — the northern death-goddess

Grim and terrible, worshipped in the north and barely at all in Purewater. It is
Hel who sends [[Magda]] -- through jarring, violent nightmares ending always in
Magda's own death -- to protect the four companions and *"lay the dragon warrior
to rest."* One does not serve the Goddess of Death without dread, and Magda curses
her patroness even as she obeys.

There is no temple to Hel in this city and Magda would not use one.

## The working cults

- **Sea-gods of the Shoals** -- small, numerous, bargained with rather than
  worshipped. Boatmen and fishers. The two boatmen who did not come back will get
  observances whether or not bodies are ever found.
- **Craft-gods of the Forge Quarter** -- the dwarven smiths' own, kept properly
  and privately, with a formality the rest of the city finds comic.
- **Nilo Valis** -- patron spirit of thieves and quick improvisation, invoked with
  affection and no ceremony at all. [[Nus]] would tell you Nilo Valis is real and
  has personally intervened on his behalf.

## And then there is demonology

**It is not a religion and it is not worship.** Nobody in this story prays to
Asmodeus. Demonology is a *technical tradition* -- a body of practice for opening,
seating, anchoring and drawing out spirits -- and it uses devil-sigils the way a
surveyor uses a benchmark, because those names hold and can be written down.

**Asmodeus**'s runic sigil is emblazoned on the demonologist's **soul-knife**,
the instrument [[Santo di Teufel]] means to use on [[Emmeralda]]. His mark on the
blade is the unmistakable signature of the fire-demonology at the campaign's
heart, and the sight of it makes even Hel's warrior recoil.

**The legal position, which the party should be told plainly if they ask:** arcane
practice is lawful and licensed in Mystamyr -- that is what institutions like
[[The Order of the Blue Star]] exist to oversee. **Seating a spirit in an
unwilling person is a capital crime under the King's law**, and has been since the
uprising, for the obvious historical reason.

This is why the evidence matters so much. A spent scroll of *Seat the Bound* in
Baron Hanzo di Teufel's own hand is not a scandal, not a rumour, and not a
theological dispute. **It is a hanging document**, and it is one of the reasons a
lawful route through [[Abel Weir]], [[Maro Quist]], the [[The Harbor Masters]]'
maritime jurisdiction, or the Blue Star's institutional weight is a genuine way to
win this campaign rather than a consolation prize.
