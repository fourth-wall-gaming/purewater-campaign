## The shape of Purewater

Purewater is not a ring of districts around a centre -- it is a city built across
**the Mouth**, the strait where the sacred **Lake** (to the **north**) drains south
into **the Open Sea**. Hold that picture and the rest follows. The city map in
`maps/purewater-map.png` is drawn to this description, and every name below is
lettered on it unless it says otherwise.

**The two shores.**
- On the **western** shore, where the Caravan Road comes in through the fields,
  stands **[[Caravan Square]]**: a walled waterfront of staging-yards and
  warehouses, and the only way into the city by land. Its gatehouse is the **Gate of
  Triumph** -- lettered **"The Land Gate"** on the map and called that by everyone
  who uses it. From the gate the **Triumph Bridge** crosses the calm landward
  channel to High Isle; a little south of it, a **Ferry Stage** serves the boats.
- Off the **eastern** shore, at the city's south-east corner and facing the open
  sea, lies **[[The Shoals]]**: a low, ragged island of fish-markets, wharves and
  smugglers' jetties, bridged to the Forges on one side and to the Eastern Causeway
  on the other. This is the seaward jaw of the Mouth.

**[[High Isle]]**, the heart, fills the strait between them. It is not one island but
**four**, its quarters cut apart by water. **The Grand Canal** runs west to east
across the middle; a broad branch runs south from it between the South and East
Quarters; and a narrow cut divides the South Quarter from the Seaward Quarter.

- **High Isle (North Quarter)** -- the long northern island, facing the open Lake.
  Its western half is houses, rich toward the Pearl and middling eastward, with a
  small inner canal cutting through it. Its eastern half is the official and noble
  quarter: the **Governor's Palazzo** ([[Prince Emeric]]), the **Hall of Justice**,
  the **Barracks**, Palace Square between them, and at the island's east end the
  walled **Tournament Ground**.
- **High Isle (South Quarter)** -- **[[The Merchant's Quarter]]**, south-west across
  the Grand Canal. Trade, guilds, the Watercrafters and the merchant princes,
  around a walled **Market Square** ringed by the **Guildhall of Merchants**, the
  **Guildhall of Coopers** and the **Guildhall of Dyers**.
- **High Isle (East Quarter)** -- **[[The Forge Quarter]]**, lettered **"The Forges"**:
  the **Foundries**, the **Forge Tower** over its south shore, yards and slips
  reaching east toward the Shoals. The Baron's **[[The DragonBarge]]** lies at its
  seaward pier.
- **High Isle (Seaward Quarter)** -- the **Docks and Wharves**, a long strip on the
  south shore behind the cut, its waterfront a comb of piers and slips, with the
  round **Harbour Master's Tower** at the tip of the longest mole.

**[[The Lullwater]]** is a separate, ragged island to the **north-east**, across open
water from the Tournament Ground and the Forges, and no bridge reaches it. It is
the poor-and-criminal quarter (the old "Maze" and "Warren" combined), the most
tangled water on the map. The map letters it **"(Thieves' Quarter)"**, and so does
everyone who doesn't live there. Its calm name is a deliberate mask.

**[[The Northern Isles]]**, scattered across the Lake's calmer waters north of High
Isle: **[[The Pearl]]** (pleasure and nightlife), close off the North Quarter's
north-west, with the walled islet of the Widow's House beside it, shuttered since
[[The Widow of the Pearl]] died; quiet **court-family retreats**; **the Singer's Rest** to the north-west and **Northlight**
to the north, smaller isles of houses and gardens; and to the north-east the
drowned ruins of **[[Lost Isle]]**, which the boatmen call **the Grey Rains** (the
map letters both names) -- [[The Mermaid's Court]] lies beneath. A few **shunned or
haunted** islets lie unnamed among them, one of them just off the Tournament Ground.
North of the Tournament Ground, alone and unbridged, sits little **[[Temple Isle]]**
with its temple and **the Sacred Confluence**.

Boats are the streets; bridges stitch it together; **[[The Catacombs]]** run beneath
it all. The set-pieces the geography invites: canal chases through the Lullwater, the
Harbor Run across the Mouth, and the steam-wreathed corruption spreading from the
DragonBarge on the seaward side.

## Around the city

West of the landward channel the shore is reed and marsh -- **Waters' Marsh** --
giving way to the ruled strips and ditches of **Farmer's Dykes**, with
**Southfield Farm** to the south and **Saltmarsh** running down to the sea. **The
Caravan Road** winds through them to Caravan Square. Both lake shores to the north
are **Redmarsh**, reed and standing water. On the eastern shore the **Eastern
Causeway** road runs north to south. It leads to **[[The Vantt Estate]]**, two miles
east, and the tide-flats lie south of it (unlettered).

## The bridges

Hundreds of footbridges cross the small canals and nobody names them. These are
the spans people give directions by. **No bridge crosses the Grand Canal itself** --
it is too wide and too busy, and it is crossed by ferry -- which is why getting
from the Merchant's Quarter to the Palazzo always costs a fare. No bridge reaches
Temple Isle or the Lullwater either.

- **The Triumph Bridge** -- "the gate bridge" to everyone who uses it. A stone span
  over the landward channel, from the Gate of Triumph on [[Caravan Square]] to the
  north-west corner of the Merchant's Quarter, with the gatehouse on the land end
  and a customs house on the island end. The only way into the city on foot.
- **The Lantern Bridge** -- the broad stepped bridge from the middle of the Pearl
  down to the North Quarter, hung with lamps on posts that are lit at dusk and never
  let go out before dawn. The front door of [[The Pearl]].
- **The Old Span** -- the oldest stone bridge in the city, from the Pearl's
  south-western lobe across to the North Quarter's west shore. Low enough that a
  standing boatman ducks.
- **The Hush Bridge** -- a narrow footbridge off the Pearl's western tip, out of sight
  of the Quay, to a landing stage in the open lake. It is the way gentlemen leave:
  by boat, unseen.
- **The Widow's Bridge** -- the private span to the small walled islet beside the
  Pearl and the shuttered Widow's House, gated at the island end. The Widow founded
  the Pearl as a pleasure district; see [[The Widow of the Pearl]].
- **The Cooper's Bridge**, **the Dyers' Bridge** and **the Tidegate Bridge** -- named
  crossings of the narrow cut between the Merchant's Quarter and the Seaward
  Quarter. There are four bridges over the cut; the Cooper's Bridge is the
  westernmost and the Tidegate Bridge the easternmost. The Dyers' Bridge stands below
  the Guildhall of Dyers, and the water under it runs blue and red on a working
  day. The Tidegate Bridge carries the sluice that holds the cut against the tide.
- **The Cinder Bridge** -- from the Forges across to the Seaward Quarter wharves, a
  heavy timber span built for iron carts and black with their dust.
- **The Fishmarket Bridge** -- from the Forges' eastern end over the narrow water to
  the [[The Shoals]] fish-market. Rope stalls along its rails, and steps down to the
  market at the Shoals end.
- **The Causeway Bridge** (unlettered on the map) -- the long trestle from the Shoals
  east across the inlet to the Eastern Causeway, and so the road in from the east
  and from [[The Vantt Estate]].
- **The Tideflats Bridge** (unlettered on the map) -- the lower plank span from the
  Shoals' south-eastern wharves to the shore south of the Causeway. It floods at the
  spring tides.
