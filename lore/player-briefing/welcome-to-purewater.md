Read this to a new player before they choose a character. Five minutes, in your
own words, and stop when they start asking questions — questions are better than
briefing.

Everything here is **public knowledge in the city**. None of it is a spoiler.

---

## The city has no ground

Purewater is built on water and does not apologise for it. Houses come straight
up out of the channel with no bank and no shore, and their doors open onto steps
that go down under the surface and keep going. There is no ground. There are
buildings, there is water, and between them there are ledges a body or two wide
with no rail.

Everything that matters goes by boat — poled, rowed, and shouted about. The rest
goes over a bridge or along a ledge, and every single journey costs a fare.

Two consequences, and both of them come up constantly:

**Anybody raised here can handle a boat.** It is like walking. Nobody is praised
for it. The half of that nobody ever says out loud is that **outsiders cannot**,
which means a stranger in Purewater cannot move a sack of flour across it without
hiring somebody who was born here.

**There are no horses.** The land stops at Caravan Square and does not resume. A
column coming down the Anminster road gets to the gate and then has to be taken to
pieces and put into boats, in public, slowly, one crate at a time.

## The water is the goddess

The channel comes down out of the lake and through the temple on Temple Isle, and
the priestesses of the **Lake Lady** say the water *is* the goddess and mean it
plainly. The Order owns the water — the channels and the pools are hers, by writ.
The isles belong to the city council.

This matters more than it sounds. It means the most sacred ground in Purewater is
also the traffic system, and it means the Order has standing that no council can
overrule and no baron can buy.

## Once there were Dragon Kings

They are gone. Every child knows it. What is left of them is old armour on border
lords, a name on a barony, and a small number of people who would like the old
power back badly enough to pay for it.

## This week

The **Lake Lady's Tourney** comes round once a year — archery, single combat, and
a scramble across the canals — and it is older than the Governor's office. For the
week of it the city fills up, everybody makes a year's money in six days, and the
law goes quiet.

It is three days away.

**Baron Hanzo di Teufel** came down the road for it nine weeks ago with Dragon
Knights at his back, and he is still here. He has entered a champion in the single
combat that nobody outside his household has been allowed to see.

His men stand on the bridges. They take what they want off the stalls and they
leave no chit, and there is no quartermaster to bill and no office to complain to,
and everybody in Purewater has worked that out privately and nobody has said it
out loud. Nobody calls it an occupation. Everybody goes round.

That is the city you are walking into. Something is going to give this week, and
the interesting question is not *whether* — it is who is standing nearest when it
does.

## What not to say

Do not explain the Baron's champion. Do not explain what the litter had in it.
Do not mention the Pearl's troubles before the player has been anywhere near the
Pearl. The scenario's whole first act is the party finding out what kind of story
they are in, and a briefing that answers it has stolen the game.
