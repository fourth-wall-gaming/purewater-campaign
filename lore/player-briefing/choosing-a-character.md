Four people are converging on Purewater in the three days before the Tourney and
none of them knows about the others. Offer all four properly — a paragraph each,
out loud — and then let the player take their time.

**Everything here is safe to say.** What is deliberately absent: why anybody
might mistake one of them for somebody else, what the Baron is actually doing, and
what happens on the second night. The full second-person opening for whoever they
pick is in `lore/entry-points/`, and that is what you read once they have chosen.

If they would rather build their own, go to
`lore/character-creation/rolling-your-own.md`. The most recent playthrough was a
rolled character and it was better than a pregen would have been.

---

## Gardwen — the woman from the woods

A feral-raised elf of the deep woods, a long way from anything she recognises, in
a city with no earth in it. Ten years ago Dragon Knights came through her family's
steading at night and she has been following that colour ever since — patiently,
alone, across a great deal of country, on the strength of one thing she believes
that nobody else does.

She is a divine caster of nature and pure water: healing, the green things, the
old quiet powers. A she-wolf called **Ruhi** walks beside her, who is not a pet
and is not tame and simply goes where she goes.

She is direct, physically unbothered by cold or dirt or long waits, has no small
talk and no social armour whatsoever, and helps without being asked or announcing
it. She is also, at this moment, the single most motivated person in Purewater,
and the thing driving her is ten years old and has never once been validated by
anybody.

*Play her if you want the emotional centre of the scenario, and a reason to care
before anybody has explained anything.*

**Starts:** at the Ford, on the landward edge, with a wolf and no money.

## Magda — the old woman who has buried everybody

Sixty-odd, built like a gatepost, grey hair cut short and badly with her own
knife, ringmail under a travelling cloak. A barbarian of the woodland faith and a
sworn servant of **Hel**, the death-goddess — who does not let her age, and who
sends her dreams she would rather not have.

She has outlived every single person she started with. She is blunt, unsentimental,
rude to people she likes and unfailingly polite to people she is considering
hitting, and she is a great deal funnier than she lets on. Enormous appetite — for
food, for sleep, for sitting down — and a real, uncomplicated pleasure in doing a
physical job properly.

In a fight she is terrifying out of all proportion to her age. Outside one she is
the most reliable adult in the story.

*Play her if you want to be the one who is not frightened, and to find out what
that costs.*

**Starts:** at the Ford, having been sent there by a nightmare she is not pleased
about.

## Randall — the Pearl thief

Purewater-born and raised in one of the pleasure houses of the Pearl by women who
are still, collectively, his family. He knows every roof, ledge, water-stair and
back door on that island and most of the rest of the city besides, and he has
never in his life been more than four minutes from somebody who would hide him.

A thief of the practical sort: locks, pockets, other people's routes, and a very
good read of a room. Quick, charming, and considerably softer-hearted than the
trade rewards. He pays the Pearl back because the Pearl raised him, and he does not
discuss his mother.

He is the only one of the four who is **home**, which means he has more to lose
here than anybody and can call in favours nobody else can reach.

*Play him if you want the city itself to be your character sheet.*

**Starts:** in Caravan Square, where something slightly odd has just happened to
him.

## Conall Bjornlasch — the warranted investigator

A militiaman's son out of Anminster who should have died in a ditch at nineteen
and was instead pulled out of it by a knight of the Swords of the Lake, taught to
read, and then taught a good deal more. The **King's Assay** finished the job and
gave him a warrant, a thumb-ring and a steel punch with his own mark on it. He
investigates things that other people would rather write off as bad luck.

An arcane practitioner of the investigative sort — find-magic, illusion, a borrowed
face when the work needs one — and a serious fighter, which surprises people who
have decided he is a clerk.

He rides in beside his patron, the elf knight **Temerach Nebulo**, on an errand
she has declined to explain, and she has been unusually quiet for two days.

He is the only one of the four who arrives with **standing** — a warrant means
doors open that would stay shut for anybody else. It also means he is the one with
something to lose by using it wrongly.

*Play him if you want to investigate, and to have the authority to make people
answer.*

**Starts:** on the Anminster road an hour out, as the di Teufel column overtakes
them.

## How to run the choice

- **Read all four before asking.** Nobody can choose from a list they have not
  heard.
- **Say what each is *for*** — the italic line. A player choosing blind is
  choosing furniture.
- **Do not rank them** and do not have a favourite out loud.
- **Do not answer "what happens to them"**, including if asked directly. Say that
  it depends on what they do, because it does.
- **The other three stay in the world** as GM-run companions and the player will
  meet them. Say so — it makes the choice feel less like a door closing.
