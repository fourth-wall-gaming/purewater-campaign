A complete scenario, separate from the Tourney plot. It needs one character with a
reason to care about the low water, and about a week of city time. It runs in an
ordinary month or after the Tourney, if the city is standing. It is an
investigation with lethal fights. Read *GM Guide: The city kills* first.

Days here are counted from the body: **day 1** is the dusk it is found.

## The truth

For nine weeks a night crew has been taking people out of the low water: drunks,
debtors, Dredger hands, apprentices nobody will chase. They go alive by black boat,
through a smugglers' run behind the Moist Oyster, to the **Warm Pool**, an old spring
of the Lady's on the Old Water beneath the Forges. There **Severin Kade**, a fire-binder
sent by [[The Awake the Dragon Movement]], tries to force an ember of Thuban's bone
into a living body through fire worked in sacred water.

Fourteen have died of it. They burn from inside: throat and lungs black, eyes milky,
skin unmarked but for cord burns at the wrists. The bodies go down the channel, come
out by the old barge pier, and are written up as *drowned* by a sergeant who is paid to.

**The buyer** is [[Cassian Bree]], at 25 GP a living body, through a third hand. He
never sees one, and nothing he does can be proved illegal. He survives this scenario.

**The cover.** The Order's alarm read the fouling as the Baron's ship, [[Ivo Calder]]
signed it off as a false reading, and it was stood down. Without the Baron in play,
use an ordinary false reading. Either way the fouling spikes on the nights Kade works.

## Who is selling them

**Tide Lord Aurelie Stane** (she/her), a senior master of [[The Harbor Masters]]. She
lives by berths, seals and impound sheds, and sits under [[Harbormaster Velen Deepkeel]],
who is honest and does not know.

**She does not do it for the silver.** The Movement's buyer delivers votes on the Anchor
Council, and with them the succession to Deepkeel. As Harbormaster she will clear the
Shoals stairs for deep-water berths and own the port. The people she sells are, to her,
*unregistered*: no berth, no guild, no entry in any book. The silver goes to her crew.
Exposure would hang her, and she will kill to prevent it.

**What makes her hard.** She can impound a boat, arrest for a maritime offence and have
evidence logged away, all with a seal. Nobody in the low water expects the harbor to care
enough about them to be behind it. **What undoes her** is Deepkeel: a properly evidenced
complaint under maritime law reaches him, and he will act on it.

**She is tidy.** Courteous, quick, bored by feeling. She corrects the figures in other
people's threats. She wants the port, and everything else is tonnage.

## Crowbill

**He is not the culprit.** Taking canal folk breaks his first rule, and the Warm Pool
breaks his third. He pays Stane to wave his cargo through, so her books can hang him, and
he does not know what she does with the rest of her nights.

When he learns, he wants her dead, quietly, with those books in his hands first. He is
merciless and useful and will spend the characters to get there. The crows chalked on
doors are *hers*, put there to frighten witnesses and to make the low water think it is
him. Showing him one is the fastest way to his table.

## The opposition, fixed before play

| Asset | Quantity | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Aurelie Stane | 1 | her office and the impound shed on the docks; never at the taking |
| Harbor patrol | 3 boats, 6 harbormen each | lawful; obey a seal; do not know, and most would balk |
| Hesk Marrow's night crew | Hesk + 6 | once Crowbill's boatmen; the *Mallard* and the *Coot*, black; gaffs, saps, a weighted net |
| Paid blades | 12 | combat 50-60, knives and cudgels, three crossbows; six on call any night |
| Sallow | 1 | a killer; garrotte and stiletto; will not face two armed guards |
| Bought Watch | Sergeant Carrow + 3 | Seaward Post, 2 GP a week; logs the drownings |
| Watchers | 10 | lamp-men and children, who think they report to Crowbill |
| Coin and paper | 350 GP | in the impound shed with her private tide-book: dates, heads, votes promised |
| Kade | 1, two apprentices | fire-binder, dangerous caster; lodges on Lantern Row as a glass merchant |
| The ember-bone | 1 | a finger of bone in a lead case, always with Kade |

Losses are real. Without Hesk there is no taking for a week. Sallow cannot be replaced.
A harborman shown what is in the shed stops obeying.

## Who knows what

- **Stane:** the buyer's third hand and the votes. Not what Kade is making.
- **Hesk:** the taking, the run and whose seal protects him. He thinks the buyers are
  temple-robbers and does not ask.
- **Kade:** the working, and the third hand who pays.
- **Carrow:** that the drownings are false, and that the harbor wants them so.
- **[[Kerrin (brown coat)]]:** that there are night boats that are not Crowbill's. Not what they carry.
- **[[Crowbill]]:** that Hesk left his boats and is spending silver. Not whose.
- **[[Harbormaster Velen Deepkeel]]:** that three impound entries do not add up.
- **[[Nus]]:** nothing. If he learns, he does something rash the same night.
- **[[Marda Blackwater]]:** has counted fourteen drowned in nine weeks, where two a
  month is normal. The Lullwater says *the Lady is taking them*.
- **[[Thorne]]:** that the Old Water had three springs the Order sealed. Not which is in use.
- **A temple sister** still measuring the eastern pools: her book spikes on fourteen nights.
- **A boatwright** who patched the *Mallard*: scorch in the bilge, an iron ring bolted
  to a thwart, a tooth, and a harbor seal on the bill. Make this somebody the character loves.

## Operations

Each has a warning and can be prevented. Roll once where the outcome is uncertain.

| When | What happens unopposed | Warning | How to stop it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Day 1, dusk | a young apprentice, two days missing, is found dead in a canal | none; this is the opening | a lamp-man across the water has stopped walking |
| Day 2, dawn | Carrow takes the body and burns it | the lamp-man ran to the Post | refuse him with authority, reach the temple first, or witness the log |
| Day 2, dusk | a harbor clerk brings a courteous word and a purse to leave it alone | asking in the low water about the drowned | none needed; refusal is remembered |
| Day 2, night | somebody is taken at the Oyster's water door | the *Mallard* moored dark from dusk | be there, follow the boat, or break it |
| Day 3, night | that person dies in Kade's gallery | the fouling rises | reach the Warm Pool by midnight |
| Day 3, night | Sallow kills a witness | an inverted crow chalked on the door at dusk | move or guard them |
| Day 4 on | the character is arrested under a harbor seal or taken outright, alive, for Kade | watchers thick, the canal empty, a patrol boat where none belongs | do not be alone; break the crew |
| A day later | somebody they love is taken, or the house burned | a chalk crow the night before | guard them |
| Day 7, night | **the full kindling**: a burning, half-mad thing with the strength of three comes up into the Forges | Kade gathers his best subject; ten blades post at the run and pier | kill or stop Kade, free the subject, or ruin the font |

If the Warm Pool is lost, Kade moves to the **Second Spring**, a round cistern under
the old pier. It is reached by the channel among the piles at low water, or by a
bricked-up stair under a deconsecrated chapel that the crew does not know exists.
Pulling the fire from its font costs him days.

## The acts

**I. The Drowned (days 1-2).** Learn what killed the apprentice, that it was not one
death, and that people are taken alive. Routes: the body, Carrow's log, Marda's count,
the sister's book, the boatwright, the lamp-man. *Unopposed:* the body burns and a
second person dies.

**II. The Low Water (days 3-5).** The opposition answers only what it actually learns.
Save a subject, keep a witness alive, break the crew, turn Carrow, get below. Nobody
here trusts a uniform: every witness is earned. *Turn:* the characters see Kade's work.

**III. The Kindling (days 6-7).** Stop it. Allies who can be won: the Prince's household
guard, who need a claim to go underground; [[The Order of the Lake Lady]], whose water
was profaned; a Sword, asked through [[Murgeroch]]; Marda's three hundred, if they
believe it; Deepkeel, whose law reaches the pier and his own Tide Lords; and Crowbill, at a price.

**The choice at the end** is how Stane falls: Deepkeel's law, which is slow and public;
Marda's crowd, which is neither; or Crowbill's knife, which leaves no names read.

**If Kade dies early, the trade ends.** There is no reason to buy more people. Act III
becomes the hunt for Stane, who runs. Hold a last chase ready, such as a night
barge in a lock with captives under the cargo, and **finish there.** Do not open a
wider conspiracy to keep the story going.

## Endings

- **Exposed.** A public trial, the fourteen names read first. Who takes the low water's
  trust, and whether it still blames Crowbill, depends on who cried the news and how.
- **Quiet.** Crowbill kills Stane and keeps her books. No trial, no names, and he owes a favour.
- **Lynched.** The crowd takes Stane off the stair, and the votes she was promised stay secret.
- **Kindled.** Something burning is loose in the Forges.
- **Horizon.** A ledger, a receipt with an ember mark, and a clerk lead towards
  [[The Ember Society]]. That is another adventure and needs its own plan.

## Running it

- The trials of force are short, lethal and shown first. A dwarf in mail beside deep
  water, a bound man in a boat and a forming fireball are hazards to name before a roll.
- The levers are people with standing: testimony over still water, an outlawry, a
  storyteller's chair, a temple's claim. Each costs somebody something.
- Kade is curious and polite and calls them vessels. Never explain him.
- Allies come when they are sent for, through their own door, knowing only what they are told.
