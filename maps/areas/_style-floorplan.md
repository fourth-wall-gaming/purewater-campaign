THE REFERENCE IMAGE IS THE AUTHORITY FOR STYLE. The supplied image is the master
map of the city of Purewater, drawn in black pen-and-ink on aged parchment. The
sheet you are drawing now belongs to the same atlas and must be drawn by the
same hand — the same parchment, the same black ink, the same lettering, the same
compass rose, scale bar, title cartouche and ruled border.

What differs is the CONVENTION, not the style. The master is a survey of a whole
city seen from above. This sheet is an ARCHITECTURAL GROUND PLAN of a single
building or site, so it uses the drawing language of a floor plan:

- Walls as solid double lines, thick for external walls and thinner for
  internal partitions.
- Doorways as gaps in the wall with a thin quarter-circle arc showing the swing.
- Windows as a thin break in the wall line.
- Stairs as a run of parallel treads with an arrow marking the direction of
  ascent; a stair going down drawn with a break line.
- Fixed furniture only where it tells you what a room is for: hearths and
  chimney breasts, bars and counters, bed platforms, tables, baths and basins,
  wells, pillars, cisterns, water-stairs where the building meets a canal.
- Each room given a small hand-lettered name in the same small-capital serif as
  the master map.

STRICT TOP-DOWN ORTHOGRAPHIC PROJECTION. The camera is straight overhead at
ninety degrees. This is drawn FLAT, as a measured plan. It is NOT a cutaway
perspective, NOT isometric, NOT oblique, NOT a three-dimensional rendering, NOT
a dungeon-crawl video-game screenshot. There are NO cast shadows, NO ambient
occlusion, NO dramatic lighting, NO glow, NO vignette, NO painterly texture, and
NO people or creatures anywhere on the sheet.

Where the building stands on the water — which in this city is most of them —
show the water in the same light grey wash with stippled shading as the master
map, and show how the building is entered from it: the water-stair, the mooring,
the jetty, the covered landing.

Monochrome: black ink, grey wash, aged parchment. Crisp, technical, archival. It
should read as a surveyor's plan bound into the same volume as the city map, not
as an illustration of a building.
