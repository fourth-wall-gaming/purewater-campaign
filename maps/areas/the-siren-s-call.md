Part high-end pleasure house, part information market, and one of the few
patches of neutral ground in the city — sited where High Isle's canals meet
the poorer districts, so the plan should show it fronting a canal junction
with landings on more than one channel.

Ground floor: a wide water-stair and a second, smaller side landing on the
lesser channel — two ways in, which is the building's whole character. An
entrance hall; a large public room with a bar and hearth where the house holds
court; and, opening off it, several small private rooms of a size for two or
three people to talk unheard, each with its own door to the passage rather
than through the public room.

Upper floor beside it on the same sheet: private chambers, and the
proprietor's own apartments with a window over both landings.

Below the water-line, mark a cellar or boat-cellar: a vaulted undercroft with
water running into it and a mooring inside, reached by a stair from the
ground floor. Draw the vaulting as hatched arcs. Leave it labelled only as
"Undercroft".
