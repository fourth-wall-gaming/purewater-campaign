THE REFERENCE IMAGE IS THE AUTHORITY. The supplied image is the master map of
the city of Purewater, drawn in black pen-and-ink on aged parchment. The sheet
you are drawing now is an ENLARGEMENT OF ONE PART OF THAT MAP — the same city,
the same survey, the same hand, simply drawn closer so that what was a few
strokes on the master becomes a street of individual houses here. It must look
like it was cut from the same atlas by the same cartographer in the same week.

Take from the reference, and reproduce exactly:

- The coastlines, canal courses, island shapes and bridge positions of this part
  of the city. Do not redesign the geography. Where the reference shows a bank,
  a channel or a quay, it goes in the same place and the same shape here, only
  larger and with more of its detail resolved.
- The palette: aged off-white parchment with mottled staining, black ink
  linework, light grey wash in the water. Nothing else. No colour.
- The hand: fine, confident, technical pen-work. Buildings as outlines, water
  with stippled and contour-hatched shading, marsh as reed tufts.
- The lettering: hand-set small-capital serif place names, letter-spaced, in
  black ink, sitting flat on the sheet.
- The furniture: an ornate compass rose with north at the top, a scale bar, a
  decorative title cartouche, a thin ruled decorative border, and a small legend
  box using the master's symbols — bridge, one-storey, two-or-more storeys,
  temple, canal.

THREE RULES, each of which a generator will break unless told not to:

1. STRICT TOP-DOWN ORTHOGRAPHIC PLAN VIEW. The camera is straight overhead at
   ninety degrees, like an architect's floor plan or a cadastral survey. This is
   drawn FLAT. It is NOT a bird's-eye view, NOT oblique, NOT isometric, NOT
   axonometric, NOT perspective. There are NO little three-dimensional house
   icons, NO pitched roofs seen from the side, NO building elevations, NO cast
   shadows, NO ambient occlusion, NO hill-shading or relief, NO vignette, NO
   glow, NO atmospheric haze, and none of the dramatic "epic fantasy atlas"
   styling. Flat, even, shadowless, archival.
2. ORGANIC, IRREGULAR BANKS. Coastlines and canal edges are ragged and
   asymmetrical, like a real silted lagoon or river delta. They are NOT smooth
   ovals, NOT round blobs, NOT concentric rings, NOT symmetrical.
3. RESOLVED HOUSE BY HOUSE. Every building is a separate small rectangular
   footprint seen from directly overhead, packed tight along the streets so the
   district reads building by building. One-storey is a plain outline;
   two-or-more is a double-lined box. This is NOT impressionistic massing, NOT
   blurry blocks, NOT low detail.

Because this sheet is an enlargement, it carries detail the master could not
hold: individual water-stairs and landing steps, mooring posts, jetties,
footbridges and their handrails, courtyards and light-wells, wells and cisterns,
boats drawn as simple flat ovals at their moorings, narrow alleys between
blocks, and the difference between a paved land-alley and a water-channel. Draw
that difference clearly — a person can walk an alley and cannot walk a canal.

Keep it monochrome: black, grey, parchment. Crisp, technical, archival
pen-and-ink cartography.
