The inland approach, where the north road out of Ashwick meets the water and
everyone entering Purewater from inland has to queue.

Draw a stretch of road country: the Ashwick Road running as hedgerow and
cart-ruts, wide enough for two carts and no wider — show the verges, and show
how little room there is to pass. Hedge lines drawn as the master map draws
field boundaries. A few farmsteads set back from it.

At the sheet's centre, the Ford itself: the road running down a shelving bank
into the water and up the far side, with the crossing marked by posts or
withies in the shallows and the water drawn pale over the gravel bar. The
queue ground on the near bank as open trampled grass, with a verge wide enough
for carts to stand off the road, and a single alehouse or shelter.

In the distance to the south, drawn small at the sheet's edge, the city as a
smear of towers and standing water.

This is country, not city: no canals, no house-by-house blocks. Fields, hedges,
road, water.
