A rough tavern deep in the Lullwater, on one of the quarter's narrowest
channels. Its sign is a half-open oyster shell — draw the sign bracket
projecting over the water as a small plan symbol.

Ground floor: the taproom taking most of the footprint, with a long bar
counter, a large hearth, and benches and trestle tables drawn as fixed
furniture; a narrow entry from the alley and a second door onto a plank
landing over the channel. A stair up in the corner. Behind the bar, a small
store and a cellar hatch with its stair drawn going down.

Draw it cramped. The building is wedged between its neighbours on both sides —
show the party walls shared with the buildings either side, and no space
between them.

Upper floor beside it: a few mean lodging rooms off a crooked passage, and a
back room larger than it needs to be with its own stair down to the landing,
for business the taproom should not see.

CELLAR, as a third aligned plan on the same sheet: a low vaulted undercroft the
full footprint of the building, barrels and stillage along one wall, the stair up
to the taproom, and -- at the end furthest from it -- a water-gate opening onto
the channel where a small boat can come in under the building. A smuggling tavern
in the Lullwater without a cellar is the one thing this plan must not be.

Mark a corner of the taproom as the storyteller's seat — a settle by the fire
with its back to the wall and a view of both doors.
