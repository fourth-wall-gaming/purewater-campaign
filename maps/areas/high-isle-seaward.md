The southern, seaward face of High Isle, where the island's quays front the
channel running down to the open sea.

A long run of timber and stone wharves standing out into the water on piles,
finger-jetties between them, ships moored alongside as flat plan outlines.
Warehouses and net-lofts packed immediately behind the quay line with their
doors to the water; capstans, bollards, stacked timber and barrels in the
yards. Narrow working canals cut in behind the quay so lighters can reach the
rear of the warehouse blocks.

The HARBOUR MASTER'S TOWER stands clear on the point where it can see down the
channel: a distinct tower within its own walled enclosure, with a signal mast
and its own landing steps.

Seaward of the quays the water carries the open-sea shading of the master map
rather than the stiller stipple of the inner canals.
