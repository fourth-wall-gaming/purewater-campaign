Two miles east of the walls, where the ground rises out of the marsh. A site
plan of the whole estate, not a floor plan of the house.

Draw: a good stone house with its courts, drawn in enough detail to show its
doors; stables for forty laid out as long ranges around a yard; orchards in
ruled rows; a walled kitchen garden; and eight acres of water-meadow running
down to the channel, drawn as open grass with drainage ditches.

In the meadow, the camp: twenty-five pavilions and tents laid out in lines,
horse-lines picketed along one side, a field forge, a cook-fire and a baggage
park with the tourney gear. Draw the tents in plan as simple shapes, regular
enough to read as a disciplined camp.

It is NOT a fortress, and the plan must make that obvious: no walls around the
estate, FOUR separate gates onto the lane, a long open river frontage, and
hedgerow boundaries rather than defences. Show every way in and mark them.

A lane running past the estate to the village; a landing on the channel.
