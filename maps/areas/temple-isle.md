A small island set apart off the north-east edge of the city, out in the
Lake's clean water, deliberately UNBRIDGED to High Isle and reached only by
the temple's own ferries. Draw no bridge to the mainland or to High Isle
anywhere on this sheet — the lack of one is the point.

This island is the exception to the house-by-house rule. It is not a tangle of
rooftops but DELIBERATE, PLANNED SACRED ARCHITECTURE, and should be drawn as a
formal architectural ground plan of the precinct: a colonnaded processional
approach up from the ferry landing, drawn as regular ranks of column circles;
the temple of the Lake Lady itself as a formal symmetrical building with its
cella and surrounding colonnade; a ring of lesser shrines around it; and the
Sacred Confluence — a great round pool, geometrically true, fed where the
purest lake-water wells up, with its channels, basins, sluices and stepped
margins all set out precisely.

Formal, measured, geometric. The contrast with the organic city is deliberate:
everything here is laid out on an axis. The island's shoreline, however, stays
natural and irregular, with the temple's own stone quays and ferry landings
cut into it.
