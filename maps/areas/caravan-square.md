Purewater's only land approach. From the western mainland a thin promontory
reaches eastward and swells at its tip into a larger outcrop: Caravan Square,
where the long borderland road meets the city and the Gate of Triumph opens
onto the first bridges and quays. This is the southern jaw of the Mouth.

Draw: the promontory narrow and ragged, with the road running its length as a
clear paved way — distinct from any canal — and the square itself as a large
open paved ground at the tip. The Gate of Triumph as a formal gatehouse
structure astride the road where it enters, with a short stretch of wall to
either side.

BOTH GATES STAND ON THE WESTERN PROMONTORY, on this side of the water, and
neither is on High Isle. The Gate of Triumph is the landward gate where the
Caravan Road arrives; THE LAND GATE stands at the square's eastern edge, where
the quays begin and everything must take to boats. Label the Land Gate on the
promontory, not across the channel.

The square's working fabric: beast-pens laid out in rows, goods staged in
marked-out lots, wagon-standing with the carts drawn as simple plan
rectangles, inns and warehouses around the edges, water troughs and a well.
Beyond the square, the first quays, where everything must take to boats — a
stepped landing with ranks of moored lighters and barges waiting to carry
cargo across the landward channel to the Merchant's Quarter opposite.

In tourney week this ground is crowded. Show it as built for crowding.
