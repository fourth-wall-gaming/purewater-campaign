The oldest house on Pearl Quay and the seat of authority over the whole
island. It has never needed to advertise, and the plan should say so: a plain
water-stair, a single door, a doorman's lodge immediately inside it, and NO
lantern brackets, no projecting sign, no lamps strung over the water.

Ground floor: the entry and the lodge; a formal receiving room where the
mistress of the house sees people, placed so that a visitor must pass the
lodge and a second door to reach it; a hearth; and a quiet garden court behind
with a wall high enough to be drawn as a heavy line.

Upper floor beside it on the same sheet: her own apartments over the quay with
windows the length of the frontage, a private stair down to the court, and
separate quarters for the household.

Everything about the layout should read as control of approach: one way in,
sightlines from the lodge, and no route to the inner rooms that does not pass
someone. It is the grandest house on the Quay and the least showy.
