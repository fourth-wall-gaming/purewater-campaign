THE FIRST REFERENCE IMAGE IS THE AUTHORITY FOR STYLE. It is the master map of
the city of Purewater, drawn in black pen-and-ink on aged parchment. The sheet
you are drawing now belongs to the same atlas and must be drawn by the same hand
— the same parchment, the same COOL BLACK AND GREY ink (no sepia, no brown, no
warm tan), the same lettering, the same compass rose, scale bar, title cartouche
and ruled border.

THE CARTOUCHE CARRIES THIS SHEET'S OWN TITLE — the name of this building or
site, given in the brief below. Never write "THE CITY OF PUREWATER" on this
sheet. Do not include the master's legend box: bridges and canal symbols mean
nothing on a building plan.

What differs from the master is the CONVENTION, not the style. The master
surveys a whole city. This sheet is an ARCHITECTURAL GROUND PLAN of one building
or site, so it uses the drawing language of a floor plan:

- Walls as solid double lines, thick for external walls, thinner for internal
  partitions. Walls are FLAT BLACK LINES — no bevel, no emboss, no drop-shadow,
  no three-dimensional thickness, no shading to suggest height.
- Doorways as gaps in the wall with a thin quarter-circle arc showing the swing.
- Windows as a thin break in the wall line.
- Stairs as a run of parallel treads with an arrow for the direction of ascent;
  a stair going down drawn with a break line.
- Fixed furniture only where it tells you what a room is for: hearths and
  chimney breasts, bars and counters, beds, tables, baths and basins, wells,
  pillars, cisterns, and the water-stairs where the building meets a canal.

EVERY ROOM IS NAMED. This is not optional and it is the single most useful thing
on the sheet. Hand-letter a short name inside each room — in the same
small-capital serif as the master map — saying what the room is FOR. A plan with
unlabelled rooms cannot be used at a table. Label every enclosed space,
including passages, courts, cellars and service rooms.

Where a plan shows more than one storey or level, draw each as a separate plan
on the same sheet, titled, and ALIGN THEM: the external wall outline must be
drawn identically and in the same position on every level, so a reader can see
what lies above and below what.

STRICT TOP-DOWN ORTHOGRAPHIC PROJECTION. The camera is straight overhead at
ninety degrees. Drawn FLAT, as a measured plan. NOT a cutaway, NOT perspective,
NOT isometric, NOT oblique, NOT a three-dimensional rendering, NOT a video-game
battlemap. NO cast shadows, NO ambient occlusion, NO dramatic lighting, NO glow,
NO vignette, NO painterly texture, NO people or creatures.

Where the building stands on the water — which here is most of them — show the
water in the same grey wash and stippling as the master map, and show how the
building is entered from it: the water-stair, the mooring, the jetty, the
covered landing.

Monochrome: black ink, grey wash, aged parchment. Crisp, technical, archival. It
should read as a surveyor's plan bound into the same volume as the city map, not
as an illustration of a building.

AREA BRIEF -- The Vantt Estate

THE TITLE CARTOUCHE ON THIS SHEET READS: The Vantt Estate

Two miles east of the walls, where the ground rises out of the marsh. A site
plan of the whole estate.

A good stone house with its courts and doors; stables for forty as long ranges
round a yard; orchards in ruled rows; a walled kitchen garden; and eight acres
of water-meadow running down to the channel, as open grass with drainage
ditches.

In the meadow, the DRAGON KNIGHTS' CAMP: twenty-five pavilions in disciplined
lines, horse-lines picketed along one side, a field forge, cook-fires, and a
baggage park with the tourney gear and a covered litter.

IT IS NOT A FORTRESS, and the plan must make that obvious at a glance:
- no wall or ditch around the estate, only hedgerow boundaries;
- FOUR separate gates onto the lane, each drawn as a gap with gateposts and
  each labelled "Gate";
- a long open river frontage with a landing on the channel and no fence to it;
- the lane running past the estate toward the village.

Mark the track the local labour uses daily: from the lane past the kitchen
door to the horse lines, which is how a farrier's boy comes and goes without
anyone searching him.

LETTER EXACTLY THESE LABELS, spelled as written, and no other place names:
  - The Vantt Estate
  - The Water-Meadow
  - The Dragon Knights' Camp
  - Stables
  - Orchards
  - The Channel
  - The Lane
