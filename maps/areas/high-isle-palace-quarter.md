The north-east quarter of High Isle: the official and noble frontage facing
across the Lake toward Temple Isle.

PALACE SQUARE as a formal paved plaza drawn as open flagging. The GOVERNOR'S
PALAZZO on its lake-facing side, built round interior courtyards. The HALL OF
JUSTICE and the BARRACKS as larger formal blocks with their own yards. The
houses of the nobility ringing the square as big double-lined palazzi with
garden courts and private water-stairs.

THE TOURNAMENT GROUND occupies the open ground between the square and the lake
shore, drawn in outline: a roped rectangle of raked sand with tiered stands on
three sides, the Governor's box at its western end, and the long archery butts
running out toward the water.

A broad branch of the Grand Canal cuts along the quarter's inner edge with
substantial stone bridges over it. Along the lake frontage, a dressed-stone
embankment with landing steps, mooring posts, barges and gondolas.

This is the rich end of the island: larger blocks, wider and straighter
streets than elsewhere, and real open ground. The contrast with the packed
tenements of the Lullwater should be obvious at a glance -- but the blocks are
still irregular, and a narrow service canal or two still threads in behind the
grand frontages.
