The north-east quarter of High Isle, the official and noble frontage facing
out across the Lake toward Temple Isle — the prestigious ground, and on the
master map the quarter holding the Governor's Palazzo, the Hall of Justice and
the Barracks beside the walled tournament ground.

Draw: Palace Square as a formal paved plaza, drawn as open flagging rather
than as buildings, with the Governor's Palazzo on its lake-facing side built
around interior courtyards; the Hall of Justice and the Barracks as larger
formal blocks with their own yards; the houses of the nobility ringing the
square in big double-lined palazzi with garden courts and private
water-stairs. A broad branch of the Grand Canal cuts along the quarter's inner
edge with substantial stone bridges over it. Along the lake frontage, a
dressed-stone embankment with landing steps and mooring posts, and barges and
gondolas at them.

This is the rich end of the island: blocks are larger, streets are wider and
straighter than elsewhere in the city, and there is open ground. Keep the
contrast with the packed tenements of other sheets visible at a glance.
