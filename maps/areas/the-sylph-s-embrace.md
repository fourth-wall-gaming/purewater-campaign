An elegant, high-end pleasure house on Pearl Quay, its walls thick enough to
muffle the night's business — draw the external and party walls noticeably
heavier than the internal partitions, because that thickness is the building's
defining feature.

Ground floor: the water-stair and covered landing from Pearl Quay; an entrance
hall and a receiving salon with hearth; a stair up; service rooms to the rear —
kitchen, scullery, laundry — opening onto a small back court with a well.

Upper floor, drawn as a second plan beside the first on the same sheet: a
central passage with the private rooms opening off it, each a separate chamber
with a bed platform, a washstand and a window to the quay or the court. One
room larger and better appointed than the others. An armoire drawn against the
wall of one chamber — a fixed piece of furniture, unremarked.

Mark the proprietor's own room and a small office or counting-room near the
entrance where the house's business is done.

Label rooms by function only. Do not mark any event, body, or crime scene on
the plan — this is the building, not the night.
