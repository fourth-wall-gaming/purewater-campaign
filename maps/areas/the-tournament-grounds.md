The ground where the campaign ends. A site plan rather than a building plan:
the whole tournament ground laid out measured and to scale.

THE LISTS: a rectangle of raked sand inside a rope, drawn with the sand
stippled and the rope line dotted. Tiered timber stands going up on THREE
sides only, drawn in plan as ranks of parallel bench lines. The Governor's box
at the WESTERN end, raised, hung with cloth, drawn as a distinct roofed
structure.

THE LONG BUTTS: adjoining, two hundred paces of flat grass marked out between
the water and the stands, with the shooting line at the near end by the
Governor's box and the targets at the far end. Mark the distance along it.

Behind the stands, the open slope where the rest of the city watches, drawn as
rising ground with contour hatching.

The whole point of this ground is that EVERYTHING IS VISIBLE FROM EVERYWHERE.
Keep sightlines open: no walls or buildings interrupting the middle. Show the
shallows at the foot of the temple steps, where the Order stands waist-deep,
in plain view of the stands, and the open water beyond them with the mainland
at the sheet's edge.

Behind the Governor's box, mark the small room where the winning champion is
presented, alone and unarmed — a single enclosed chamber with one door.

Include a scale bar in paces as well as feet.
