Purewater's poor-and-criminal quarter, across the canal east of High Isle's
residential edge, sloping south-east toward the docks of the Shoals.

The narrowest and most crooked canals in the city: a drowned maze, branching,
doubling back and dead-ending, some barely a boat wide. Tenements leaning over
them until they almost touch overhead. Building footprints small, irregular
and jammed together with no gap, built out over the water on piles where the
ground has given out. Blind courts and alleys that lead nowhere. Plank
footbridges everywhere.

Mark the Moist Oyster as a tavern wedged on an inner channel, and the
Dredgers' Hall as a larger plain block with its own working landing and boat
shelter. At the south-east edge, where the quarter runs into the Shoals, small
unofficial jetties pushed out among the legitimate ones.

It must read as somewhere a stranger gets lost, and the contrast with the
broad straight streets of the Palace Quarter is the point.

Two or three channels end at a stone arch or a stair-mouth going down below
the waterline. Draw these as unlabelled stair symbols only.
