Purewater's poor-and-criminal quarter, across the canal east of High Isle's
residential edge and sloping south-east toward the docks of the Shoals. On the
master map it is the low sprawling district labelled the Thieves' Quarter.

Draw the narrowest, most crooked canals in the whole city — a drowned maze,
branching and dead-ending, some channels barely a boat wide, with tenements
leaning over them until the buildings almost touch overhead. Footbridges
everywhere, many of them plank crossings rather than stone. Building
footprints small, irregular and jammed together with almost no gap, built over
and across the water on piles where the ground has given out. Courts and
blind alleys that lead nowhere.

Mark the Moist Oyster as a tavern on one of the inner channels and the
Dredgers' Hall as a larger plain block with its own working landing and boat
shelter. At the quarter's south-east edge, where it runs into the Shoals,
small unofficial jetties pushed out among the legitimate ones.

It must read as somewhere a stranger gets lost. The contrast against the
broad straight streets of the Palace Quarter is the point of the sheet.

Some channels end in a stone arch or a stair-mouth going down below the
waterline. Draw two or three of these as unlabelled stair symbols only.
