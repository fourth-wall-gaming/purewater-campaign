The eastern point of High Isle, the city's industrial belt, crowding the
seaward channel. Many of the works are Dwarven-run. On the master map this is
the quarter labelled The Forges, at the island's east point.

THE INDUSTRY MUST BE UNMISTAKABLE AT A GLANCE. A forge is not a house with a
different label on it. Draw the works as deep, wide-span sheds two or three
times the footprint of a dwelling, with an open working yard in front of each, a
chimney stack as a heavy filled circle, and no domestic subdivision inside.
Scatter the furniture of the trade across the yards -- charcoal and ore heaps as
stippled mounds, quenching troughs, stacked billets, cranes at the water -- so
that with every label covered the quarter still reads as industry. Tanning pits
in regular rows behind the tanneries, each a small dark rectangle. Keep actual
housing to a thin fringe at the inland edge, visibly smaller and finer-grained.

Draw also: boatyards with slipways and building-berths cut into the bank with the
ribs of part-built hulls on them; tanning pits in rows behind the tanneries;
charcoal and ore heaps; cranes at the water.

At the quarter's seaward edge, a private pier standing apart from the working
berths with a single large ornate vessel moored at it — the DragonBarge —
drawn in plan from directly above, with its dragon-head prow and square stern
clear. Label the pier and the vessel.

Chimneys may be drawn as small circles in plan; do not draw smoke in
perspective, and do not let any of it become three-dimensional.
