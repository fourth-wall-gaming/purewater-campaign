The seaward jaw of the Mouth: a peninsula reaching in from the north-east to
form the eastern side of the strait, where the Lake's outflow meets the open
sea. On the master map this is the labelled district of fishmarkets and
wharves on the eastern side.

Draw: the working waterfront in full — fish-markets as open halls with rows of
stall footprints and drying-racks behind them; ship-chandlers and net-lofts;
the wharves standing on piles with fishing craft moored in ranks; slipways;
the long timber groynes and breakwaters that take the sea's weather.

Inland of the quay line, the housing of the people who work it: small, plain,
tightly packed, poorer than High Isle. Toward the west, where the peninsula
runs back toward the Lullwater, the channels narrow and the jetties become
irregular and half-hidden, tucked behind warehouses and under buildings.

Small shrine-niches to drowned sailors at the heads of the jetties, drawn as
tiny plan symbols. Mark the Harbour Master's Tower if it falls within this
sheet's frame.
