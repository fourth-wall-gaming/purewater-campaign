Draw the plan of THE LISTS, the walled tournament ground of a canal city, as one survey sheet, landscape 16:9. Attach the Purewater city map as the reference for style only.

WHERE IT STANDS -- this must agree with the city map:
- A walled ground about 600 feet across, on the north-east corner of the city's northern island, where the island meets the open lake. North is up.
- Its WEST wall is long and straight, with two square towers along it and a gatehouse near its middle opening onto a street of tall houses. Its NORTH wall runs east-south-east from the north-west corner tower, with a square tower and a small gate in it, and dense houses outside. Its EAST wall runs north to south along the LAKE SHORE: a stone embankment with the lake's grey water right against its foot, and a water-gate in it. Its SOUTH wall is not straight but curves along the streets, with a cluster of gate-buildings at its south-west and a second gatehouse toward the south-east.
- Just off the north-east, a small rocky islet in the lake. Show the lake in grey wash with contour hatching along the shore, a few boats, and the street-edges of the city around the other three sides, cut off by the sheet.

THE SHEET: the whole ground as a SITE PLAN filling the left two-thirds, with a scale bar in feet and a faint grid of ten-foot squares. In the right third, two INSET PLANS at a larger scale, each in its own ruled frame with a faint five-foot grid: the Governor's box with the presentation room behind it, and the water-gate.

THE SITE PLAN:
- In the middle of the ground, THE LISTS: a six-sided arena of raked sand, its long axis running from the north-west to the south-east, ringed by a low rope barrier on posts and a sanded walk. Around it, on all six sides, tiered timber STANDS, drawn in plan as rows of narrow parallel benches stepping outward, with radial stairs cut through them at intervals and the timber framing showing at their outer edge.
- On the WEST side, built into the stands, THE GOVERNOR'S BOX: a raised, covered pavilion wider than the rest of the stand, with its own stair, a canopy shown by a fine dashed outline, and rows of chairs. Behind it, against the outer side of the stand, a small enclosed timber-and-stone building: THE PRESENTATION ROOM.
- Two gaps through the ring of stands for the combatants, east and west, each a short gated passage from the sand to the open ground.
- Between the stands and the NORTH wall, a long strip of open grass, THE LONG BUTTS, about two hundred paces: a shooting line of pegs at the west end, and straw butts and targets at the east end, with a low earth bank behind them.
- In the open ground inside the walls: the COMPETITORS' ENCLOSURE in the south-west, a fenced yard of square pavilion tents with guy-lines, weapon racks and an arming bench; a horse-line and picket along the south wall; a small stone HERALDS' HOUSE by the west gate; a row of stalls along the inside of the south wall; a well; and a small timber infirmary near the east gap.
- At the WATER-GATE on the east wall, broad stone steps go down into the lake, with mooring posts and boats tied up.
- The open ground is beaten earth with worn paths, drawn with fine stipple; the sand of the lists finer and paler.

INSET 1 -- THE GOVERNOR'S BOX AND THE PRESENTATION ROOM, at about four times the scale:
- The box: a raised timber platform at the front of the stand, a front rail, two rows of good chairs with one larger chair in the middle, a canopy on posts shown dashed, a short private stair down at the back.
- Behind it, through a single door, THE PRESENTATION ROOM: small, about fifteen feet by twenty, stone-walled, with one heavy door from the box stair and a second door to the outside, no windows, a table, two chairs, a stand for a cloak, and a brazier. Its doors are drawn shut.

INSET 2 -- THE WATER-GATE, at about four times the scale:
- An arched gate through the thick east wall with a portcullis slot, a guard-room on one side, and broad steps going down into the lake between mooring posts, with two boats tied up.
STYLE -- the same cartographer as the attached map of Purewater, now drawing one building at the scale of an architect's survey:
- Black and cool grey pen-and-ink on aged off-white parchment with soft mottled staining. No colour: no sepia, no brown, no warm tan. Black on bone.
- Strict top-down orthographic FLOOR PLAN, as if the roof and upper floors were lifted off: a horizontal cut through each storey about four feet above its floor. NOT isometric, NOT perspective, NOT a cutaway, NOT a bird's-eye view. No cast shadows, no 3D, no glow, no vignette.
- Walls as solid black poché, thick for outer and party walls, thinner for partitions. Doors as a gap with a thin quarter-circle swing; windows as three fine lines across the wall; stairs as rows of parallel treads with an arrow line; fireplaces and chimney breasts drawn into the walls.
- Furniture in plan, fine and specific: beds, chairs, tables, chests, wardrobes, screens, rugs as ruled rectangles with fringes, barrels, crates, lamps as small circles.
- Floors shown by material: floorboards as fine parallel lines, flagstones as an irregular stone grid, tiles as a small square grid.
- Water exactly as the city map draws it: grey wash with stipple and contour hatching along the edges; boats as simple flat ovals; water-stairs as treads running down into the water.
- A faint square grid for play (ten-foot squares on the site plan, five-foot on the insets), fine enough not to fight the drawing. A scale bar in FEET. A small compass rose with north up.
- A thin ruled double border round the sheet, and an empty plain title panel in one corner.
- NO LETTERING ANYWHERE. No room names, no numbers, no labels, no text of any kind, not even on the scale bar or title panel. They will be added afterwards.
