Formally *the Assay of Mystamyr*; universally **the Assay**. Not a college, not a
cult, and emphatically not a guild of adventuring wizards -- a **chartered
inspectorate**, established by the Crown in the years after the uprising for the
plainest possible reason: the realm had just finished a war against men who ruled
by magic, and it never intended to be surprised by that again.

**What they actually do.** License practitioners. Inspect and hallmark enchanted
goods so a buyer knows what he is holding. Keep **the Register** -- who may work,
what they may work, and what they have made. Investigate unlicensed practice, and
prosecute the proscribed kind.

They are, in consequence, about as popular as any body that can close your
workshop.

**Ranks:** Prentice, Marker, Assayer, Master Assayer, and a governing **Table** of
seven Masters who keep the Register at Sharna.

**The marks of the office** -- and there is nothing on the body, no sigil, no
brand:

- **The warrant.** Folded, sealed, renewed each year. It is the authority, and
  producing it changes a conversation.
- **The punch.** A small steel die bearing the assayer's own mark, struck into
  everything they pass. Each punch is unique and recorded in the Register. **An
  assayer who loses their punch is in very serious trouble**, because a punch in
  the wrong hands licenses anything.
- **The thumb-ring**, assay silver, struck with the bearer's own mark. Everyday
  identification, and hard to forge precisely because the mark is on file.

## Who they take

**Outsiders, on purpose, and for entirely unsentimental reasons.**

An inspectorate is only worth its charter if it cannot be leaned on, and a man
with a great family behind him can always be leaned on -- through the family. So
the Assay recruits the people nobody can apply pressure to: foundlings, foreigners,
the second sons of nobody, a militiaman's foster boy pulled out of a ditch at
nineteen and taught to read. It is the most nakedly meritocratic institution in
Mystamyr and it did not get that way out of principle. It got that way because a
Register kept by well-connected men is worth nothing.

The result is a body of competent, unplaceable people with no natural constituency
and no social protection, holding a warrant that outranks local convenience. The
city's comfortable arrangements do not include them, which is the whole point, and
which is also why nobody in Purewater is glad to see one.

**It is not connected to [[The Swords of the Lake]], and there is no arrangement
between them.** A Crown inspectorate and a goddess's knights have separate
charters, separate loyalties, and no institutional business with each other at
all. [[Temerach Nebulo]] did not come to Conall through the Assay; **Old Johz
delivered Conall to *her***, personally, and then left -- and neither body was
consulted.

But an elf who rose to greatness among human knights and a ditch-boy who rose
among Crown assayers **recognise each other on sight**. Both were let in by an
institution that judged them on what they could do and not on who they came from.
Both hold an oath that answers somewhere other than this city. Neither of them
belongs here, and neither of them can be bought with anything Purewater has to
offer. That kinship is real, it is entirely personal, and it is not a treaty
between orders -- which is exactly why it will hold when treaties would not.

**Stated aim:** the responsible stewardship of arcane knowledge. **Real aim:** the
same, honestly -- plus an institution's ordinary appetite for jurisdiction,
precedence, and being the body that other bodies must consult.

## Where they stand, Tourney week

**Their man in Purewater is here by accident and holds no instructions.**
[[Conall]] came as [[Temerach Nebulo]]'s companion, on an errand she has not
explained. He carries the training, the methods, the warrant and the authority by
association, and nobody sent him.

The Assay itself is *elsewhere* -- a Table at Sharna, a correspondence, and a
distance measured in days. That is deliberate. It is a resource the party reaches
for and cannot command, and the delay is the point: writing to the Assay is a real
action with a real cost in time, and the answer arrives after the thing has
happened.

**And this is squarely their jurisdiction.** Seating a spirit in an unwilling
person is a capital crime under the King's law, and the Assay is the body that
brings that charge. A borderland baron holding a spirit inside a living child for
ten years, working from nine folded sheets in a locked case, is not a scandal to
them. It is the exact thing they were chartered to find.

## The door

**Old Johz** -- the Master Assayer who recognised [[Conall]] as the Baron's son
when he was a boy in Anminster, healed him years later in Sharna, trained him
*"so that power didn't fall into the wrong hands"*, and then delivered him to
Temerach and abruptly departed. Nobody has said where he went, and an assayer
walking away from the Register without a word is itself irregular. His absence is
a live question.

**For anyone else:** the Assay keeps correspondents in most cities of size, and a
warrant opens doors. It is worth far more with [[Abel Weir]], [[Maro Quist]] and
[[Harbormaster Velen Deepkeel]] than with any criminal in Purewater -- and rather
less than nothing with [[Crowbill]], who has spent his life avoiding exactly this
kind of paper.

## What they can do for you, and the price

| | |
|---|---|
| **Warranted authority** | a jurisdiction that is neither the city's nor the army's, and that the standing order does not touch |
| **A written finding** | Assay findings are evidence a council cannot wave away, and the basis of a capital charge |
| **Counsel on the possession** | the likeliest expertise on what was done to [[Cailan]] and to [[Emmeralda]] -- by letter, slowly |
| **The Register** | who is licensed, what they made, and whose mark is on it. Nine folded sheets in an unregistered hand are already an offence |
| **Identification** | Read Magic, Detect Magic, and the vocabulary to know what you are looking at |

**The price is the doctrine: *establish, record, refer.*** Assayers investigate
and write it down; they do not act, and they certainly do not improvise. Every
genuinely decisive thing the party does this week will be something the Assay
would have advised against, and Conall will have to decide -- more than once --
whether he is an investigator or a man with a sword and a brother.

## Friction

Structurally resented by [[The Watercrafters Guild]]: a guild that *makes*
enchanted goods and a body that decides whether they may be sold is a quarrel with
no end, and the guild has been fighting Assay hallmarking for two hundred years.
Respected by [[The Order of the Lake Lady]] as a fellow institution, with the usual
condescension in both directions between a water-faith and a Crown inspectorate.
Useful to and used by the [[The City Watch]] and [[The Harbor Masters]], who like
anyone with a jurisdiction they can refer things to. Regarded by [[The Quiet Hand]]
as well-meaning, honest, and far too slow to be of use.

**A note on Hesper.** Because the Assay is the most visible arcane authority in
these parts, the sorcerer [[Hesper]] -- Marisette's enforcer in the Pearl -- is
sometimes loosely assumed to hold a warrant. Hesper does not, and the assumption
suits Hesper fine. *(GM: Hesper's true allegiance lies elsewhere -- see [[The Quiet Hand]].)*

## Hooks

- Conall writes to the Table. What he puts in the letter and what he leaves out is
  a real decision, with an answer that arrives late.
- Where did Old Johz go, and why immediately after handing Conall to Temerach?
- A warrant produced at the right moment gets the referral book opened, the guild's
  assessment reviewed, or a councillor to grant an interview.
- **Somebody tries to steal an assayer's punch.** With it you can license anything,
  and the Register will back you until the fraud is found.
- The doctrine says establish and report. The week says act now. Play the cost of
  both, and make him choose in front of people.

**Agendas on the clock:** none. The Assay is deliberately off the board --
reachable, slow, and not a lever anyone can pull twice.
