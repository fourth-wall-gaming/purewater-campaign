A magical order of arcane practitioners -- a true institution, with a hall, ranks, and a doctrine -- recognizable by the blue star that blazes at a master's forehead. They represent institutional magical authority and take a long, careful interest in dangerous power falling into the wrong hands.

**They are not a mystery cult and they are not adventurers.** The Blue Star is closer to a chartered profession: examinations, a hall, correspondence, seniority, and an intense institutional preference for *reporting* over *acting*. A Blue Star investigator is expected to establish what is true, write it down accurately, and let the order decide. That doctrine is a straitjacket in a week like this one, and it is the source of most of the interesting pressure on [[Conall]].

**Stated aim:** the responsible stewardship of arcane knowledge. **Real aim:** the same, honestly -- plus an institution's ordinary appetite for precedence, reputation, and being the body that other bodies consult.

## Where they stand, Tourney week

**Conall is their man in Purewater and he was not sent here.** He came as [[Temerach Nebulo]]'s companion, on an errand she has not explained. That makes his position ambiguous: he carries the order's training, its methods and its authority-by-association, and no instructions.

The order itself is *elsewhere* -- a hall, a correspondence, and a distance measured in days. This is deliberate. The Blue Star is a resource the party reaches for and does not command, and the delay is the point: a letter to the order is a real action with a real cost in time, and the answer arrives after the thing has happened.

**What the order would want to know**, if asked: that a borderland baron has held a spirit inside a living child for ten years using a school of magic that is not supposed to exist any more, and that there are nine folded sheets in a locked case proving it. That is precisely the kind of thing the Blue Star exists to take an interest in.

## The door

**Old Johz** -- the wizard who recognised [[Conall]] as the Baron's son when he was a boy in Anminster, healed him years later in Sharna, trained him *"so that power didn't fall into the wrong hands"*, and then delivered him to Temerach and abruptly departed. Nobody has said where he went. He is the order's face in this story and his absence is a live question.

For anyone else: the order has correspondents in most cities and a seal that opens doors. A Blue Star badge is worth more with [[Abel Weir]] and [[Maro Quist]] than with any criminal in Purewater.

## What they can do for you, and the price

| | |
|---|---|
| **Counsel on the possession** | the likeliest source of expertise on what was done to [[Cailan]] and to [[Emmeralda]] -- by letter, slowly |
| **Institutional weight** | a written finding from the Blue Star is evidence a council cannot wave away |
| **Direct intervention** | possible, and slow. They may well move against the Baron -- afterwards |
| **Standing with lawful men** | the badge is a warrant everywhere that respects warrants |
| **Training and identification** | Read Magic, Detect Magic, and the vocabulary to know what you are looking at |

**The price is the doctrine.** They expect to be told the truth, in order, in writing -- and they expect their investigators not to improvise. Every genuinely decisive thing the party does this week will be something the order would have advised against.

## Friction

Respected by the [[The Order of the Lake Lady]] as a fellow institution, with the usual condescension in both directions between a water-faith and an arcane college. Watched warily by the [[The Watercrafters Guild]], who guard their monopoly. Of professional interest to [[The Quiet Hand]], who consider institutions slow.

**A note on Hesper.** Because the Blue Star is the most visible mage-order in these parts, the sorcerer [[Hesper]] -- Marisette's enforcer in the Pearl -- is sometimes loosely assumed to be one of its wizards. Hesper is **not** of the Blue Star, and the assumption suits Hesper fine. *(GM: Hesper's true allegiance lies elsewhere -- see [[The Quiet Hand]].)*

## Hooks

- Conall writes to the order. What he chooses to put in the letter, and what he leaves out, is a real decision with a real answer arriving late.
- Where did Old Johz go, and why immediately after handing Conall to Temerach?
- The order's methods say *establish and report*. The week says *act now*. Play the cost of both.
- A Blue Star seal gets the referral book opened, the guild's assessment reviewed, or a councillor to grant an interview.

**Agendas on the clock:** none. The Blue Star is deliberately off the board -- reachable, slow, and not a lever anyone can pull twice.
