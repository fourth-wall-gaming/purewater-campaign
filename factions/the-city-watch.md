Ninety-odd watchmen in blue-grey, under **Watch-Captain Ottilie Sarn**, run out of
posts on each of the big isles. They are not soldiers. Their ordinary work is
drunks, cutpurses, canal-right disputes, dead men fished out of the Lullwater, and
keeping the Pearl from catching fire. They are reasonably honest by the standards
of a port and thoroughly outmatched by anybody in armour.

**The standing order.** Seven days before the Tourney -- the morning after the
first complaints about the Baron's advance detachment came in -- the watch
received a written instruction from the Governor's council: *the Baron's people are guests of the
city and of the Crown's representative; complaints against them are to be recorded
and referred, and not acted upon, until the Tourney is concluded.* Recorded and
referred. Not acted upon.

It came down over Prince Emeric's signature. He signed it because three men on his
council told him it would keep the peace during the festival, and he is
two-and-twenty and badly advised. **The same three men later have him sign the
instruction that silences the temple's fouling alarm.** A party that pulls on the
watch's order is pulling on the same thread as the party that investigates the
Lake's corruption, and neither of them knows it yet.

**What this does to the watch.** It humiliates them. Sergeants have started
routing patrols away from the Merchant's Quarter rather than be seen not acting.
The referral book at the Isle post is four inches thick and it is only a week old. Some of them have decided
that recording is at least something and are doing it obsessively; others have
started drinking off duty in places where dragonshits do not go.

**Sergeant [[Abel Weir]]** keeps the book. He is the crack in the order and the
party's most useful lawful ally.

**Stated aim:** keep the peace through the festival. **Real aim,** among the ones
who still care: get one of these incidents in front of somebody who outranks the
council -- the Prince himself, the Order of the Lake Lady, or a Sword of the Lake
-- without any watchman having to be the one who disobeyed.
