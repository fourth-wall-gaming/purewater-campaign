Born as an association of canal dredgers who clear silt and refuse from the waterways, now a broad movement of laborers, fishers, and the working poor of [[The Lullwater]] and [[The Shoals]]. They keep a guildhall in the Lullwater and safe houses across the poor districts, and wear plain black armbands marked with a white wave.

**Ranks:** Hands, Voices, Channels, Harbors, and an executive committee of Pilots.

**Leadership.** [[Marda Blackwater]] -- a broad-shouldered woman in her forties who rose from the dredge-boats during a labor dispute five years ago; practical, fiery, and devoted to the working class. Brawn 75, Oratory 78, Influence 75, and the only person in Purewater who can put three hundred bodies anywhere she likes.

**Stated aim:** fair wages, safe conditions, respect for essential work. **Real aim:** to remake Purewater's power structure through worker solidarity and a seat in its governance -- the radical wing wants to overthrow the order entirely.

## Where they stand, Tourney week

**They are the powder.** Everything the occupation does, it does to them first. The Knights commandeer boats off the water-stairs, and the stairs are Dredger work. They take food off stalls the Dredgers' families keep. A dockhand named **Emmit Rill** had his jaw broken with a mailed hand six days before the Tourney and has not worked since -- complaint 31 in [[Abel Weir]]'s referral book. **Two boatmen rowed out by Knights have not come back**, and no body has been found, which is somehow worse.

Marda has petitioned the Harbor Masters and got sympathy and a referral. She has petitioned the council in writing and been ignored. She does not want a riot and she is going to get one anyway, because she has three hundred angry people, a watch under written orders not to help, and the whole city's Tourney provisioning passing through her members' hands.

**If nobody helps her get redress the lawful way, the stairs stop on `d-1/dawn`** -- no cartage, no fish, no ice, no lamp oil, and no competitors' gear moved to Temple Isle. A stopped port during a sacred festival is a crisis, and it is the pressure point at which even the Baron has to give something up.

## The door

**Marda herself**, and she is the most approachable major figure in the city -- she will talk to anyone who takes the dockhands seriously, in the guildhall, in daylight, without ceremony. She is also the easiest to *help*: get Rill's wages paid, get the two boatmen accounted for, or get somebody official to hear the complaint, and she will hold her people another day. Every single time.

Below her, any Hand on the Shoals stair will talk to a stranger who buys the beer and does not talk down.

## What they can do for you, and the price

| | |
|---|---|
| **Eyes on every quay** | who landed, what was in it, which pier it went to. Nothing moves on this water unseen |
| **Boats and boatmen** | including men who will row somewhere they should not, at night |
| **Three hundred bodies** | a crowd, a picket, a wall of people -- the one thing the Baron's plan cannot survive on the Tourney ground |
| **Hands and backs** | anything needing lifting, digging, dragging or hauling out of water |
| **A bed in the Lullwater** | where nobody official goes |

**The price is solidarity, and it is checked.** Marda gives nothing to people who treat her members as scenery. Help once, publicly, and the whole guild knows your face by evening.

## Friction

Bitterly hostile to the [[The Dragon Knights]] as the purest face of aristocratic oppression, and now with names and injuries attached. Open contempt from the [[The Harbor Masters]], returned with interest. Complicated toward [[Crowbill]] -- he employs their people and undercuts their leverage, and his code is the only law on the water that has ever been enforced. Cautiously allied with the progressive wing of the [[The Order of the Lake Lady]], who need labour for the Tourney and dislike what is being done to the poor.
