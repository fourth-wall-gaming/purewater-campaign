Purewater's criminal underground: canal smuggling, gambling dens, and protection rackets, run by [[Crowbill]] -- a wiry, forgettable man whose only distinctive mark is a small crow tattoo on the back of his right hand. His people signal with crow symbols, and he keeps hidden boat-paths through the canals and safe houses across the city. He works out of [[The Catacombs]] and is very hard to find on purpose.

**The code.** No murder of locals. No harm to children. No touching the Lake Lady's sacred sites. *(In the older Zorastor telling this was Jubal's Talons and the thief Randall's home turf; in Purewater the role is Crowbill's, given an explicit ethic.)* The code is not sentiment — it is why the city tolerates him, why the watch works around him rather than against him, and why he has lasted.

**Stated aim:** profit and quiet. **Real aim:** to keep Purewater *level* -- a city where everyone's business is predictable -- because a level table is worth more to him than any single score.

## Where they stand, Tourney week

**He was content to profit from the Baron's arrival and he is not any more.** The Dragon Knights are breaking the spirit of all three of his rules and he cannot enforce a code against armoured men without starting a war he loses. Every day the occupation goes unanswered costs him standing with people who pay him precisely because he keeps things level.

He is also the only power in the city with an intelligence network that is *already* watching the di Teufels -- [[Kerrin (brown coat)]] and others, on anyone interesting who comes through the gate, from the hour they arrive.

This is what makes him gettable. He does not need converting; he needs a *route*. Someone else has to be the one who acts, and he would like to know who they are before he decides.

## The door

**Whoever is already following you.** Crowbill's watchers are put on strangers who ask the working canals about criminals, mercenaries or untaxed cargo, and the fastest way in is to notice one and buy him a drink instead of losing him.

Otherwise: [[Nus]], [[Randall]], [[Mariarta]], or a crow chalked by a doorway. You do not find Crowbill. He fetches you, at dawn, off a ledge, and it is not a negotiation.

**He asks for nothing at the first meeting and offers nothing.** He is deciding what you are. Straight dealing works on him far better than leverage: tell him you cannot promise a thing and that you will come and say so to his face if it ever comes to that, and he will take it.

## What they can do for you, and the price

| | |
|---|---|
| **Hidden boat-paths** | move through the canals unseen, including past the harbour watch |
| **The network** | he found a specific boatman in one guess; there is very little he cannot locate in a day |
| **Safe houses** | across the city, including the Catacombs |
| **Theft to order** | [[Nus]] can lift almost anything off almost anyone -- including a locket off a Baron |
| **Enforcement** | he will guarantee Marisette's boycott for a price, and he honours guarantees |

**The price is the code.** Break it in his city -- hurt a local, hurt a child, foul a sacred site -- and no fee buys you back.

## Friction

Old paying relationship with [[The Pleasure Houses of the Pearl]]. An uneasy secret arrangement with the [[The Harbor Masters]], who publicly parade strict regulation while waving his cargo through. Complicated with [[The Dredgers]] -- he employs their people and undercuts their leverage. **Increasingly and dangerously opposed to the Baron**, whose men are ruining the equilibrium and whose heir is the exact kind of noble the code exists to survive.
