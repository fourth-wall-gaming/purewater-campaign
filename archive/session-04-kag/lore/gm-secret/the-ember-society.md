## What anyone can find out

The Ember Society meets eleven times a year in a hired upper room off the
Merchant's Quarter. Subscription is two crowns; there is a card. Dinner at eight,
an address at ten, port and argument until midnight. Sixty names on the list --
merchants, two guild masters, a retired judge, a councillor, several sons.

Its stated purpose is the study of the age before the Lake: the architecture, the
verse, the metallurgy, the lost roads. The addresses are printed afterwards and
bound, and any bookseller on the Quarter can sell you the last four years for a
few coppers. They are extremely boring and entirely legal.

**It was real once.** Founded thirty-one years ago by genuine antiquarians. That
is why it has old members, respectable cover, and a printing history nobody can
fault. **[[Thorne]]** attended in the seventies and can tell you what it used to
be, which is the single fastest way for a party to learn that it is not that any
more.

## What it is now

Eight years ago the Society acquired a new secretary and stopped being a dining
club with a subject and became **a funnel** for [[The Awake the Dragon Movement]].
It was not infiltrated in any dramatic sense. The subscriptions still get taken,
the dinners still happen, most of the sixty still just come for the port.

**The doctrine is never stated.** Read all four bound volumes and you will find no
dragons, no magic, no politics, and nothing actionable. What you will find, in
every single address, is **decline** -- the argument, made in careful and rather
beautiful prose, that the age is smaller than the one it replaced; that the men
who broke the Dragon Kings broke something else at the same time; that we live in
the ashes of a fire nobody alive has seen. Respectable melancholy. It passes at
any table in the city.

That is the whole recruiting instrument. By the time anyone is asked for anything,
they have spent two years agreeing, pleasantly, over port, that the world has got
smaller -- and being asked has nothing to do with belief.

## The three rings

| | Who | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| **The Table** | ~60 | Dinners, the printed address, the card. **Asked for nothing, ever.** Most subscribers die of old age at this ring having noticed nothing. [[Maro Quist]] is here. |
| **The Readings** | ~12 | Invited, flatteringly, to private evenings where genuine pre-uprising texts are produced and discussed. Real documents, real scholarship, and a strong sense of having been let in. Asked for *opinions*, not acts. |
| **The Four** | 4 | Asked, once, for one specific thing that only they can give: a signature, a survey, a berth, a name, a key. Never told why. Never asked twice in the same year. **Not one of them has been told what the Movement is.** |

**Nobody in this Society is ever asked to believe anything**, which is why it has
survived thirty years and why it will survive the party.

## The two names that matter

**[[Maro Quist]] -- third on the list, and innocent.** Seventy-one, three dinners
attended, thought the wine was poor. He is on that list because he enjoys being
read to about the fall of the Dragon Kings. A party that finds the subscription
list will find his name near the top, next to the councillor who signed both of
the Baron's instructions, and will be *certain*. Let them be. Accusing him in
public is the most expensive mistake available in the political thread.

**[[Ivo Calder]] -- fourth name down, and one of the Four.** Tide Master of the
[[The Watercrafters Guild]], assessor by appointment to the city. Six weeks ago
the Order of the Lake Lady's fouling alarm registered the corruption bleeding off
the Baron's hull. The assessment was referred to the guild. **Calder signed it as
a false reading**, and on the strength of his signature three councillors carried
an instruction standing the alarm down for the duration of the festival.

He was asked for exactly one thing, by a man he respects, at a private reading,
and he was not told why. He has told himself a version in which the guild's
interest in studying the barge justified it. He is not sleeping well.

**This is the payoff of the whole thread:** the obvious name is the false lead and
the true one is a line further down a list nobody reads to the bottom.

## The secretary

**[[Cassian Bree]]** -- forty-four, secretary these eight years, and the only
person in Purewater who is actually a member of the Movement. He is a small,
courteous, slightly damp man who keeps immaculate minutes and is boring on
purpose. He takes subscriptions, books the room, prints the addresses, and decides
who gets invited to a reading.

Under investigation he is a nightmare, because **everything he does is legal and
he will help you with all of it.** He will produce the subscription list
unprompted, apologise for the state of the accounts, and offer you the bound
volumes. He has no contraband, no ritual room, and no tattoo. What he has is a
locked correspondence file with four letters in it, and a memory.

He is also the contact for the courier who arrives on `d-1/night`.

## Running it

- **Ways in:** Quist's card; the bound addresses at any bookseller; Thorne, who
  remembers what it was; Hesper and the Quiet Hand, who have wondered about it for
  years and have nothing provable; or the guild, if the party works backwards from
  the fouling alarm rather than forwards from a list.
- **Working backwards is the correct route and the harder one.** Ask *who signed
  the false reading* and you get Calder in a day. Ask *who is in the cult* and you
  get Quist and a disaster.
- **Breaking the Society does not stop the Baron.** He is the Movement's
  experiment, not its servant, and nothing in that upper room is load-bearing for
  what happens at noon on `d0`. What it buys the party is *Calder* -- and Calder
  recanting his assessment reopens the temple's alarm, which puts the Order of the
  Lake Lady officially onto the corruption three days before the Tourney.
- **It is a horizon thread.** Sixty names, four that matter, and the GM never has
  to say which four. It survives this adventure by design.
