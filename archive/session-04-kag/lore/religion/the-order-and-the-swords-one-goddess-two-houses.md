Two institutions serve the Lake Lady. **They are not the same body, and neither
can give the other an order.** This is the single most load-bearing fact in the
campaign's political thread, so it is worth being exact about.

## The division

**[[The Order of the Lake Lady]] is the priesthood.** Temples, sacred pools,
rites, tithes, the calendar, and the Lake Lady's Tourney. It is a civic power with
property and politics, and it is bound by **temple law**: the clergy may not
intervene directly in the affairs of the city *unless the sacred sites themselves
are threatened.* [[High Priestess Nerissa]] is not being timid. She is bound, and
she has been bound for her whole career.

**[[The Swords of the Lake]] are sworn to the goddess, not to her clergy.** A
realm-wide martial order, austere, propertyless by rule, holding battle honours
from the uprising against the Dragon Kings. They take no tithes, keep no temples,
and answer to their own chapter and to the goddess who holds them to account
directly.

**So:**

- A High Priestess **cannot command a Sword**, or discipline one, or forbid one
  anything.
- A Sword **cannot command clergy**, or claim temple revenues, or speak for the
  Order.
- Neither can excommunicate the other. Only the goddess is held to do that, and
  she does it in her own time and in her own way -- which both houses will tell
  you, at length, means something different.

## Why the split exists

At the uprising, the water-faith did two things at once: it fought, and it kept
the pools. Afterwards the question of **who held the goddess's authority** was
never settled so much as divided. The priesthood took the waters and the
calendar. The knights took the war and the road.

The sore point is what came after. Generations ago the Order accepted **civic
power** -- temples on Temple Isle, a seat at the city's table, the running of a
tournament that brings in a third of its annual revenue. The Swords regard that as
the compromise that produced temple law in the first place: a priesthood that
cannot act because it has too much to lose.

The Order's reply, delivered with great courtesy for four hundred years, is that
the Swords have the luxury of purity because they have never had to govern
anything, feed anyone, or keep a city's water clean through a bad decade.

**Both positions are correct.** They are not enemies and they do not feud. It is
closer to two branches of one family who love each other and cannot spend a week
in the same house.

## The courtesies

These are formal, ancient, and playable:

- **Bread, water and a bed.** Any Sword may claim hospitality at any temple of the
  Lady, and the use of the pool. It is never refused.
- **The Rite of Account.** A Sword may ask the clergy to put one question to the
  goddess on their behalf. The priesthood performs it; the answer, such as it is,
  belongs to the Sword.
- **The road.** Clergy travelling may claim a Sword's protection, and a Sword may
  not decline.
- **No orders, either way.** A request between the houses is made in the open, in
  plain words, and may be refused without insult. Neither house has ever developed
  a polite way of *asking a favour*, because the forms assume equals who need
  nothing.

## Tourney week, and the deadlock

This is where the arrangement bites.

The Order's fouling alarm has been silenced by an instruction over the Prince's
signature, on the strength of [[Ivo Calder]]'s false assessment. Nerissa knows the
reading was right, and temple law says she may not act unless the sacred sites are
threatened -- and a corrupt channel is not yet, technically, a threatened site.

**[[Temerach Nebulo]] is bound by none of that.** The Governor's council has
ordered the watch to stand down; the Swords do not answer to the Governor's
council. She is the only armed authority in Purewater who can move against the
Baron's people without disobeying anyone.

So the Order has the knowledge and cannot act. The Sword can act and does not have
the standing to *declare* anything about the water. Each could solve the other's
problem in an afternoon.

**And neither will ask**, because the forms give them no way to ask that does not
read as one house claiming precedence over the other -- and because Nerissa is
proud, and Temerach is here on an errand of her own that she has not explained to
anybody.

**This is a party-shaped hole.** Somebody who belongs to neither house can carry
the words between them, and it costs nothing but the walk. It is one of the
cheapest and largest wins available in the whole campaign, and it is invisible
unless a player thinks to look at the two of them and ask why they are not already
working together.
