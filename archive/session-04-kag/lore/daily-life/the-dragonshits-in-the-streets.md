**An advance detachment** -- a dozen Knights under a Smoke Lieutenant -- came in
eight days before the Tourney to open the estate, provision the barge and hold the
Baron's moorings. That is the week the city has already had. **The column arrives
on `d-3`** with the Baron, Blau, Tat Atarer and the rest, and doubles them.

So the party does not walk into a city bracing for an occupation. They walk into
one that has been living with a small one for a week and is watching it treble in
an afternoon. This is the texture under every outdoor scene until somebody changes
it.

## What it looks like

- **Threes and fours, everywhere.** The Merchant's Quarter, the Forge Quarter, the
  bridges, the Pearl after dark. They take the middle of a bridge and the traffic
  goes round them.
- **They do not pay.** Food, drink, a bottle off a stall, a night on the Pearl:
  *the Baron will settle*. He settles late and light, and the stallholders have
  started pricing it in, which means everyone else's bread has gone up.
- **They take boats.** A skiff off a water-stair with the boatman still in it,
  rowed where they want, left where they finish. Two have not come back.
- **Six days ago**: a dockhand named Emmit Rill was slow moving a barrow and had
  his jaw broken with a mailed hand in front of forty people. He is still not
  working. The complaint is number 31 in the referral book.
- **Nobody is dead yet.** Everybody expects somebody to be. That expectation is
  the mood of the whole city.

## The standing order

Seven days ago -- the morning after the first complaints about the advance
detachment reached the Isle post -- the **[[The City Watch]]** received a written
instruction from the Governor's council: the Baron's people are guests of the
Crown's representative; complaints against them are to be **recorded and referred,
and not acted upon**, until the Tourney is concluded.

It went out over **[[Prince Emeric]]**'s signature. Three of his councillors --
**[[Alderic Vantt]]**, who drafted it, **[[Hesketh Pyle]]**, who seconded it, and
**[[Maro Quist]]**, who carried the chamber for it -- told him it would keep the
peace through the festival. He is two-and-twenty and badly advised, and it is the
second time this month those same three have got his name onto something.

They are not cultists and they did not do it for the same reason. See *[[The Governor's council]]*.

The watch is not corrupt. The watch has been *ordered*, in writing, and every
sergeant in the city knows exactly how that will read afterwards.

## What it does to the board

- **The Dredgers** work the water-stairs the Knights keep commandeering. [[Marda Blackwater]] has three hundred dock labourers, a grievance, and no legal route.
  She is the powder in this.
- **[[Crowbill]]**'s code is *no killing locals, no harming children, no touching
  the Lady's sites*. The Knights are breaking the spirit of all three and he
  cannot be seen to enforce a code against armoured men without starting a war he
  loses. It is costing him standing daily.
- **The Pearl** is taking the worst of the nights and is the least able to
  complain, because complaining is bad for trade.
- **[[The Swords of the Lake]]** answer to their order and not to the council,
  which makes [[Temerach Nebulo]] the only armed person in the city who could act
  and not be disobeying anything. She knows it. She is also holding an archery
  entry she has spent four years earning.

## For the GM

Put this in the background of ordinary scenes and let the players decide when it
stops being background. A single incident witnessed early -- a broken jaw, a
commandeered boat, a sergeant writing in a book and doing nothing -- teaches the
city's rules faster than any amount of exposition, and it makes the party's first
act of interference feel enormous, because it is.
