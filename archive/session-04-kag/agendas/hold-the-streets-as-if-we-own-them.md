**What they want.** Not conquest. *Habit.* Every day the Knights take a bridge, a
boat or a bottle and nothing happens is a day Purewater learns what it is now, and
by the day of the Tourney the Baron wants a city where his men walking somewhere
is not an event.

The clock starts at **2 of 6** because the advance detachment has already had a
week of it.

**How it advances.** One tick per unanswered public incident. It advances on its
own whether or not the party is watching, because the whole point is that nobody
is stopping it.

| Clock | The city |
|---|---|
| 2 | People step off the pavement. The referral book gets thicker. |
| 3 | Stallholders price the theft in. Bread is up. Boatmen work the far stairs. |
| 4 | Two Knights beat a man badly in front of a watch patrol that walks away. |
| 5 | A woman is taken off the Pearl street and the house that objects is burnt. |
| 6 | **A local is killed in the open and nothing is done.** The city stops being a city and starts being an occupied one, and the Dredgers move whether Marda wants them to or not. |

**What stops it.** Not force -- the party cannot fight twenty-five armoured men.
It stops when an incident lands somewhere the standing order does not reach: the
Prince in person, the Order of the Lake Lady, a Sword of the Lake, or the Tourney
ground itself where a di Teufel cannot afford a scene. **[[Abel Weir]]**'s
referral book is the ammunition; the party's problem is finding the gun.

**What it costs the Baron.** He does not want a riot two days before he walks into
a room with the Crown's nephew. Tat Atarer knows that and has said so. Push this
clock hard enough in public and *Hanzo himself* has to rein in his own men, which
is the party's first real win against him and does not require a single sword.
