THE UNMASKING WINDOW, and it is the day BEFORE the sand.

By long form every entrant in every event is presented to the Crown's representative on the eve of the Tourney: names read, colours shown, and each competitor bare-headed for the space of a bow. It is ceremony and it is boring and the whole city half-watches it.

The Baron cannot avoid it without forfeiting the entry.

So the champion is walked out in harness with the rest of them, and for perhaps four seconds the helm is off, and a seventeen-year-old with a decade of being opened every morning stands in daylight in front of several hundred people.

**If Gardwen is on that ground she sees her brother.** Ten years, and he is thirty yards away in somebody else's colours.

**And if she is not, somebody else may recognise him anyway** -- from Sana's account, from the ship, from the shape of a boy who is too thin for the armour he is in.

This is the last moment before the bout when anything can be arranged calmly. After this there are hours; after the final there are minutes.

Play the recognition. Do not resolve what she does about it -- hand it back.
