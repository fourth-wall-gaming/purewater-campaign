The inciting wound of the campaign, and the first real test of the
staging rule.

He was refused at dusk. By full dark he has taken a room in the same house and
paid for Emmeralda's evening, which nobody can stop him doing, and he has the
scroll out of his father's case in his coat.

**What he thinks he is doing.** Making her *willing*. He has watched his father
seat a spirit in a living body and be obeyed by it for ten years; he means to put
something small and pleasant in this girl so that she wants to be taken to the
barge, and he has one use of a stolen scroll with which to do it and no training
whatsoever.

**What actually happens.** The channel opens -- that much he can do. The seating
fails. Whatever came for the invitation does not go where he tells it, the scroll
burns out in his hand, and he finishes the working the only way panic knows, with
the barbed Asmodeus knife: a long ritual cut, opening her the length of her body.
The girl screams. He runs, and in running he leaves the knife behind the armoire.

**Onscreen** -- PCs in the house hear the shrieks and can reach her while she is
still bleeding. First Aid or healing magic saves her life; without it she dies
before dawn. They may catch a glimpse of a fleeing man they will later realise
looks like someone they know. Whoever searches the room properly finds the knife,
and whoever searches it *well* finds the spent scroll -- which is written in the
Baron's own hand.

**Offscreen** -- it happens exactly the same way, minus rescue. Emmeralda dies or
survives on Constantine's own resources (survives, badly, if Gardwen is anywhere
in the city and can be fetched; dies if the party is far away). The party meets
the aftermath: a house in uproar, a girl marked with demon-taint, a knife still
hidden behind the armoire for whoever thinks to look.

**What is actually in that room afterwards** -- because he left in a panic and
took nothing:

- the **barbed Asmodeus knife**, kicked behind the armoire, which ties the
  di Teufels to demonology;
- the **spent scroll**, burnt out but still legible, **in the Baron's own hand**;
- a **chalk seat** drawn on the boards under the rug, unfinished and still live
  until somebody sweeps it;
- his **coat**, over the back of a chair, with a Purewater tailor's chit in the
  pocket and the di Teufel crest under the collar.

Nobody in the house is going to search a room while a girl is bleeding to death in
it. Whatever is not taken that night is still there in the morning -- and so is the
man who wants it back.

Either way Marisette's clock starts, and the hunt for a man with that face begins.

**He has not stopped wanting her.** That is the part the party will not see
tonight, and it is what turns a crime into a rescue with a clock on it.
