**This beat has no time and never fires on its own.** It is an opportunity, not
an event: it happens the first time a PC gets aboard the ship, or gets near the
boy by any other route — a delivery, an invitation, a disguise, the Washing,
somebody else's errand.

## What is under way

A man in his fifties comes up out of the after hold carrying an empty basin.
Heavy forearms, sleeves rolled past the elbow, liniment and lamp oil. He is the
last person on that ship anybody thinks about, and he has been aboard longer
than everyone on deck bar the shipmaster.

He does not shout. He sets the basin down without looking at it and tells the
intruder exactly what is supposed to happen next — that he calls out, six men in
mail come up the forward hatch, and nobody ashore ever hears about them again.

Then: *"So before I do — what are you."*

## What decides it

**Not a roll on the PC's side.** Nothing they can say is cleverer than the thing
that actually works, which is the truth — and the truth is the one thing he has
not been offered in ten years. A PC who lies to him should get the six men.

It is decided by **his own passions, rolled against each other in front of the
player**:

> Love (the boy) **70** vs Fear (the Baron) **65**

- **Love wins.** He does not move out of the way — he sits down on the coaming
  like a man whose legs have stopped agreeing with him. What he gives is not
  access, it is *knowledge*: the boy's real name, which nobody has said aboard
  that ship in ten years, and the thing he has never told a living soul —
  **Gabriel holds a cup left-handed, and the thing wearing him is right-handed,
  and every time it puts a cup down and the boy surfaces for a second he moves
  it across.** Ten years of watching, and nobody to tell.
  Then twenty minutes, boots off, and a warning: the third step complains, and
  the watch changes at the turn of the glass.
- **Fear wins.** He does not betray them either — he is not a cruel man. He
  tells them to get off his ship, and he means it, and he will be at the top of
  that companionway every time they come back. **Do not make this a failure
  state.** He is now a person they have met and could try again on, and the
  second approach should be harder and possible.
- **Both critical, or both fumble.** He asks them to take the boy with them
  tonight, which they cannot do, and will not forgive them for refusing.

## Why he matters more than the access

He is proof. Not of anything the plot needs — of the fact that somebody has
watched this boy closely enough for ten years to know which hand he reaches
with. Everything else in the campaign says Cailan is a weapon that used to be a
person. Orrin is the only evidence that he is still in there, and the party gets
it from a servant nobody has ever asked a question.

**He will not help them steal the boy.** Ask him and he says no, and he is
right: taking the body does not free anyone while the anchor holds, and he has
worked that out for himself over a decade.
