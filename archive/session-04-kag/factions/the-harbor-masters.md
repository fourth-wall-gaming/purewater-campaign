The body that controls maritime traffic in and out of Purewater: docks, berths, tariffs, and the enforcement of maritime law. They work from a harbor tower and dockside offices, in dark blue uniforms badged by rank.

**Ranks:** Dockhands, Pilots, Harbormen, Tide Lords, Port Captains, and the seven-master Anchor Council.

**Leadership.** [[Harbormaster Velen Deepkeel]] -- stern, weathered, forty years risen through the ranks; meticulous and traditional, publicly incorruptible, and genuinely so. Seamanship 85, Navigation 82, Maritime Law 80. The rot is beneath him, not in him, and it is the thing he would least like to look at directly.

**Stated aim:** safe, efficient harbors and the city's standing as a trade hub. **Real aim:** to keep their near-monopoly on maritime commerce and its wealth; the upper ranks are increasingly corrupt, taking bribes to wave through smuggling while parading strict regulation.

## Where they stand, Tourney week

**They have the one document nobody has asked for.** The DragonBarge lies at a private pier on [[High Isle]]'s seaward side, and a private pier still requires a berth registration, a tariff assessment, a manifest, and a pilot to bring a hull that size through the Mouth. **All of that is on file in the harbour tower**, including what the Baron declared he was carrying and what the pilot who brought her in actually saw on her deck.

The paperwork is the softest evidence in the campaign and the least guarded, because it is *administration*.

They are also feeling the occupation from an angle nobody else is: the Knights are commandeering boats, which is a maritime-law matter, which is *theirs* -- and the standing order has removed their appetite for enforcing it. Deepkeel has recorded the incidents and referred them, exactly as instructed, and it is eating him.

## The door

**A Harbourman at a dockside office**, for anything routine -- and routine is where the value is. A berth registration is a public record; the manifest is not, but a clerk can be persuaded, bribed, or shown an Assay warrant.

**[[Harbormaster Velen Deepkeel]]** himself is reachable, formal, and immovable on procedure. He will not be bribed, will not bend a rule, and *will* act on a properly evidenced complaint under maritime law -- which is a much shorter road than the criminal law the watch has been forbidden to use. Nobody has thought of this. It is one of the best unpulled levers in the city.

## What they can do for you, and the price

| | |
|---|---|
| **The barge's papers** | berth registration, tariff assessment, declared manifest, and the name of the pilot who brought her in |
| **The pilot** | a man who stood on that deck and looked around while conning her through the Mouth |
| **Maritime law** | a jurisdiction the standing order does not cover -- boats, berths, cargo, and the water itself |
| **Berths and passage** | a hull, a slip, or a quiet departure |
| **Corruption, if you want it** | the upper ranks will wave things through for money, and Deepkeel must not find out |

**The price** depends entirely which door you use. Deepkeel's price is procedure and evidence. His subordinates' price is cash, and using them puts you on the wrong side of the man you actually need.

## Friction

Uneasy secret arrangement with [[Crowbill]] -- officially opposed, quietly facilitating. Open contempt for [[The Dredgers]], warmly returned. Strong ties to the Governor, the council, and the shipping merchants, [[Alderic Vantt]] among them. A wary, opportunistic eye on the Baron, whose berth fees are enormous and whose men are becoming a maritime nuisance.

## Hooks

- Somebody finally asks to see the DragonBarge's manifest, and the declared cargo does not include a covered litter.
- The pilot who brought her through the Mouth remembers the after deck being cleared and a man in harness being walked on it.
- Deepkeel is shown a boat-commandeering complaint he cannot refer away without breaking his own law. Watch him choose.
- A Port Captain takes the Baron's money and a party can prove it -- worth more as leverage than as a scandal.

**Agendas on the clock:** none of their own. They are a lever, not an actor -- which is precisely why nobody is watching them.
