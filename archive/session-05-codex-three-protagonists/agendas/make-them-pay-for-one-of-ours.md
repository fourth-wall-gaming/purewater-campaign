**What she wants.** Emmit Rill has a wire-bound jaw and no wages, and two boatmen
who were rowed out by Knights have not come back. [[Marda Blackwater]] wants them
paid for and she wants it done properly, in daylight, by somebody official, so
that it counts.

**Why she is dangerous.** Because she does not want a riot and she will get one
anyway. She has three hundred dock labourers who are angrier than she is, a watch
that has been ordered not to help, and a Tourney week in which the whole city's
provisioning goes through her people's hands. If the lawful route stays closed she
will use the only lever she has -- **stop the water-stairs** -- and a stopped port
during a sacred festival is a crisis that will absolutely get somebody killed.

**How it advances.** With the Knights' clock. Every unanswered incident advances
*Hold the streets* by one and this by one on alternate ticks.

| Clock | Marda |
|---|---|
| 1 | Petitions the Harbor Masters. Gets sympathy and a referral. |
| 2 | Petitions the Governor's council in writing. It is not answered. |
| 3 | Calls a meeting on the Shoals. Speaks well. Barely holds them. |
| 4 | **The stairs stop.** No provisioning, no cartage, no boats for hire -- including the Tourney's. |

**Where the party comes in.** She is the most reasonable person in this and the
easiest to help. Get Rill paid, get the boatmen accounted for, or get her in front
of somebody who can act, and she will hold her people for another day every single
time. She is also the fastest way to three hundred bodies if the party ever needs
a crowd -- and a crowd on the Tourney ground is a thing the Baron's plan cannot
survive.

**She is not a fool about the Baron.** She knows exactly whose men these are. She
does not know, and would give a great deal to learn, that he intends anything
beyond swagger.
