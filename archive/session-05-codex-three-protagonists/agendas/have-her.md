**What he wants.** Santo sees Emmeralda dance on his first evening in Purewater and decides he will take her back to the DragonBarge, kept and made willing.

**How he pursues it.** He buys her evening anonymously. He has watched his father bind spirits into living hosts, has stolen one scroll of Seat the Bound, and has no real training. He intends to seat a lust spirit in Emmeralda so that she will want what he wants. The scroll is consumed whether the working succeeds or fails, and it burns his right hand either way.

If the spirit takes, he tries to walk her out through the water-door. If he is stopped, he returns by boat the following day. If the working fails, panic and violence follow, and the room is left full of evidence connecting the crime to the di Teufels. Thwarted, he loses his temper publicly with fire.

**What changes his mind.** Fear: exposure, direct force, or Hanzo learning what he stole and attempted. Reason does not.

**What this is for.** This is the campaign's front door: demon-craft against a woman under a Purewater roof, witnesses who saw a face shared by two innocent men, evidence in the Baron's own hand, and a crime serious enough to turn the Pearl against the Dragon Knights.
