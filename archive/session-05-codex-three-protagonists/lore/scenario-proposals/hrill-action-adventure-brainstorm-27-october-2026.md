# Hrill: adventures for a reluctant fighting knight

OOC brainstorming, 27 October 2026. **Proposals, not activated adventures.**
Play and Hrill's native clock are held at d25/dusk, minute92, Listening Reeds.
No candidate is selected. The earlier beacon suggestion is one candidate only.

## Updated player-experience brief

The player explicitly wants an adventure with substantially more action. Hrill
is a knight and fighter even though he is often reluctant to fight. Reluctance
does not mean pacifism, avoidance of adventure or preference for administration.
Provide dangerous armed opposition, protection and rescue, physical exploration,
tactical decisions and purposeful chances to use sword, shield and military
experience. Social judgement and strategy support those activities. Compress
routine travel, cargo handling, introductions, explanations and negotiations.

Further player clarification: trouble should be close by, or meet Hrill on his
voyage. Build immediate, credible involvement from his present surroundings;
do not depend on his volunteering for a distant destination. Distant proposals
need a nearby opening encounter and a reason earned in play to pursue them.
No attack at an inn is established by this review; the beacon assault is only
an unselected proposal.

Retain the earlier preferences: exploration beyond Purewater; meaningful risk;
mythic/cinematic development; honor, being a good man, stopping needless killing
and loyalty to the Crown. The player owns Hrill's emotional conclusions. Do not
require him to kill, enter service again, hunt harmless animals or guarantee
other people's safety. He can choose violence when protection or another worthy
purpose calls for it. Give him consequential work rather than carrying messages
between NPCs who supply every insight and solution.

## 1. The Crown's Last Light

**Military rescue and a small siege.** Proposed Greywatch Beacon is a Crown
post on the lake shore, reachable from Hrill's current voyage. A small armed
company tries to seize its signal tower, stores and landing. An ordinary keeper
fleeing toward a boat gives Hrill a direct entrance into the action. He can
protect people on the quay, get through the blockade and help the outnumbered
garrison. The opposing commander is a dangerous knight who intends to hold the
post for an imperial restorationist cause.

**Act movement:** win the first rescue and secure an entrance → discover the
seizure's logistical purpose and break the company's advantages → relieve the
post, force withdrawal or defeat its surviving command.

Proposed cast: Dame Avel Veyr, commander seeking a permanent foothold and the
legitimacy of holding royal ground; Captain Surn Pike, an independent transport
contractor seeking payment and a valuable landing rather than an ideological
war; Corporal Dera Venn, an honest Crown officer protecting her small command;
Keeper Halwen Tarr, responsible for the beacon and its civilian staff.

Two opposing interests: Avel will capture resisting staff and use lethal force
to take the post; Pike will blockade/intercept a relief landing if paid and
capable, but can abandon the enterprise rather than lose his boat. Proposed
finite ceiling: Avel plus five fighters; Pike plus three boat crew, only two
capable bow hands. Crown defence: corporal, two guards, two civilian keepers.
No instant reinforcements. Actual pay, equipment and positions need preparation.

Proposed places: Greywatch tower/courtyard; a rock quay below its stairs;
Flint Cove transport camp; an older seaward cistern stair. All are new local
geography to fit the existing map, not labels already found on it.

Hrill's work: close fighting at the quay; holding a choke point; organising a
sortie or relief landing; finding an approach with the historical shore records;
offering surrender from a position he has earned. A duel is possible, never a
compulsory substitute for tactics. His Crown loyalty has a concrete honest
institution to support, without enlistment or unlimited obligation.

Without intervention, Avel attempts capture and occupation; outcomes require
actual opposed resolution. Danger includes blades, bows, falls on stone stairs,
capture and loss of the signal post. Visible armed demands, occupied approaches
and a request for help provide warning before irreversible loss. Lake withdrawal,
covered approaches and division of the finite force remain available.

Global connection: **causal**. Holding the landing and stores would give a new
restorationist company a recruitment/supply foothold. Saving them denies that
capacity. This commander is independent of Hanzo unless selected preparation
establishes an actual connection; no surprise replacement lieutenant.

Counterfactuals: refusal leaves a real Crown defence to resolve; authorities can
send relief after actual travel; negotiating with Pike can remove his blockade;
captured equipment and lost fighters reduce the assault; an early surrender can
end the siege. The beacon remains saved even if Hrill declines a larger war.

## 2. The Knight Who Would Leave

**Rescue, pursuit and a dangerous household stronghold.** A plea reaches Hrill
to help bring a Dragon Knight out of Hanzo's household. The person needs a
protector able to stand against armed retainers, not another patron claiming
ownership. Pursuers want the matter settled privately; a competing noble wants
the knight as valuable evidence. Hrill must reach the person, make a practical
extraction plan and defend their freedom through the resulting pursuit.

**Act movement:** protect the source of the plea and reach the captive → learn
the particular constraints on release and weaken the household's hold → carry
out an extraction and secure a lasting refuge or surrender on acceptable terms.

This is the **one direct Hanzo candidate**. It may reopen an existing parked
household fate if chosen; it must not invent a replacement champion or presume
a cure, escape, injury or recovery already happened. Identity and opening access
must be reconciled with that existing record before play. No old tournament
chance or released prisoner is restored.

Proposed new cast: Lord Lethren Caul, a rival noble seeking custody and political
advantage; Sael Thann, a forest spiritual practitioner with independent reasons
to offer or refuse help; Rowan Pell, a household servant trying to get the plea
delivered. Hanzo retains his established personality, powers and private aims.
Caul's wish to control the witness can oppose freedom without making every Crown
official dishonest. Honest officials and protectors remain useful alternatives.

Proposed places: Emberhold's outer household court; a Scorched Hills pursuit
road; an independently consenting forest refuge. Multi-day travel from the lake
must actually be accounted for. No free Deep Glades access.

Finite forces: audit Hanzo's surviving household before assigning a retrieval
party, provisionally at most four escorts from that roster. Caul proposes three
retainers, no army. Their pay, transport, knowledge and commands require full
preparation. No one knows Hrill's plan until someone learns and reports it.

Hrill's work: protect the messenger; enter or assault a guarded place; seize the
particular means of coercion; cover a withdrawal; defend a willing specialist
while performing his own indispensable physical objective. He must not become
a delivery man waiting for a magician to solve the entire adventure.

Danger: capture, lethal pursuit and harm to the person being freed. Warning
comes through the plea, visible custody and actual pursuit. Several entrance,
extraction and refuge routes must be designed. The magic, if involved, needs
specific CFI rules; no generic immunity or automatic cure by touching a relic.

Global connection: **direct and causal**. A successful rescue removes one actual
person/capacity from the Baron's control and can yield evidence or allies.
Hanzo may bargain or lose. Hrill need not kill him, cure everyone or join a war
for the local rescue to be a complete victory.

Counterfactuals: decline and the plea can reach another protector; Crown help
can secure a refuge or custody hearing; accepting another patron's help has
specific terms rather than automatic betrayal; an early negotiated release
stands; loss of the control mechanism changes all dependent pursuit plans.

## 3. The Road of Ash

**Border pursuit, defence and command.** On the roads near Ashwick, a reaver
company is driving people from their homes. A restorationist landholder offers
protection in exchange for their service and land. Hrill meets the immediate
human danger at a ford: frightened travellers, baggage and armed pursuers. He
can lead people out, fight for a defensible refuge and then take the initiative
against the force making the road unsafe.

**Act movement:** secure a withdrawal and refuge → pursue the raiders and
discover the competing protection offer → break their actual capacity and help
the survivors choose a durable defence.

Proposed cast: Captain Reis Varr, an independent reaver seeking loot and ransom;
Lady Merev Dorn, an imperial sympathiser seeking dependent households and a
local levy; Warden Hess Tal, an honest local defender; Brenna Clay, a shepherd
who knows the ford and upland paths. The reavers are not secretly Dorn's paid
men unless selected preparation supplies a compelling independent reason.

Proposed places: an Ashwick approach road; Little Ashford ford; Mill Hill
steading; the reavers' temporary upland camp. They require actual travel from
the lake. They do not replace Wind Stair or cancel its restored road.

Proposed finite forces: seven reavers including Varr; Dorn has four retainers
and food/protection she must actually pay for; Hess has three volunteers.
Hrill owns no horse: riding requires an actual willing loan or purchase,
not automatic equipment. The campaign can work on foot.

Hrill's work: tactical withdrawal, holding a crossing, rescuing actual captives,
night reconnaissance, a decisive camp attack or negotiated surrender after
removing the raiders' advantage. His experience of repeated costly campaigns
is relevant; the player decides what he thinks of this one.

Danger: pursuit, arrows, fire and prisoners taken for ransom. Warning includes
actual tracks, scouts, threats and raiding preparations. Successful evacuation
prevents harm; no mandatory massacre or death of a named friend.

Global connection: primarily **thematic**, with a possible causal levy dependency
if chosen. The wider question is why frightened people might accept an older
tyranny's promises of order. Dorn's recruitment can be defeated by an effective
independent defence. This does not establish a new Hanzo operation.

Counterfactuals: refugees and honest defenders can act without Hrill; proper
official assistance helps but must travel; reavers can release prisoners and
surrender; Dorn can provide protection under altered terms if she genuinely
agrees; defeating the raid does not conjure another band to make the offer
inevitable. A secured refuge is an accomplishment worth keeping.

## 4. The Broken Gates of Drakesfall

**Ruins, physical exploration and fantasy combat.** The old shore records lead
to a newly accessible part of Drakesfall. A hostile expedition is opening an
imperial armoury beneath the fallen works, while an older guardian defends the
place for reasons of its own. Hrill can enter through broken towers and water
stairs, fight for access and decide who leaves with people, knowledge and arms.
The past becomes a place he can put his boots in.

**Act movement:** reach the exposed works and win an entrance → learn what the
expedition can actually recover and how the defences function → seize, deny or
settle control of the extraction before surviving opponents remove it.

Proposed cast: Dame Rovena Sel, an independent restorationist financing the dig;
Eilen-of-Stone, an older guardian defending a particular boundary rather than
attacking every visitor; Mara Quell, an archivist seeking an accurate record;
Renn Daal, a boatmaster who values living passengers above an employer's salvage.
The guardian's nature and CFI rules require selection and preparation.

Proposed places: Drakesfall's broken outer ledge; a partly drowned armoury;
a collapsed tower bridge; the expedition's extraction landing. Drakesfall is
on the north-west shore at the Wildwood edge, several actual travel days away,
not a ruin just outside Listening Reeds.

Finite proposal: Rovena and five hired blades, one boat with two civilian crew,
one guardian; six potentially recoverable old armour sets plus records and
limited treasure. These are proposed limits, not live free equipment or an
inexhaustible imperial arsenal. Worth and usability must be established later.

Hrill's work: climbing and choosing approaches, close fights in confined works,
making passages usable, protecting companions, reading military layout and
denying a hostile extraction. Poor swimming remains relevant; bridges, boats,
ropes and land routes must offer real alternatives rather than a compulsory dive.

Danger: falls, flooding, armed expedition members and a powerful guardian whose
boundaries can be learned. Evidence of previous failures, visible water levels
and recognisable defensive positions give warning. Withdrawal with partial
knowledge or rescued people is meaningful.

Global connection: **causal**. Rovena seeks a bounded resource that would equip
and legitimise her own restorationist following. Denying it removes that
capacity; understanding the older resistance also creates useful relationships.
No universal counterspell and no secret order from Hanzo is required.

Counterfactuals: an early settlement with the guardian can open a route; a lawful
recovery expedition or Crown help must still face physical access and travel;
Rovena can bargain or be defeated before extraction; destroying her boat removes
that transport but does not erase foot exits; securing the armoury can finish
the local story without a mandatory awakened dragon or replacement boss.

## 5. The Muster of the Dead

**Supernatural military adventure.** In the Scorched Hills, an old war standard
is calling soldiers to a muster. Some are dead; living people are also answering
because a local lord promises pay, purpose and protection. Hrill first encounters
the consequence on the road: an armed levy taking people toward an abandoned
fort. He can protect those people, follow the muster and confront the commander
who intends to finish an ancient war.

**Act movement:** stop one forced march and reach the battlefield → discover
who sustains the muster and separate living recruits from the old command →
break or settle that command and prevent another company being raised.

Proposed cast: Marshal Othrane, an imperial dead commander who wants his old
orders fulfilled; Lord Kerr Voss, a living landholder seeking a controllable
force and willing to supply recruits; Rell Penn, a living volunteer seeking
purpose who can refuse to be treated as a captive; Sister Annet Low, a local
keeper of graves who wants the dead released and the living spared.

Proposed places: Ashen Pass road; the Barrow Line battlefield; Empty Keep.
These are new places within the Scorched Hills region, not an invented relocation
of Emberhold or a secretly occupied prior PC victory.

Finite proposal: the marshal and seven dead soldiers; Voss and three living
retainers; six living recruits, some voluntary. The standard sustains cohesion
and recruitment, not an unlimited spawn. Its ritual, limits and interaction
with CFI damage/magic must be designed before play. No compulsory PC possession.

Hrill's work: armed rescue, separating formations, defending a position against
a disciplined foe, reaching the standard and deciding how to end the command.
A formidable adversary can offer a duel, but other physical and negotiated
approaches must remain effective. No required revelation about Hrill's own
former company, family, guilt or fallen comrade.

Danger: real supernatural fighting, capture and recruitment of additional
people while the actual rite remains possible. Witnesses, visible muster
preparations and finite columns provide warning. Freeing recruits and taking
the banner remove capacity. No endlessly returning legion after victory.

Global connection: **causal and thematic**. A living restorationist is trying
to recover an old coercive military power; the command becomes possible through
his actual resources and choices, not an inevitable return. Neither patron nor
wight needs to serve Hanzo. Refusing a new tyranny can be compatible with Hrill
finding worthwhile service.

Counterfactuals: authorities can help protect roads and hold freed recruits;
Voss can lose his supply/recruitment capacity or surrender; the marshal can be
defeated early; the standard must not require a single hidden password; sincere
volunteers retain agency; leaving preserves prior rescues while the surviving
operation proceeds only through actual dependencies.

## Comparative check and recommendation

Scores are a design check on these outlines, not proof of readiness or promises
of fun. Eight criteria from brainstorming-method.md, each0–2: picture/activity,
distinctiveness, causality, protagonist agency, sustained development, peril,
varied play/reward, coherent development.

|---|---|---:|---|

| Road of Ash |2,1,2,2,2,2,1,2|14| Stronger distinctive physical set pieces and local military geography |
| Broken Gates |2,2,1,2,2,2,2,2|15| Particular guardian, excavation dependencies, full access and flood rules |
| Muster of the Dead |2,2,1,2,1,2,2,1|13| Bound recruitment/magic mechanics; avoid generic cursed-banner solution |

Recommend **Crown's Last Light** for the quickest transition to fighting,
protection and field command from the current position. Recommend **Broken Gates
of Drakesfall** for stronger mythic exploration and dangerous ruins. The direct
Baron option is the most personal confrontation with his household, but should
be chosen for that desire rather than forced upon an unknowing Hrill.

All five are proposals with developed dramatic outlines. **None is a verified
playable adventure.** After selection, reuse the complete experience brief,
prepare the full cast/mechanics, knowledge, operations, maps and three-act
situations, persist and read them back before resuming. Do not ask the player
to repeat the action-versus-politics interview.
