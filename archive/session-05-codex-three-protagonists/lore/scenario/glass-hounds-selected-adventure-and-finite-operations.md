## Acts, outcomes and stress test

Act I offers the live hunt and a real chance of early recovery, capture or dismantling. Substantial gains can include teacher, book, specimen, handler or route. Act II concerns workshop, crew, control and transport dependencies, approached by raid, disguise, bargain, research or official help. Rest is possible; only actual operations advance meanwhile. Act III resolves whichever transport, custody or settlement opportunities survive. It need not include every location or a final duel. Six to ten sessions is a possible scope, never padding owed after victory.

Clear victory: teacher and book recovered, coercive hunt disabled, client pays agreed8GP on return; additional research or patron rewards require actual agreement. Partial victory can preserve lives or book while pack exports, or stop pack while commission remains unresolved. Negotiation may buy out Senn, enforce contract, retain salvage models and settle Rennic's report. Failure can include actual capture, injury or export, following checks and warning. Withdrawal leaves those actors to their real attempts and other opposition. No forced sacrifice, compensating tragedy or automatically villainous fellowship.

Refusal remains valid; no forced abduction. Authorities supply real help with evidence and time, not endless paperwork. Early enemy defeat removes capacity and can end the adventure. Negotiation and dividing opponents work because their goals differ. Allies contribute actual routes and tools. Out-of-order recovery is valid. Independence, fellowship loyalty and respect for Rennic create a possible passion conflict; the player decides whether to invoke it. Nimras's signature opportunity is a physical magical opponent vulnerable to his counters and force, alongside genuine stealth and deception routes.
