# The Road Beyond the Banners — GM preparation

Read hrill-road-story.md for the arc. This is a live preparation document; future
outcomes remain conditional. Dates below are earliest attempt windows, not results.

## Experience, opening and global storyline

Reuse the interview: exploration beyond Purewater, a veteran seeking purpose beyond
fighting, honor, intelligence, people and strategy. Room for warmth, wonder, honest
help and credible danger. Hrill is 42, Rank 2 Fighter; Influence55 Insight59
Willpower50, Strategy75, MilitaryHistory70, Engineering40. No invented former
campaign, military shame, order, family or emotional conclusion. Character's three
Passions are Be a good man58, Stop the killing48, Loyalty to the Crown43.

At d7/day late afternoon Hrill stands on the southern approach to Ashen Span.
Wet grit, running water, rope under strain. A supply cart has broken the rotten
edge of the timber deck. Its driver Len is on the surviving centre beam, his boot
caught beneath the tilted cart's shaft; his mule is loose on the southern bank.
Maelin, on the same bank as Hrill, has a rope belayed to a stone post. It holds the
cart, not Len. The post is sound. Cart movement threatens to crush Len's ankle or
pull him into the stream. A round timber lies beside Hrill; Len is twelve feet away.
No mortal wound, roll or injury has already occurred. Opening asks what Hrill does.
The cart holds four medicine bundles and two food sacks for Three Hearths Hospice.
Hrill has accepted no employment and holds no special warrant. Initial objective
is his own travel; the driver and crossing present an immediate opportunity.
Maelin is the first contact. A painted board on the cart gives the hospice name
and an arrow north; no secret evidence is handed over in the opening.

Global question: what can people build when present government fails them, and
why might they accept an older tyranny? Sir Aldren Vaust offers something real:
food and a safer road. He seeks exclusive control of Wind Stair, binding local
service pledges and removal of an elven teaching settlement from the pass. He
also intends to transport one stolen imperial funerary standard to a restorationist
collector beyond Hythe. This is a new, finite venture unrelated to the logistics
of Hanzo's defeated original possession. No ties to Rusk's specific operation,
royal complicity or ancient army are presumed. The standard is not an active
weapon or a universal ritual key. No casting, release rite or spirit manifestation
is scheduled in this adventure; any later proposed magic needs CFI preparation.

The mythic image is a road laid over successive fallen kingdoms: half-buried
imperial reliefs, Crown waystations, household hearths built with their stones.
Wind whistles through drilled old mile-markers; acoustics can locate them. No
automatic prophecy, allegiance or magical revelation. Hrill can choose what his
experience helps preserve. His loyalty is tested by conduct, not by a false decree
that automatically obliges obedience. Vaust has a valid repair licence from a
Wendlow reeve, not a warrant for eviction or forced recruitment.

## Geography and finite assets

Ashen Span to Three Hearths: half a day on foot via an hour-long sheep-track detour
if the bridge remains closed. Hospice to Wind Stair: a full day's travel; Wind Stair
to White Cairn camp: half a day on a ridge lane. Alder Hame lies two hours down the
east ravine from Wind Stair. Wendlow's nearest honest reeve is a day south of the
span; sending a runner and returning with help takes at least two days. No one
travels from Purewater instantly. These locations extend the eastern Downs and
Greyspine foothills; they do not relocate the Wildwood or established landmarks.

Vaust: himself, Sergeant Cessa Dorn, six paid retainers (Harl Teven, Oda Vennick,
Merrit Caul, Fenn Ash, Joss Veller, Ina Kett); four labourers, two riding horses,
two draft horses, four carts, twenty grain sacks, one relic chest and sixty GP.
At start all at White Cairn. Retainers earn 1GP each daily, labourers 2SP each.
No unpaid replacements, free garrison or secret reinforcements. Plans use named
subsets, never the same guards in two places. Six grain sacks are reserved as aid,
fourteen remain. Losses recorded in Vaust extras before successor operations.

Maelin: one sound sixty-foot rope, a jack, two six-foot timbers, survey tools.
Len: one cart, mule, four medicine bundles, two food sacks. He owns the cart;
the supplies belong to the hospice. Anwen: hospice, ten beds, twelve days of
current food for six residents, twelve willing part-time local workers among
eight households. These are willing civilians, not a free army. Serevan: one
teaching house, two adult pupils and a particular funerary rubbing identifying
the standard; holds no authority over all elves. Local families have independent
plans and can choose to help, leave or accept Vaust's offer.

## Cast: intention, limits and knowledge

Maelin Voss, surveyor: wants Len free and a survey completed before winter. Owes
Anwen a reliable route; follows sound work but can become brusque when rushed.
Next: stabilise cart, then identify repair timber. Fallback: sheep-track and carry
medicine by hand. Knows hospice, detour, wind-markers and Vaust's public offer;
does not know his relic or eviction plan. Bargains for paid workers and fair road
access; will not knowingly send unprotected civilians into a fight. Can give
Hrill responsible work but does not appoint him commander.

Len Rook, carrier: wants his boot free and his mule/cart intact; owes the hospice
delivery. Next: remain on beam while cart is secured. Fallback: abandon cart,
save himself and whatever medicine can be reached. Knows loads and hospice route,
not politics. Rough short speech, warm when safe, no hidden conspiracy or debt.

Mother Anwen, lay hospice keeper: wants winter shelter open to all travellers;
owes residents safety. Next: hear Vaust's offer without surrendering guest access.
Fallback: disperse stores to households. Knows public repair licence, short food
stocks and Serevan's location; does not know secret cargo. Accepts grain without
exclusion pledges. Will temporarily close rather than make the hospice a barracks.
Does not replace Hrill as negotiator or investigate every secret for him.

Serevan Ashleaf, displaced elven teacher: wants his pupils safe and stolen heritage
returned; owes neighbours information but distrusts armed visitors. Next: survey
ravine escape path; fallback: move pupils with household goods. Knows the standard
was removed by Vaust's retained men (saw their cart at the dig, not the sealed chest
now). Knows its old funerary use, not any completed new binding. Will share the
rubbing and negotiate access after specific respectful conduct. Independent ally,
not a captive by design or an infallible magical solution. No animism subsystem
or spell is awarded by narrative description.

Sir Aldren Vaust: wants the pass as his permanent command and a place among
restorationist patrons. Ought to protect road users under a narrow repair licence.
Next: offer hospice aid, then move toll frames to Wind Stair if workers and access
remain. Fallback: take the relic chest and remaining money toward Hythe. Public
reason is winter safety; driver is resentment of being disposable in royal service.
Knows his theft, intended buyer and local layout; knows no PC, Nimras adventure
or secret past of Hrill. Offers pay or a nonexclusive toll compromise if pressed;
will order eviction and violent seizure if a viable route threatens his control.
Will kill an armed blocker only when lesser methods fail and his men can win.
Surrenders for protection from retaliation when cornered; no mandatory death duel.

Sergeant Cessa Dorn: wants her people paid and out of a failing contract; ought to
obey Vaust. Next: escort aid with Oda and Harl; fallback: bring them back to camp.
Knows licence limits and aid conditions, initially not relic identity. Accepts
safe withdrawal with earned pay, credible evidence of an illegal order, or honest
official intervention. Is not guaranteed to defect. No automatic Insight catch;
stat checks decide disputed deception. Other retainers know their assignments,
not all secrets. No one knows Hrill until an actual meeting or report.

## Evidence and per-character boundaries

Facts are seeded through CLI with only listed knowers. Hrill initially knows his
visible bridge situation and the painted hospice destination. He knows neither
strangers' names nor Vaust. Do not narrate a prior encounter or familiar campaign
emblem without agreeing Hrill's past.

Repair licence: copy in Anwen's kitchen, original in Vaust camp, reeve's office.
Shows lawful bridge repairs and ordinary toll proposal, no eviction authority.
Aid conditions: written proposed pledge in Cessa's bag; identical copy on camp
table; any recipient can testify after reading. Local food can be accepted on
different terms if negotiated; nobody signs automatically.
Secret purpose: Vaust's buyer letter in his strongbox, the standard/rubbing
comparison, and testimony from the cart-driver retained for the dig (create a
record if approached). No clue requires a single successful Insight check.
Wind Stair's bypass: Maelin's incomplete survey, Serevan's ravine knowledge and
physical mile-markers. Finding one route supplies actionable bearings for others.
Force preparations: frames, picks and measured chain at camp; Cessa's deployment
orders when issued; a scout can see men leaving. Do not give Cessa information
before Vaust actually sends the order. Future results stay not-yet-true.

## Playable situations and resolution

H1, The broken crossing (Act I): actor Len wants freedom, Maelin stabilisation;
current prerequisites above. Forty-foot bridge, twelve-foot compromised deck;
beam still intact. Stream ten feet below, stony shallows. Southern post sound;
jack and timbers within reach; steep but passable downstream bank thirty feet
away. Warning: cart creaks when weight shifts. No automatic failure timer by chat
turn; sustained risky movement earns danger. Standard Brawn for an organised
haul with sound anchoring, hard solo; standard Athletics to balance unsecured,
easy if belayed; Engineering standard to assess and plan lift. Influence standard
only for frightened uncoordinated helpers, not permission to talk normally.
Accept other workable approaches and Passion augmentation. Ordinary tying with
proper equipment no roll. A failed risky action shifts cart or loses a sack and
creates another decision; it does not automatically kill Len. Fumble or continued
unsecured exposure can cause a fall. CFI ten-foot fall: CLI1d6 to a random location,
plus normal damage modifier; armor ignored. Resolve any crushing through actual
damage, never impose a wound without resolution. Win: Len/supplies intact;
partial: person saved, cart or food lost; retreat: Maelin/Len attempt their own
escape through CLI if no safer plan. Medicine recovery independent of bridge repair.

H2, Hearths with a price (Act I/II): Cessa proposes six sacks in exchange for
exclusive patrol/toll rights and exclusion of Serevan's household from hospice.
Earliest d9/day, three escort personnel only, requires road/travel and intact aid.
Hospice courtyard sixty by forty feet, two gates, kitchen rear door, stone trough
cover. No weapons drawn initially. Hrill can negotiate, inspect the licence, turn
aid into a fair bargain, refuse, warn others or travel onward. Opposed Influence
vs Cessa Influence for disputed bargain; Insight vs Deceit only on an actual lie.
Her written conditions are obvious without a roll. Failure means a narrower offer,
withdrawal or delay, not automatic recruitment. If no agreement Cessa returns with
remaining sacks and a report taking half a day from Wind Stair camp routes plus
hospice travel; allow a day and a half back to White Cairn. Report does not teleport.

H3, The listening stones (Act II): Hrill can explore with a local guide or alone.
Wind Stair: eighty-foot exposed traverse, five-foot shelf, two refuge niches;
lower path visible in ravine, wind-markers have drilled mouths. Standard Perception
locates worn stairs; alternative map/rubbing/guide. Hard Athletics only if hurried
across loose shelf without rope; securing traversal and waiting out squalls works.
MilitaryHistory standard distinguishes imperial reliefs from later repairs;
Strategy standard identifies what a patrol can and cannot control. Success can
find a caravan bypass after two half-day work parties; failure costs exploration
time or damaged kit, with return bearings intact. No secret damage from hearing
wind. Turning condition: reliable alternative access removes Vaust's monopoly.

H4, A guest at Alder Hame (Act II): Serevan prepares household movement, not an
ambush. Teaching house thirty by twenty feet, front garden, rear ravine path,
dry spring shelter. Travel two hours from pass. Objects: funerary rubbing,
imperial tool impression, packed teaching slates. Hrill can earn cooperation,
verify claim, help improve escape route or arrange safe neighbours. Courtesy
standard for disputed etiquette; Influence vs Willpower only for an opposed request.
Good treatment earns ordinary answers without rolling for every sentence. Refusal
leaves visible ruins, map and camp evidence routes. Neither order nor cooperation
gives Hrill automatic access to an entire elf nation's secrets.

H5, White Cairn camp (Act II/III): quarry basin one hundred by sixty feet, two
cart entrances, twenty-foot rock shelves, timber shed and canvas command tent.
Six retainers and both officers at maximum; H2 or patrol assignments reduce this
roster. Four workers are noncombatants. Relic chest in wagon under shed, key Vaust,
iron lock and padlocked lid; letter in command strongbox. Standard Stealth vs
actual guard Perception, standard MilitaryHistory/Strategy for deployment study;
actual lockpicking requires training, but key, bargain, seizure or breaking lid
with tools are possible. Negotiation can produce withdrawal, fair tolls, restitution
or lawful detention with evidence. A frontal fight against everyone is dangerous;
warnings: visible sentries, numbers and escape routes before commitment. Guards
seek surrender before lethal force, then fight to keep route; retreat when leaders
fall and safe departure exists. Resolve PC attacks with attack-roll/effect choice.
Removing letter/chest or persuading soldiers changes capacities; no replacement assets.

H6, The road is opened or taken (Act III): derive objective from surviving situation.
Opening convoy needs verified bypass, two days of willing labour, Len's cart or
a paid replacement and consent of families. Vaust can attempt interception only
if he learns route in time and still has men. Deployment requires an actual scout
or report; no omniscience. If coercion survives, H6 can instead be a threatened
home defence, preventing eviction or intercepting the sole relic cart. Wind Stair
or bridge geography applies; no unavoidable combined set-piece. Strategy vs Vaust
Strategy for concealing a chosen deployment, not a roll that decides a whole battle.
Each actual risky crossing, deception, attack or flight resolved normally.
Clear victory: safe independent route, residents free, theft prevented/restituted.
Negotiated: supplies and agreed protection without exclusion or conscription.
Partial: people saved, relic escapes, or road open with limited conditions.
Failure: company truly occupies pass or exports relic after achieving prerequisites.
Withdrawal: Hrill leaves; those present act through their capabilities. No automatic
death or full imperial restoration follows any one local failure.

## Operations, warnings and cancellation

Aid attempt earliest d9/day. Evaluate weather, available bridge/detour and march
before firing; progress only on actual proposal/response. Occupation earliest d11/dusk
requires returned report or independent reconnaissance, at least four fit armed
personnel, two labourers, toll-frame cart and access; civilian resistance gets a
roll or negotiated result. Threat notices and marked timbers precede actual eviction.
Eviction earliest d12/day requires an actual refusal known to Vaust plus deployed
men; targets Serevan's house, never a forced already-captured teacher. Relic departure
earliest d13/day requires chest, cart, team, paid crew, known destination and safe
route. No stolen relic exports itself. Independent route completion and authoritative
settlement can thwart occupation; actual enemy surrender ends dependent threats.
Victory facts have CLI consequences where relevant. Hidden goals have initial
knowers, not retroactive outcomes. Time advances only after played durations.

## Readiness and counterfactuals

Refusal: Hrill can continue via sheep-track; independent communities still act.
Authority: valid evidence and a runner can obtain reeve intervention in two days;
Vaust's real licence does not confer criminal immunity. Negotiation: aid without
exclusion, paid withdrawal, civilian road ownership, restitution available.
Allies: Maelin's technical help and Anwen's workers matter; no ally performs every
decisive act. Early defeat: ends Vaust operation; road restoration and restitution
can conclude quietly. Out-of-order: can reach camp through ridge lane before
hospice, find relic before letter, or approach elves first; no forced revelation.
Knowledge: no cross-PC knowledge or prior acquaintance granted. Finite resources,
locations, NPC sheets, score/doing, agendas, operations and facts persisted by
setup-hrill-road.py. Review manifest and consistency before opening. Read H1 and
NPC/location briefs before narration. Alignment fields remain open; player-approved
Passions already supply applicable moral commitments, not compelled thoughts.

Travel audit: Cessa needs a day and a half between camp and hospice. Earliest aid arrival d9/day, report back d11/day, occupation attempt d11/dusk, eviction d12/day, export d13/day. Actual delays can push all later. No operation occurs solely because its date arrives.
