Developed at the player's request on 2026-10-23 for live d6/dusk, after the tournament and Hanzo's departure. No clock, scene, Halla outcome or mechanical state is changed.

Purewater has a few dozen surface-dwelling arcane practitioners, including learners. Earlier figures of nineteen or twenty-two were rough estimates, not a census. The five newly developed independent masters are Rill, Vey, Sere, Voss and Caldris; the already established Hesper is an additional major arcane power. Visitors vary; the proposed PC would occupy one learner place. This estimate excludes divine clergy, merfolk traditions, noncasting scholars and minor folk gifts. The existing King's Assay, Watercrafters Guild and Currents remain intact. No new residence or rank is assigned to Conall. Existing Water and Fire lore remains valid; ordinary learned illusion, alteration and divination are not automatically dragon demonology.

Mistress Ysabet Rill specialises in alteration and protection. Her Stillhouse is a former distillery in the Merchant's Quarter near the Forge Quarter: workshop, dry book room and old copper experimental vessels. Guild commissions support her research. She employs assistants and teaches very few pupils. She values repeatable observations and wants practical discoveries accessible beyond wealthy patrons.

Master Oren Vey specialises in divination, illusion and old arcane texts. The House of Small Windows on High Isle is his home, workroom and private library: a few shelves of precious working books. Private instruction and patron commissions support him. He lends selectively and believes dangerous procedures should circulate with instruction. His disagreements with Rill concern publication, patron ownership and responsibility. Neither master is omniscient, a secret ruler or a compulsory enemy.

There is no magical university or citywide mage guild. Apprentices learn through employment, copied texts, workshop assistance and supervised experiments. Research needs dry storage, ink, vessels, lenses, rent and time. Much scholarly work is textual comparison or physical measurement. Noncasting scholars contribute substantially. The King's Assay and Watercrafters provide existing institutional routes into practical magical work; the Currents remain an established divinatory tradition without their private workings being redefined here. Divine teaching remains the temple's own tradition.

Books are scarce rather than universally forbidden. Patron contracts, personal ownership and master's caution constrain circulation. The established King's Assay also licenses practitioners, hallmarks enchanted goods and investigates unlicensed or proscribed work. Independent study and membership of an informal circle confer no exemption. No new blanket prohibition on scholarly discussion is introduced. A household flame or ordinary arcane experiment does not by itself imply forbidden dragon ritual. The more detailed account in purewater-magic-and-power.md governs the relationships and jurisdiction outlined here.

The Understairs is an informal circle of about seven of those nineteen casters: two competent practitioners and five learners, most with ordinary day jobs. They meet beneath a bookbinder's shop in the Lullwater; the sympathetic noncasting bookbinder is outside the nineteen. They pool paper, candles, lessons and borrowed texts. Their shared notebook has several hands and corrections disputing corrections. Discretion protects jobs, patron relationships and sometimes unauthorised copying. Membership does not imply demonology or automatic criminality. There is no formal leader, magical police or obligatory secret conspiracy. Members can refuse dangerous research. Their shared study never grants an unlearned spell.

Proposed PC: Tavin Reed, a human arcane beginner in his late twenties, formerly a paid copyist and research assistant at Rill's Stillhouse. He rents over a sail-mender's workshop in the Lullwater, earns money copying technical instructions and investigating practical problems within his abilities, and participates in the Understairs. His proposed interest is finding and unpicking magical workings, with modest illusions and protective craft. His repaired book is wrapped in oiled cloth; his coat holds labelled samples and a brass lens. All actual spells must be selected under existing CFI rules: no unrestricted supernatural detection, counterspell or forensic reconstruction is granted.

Tavin's name, gender, history and manner are freely changeable by the player. Suggested passion collision: loyalty to his teachers versus sharing useful knowledge. No PC thoughts, decisions, dialogue, debt, crime, betrayal or relationship with Halla is prescribed. This is a concept only; no character sheet, stats, rank or spell repertoire has yet been created.

Revision requested by the player: the earlier account describes the visible working community, not the full extent of arcane power. Nineteen is the approximate publicly identifiable circle. Three additional reclusive powers bring the working estimate to roughly twenty-two, with considerable uncertainty. Rill and Vey are the two publicly approachable masters among about five major resident powers; the other three do not offer ordinary public services. This supersedes any implication that Purewater has only two powerful mages.

Rill's public workshop is genuine but not the limit of her capabilities. Her long private project is to preserve structures against decay: not merely patching stone, but investigating why matter loses its form. The old copper vessels support experiments whose significance her commercial patrons do not understand. Her useful commissions buy independence rather than occupying all her attention.

Oren Vey's consulting income similarly supports a much larger pursuit: recovering a vanished predecessor's dispersed library before a rival reconstructs its most dangerous work. His agents deal in inheritances, wreck salvage and forgotten family collections. His disputes with Rill are real but do not exhaust either person's life.

Maudren Sere lives behind the sealed frontage of a former bathhouse in the Northern Isles. An ancient, profoundly accomplished magician, Sere is investigating how a person might survive a change of body without losing continuity of self. Sere works through a courteous mortal agent, pays well for difficult acquisitions, and has interests extending outside Purewater. Statements about Sere's age and current appearance circulate as contradictory rumours. No involuntary body change or victim is established by this concept.

Lady Ilyra Voss maintains a walled garden on High Isle and a household supported by old land revenues. She is a formidable magician engaged in binding and bargaining with nonhuman powers, seeking release from an inherited compact which benefits her family while placing obligations on it. Her household purchases strange things and retains lawyers, sailors and occasional hired adventurers. The terms of the compact and the identities of its parties remain GM preparation, not settled facts. She can offer genuine protection and formidable hospitality without being benevolent in every affair.

Caldris lives in a shuttered observatory above the Merchant's Quarter. A powerful scholar of passage and distance, Caldris is attempting to reopen a route to a lost place. Long absences, paid intermediaries and competing claims to recovered objects complicate that work. The destination is not yet fixed, and no route or new travel power is mechanically granted by this outline.

These people have limited attention, conflicting interests and things to lose. Wealth, knowledge, servants, contracts and private research matter alongside spellcasting. They are independent actors rather than one secret ruling council. They can cooperate on one matter and obstruct each other on another. Their power does not make every assertion true or every attempt successful, and the existing Crown, temple, guilds, merfolk and street organisations retain agency.

Possible street-level spillovers, not past or scheduled events: rival agents bid for a salvaged chest while its owners try to sell it; a protective experiment preserves a bridge but interferes with the business below it; something acquired for a private study escapes confinement; two workshops receive incompatible promises of protection; a person is paid to retrieve an apparently insignificant page another master urgently wants. Consequences can be useful, alarming, comic or violent. None happens automatically, undoes Halla's victories, or becomes a compulsory adventure.

The Understairs has a further role: a place where juniors compare commissions, recognise recurring intermediaries and discover when separate errands serve opposed designs. Members are neither all innocent nor all covert servants. The proposed PC need not accept any master as patron.
