## Experience and central conflict

Three substantial, independently winnable adventures: a modest Purewater recovery job that can develop into a magical heist,
a borderland infiltration and extraction, then a contested expedition at the
Wildwood's edge. Magic dominates: authentication, disguise, counter-magic,
concealment, misdirection and exploiting physical workings. One or two possible
purposeful fights per adventure, never a compulsory quota. Genuine danger,
retreat, recovery and negotiation; no mandatory betrayal or capture.

Nimras is an independent entrepreneur. His code permits theft from willing
participants but protects ordinary people and the helpless. Clients offer pay,
useful introductions and a share of recoverable property with actual ownership
considered. He need not become an Assay employee, swear to the Crown or agree
to save the kingdom. Each contract can end before another is offered.

The central antagonist is **Severin Rusk**, a newly designed former factor in
Hanzo's border estates. He administered payments, transport and confiscated
property, not the Baron's private possession rite. With the Baron's Purewater
venture ended, Rusk wants to turn assets he can actually control into independent
wealth and patronage: a sale of a reproducible spirit-binding demonstration to
a small group of restorationist buyers. Successful delivery buys an estate,
protection and a place among people who formerly treated him as a servant.
Exposure links him to theft, unlawful detention and persecution. He will order
an abduction or kill an informed witness when he has a credible means to do so.
He is capable, personally accountable and defeatable before the third adventure.

This is a **new derivative venture** using surviving estate records and looted
relics. It does not supply, restart or replace Hanzo's original possession plan.
Hanzo's current fate is not invented; his return is not required. Rusk is not
retconned into the architect of Halla's campaign. Old magical lore's scripted
future scenes and prescribed immunity do not override actual played outcomes.

### The disputed object and its use

The **Ash Register** is a small brass reliquary containing the name-tablet of
one dead imperial oath-keeper and the estate survey that identifies where the
rest of that person's funerary remains were deposited. It is a specific anchor
and research lead, not a universal key, limitless weapon or irresistible curse.
The accompanying seizure receipts identify forest people deprived of their dead.
These physical records can tie Rusk to the Baron's estate administration.

Rusk's specialist believes a controlled demonstration can prove that an officer's
body can carry a bound military spirit. That prospect attracts a particular
restorationist patron; it is not proof of an existing army or royal complicity.
A complete authentic anchor, access to the remains, a practitioner, a prepared
working and an intended host are separate dependencies. Taking one away can
prevent the demonstration. Destroying a working can interrupt harm without
being a complete spiritual remedy. The bound spirit has its own identity and
will; an animist can negotiate release rather than simply operate a quest key.


## Player refinement: build the business gradually (25 October 2026)

Start with modest risk while Nimras establishes his independent business. His
willingness to break corrupt laws is permission for later work, not a request
for immediate dangerous criminal entanglement. Begin with the small tracing and
recovery commission above, clear fee and ownership, an approachable broker, and
room to negotiate or decline. Do not secretly turn that first routine job into
a mandatory ambush or an irreversible conspiracy commitment.

The auction recovery and guarded lots become a later opportunity within adventure
one after he has completed an initial job and built a contact. Separate the
ordinary tools/case return from any request to recover the disputed Ash Register;
the original fee does not oblige him to steal a guarded relic. The broker can
return the case while identifying where its separately consigned contents went.
Rusk's recovery agents become relevant only if Nimras knowingly takes custody
of their target or interferes and they learn enough to act. No danger merely
for opening his business. Advertise the increased risk before a new commitment.

Progression: straightforward recovery and appraisal → discreet commissions and
chosen auction infiltration → deliberate estate theft/heists and illicit deals.
Contacts, fees and reputation should develop through played work; create no
already-established business or accepted contract in the save. Later criminal
work remains optional and respects his protection of ordinary people. Existing
three-adventure geography and demonology revelations remain the wider arc.

## Adventure one — The Midnight Exchange

**Public job:** help Nessa locate and reclaim her missing appraisal case from a
consignment broker serving the floating auction in Purewater. Begin as paid
tracing, authentication and negotiation; evidence may later establish theft.
Nimras can finish this first modest commission through a cooperative return or
a compensated release without burglary or combat. The case holds her working tools, fee records and a sealed
consignment she was paid to authenticate. She does not demand indiscriminate theft
of other lots. Nimras can inspect her retained rubbing before taking the job.

**Practical opening:** once the player chooses to return, establish travel and
rest through normal play. Nessa meets him at her rented workshop in the Merchant's
Quarter; Three Steps is an available paid base if he chooses it. A genuine letter
of introduction may be requested from Vey/Rennic, never assumed already sent.
The first action is comparing the workshop's consignment receipt, case description
and broker's acknowledgement to locate the missing property. These point to Velvet Hold's berth and its preview, not
to the principal enemy standing in front of him. Nimras knows only the offer and
facts he examines. Arrival date and auction deadline are set after travel, with
at least a full day of advertised preview before the bidding; no retroactive timer.

- **Act I — Enter the market:** trace the missing case, learn access and choose an
  approach. Venn wants the lot appraised and sold. The case's unusual value and
  a rival inquiry provide warning that someone will pay to recover it.
- **Act II — Contest the consignment:** inspect, exchange, steal, purchase or
  recover the case. The Ash Register's receipts disclose a real border estate
  connection and a living claimant, while the magic requires further work to
  understand. Rusk's two agents can attempt recovery only if they identify the
  actual holder and can reach them. Winning the case is a genuine success.
- **Act III — Get clear and settle:** deliver the case, retain/copy evidence by
  agreement, protect anyone endangered by a witnessed recovery, settle with Venn
  or the Watch as appropriate. A pursued exit, negotiated handover or quiet
  departure can each finish the adventure. No compulsory dockside fight.

**Situation cards:**
1. Workshop investigation (initial low-risk business commission): front door and rear yard, 20ft room, bench/lockbox as
   cover, ordinary neighbours outside. Nessa wants recovery and grants inspection.
   Receipt gives berth without a roll; skill/magic can recover additional detail.
   A failed examination costs time or leaves one detail unresolved, not all leads.
2. Auction preview: barge 70 by 20ft, two decks, gangplank and stern cargo hatch,
   paired display cabinets, mooring ropes and a shore ladder. Four guards shared
   across entrance, display and reserve; six guests and ordinary crew are people
   to protect. Invitation, bidder deposit or work as an appraiser gives access;
   burglary, disguise and bargaining remain alternatives. Locks and a specific
   alarm require CFI-compatible preparation before play; no universal antimagic.
   Taking the case changes the inventory immediately; guards cannot duplicate it.
3. Recovery attempt: Rusk's two agents need a sighting or named lodging and an
   intact report route. They observe the exit, ask staff or follow someone; clues
   include repeated appearances and a paid porter inquiry. Their demand, attempted
   theft or attack uses actual opposition rolls. They withdraw if divided or
   badly hurt. A 30ft quay with side alley, crates and boats offers retreat and
   separation; no attacks on crowds for spectacle. Capturing one removes one agent
   and yields only the information they actually hold.

**Reward/ending:** agreed 10GP total and a repeat client on delivery. Returned
objects, preserved anonymity, compromised cover, bargains and public evidence
are separate outcomes. Rusk loses the Register if Nimras secures it; later rites
must then remain impossible unless an actual recovery succeeds.

## Adventure two — The Ashwick Papers

**Public opportunity:** the recovered trail can support a paid theft of Rusk's
border account book, a claimant's restitution commission or a negotiated venture
with Nessa/Marek. Choose a real payer and settle terms in play; suggested budget
15GP plus expenses with 5GP advance included. No automatic unpaid conscription.
The journey through Ford and Ashwick introduces the March, its landlords,
confiscations and competing accounts of the empire.

- **Act I — Follow the property:** identify the actual depot and assess who works
  there willingly. Talk to a carter or claimant, compare receipts, scout the yard.
  The important change is proof that the seized objects belong to living
  communities, and Rusk's accounts really served the Baron's estate apparatus.
- **Act II — Take the papers:** infiltrate the depot and obtain the experiment's
  notebook or accounts; contact Talarin independently through a restitution lead.
  If Rusk learns of the breach, he can move his existing papers or attempt to seize
  Talarin using the retainers he still has. Warning precedes a reachable attempt.
  Talarin may already evade them; rescue is a possibility, not a fixed scene.
- **Act III — Break the expedition's preparations:** secure the records, recover
  stolen remains, protect a threatened witness or intercept the departing cart.
  Confronting Rusk here can finish his venture. The revelation is concrete:
  demonology is being sold as restoration, and suppression of forest animists
  removes people who can undo the binding.

**Situation cards:**
4. Carters' yard: 60 by 40ft, two gates, raised office, stacked wool bales and
   animal pens. Depot staff provide schedules for fair pay or proof of danger;
   they are not disposable guards. A paid examiner/merchant identity, stakeout or
   paperwork comparison gives distinct access. Travel time and local contacts
   must be established before any deadline. No invented anti-elven checkpoint
   sentence is imposed as unavoidable flavour.
5. Estate depot heist: 80 by 50ft walled compound, gatehouse, stable loft,
   rear culvert (crawl, no armour), office window over roof, locked study.
   Four retainers total, ordinarily two awake on watch. Accounts in study;
   notebook with Malrec if present, otherwise its actual recorded location.
   Repair, disguise, stealth, observation and negotiated access can all matter.
   Alarm gathers only surviving reachable guards. Rusk can surrender or escape
   by an existing gate; no secret tunnel invented to preserve him.
6. Conditional seizure/interception: requires Rusk's verified location for
   Talarin, two available retainers and travel. Their hired cart and bought rope
   are warnings; carter, scout or discovered order can expose the plan.
   A fork in the road, ruined sheepfold and stream crossing offer observation,
   division and evacuation. Resolve abduction against Talarin's actual abilities,
   not a clock that automatically succeeds. Lost captors cannot guard the depot.

**Ending:** restitution, intact evidence, freedom of witnesses and disabled
capabilities count as victories. If the experiment is prevented here, adventure
three becomes a claimant-funded recovery and release expedition; it does not
invent a more powerful replacement villain.

## Adventure three — The Unbound Dead

**Public opportunity:** travel toward Drakesfall and the Wildwood's edge to settle
what the evidence has uncovered. Routes, boats and intermediate camps connect
the map; elapsed travel is real. Establish provisions and a rest opportunity.
The site is a newly designed ruined estate chapel near Drakesfall, not an assertion
that Hanzo's old primary anchor tower has moved or that Cailan's arc is unresolved.

- **Act I — Reach the place on workable terms:** negotiate access with Talarin and
  local people, find the funerary chamber and learn what a safe release requires.
  Evidence challenges the auction catalogue's story of voluntary imperial service.
  Nimras can inspect, translate, counter or steal without accepting a faction oath.
- **Act II — Control the working:** if Rusk retains the necessary assets, he and
  Malrec attempt the promised demonstration. Recover the anchor, cut access to
  the chamber, extract an intended host, turn an employee or seize the notebooks.
  If already thwarted, the challenge is safe restitution and a contested claim to
  valuables, with danger from the specific unstable binding rather than new troops.
- **Act III — Settle the dead and the sale:** prevent or break the surviving rite,
  enable the spirit's release, and decide which evidence and treasure leave with
  whom. Exposure can identify this venture's buyer and network ties without
  demanding an immediate war against the entire Movement. Nimras's gains and the
  freedom of actual people survive the ending.

**Situation cards:**
7. Forest-edge parley and reconnaissance: approach via old causeway or scout's
   track, camp outside a stone boundary. Talarin wants restitution and safety;
   receipts, recovered property and observed conduct carry more weight than an
   alignment speech. Refusal closes that person's cooperation, not every route.
   A charcoal-burner's map, survey and visible ruined roof provide other bearings.
8. Chapel infiltration: courtyard 60 by 40ft, broken west wall, intact south door,
   roof stair, 20ft descent into a two-exit crypt; stone piers break lines of sight.
   Working occupies a visible central table; anchor fits in a portable brass case.
   Only Rusk's surviving deployed retainers attend. Malrec needs hands, time and
   access; interrupting him is meaningful. A hired host's consent cannot substitute
   for informed consent: if any coercion is discovered, rescue remains possible.
   Final host identity, participation and mechanics must be fixed before play,
   never retroactively chosen to punish Nimras's approach.
9. Release or extraction: crypt, courtyard and clear route back to camp remain
   usable. Talarin needs possession of the real remains, communication and time
   for a bounded rite; Nimras can secure access, sever an active channel, protect
   the host, fetch an inaccessible fragment or negotiate directly. Establish the
   spirit's actual request and mechanics before entry. No automatic waves while
   an NPC performs the real ending. Quiet release is earned if enemies are beaten.

**Ending range:** complete prevention and release; stopped sale with an anchor
secured for later remedy; negotiated custody and restitution; rescue without
capturing the research; or retreat leaving a working prototype if opponents truly
complete it. A prototype is one dangerous binding, not an overnight imperial army.
Evidence can expose the Baron's estate network and a specific purchaser. Payment
and recoverable valuables are negotiated before departure; rewards never include
new CFI rank/spells without their actual advancement requirements.

## Operations and dependency discipline

Use dates relative to the accepted job, not d7 planning time. Define real travel
and durations when opening state is known. The preview advertises the auction
at least a day ahead. Rusk's next transfer can be learned from a dated receipt;
the depot's departure requires a prepared cart, crew, notes and destination.
The chapel demonstration requires Register, remains, Malrec, apparatus, host and
patron's agreement. A deadline schedules an attempt; it establishes no success.

Track the independently supplied guards (Venn four, Marek two, Rusk six:
twelve total across three separate parties) accurately: only Rusk's
six are committed to his scheme. Venn and Marek do not replenish his losses.
Do not double-count travelling guards at the depot. Ordinary employees are not
combat resources. Money paid, horses lost, seized notes and missing relics remain
lost unless a concrete operation changes custody. Rusk may negotiate with Venn;
he cannot command her. Malrec cannot reproduce the authentic anchor by fiat.

Before activation convert these plans into individual characters, inventories,
knowledge facts/edges, locations, conditional agendas and operations in TypeDB.
For every operation encode prerequisites, intended actor/target, warning routes,
attempt timing, results and cancellation effects. Future outcomes remain
not-yet-true. Retire downstream attempts when assets are removed.

## Stress test and preparation gates

- Refuse a contract: no PC acceptance or pursuit invented. The market persists;
  evaluate any activated NPC plan honestly. Offer a distinct voluntary lead later.
- Inform Conall/Hesper/Watch: actual evidence can secure protection/arrests. An
  ordinary theft can be resolved without finishing all three adventures. Distance
  and jurisdiction have real limits, never automatic institutional incompetence.
- Defeat Rusk early: cancel the sale and his recovery attempts. Restitution and
  exploration may fill later adventures if the player wants them; no replacement
  mastermind. If that concludes the story, conclude it.
- Negotiate: Venn, Marek, hired guards and Rusk have different acceptable terms;
  loss of a patron can stop the sale without killing everyone.
- Go straight to the chapel: allow it if the route is discovered. Missing papers
  complicate interpretation, not entry by arbitrary plot lock.
- Bring Rennic or allies: use actual willingness, travel and sheets. Nimras need
  not be isolated to make a challenge. No hostage tax on his relationships.
- Keep the Register or destroy it: it genuinely blocks the planned demonstration.
  Verify destructive action's mechanics and spiritual consequences before the
  player commits; no retroactive disaster for clever prevention.
