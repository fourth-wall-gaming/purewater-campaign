# Hrill Helmdance: exploration beyond Purewater

Unselected proposals, 25 October 2026. No new places, people, operations or
outcomes below have been made live canon. Existing geography comes from live
lore The Lands of Thornmere and The Realm of Thornmere. Starting boundary:
d7/day, shared Purewater continuity; do not rewind either existing protagonist.
Hrill's character creation record holds the confirmed experience brief.

## Twelve seeds

1. Reopen an upland road: a milestone lies in a tree's roots; survey and repair.
2. A pilgrims' hospice: birds nest in its bell; exploration and building a home.
3. A migrating lake creature: a roof-sized shell moves past a jetty; observation.
4. An inland beacon chain: a lantern burns without a keeper; hill travel and rescue.
5. A drowned orchard: apples float up from beneath clear water; lake exploration.
6. A missing seasonal fair: painted wagons wait at an empty ford; pastoral journey.
7. A shepherd's high pass: wool catches on ancient bronze gates; mountain passage.
8. A woodland guest-road: a feast table grows through a fallen wall; diplomacy.
9. A veteran's walking map: old billets have become villages; revisit and reconnect.
10. A travelling mill: its sails turn on a wheeled tower; practical itinerant work.
11. A dry river expedition: boat moorings hang above grass; survey and restoration.
12. A cliff monastery's seed archive: wind scatters labelled packets; salvage journey.

Selected for diversity: road expedition, living landscape, lake natural history,
woodland diplomacy, itinerant restoration. No returning antagonist required.

## 1. The Road That Fell Off the Map

Public premise: Wendlow's old upland road has become impassable. Settlements
beyond it want a route opened before winter. Hrill can accompany a survey,
cross deserted waystations and discover who still lives beyond the last bridge.

Proposal cast: surveyor Maelin Voss wants a safe cart route; upland guide Ruskell
Fen wants visitors to respect local pasture rights; hospice keeper Mother Anwen
wants winter medicine. Opposing interests: toll-holder Osric Fell wants to retain
traffic on his dangerous detour (six retainers, one toll bridge, loses income if
bypassed); upland households resist a road cut through their scarce grazing.
Fell might forcibly seize survey tools if his monopoly is threatened; households
will bargain over route and labour. No hostility is inherent to local people.
New places: Bent Mile, Three Hearth Hospice, Wind Stair. Fantasy: wind sounds
through buried waymarkers, useful for locating the old route but no automatic map.
Without help the route remains closed and supplies become unaffordable.
Entry: paid survey escort or a hospice supply party encountered independently.
Hrill can survey, mediate access, repair crossings and protect workers. Authorities
can fund labour and restrain Fell; they cannot establish the route from a desk.
Acts: reach the cut-off settlements → explore and negotiate a viable route →
attempt the first provision crossing. Wins include a usable road, friends and
possible stewardship; failure or withdrawal leaves specific stretches unresolved.

## 2. Where the River Went

Public premise: a river below the Greyspine has abandoned its bed overnight.
An old drowned valley stands open, with stairs leading into the hillside.
Downstream farmers need the water; upstream families fear its return.

Proposal cast: millwright Tessa Marrin wants water back; valley elder Bel Torren
wants houses protected; naturalist Orrel Dain wants to understand the watershed.
Opposing interests: reeve Soren Pell wants an immediate breach (twelve labourers,
two teams, limited tools); newly exposed valley residents oppose flooding their
homes (eight boats converted to shelters, stored food for ten days). A confrontation
over the breach can become violent because both communities' homes and livelihoods
are at stake; Pell will accept a workable alternative water supply.
Places: Silent Mill, Stair of Reeds, Hollow Spring. Fantasy: a seasonal stone
creature has shifted the watershed; its ecology can be understood and worked with.
No compulsory monster fight. Entry: investigate the dry river or join a millwright's
survey. Without effective action crop losses and a reckless breach threaten both
communities. Officials can supply engineers; the hidden channels need exploration.
Acts: follow the empty river → explore the watershed and inhabited valley →
attempt a workable water settlement. Gains: restored water, saved homes, knowledge;
partial results follow which channels and precautions actually work.

## 3. The Long Shore

Public premise: Hrill joins a small boat travelling north along the Sacred Lake,
checking abandoned landing places. A huge unfamiliar animal has appeared in the
northern reaches, and fishers disagree over whether to follow it or drive it away.

Proposal cast: boat-owner Edda Vale wants reliable landings; young chartmaker
Perrin Moss wants accurate shoals; fisher Nera Quill wants safe nets and catches.
Opposition: hunter Garr Hew wants valuable shell-ivory (one harpoon boat, four
crew, two heavy harpoons); shore warden Ilyen wants all strangers expelled from a
breeding inlet (three wardens, no authority over the whole Lake). Garr may ram a
boat preventing his hunt, but will accept a credible profitable alternative.
Places: Bell Reed Landing, White Shingle Isle, Narrow Water. Fantasy: the animal
carries a mobile reed habitat; discovery rests on observation, not prophecy.
Entry: paid passage work or a stranded fishing family requesting assistance.
Without intervention a hunt may disrupt fishing and place boats in danger;
capture is an uncertain operation, never an automatic dated outcome.
Lake authorities can regulate hunting once informed; distant patrols cannot
chart every inlet. Acts: travel and chart landings → learn the animal's movements
and competing needs → attempt a safe passage or workable shore agreement.
Wins include a trustworthy chart, safer fishing and continuing companionship.

## 4. Guests of the Wildwood

Public premise: a border village asks Hrill to accompany the return of an elven
traveller's belongings. A neglected guest-road leads toward communities with whom
Thornmere has had little peaceful contact. Crossing it requires hospitality,
observation and respect for people outside the King's authority.

Proposal cast: traveller's sister Selaith wants an honest account; beekeeper
Maren Holt wants missing hives recovered; interpreter Dain Mere wants dependable
neighbourly contact. Opposition: charcoal master Berent needs a winter kiln site
(seven workers, three wagons); grove guardian Iravel protects living burial trees
(four scouts, authority limited to the edge settlement). Berent will defend his
workers against attack; Iravel will use force to stop felling. Both can accept
another fuel site. Neither represents all humans or all elves.
Places: Honey Gate, Guest House of Rain, Listening Glade, all at the Wildwood's
edge; no invitation to the established Deep Glades is presumed. Fantasy: certain
trees preserve sounds, without supplying infallible interpretations.
Entry: accompany the belongings or help a stranded beekeeper. Inaction means
failed contact and a preventable land-use clash. King's officers can assist
their subjects but cannot command elves. Acts: reach the border community →
travel the guest-road and understand local needs → negotiate or physically
establish a safer shared boundary. Possible gains: friendship, access, purpose.

## 5. The House of the Open Door

Public premise: an abandoned roadside hospice in the Wolds could shelter
travellers again. Making it habitable takes Hrill across the countryside to
find its scattered tools, water source and people willing to help.

Proposal cast: mason Bethan Reed wants sound walls; herbalist Oswen Pike wants
a place to teach; former keeper Jessa Linn wants travellers welcomed again.
Opposition: shepherd Hal Vern uses its only reliable spring for wintering sheep
(three households, one cistern); relic seeker Corra Venn wants to excavate under
the chapel (two diggers, limited money). Corra will undermine unsafe foundations
for a valuable find if unchecked; Hal will block diversion until his flock has
water. Neither must become a villain. Bargains and practical alternatives matter.
Places: Open Door Hospice, Slatewater Spring, Chapel Below the Hill. Fantasy:
old hearthstones retain summer warmth, if their source can be found and restored.
Entry: take shelter there or accompany the mason. Without help winter travellers
lose shelter and excavation threatens the building. Local authorities can clarify
ownership and supply modest funds; restoring water, heat and staff needs action.
Acts: make the house safe → explore its scattered dependencies → attempt a
working first winter opening. Success can provide a home or a base to leave;
Hrill is never compelled to settle permanently. No mandatory lethal opponent.

## Review direction: the veteran and the realm

Accepted design direction, not a selected premise: give Hrill an opening practical
victory, a discovery with personal significance and a decisive opportunity shaped
by his earlier actions. His experience should help people build or preserve
something beyond military victory. A ruined waystation bearing a familiar campaign
emblem is a possible opening image. Which campaign, what he did there and who he
served require agreement; no guilt or response is assigned. Different memories
are evidence to investigate, not proof that his entire life was a lie.

## Possible connections to the Dragon Kings — OOC proposals

Established foundation: the Dragon Kings were overthrown; restorationists seek
to revive their power. The accepted kingdom backdrop connects revisionist imperial
history, anti-elven persecution and demonological binding. Forest animists hold
specialist knowledge for addressing bindings. Halla's completed outcomes stand.
Nimras's Midnight Exchange arc is selected preparation, not accomplished history:
its Ash Register, Rusk venture and Drakesfall-edge expedition cannot be assumed
to have occurred. No particular new Hrill connection below is live canon.

### Road: who gets to rebuild the realm?

Possible development: an imperial restorationist offers repairs, food and patrols
to isolated settlements, seeking control of a strategic pass and local service
oaths. His appeal rests on real neglect. Privately he intends to use the pass to
move particular confiscated anchors and a binding specialist; this would be a
new venture, not a supply line retroactively assigned to Hanzo's original rite.
Give the principal opponent an identity, finite force, timetable and vulnerable
logistics during selected preparation. Do not make the old toll-holder a secret
mastermind merely to preserve the first draft.

Hrill opportunities: lead the first crossing and save usable supplies; examine
an old campaign station using agreed service knowledge, compare its account with
local testimony and inspect present coercion; explore an alternative route and
organise a community-controlled crossing that denies the restorationist his
monopoly. His final action can secure a route, protect a settlement or stop an
actual transfer. Negotiation, engineering, alliances and selective force all matter.
Risk: falls, exposure, seized food, coerced service and a finite armed seizure
attempt if the opponent has intelligence and means. No required betrayal or battle.
Global effect: a denied transport corridor and durable independent communities,
not merely information for a later hero. Strongest current integration candidate.

### River: a power people can live with

Possible development: the exposed valley contains a water-power once resisted
by the Dragon Kings. A restorationist excavator wants to damage its refuge so
one specific future binding site becomes accessible. Define this dependency and
the being's agency before play; it is not a universal imperial on/off switch.
Hrill can follow the dry bed, reach endangered inhabitants, explore the channels,
test an alternative water course with local experts and protect the refuge while
restoring downstream supply. Flood, collapse and violent interference are credible
risks. Global effect: preserve an independent older power and deny access to one
usable site. A local water settlement remains a complete accomplishment.

### Shore: recover a route to the older world

Possible development: the creature's seasonal movements reveal a safe passage
to a community with surviving memories and practices of resistance. A particular
restorationist survey party seeks the same passage. The ivory hunter can remain
an independent threat; the animal is neither an imperial weapon nor a quest token.
Hrill can chart shoals, save a threatened boat, earn permission to visit and keep
an actual survey or coercive landing from exposing the refuge. Risk: cold water,
boat damage, separation and the competing expedition's finite capabilities.
Global effect: create an agreed route for people and specialist knowledge, or
deny hostile access. Residents decide whether to share knowledge or travel.

### Wildwood: the people who can answer the binding

Possible development: imperial agents target a particular community's teachers
and funerary places to suppress counter-binding knowledge. Hrill enters through
hospitality and a practical local task, not an automatic rescue appointment.
He can explore an abandoned guest-road, establish trustworthy contact, discover
an actual raiding approach and help locals keep an escape or resupply route open.
If raiders are defeated early, do not manufacture captives for a finale.
Risk: a discoverable, preventable raid or site seizure, alongside forest hazards.
Global effect: the community retains freedom, teaching capacity and choices about
cooperation. Specialist allies have their own intentions; Hrill must have a
decisive physical objective rather than only escort the person who resolves things.

### Hospice: something that can survive a returning empire

Possible development: a restored hospice becomes a freely chosen refuge and
meeting place for travellers, including people displaced by restorationist
pressure. Its water and heat can remain independent local problems. A specific
restorationist authority may later demand guest lists or exclusion of elves,
only after learning what the house does and acquiring an actual means to enforce
that demand. Do not punish hospitality with a compulsory raid.
Hrill can reclaim the spring, explore the older foundations, recruit willing
keepers and establish a workable protected departure route if real pressure
develops. Global effect: an enduring refuge and communications link that makes
persecution harder. Warmth and shelter are genuine victories; escalation is optional.

## Shared protagonists and a possible long arc

Distinct lenses, subject to each player's choices: Halla can encounter the
struggle through public protection and honor in service; Nimras through chosen
contracts, magical acquisition and binding research; Hrill through travel,
historical memory and communities resisting domination. These describe possible
roles, not assignments or knowledge grants. None is the other's required courier.

A possible global development is scattered local ventures → evidence of particular
restorationist alliances and practical dependencies → opportunities to prevent
specific manifestations of imperial power. This is not a scheduled world event.
The Dragon Kings' return can be prevented, limited or changed by play. If a later
manifestation does occur, specify whose return, what form it takes, what enabled
it and how it can be opposed. No inevitable apocalypse or army produced by one relic.

Possible intersections: a usable road alters transport options; a protected
animist community can choose to assist a binding investigation; evidence from
Nimras's actual work can identify a patron Hrill encounters; Halla's actual official
connections can help secure protection if asked. Apply real travel and knowledge
edges. No automatic reunion, shared briefing, stolen victory or required order.
Hrill can complete an adventure, decline another and retain his gains.
