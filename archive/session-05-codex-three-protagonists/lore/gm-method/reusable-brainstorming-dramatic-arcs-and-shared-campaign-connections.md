# Five Campaign Proposals

Reusable planning method agreed from Halla's campaign discussion, 2026-10-22.
This governs proposal generation. It does not make proposals live canon.
User preferences take precedence over older SCENARIO.md and CAST.md prescriptions.

## 1. Establish the starting position

Read the live save, not the original adventure's intended ending. Record the
PC's capabilities, relationships, resources, unfinished obligations and player
interests. Distinguish an earned victory, an unresolved fact, and a rejected GM
invention. Resolve or explicitly park the previous chapter before introducing
a new campaign. Never quietly import an alternative timeline.

For Halla: adult dwarf, tournament champion, capable fighter, practical canal
skills, inexperienced Crown guardsman, honest ambitions, vulnerable criminal
past. Purewater remains home. New districts, islands, underground spaces and
communities can expand it. Familiar NPCs may connect him to an adventure; the
world does not need to be a reconstruction of his personal history.

## 2. Generate twelve seeds before selecting five

Cross several genres with places, independent conflicts and physical objectives.
Draw from history, folklore, geography, adventure fiction, occupations, ecology
and ordinary human ambition. Transform inspiration into original situations.
Research only when a specific factual or cultural claim requires it.

Useful genre spread: criminal struggle; expedition and discovery; military
survival; supernatural mystery; pursuit and monster hunting. Do not produce
five mysteries involving the same patron, five hostage rescues, or five villains
with different names doing the same thing.

Each seed needs a concrete opening image and an activity the player can enjoy.
Examples: crossing roofs during a pursuit, choosing an approach to a flooded
ruin, commanding a dangerous withdrawal, boarding a stranded ferry, hunting
through abandoned reservoirs. A conversation may open a door; it is not the
entire adventure.

## 3. Build five independent situations

For each survivor, specify:

- What is happening without Halla, why now, and what changes if no one intervenes.
- Two opponents with distinct purposes, resources, limits and methods. They need
  not both be villains, and either may bargain or lose.
- At least three new consequential NPCs, three new locations, and one unusual
  fantasy element or distinctive human conflict.
- A credible entrance for Halla, plus a second entrance if he declines the first.
- Why Halla can materially affect events, and why referring the issue to Ward
  does not instantly settle the whole situation. Official help must still matter.
- A motivating stake someone would kill for if pushed, with a named holder and
  credible reason; adapt the commitment threshold for an agreed nonlethal genre.
- Things to win as well as things at risk: freedom, treasure, allies, knowledge,
  responsibility, a home, a cause, or a place in the world.

At least three of the five options must work without any returning antagonist.
Use at most one familiar major antagonist per proposal unless the user requests
an ensemble continuation. Do not manufacture crimes or secrets to create leverage.

## 4. Give each a three-act arc

Act I: a specific disruption, tangible stakes, and a first substantial objective.
Its culmination changes the situation or opens the wider adventure.

Act II: several approaches, expeditions and encounters; opposition responds to
actual losses and information. Discovery changes the available choices. Allies
have purposes of their own. Preparation, fighting, ingenuity and withdrawal all
need room to matter. A successful early solution may shorten or replace an act.

Act III: a decisive opportunity created by earlier events. Specify the target,
location, competing interests and possible outcomes, not Halla's decision or a
required final speech. Victory, partial victory, defeat, negotiated resolution
and withdrawal may all be possible. Show the resulting changes afterward.

Three acts are a design aid, not compulsory events. No forced capture, betrayal,
death, moral lesson, lost loved one, or minimum loss. Build enough material for
roughly six to ten sessions, but let effective play resolve it sooner.

### 4a. Dramatic strength and the protagonist's perspective

Added after Hrill's design review, 25 October 2026. Apply to every protagonist,
using the player's desired genre and tone. A pleasant activity, evocative landscape
or three act labels is not evidence of a developed story arc. For mythic or
cinematic proposals, explain the following before recommending one:

- The dramatic through-line: an achievable opening accomplishment, discoveries
  that change the conflict's meaning or control, and a decisive opportunity
  created by earlier actions. Identify the mythic image or historical force and
  its concrete effect on places and people. Scale and spectacle alone are insufficient.
- Active opposition: who wants what, what they will attempt next, their finite
  means, who can be harmed, and what changes if they win. Distinguish villains,
  competing legitimate interests and environmental hazards. Do not require an
  evil mastermind in a restorative or non-adversarial game, but do not sell gentle
  friction as a powerful antagonist.
- Danger and risk: physical hazards and credible enemy actions, something the
  protagonist values that can actually be lost, warnings, counterplay and retreat.
  Avoid both compulsory suffering and a picturesque journey with no consequences.
- Character-specific opportunities in each act: what the PC can physically do,
  discover, protect or decide because of their approved experience, relationships,
  capabilities or values. State what their action could change. Ask whether a
  generic capable traveller could replace them without altering the design; if
  so, strengthen the connection. An NPC delivering a personal revelation is not
  enough. The PC must have consequential work at the decisive opportunity.
- Personal meaning remains open: put evidence, relationships and choices before
  the player; do not prescribe guilt, disillusionment, redemption, a lesson,
  renewed military service or any other emotional conclusion. Agree invented
  service history before using it. Do not manufacture a secret crime or disgrace.

For Hrill specifically: exploration beyond Purewater, an old campaigner's weariness
with fighting, honor and the search for purpose must shape the situations and
possible resolution. Give experience uses beyond combat, relationships worth
forming, and accomplishments worth keeping. A proposed arc may bring him into
contact with the realm he served and people whose memories differ; the player
decides what those encounters mean. Neither universal corruption nor compulsory
pacifism follows from his dislike of Purewater.

Label design maturity honestly: seed, proposal, developed outline, or verified
playable adventure. Recommend on dramatic strength only when the actual outline
supports it. The first Hrill exploration proposals were atmospheric seeds with
thin personal connections; their numerical self-scores did not establish quality.

### 4b. Give the setting a global storyline

General setting-design principle, clarified by the player 25 October 2026:
**a setting should have a global storyline that ties its individual stories to
an overarching theme and narrative.** Establish this at the setting level before
designing connections for individual adventures. It should explain what is
changing in the world, which forces drive or resist that change, what is at
stake across communities and generations, and the central dramatic question
that different protagonists encounter from different perspectives.

Write a short global-storyline brief containing:

- The overarching theme and dramatic question.
- The historical situation and present change that make the question urgent.
- The major actors, their competing visions, methods and limits.
- A possible development across several adventures, with turning conditions
  and multiple outcomes rather than a fixed sequence or inevitable climax.
- How different regions, communities and protagonists experience this story.
- What local choices can change globally, and how wider changes affect local life.

The global storyline supplies cumulative meaning and direction. Adventures can
advance it directly, reveal another perspective on it, show its consequences,
or explore its central theme through an independent local conflict. Not every
antagonist must serve the same organisation, and not every adventure must uncover
another piece of a conspiracy. Deliberate thematic connections are valid; label
them as thematic rather than claiming they causally advance a global operation.
Allow quieter independent stories to show what people value and what larger
conflicts threaten. Do not prescribe the PCs' beliefs or make them accept a
single interpretation of the theme.

For Thornmere, the established overarching narrative concerns attempts to return
the Dragon Kings' imperial power, the older powers and peoples who can oppose
it, and the realm's response. A proposed thematic formulation is: **what will
people build in place of an unjust or failing order, and why might they embrace
the return of an older tyranny?** This question can connect honor and public
service, independence and magical enterprise, and a veteran's search for purpose.
The formulation is a design lens, not a required moral lesson or a new world event.

### Connect local adventures to that storyline

For an ongoing shared setting, include a brief global connection for each
candidate. Read the live overarching conflict and other PCs' actual outcomes
and preparation. Separate established history, current facts, selected GM plans,
and speculative connections. Identify whether the connection advances the
global plot, reveals its consequences or explores its theme. A thematic
resemblance is not a causal connection, but can be a deliberate narrative tie.

For a causal plot connection, record: the wider actor or force; the concrete dependency contested locally
(route, refuge, knowledge, recruits, legitimacy, site or object); how the PC can
encounter evidence; what successful local action removes or creates; and what
that changes for the wider struggle. A shared emblem or final-page cult reveal
alone does not suffice. A local adventure must also have a satisfying ending
and lasting gains without accepting the next world-saving mission.

In Thornmere, consult the established Dragon Kings history and the accepted
kingdom backdrop in midnight-exchange-world-brief.md: restorationist ideology,
anti-elven persecution and forbidden binding are related; forest animists hold
specialist counter-knowledge. The return is a contested ambition with material
and magical prerequisites, not an inevitable event. Do not revive Hanzo's defeated
Purewater operation, invent an already-restored empire or make every local
problem a branch of one conspiracy. Honest institutions, ordinary life and
independent antagonists remain consequential. Define any new magical mechanism
and its rules before play rather than treating mythology as an automatic power.

Multiple PCs can affect different dependencies of the same struggle. Preserve
their independent objectives, victories and knowledge. A shared location or
contact can connect threads, but require actual information travel and shared
elapsed time; never grant one PC another's discoveries, restore a destroyed
asset for another adventure, require a particular play order or make one PC's
unplayed success a prerequisite. If one prevents an operation, revise the other
thread to honor that prevention. Local success can materially weaken or prevent
the larger threat; no replacement villain or compensating catastrophe is owed.

## 5. Stress-test the candidates

Score each 0-2 on these eight questions; revise anything below 12/16, and reject
any candidate scoring zero on causality or player agency. Scores help compare
drafts; they are not evidence that a campaign will be fun.

1. Is the premise easy to picture and exciting to play?
2. Is it materially different from the other four?
3. Are motives strong enough to justify the conflict, with a concrete stake and credible information routes?
4. Can Halla act as protagonist and choose approaches?
5. Does it support sustained development beyond the initial problem?
6. Can the opposition actually threaten harm, with credible escalation, warning and counterplay?
7. Does it offer varied physical play, discovery and worthwhile rewards?
8. Do the acts connect through consequences and support a satisfying ending?

Also apply the character-replacement test in 4a and causal global-connection
test in 4b. Repair failures before recommending a dramatic campaign. Score the
actual supporting design, not intentions; disclose thin areas during GM review.

Run counterfactuals: Halla tells Ward; pays his debt; refuses the patron; defeats
the first opponent; tries negotiation; brings allies; investigates out of order.
Accept clever victories. If the entire campaign only survives by blocking a
reasonable choice, redesign it. If the first problem is easily resolved, let
that reveal a larger independent situation rather than secretly cancelling the win.

## 6. Present five short pitches

Use a title, genre, concrete premise, and a single line describing the broad
three-act movement. About 60-90 words each. Disclose the experience and public
stakes; withhold culprit identities, betrayals, hidden mechanisms, solutions and
outcomes. State which parts are proposals. A recommendation may name the player's
stated preferences as its reason. Never sell an option as objectively best.

Make the nature of opposition, tangible danger and the protagonist's personal
stake visible without revealing hidden identities or solutions. For a shared
campaign, briefly signal the possible broader connection. In an OOC design
review, answer directly about antagonists, risks, specific PC opportunities and
design weaknesses; the pitch's spoiler restraint does not justify evasive answers.

## 7. Develop only the selected campaign

Before deeper preparation, briefly interview the player about the desired mix
of combat, intrigue, exploration, magic and other relevant activities. Record
which should dominate and tailor the design to that answer. Reuse preferences
already supplied rather than asking again. The interview procedure is in the
construction method linked below.

Follow [adventure-construction-method.md](adventure-construction-method.md) to
build the cast, active antagonists, per-character knowledge, discoveries,
three-act development and concrete encounter/mission cards after selection.
Use [consequence-driven-design.md](consequence-driven-design.md) to turn those
plans into executable operations and consequences. Write
the likely harmful outcome without intervention, warnings, preventable stages,
resource dependencies and multiple possible endings before activating it.

Create NPC sheets and knowledge, maps, factions, assets, actual operations,
conditional clocks and the first playable situation. Persist those in TypeDB.
Keep unchosen candidates in documents, outside the live world clock. New NPCs
may be consequential from the outset; they do not require several appearances
before they can have an agenda.

After each session, check whether the player made consequential decisions, used
their capabilities, discovered something useful, and enjoyed the activities.
Ask what dragged. Revise the next situation accordingly. Difficulty and loss
alone do not measure quality, and the GM must not guarantee safety or success.
