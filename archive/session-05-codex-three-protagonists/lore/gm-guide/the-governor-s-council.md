Purewater is governed by a Crown appointee -- **[[Prince Emeric]]**, two-and-twenty
-- advised by a council of merchant princes who handle the day-to-day. Emeric signs
what is put in front of him. Two documents this month have mattered:

1. **The standing order** (seven days before the Tourney): complaints against the
   Baron's people are to be recorded and referred, not acted upon.
2. **The temple instruction** (four days before): the Lake Lady's fouling alarm to
   be stood down as a false reading for the duration of the festival.

Both were carried by the same three men. A party that gets that far will
reasonably assume it has found a cult cell inside the city government.

## It is not a cult cell, and that matters

**The Baron's scheme survives on not looking like a conspiracy.** Four generations,
two anchors, a boy, and a room. He does not recruit magistrates and he does not
need to. What he does is far cheaper and completely deniable: **he arranges for
three different men to each do one small, defensible thing for three unrelated
reasons.** None of them knows about [[Cailan]]. None of them has heard the name
Thuban Eta. Not one of them could betray the plot under torture, because not one of
them knows there is a plot.

If you make them cultists you get a worse story: the investigation becomes a hunt
for villains instead of a hunt for *how an honest city let this happen*, and the
Baron stops being frightening in the specific way he is frightening.

## The three

| | | |
|---|---|---|
| **[[Alderic Vantt]]** | **Bought** | The Baron quietly covered four thousand of his debt two winters ago and asked for nothing. He is the mover: he drafts, he proposes, he hosts the company on his own land. Not a believer in anything except the Vantt name. |
| **[[Hesketh Pyle]]** | **Frightened** | The Baron's people made his son's killing go away and kept the deposition. He seconds whatever Vantt proposes and hates himself. **The crack.** |
| **[[Maro Quist]]** | **Sincere** | Seventy-one, incorruptible, the best mind in the chamber. Voted for both on the merits, having been given true facts in the right order. **The wall** -- and the single most valuable ally in the city government. |

## The trap

**Quist subscribes to the Ember Society** -- eleven dinners a year, borderland
gentlemen, learned melancholy about the age that ended at the Lake. It is an
**[[The Awake the Dragon Movement]]** recruiting front. He has attended three
dinners and thinks they are antiquarians.

So the subscription list, when the party finds it, puts the *innocent* man's name
on a cult document while the bought man and the frightened man are not on it at
all. Let them chase it. Let them be certain. The correction, when it comes, is the
best scene in the political thread -- and Quist, accused wrongly and in public,
becomes the hardest ally in the city to recover.

The Society has its own write-up: *[[The Ember Society]]*. Sixty subscribers, of
whom four matter -- and **the fourth name on the list is [[Ivo Calder]]**, the
Watercrafters' assessor who signed the temple's fouling alarm off as a false
reading. That signature is what let Quist carry the temple instruction in good
conscience. Work backwards from *who signed the assessment* and the thread pays
out; work forwards from *who is in the cult* and it hands you Quist and a
disaster.

## How the party actually wins this

Not by exposing a conspiracy -- there isn't one to expose -- and not by
prosecuting anyone, because nobody in this city can prosecute a lord. By **making
the instructions expensive**, and by reaching the one man in Purewater who can act
in the King's name:

- **Vantt** moves the moment a Knight's crime looks like it will carry his name.
  Fear of the Baron works; the debt does not, and he will call it friendship.
- **Pyle** confirms almost anything if he is convinced it is already known and his
  son is not the price. Protect Corin Pyle and he will say it in open council.
- **Quist** changes his position in public, and takes the council with him, if he
  is shown evidence and not accused. [[Abel Weir]]'s referral book is exactly the
  document he responds to.

Any one of the three reverses the standing order. Quist reverses it permanently.

## Emeric himself

He is not stupid and he is not complicit; he is twenty-two, badly advised, and
schooled in law rather than knives. He can be reached directly -- and reaching him
past his handlers is one of the campaign's real objectives, because **the whole of
the Baron's plan ends in a private room with this young man in it.**

The bitter joke underneath the political thread: every instruction that made the
Baron's week easier was signed by the person he intends to destroy.
