# Magic and power in Purewater

Established during collaborative worldbuilding on 23 October 2026. The live campaign remains at d6/dusk, after the tournament and Hanzo's departure. These are institutions, relationships and interests, not events that have just occurred. No new employment, knowledge or obligation is assigned to the proposed PC.

## The distribution of power

Purewater's great mages have houses, patrons, creditors, correspondents, agents and places they cannot enter without asking. Their interests extend well beyond their public businesses. Magical ability is one source of power alongside Crown jurisdiction, temple standing, underwater access, waterfront labour, money and intelligence.

The five newly developed independent masters are Rill, Vey, Sere, Voss and Caldris. Hesper is an already established sixth major arcane power, whose existing history and allegiances take precedence. Conall is an accomplished warranted investigator, not a newly promoted archmage. Other Assay masters exist outside the city. Merfolk practitioners and the divine priesthood belong to traditions of their own and are not junior branches of the human arcane community. A few dozen human and surface-dwelling practitioners is a useful scale; earlier exact totals were rough estimates, not a census.

There is no single magical council. People who cooperate on one question may refuse each other on another. There is no hidden institution secretly responsible for all waterfront conflict.

## The Crown and the King's Assay

The Assay's established charter licenses practitioners, hallmarks enchanted goods, investigates unlicensed or proscribed work and refers findings to the Crown. Its central Register and seven-Master Table are at Caldreth. Its ranks remain Prentice, Marker, Assayer and Master Assayer. A sealed annual warrant, personal steel punch and marked silver thumb-ring signify the office. It recruits outsiders to reduce local dependence, although individuals and the central Table remain vulnerable to pressure.

This corrects any earlier implication that the city has no arcane licensing. Independent learning does not create immunity from the Assay. Nor does membership of an informal study circle establish guilt: what people practise, produce or distribute matters. The precise treatment of a disputed activity requires its actual circumstances rather than a blanket new criminal law.

Conall's warrant remains genuine and his arrival remains personal, with Temerach, rather than an invented assignment from headquarters. He is neither chief of a large resident Assay chapter nor commander of Purewater's wizards. He can investigate, make findings and seek assistance; he cannot summon a central response instantly. His personal connection with Temerach does not constitute an alliance between the Assay and the Swords of the Lake.

Three additional people make this institution usable without replacing Conall:

**Beren Holt**, resident correspondent, keeps a modest correspondence room adjoining an instrument-maker's shop in the Merchant's Quarter. Holt is a noncasting clerk and former instrument-maker, paid a retainer to receive sealed packets, arrange witnesses and preserve local copies. Holt has no warrant or personal punch and cannot license a mage. Their connections are to Abel Weir, harbour clerks and workshop owners. Their independence comes from keeping the Assay's work separate from favours to old neighbours; that separation is frequently awkward. They speak readily about people and reluctantly about interpretations. Holt wants the local correspondence to remain accessible and reliable, and will protect a witness's address before protecting an official's convenience.

**Master Assayer Sabeth Krail** sits at Caldreth and supervises correspondence involving Purewater. A formidable magician and examiner, Krail thinks a defensible finding is a weapon the Crown must actually be willing to use. She resists both guild capture and exciting allegations which cannot survive a hearing. Her power is access to the Register, central expertise and the Table's attention. Her vulnerability is dependence on distant witnesses whom she cannot protect merely by writing. She speaks in complete, economical statements and does not enjoy speculative conversation. She has institutional responsibility for Conall's work, not a newly invented personal mentorship. She is not currently established as arriving in Purewater.

**Assayer Damar Quill** travels the coastal circuit, examining salvage, enchanted cargo and disputed hallmarks. He is a capable field magician, socially easy, fond of a good meal and inclined to accept a harbour officer's practical account before suspecting a fraud. He wants trade to keep moving and ought to stop it when a finding demands it. He knows Deepkeel professionally and argues with Watercrafter assessors over standards. He can become an ally, rival investigator or inconvenient second opinion according to the evidence. His next visit has not been scheduled and he is not teleported into the city by this preparation.

**Old Johz** remains Conall's established Master Assayer and mentor. His unexplained absence remains unresolved. None of these new people knows his whereabouts by authorial convenience.

## Hesper, Marisette and the Pearl

Hesper uses they/them. They are already a master magician, publicly attached to Marisette as her enforcer. They do not hold an Assay warrant. Their existing private allegiance is preserved and is not public knowledge. Hesper must not be made a member of Conall's order for convenience.

Marisette supplies rooms, trusted staff, money and access to the city's influential visitors. Hesper supplies formidable protection and the capacity to make threats against the Pearl expensive. Neither can simply command the other in every matter. The houses' existing rule against selling people off the islands stands.

Hesper and Oren Vey maintain a narrow professional exchange through sealed requests: Vey can authenticate an old arcane text; Hesper can sometimes arrange discreet access to an owner. Neither receives the other's entire purpose. Rill respects Hesper's control of dangerous magic but dislikes the conditions surrounding private commissions. Hesper can sometimes obtain an introduction to Rill and cannot promise her cooperation.

Ravella's Siren's Call remains within Marisette's commercial network, while Ravella has an information business and loyalties of her own. Hesper does not automatically know everything Ravella knows. Crowbill can arrange transport or protection for the houses, but does not thereby command Hesper or gain access to their private affairs.

## The merfolk and the Mermaid's Court

The merfolk have their own households, routes, sacred places and disputes. They are not merely the source of human water magic. Below-water passage, access to submerged structures and local knowledge of the lake bed give them leverage which a surface title cannot reproduce. No new general ability to control all water or close every route is granted.

The established Mermaid's Court preserves old pure-water traditions from chambers beneath Lost Isle. It includes humans, half-merfolk and water-touched people and has a seven-member Deep Council. Its leader is the Nacre. The Nacre's nature and identity remain uncertain. The Court is an influential bridge between communities, not a government commanding every merfolk family. Its long aim of reclaiming and cleansing Purewater remains distinct from the interests of any particular household.

Two people provide concrete access to its competing tendencies:

**Thessa Under-Reed** is a merfolk envoy authorised by the Deep Council to negotiate particular exchanges with surface people. She receives visitors in an air-filled chamber reached from a partially flooded stair beneath Lost Isle; entry requires an invitation or another earned route. She carries narrow mandates rather than a universal power to make treaties. She wants durable surface obligations concerning discharge, dredging and protected passages. She can negotiate access and guided passage, introductions to aquatic practitioners and the return of recovered objects. She needs people on land who can make an agreement matter beyond one household. Her speech is direct and occasionally impatient; she does not turn every sentence into a water metaphor.

**Orun Shell-of-Night** is an elder on the Deep Council and a powerful practitioner of the Court's own tradition. Orun opposes the removal of sacred objects from submerged sites and the casual disclosure of old routes. His influence rests on respected families, ritual knowledge and support among those who remember failed surface bargains. His objection to cooperation is political and historical, not hostility to every human. He wants the Court to stop trading away access which the next generation cannot recover. He can withhold his own household's assistance and argue for wider refusal; he cannot magically compel the entire lake. His formidable magic requires a properly prepared repertoire before use in play.

**Ravella** has relationships across the boundary but is not the Court's ambassador. Her priority includes mixed and hidden waterfolk whom prestigious merfolk families may exclude. She can put someone in touch with Thessa, decline to do so, or advocate against a Council policy. Her ancestry and protective work remain matters discovered through play, not a new public announcement.

The Lake Lady's priesthood and the Court recognise overlapping sacred interests without sharing a chain of command. Nerissa's rites, Lilura's institutional work and the merfolk's practices remain distinct. The new relationships do not resolve inconsistent older descriptions of Lilura's exact office or age, or invent a new High Tide decision. Temple relationships should be played from the authoritative character record and actual scene, with conflicting material reconciled separately if it becomes necessary.

## The independent masters and their avenues of influence

### Ysabet Rill: workshops, guilds and the temple

Rill's Stillhouse occupies a former distillery in the Merchant's Quarter near the Forge Quarter. Its real laboratory lies beyond the public workshop. Her investigations concern preservation, form and decay; commercial contracts finance the long work.

Torval Bluehand commissions tests and supplies access to materials, guild facilities and engineers. Rill refuses exclusive ownership of every result. Their practical cooperation carries a continuing dispute over what the guild may keep secret. Dwarven repair shops and independent makers are an alternative source of expertise, so Torval cannot withdraw the whole city's skilled labour from her.

Nerissa can request examination of stone, vessels or substances affecting sacred sites; Rill's arcane analysis does not confer the priestess's water-sense or divine authority. Thessa wants Rill to test materials before they are placed near protected submerged structures. Rill wants access to structures which have survived underwater for centuries. Neither has given the other unrestricted access.

Her political weight comes from useful independent testimony, workers who trust her and patrons who require her judgement. She cannot legislate or command guild members. She welcomes competent juniors and expects them to disagree with her experiments, a habit which can sit awkwardly beside her impatience with criticism of her judgement.

### Oren Vey: court households, inheritance and information

The House of Small Windows stands on High Isle. Vey's income includes old investments and private patronage. His long pursuit is a vanished magician's dispersed library. His social position gives him invitations, introductions to executors and access to private collections.

He advises particular wealthy households, not Prince Emeric by automatic appointment. Any access to the Prince requires the household's normal arrangements; Captain Ward remains responsible for security. Vey prefers a quiet invitation to a public demand and makes small social exclusions when refused.

He exchanges limited services with Hesper and pays independent informants, including occasional Currents intermediaries, without controlling either. He seeks authentication of merfolk-origin objects through Thessa. Orun objects when he treats something recovered from a sacred site as an ordinary inheritance. Vey believes careful custody justifies retaining dangerous material; he is less good at recognising how often this leaves him in custody of it.

### Maudren Sere: old capital, bodies and private medicine

Sere's bathhouse in the Northern Isles is a substantial private establishment rather than a public business. Old holdings outside Purewater support agents, servants and research. Conflicting descriptions of Sere's age and appearance remain rumours. The underlying project concerns continuity of identity across changes of body, not a newly established atrocity or involuntary transformation.

Sere obtains uncommon supplies through intermediaries who sometimes use Crowbill's transport network. Crowbill knows the cargo he actually handles and the fees he receives, not Sere's whole research programme. The arrangement gives both sides leverage over particular deliveries, not ownership of the other.

Sere seeks consultation with water-touched practitioners about bodily adaptation and memory. The merfolk can refuse; no living person is declared a specimen or a supplier's property. Rill recognises the scholarly importance of the questions and disputes Sere's preference for secluded work. Temple healers can object to an actual practice without automatically having authority over all research. An Assay inquiry is possible on evidence, with findings subject to Crown judgement. Sere's age is not an exemption from the charter.

Sere's habitual method is to offer a generous, unusually specific exchange, then use another intermediary or wait when refused. Long patience is part of the threat. No present victim, kidnapping, illegal experiment or completed bargain is invented here.

### Lady Ilyra Voss: land, family and the older waters

Voss's household and walled garden on High Isle are supported by estates and revenues outside the city. Kinship and property give her access to councillors and merchant families. Her formidable bargaining magic supports a long effort to release her household from an inherited compact.

The compact involves an older power of the lake. It does not make the Lake Lady a villain, identify the Nacre, or put the merfolk under Voss's command. Thessa is a possible negotiator for questions affecting present households; Orun insists that Voss cannot purchase release by transferring an obligation onto people who never agreed to it.

Voss asks Vey for historical interpretation and Nerissa for religious counsel. Neither has promised release or endorsed her interpretation. Sere interests her as someone whose experience might extend beyond the surviving written terms; Sere wants something sufficiently rare in return. Her protection can be real and valuable. Her household is especially dangerous when a promise is phrased more broadly than the beneficiary understood.

She begins with hospitality and practical help, then negotiates an obligation. Under pressure she uses family and political influence before reaching for magic. The exact compact and its remedies remain private preparation; no compulsory victim or sacrifice is settled.

### Caldris: shipping, salvage and passages

Caldris works from a shuttered observatory above the Merchant's Quarter. The project is to reopen a passage to a lost place. Its destination is unsettled; no free travel capability is granted by this account.

Shipping investors and salvage partnerships finance expeditions and receive negotiated claims to recoveries. Deepkeel's office grants or refuses the permits within its actual maritime jurisdiction. Caldris dislikes the delays but depends on lawful berths, pilots and crews. Some consignments use independent boat owners, who can refuse work or demand better terms.

Torval wants technical findings. Thessa can negotiate specific submerged access; Orun opposes interference with protected passages. Caldris offers charts, records of recoveries and particular results in return. The Currents can sell reports of competing expeditions; their information is neither infallible nor free.

Caldris is expansive about possibilities and poor at remembering the ordinary work required to pursue them. He asks for a demonstration, offers a share in something uncertain and proposes a larger expedition when the small one fails. He cannot pay every disappointment away.

## Where these relationships become physical

The pressure points are places and resources rather than a sequence of briefings: a flooded access stair, a workshop with valuable apparatus, a lighter carrying an old copper vessel, a salvage site with several claimants, a quay where a merfolk envoy is waiting for an agreed escort.

The present waterfront struggle can affect shipping, workshop supplies and access without being secretly caused by the mages. Sella Marr, Oth Bren, Crowbill, the independent crews and Crown officers retain their established goals and resources. None receives magical reinforcements, a secret patron or knowledge of a wizard's research through this document. If a mage becomes involved, establish the concrete interest, route of information and actual decision at that time.

Possible future collisions include Vey purchasing a chest whose recovery Orun regards as theft; Rill insisting that a protected structure be made safe before an experiment continues; Caldris hiring a crew with a politically disputed landing; or Voss offering sanctuary on terms the recipient must read carefully. These are opportunities, not scheduled outcomes or established past incidents.

The Understairs sits across this network. A learner may copy for Vey, help at Rill's workshop or earn a fee preparing an expedition's apparatus. Comparing commissions can expose an overlap without revealing every master's intentions. A patron's offer includes a task, payment, risks and limits; it grants no ownership of a junior wizard. Tavin has accepted none of these offers merely by being proposed as a PC.

## Preparation boundary

The new named people's identities and relationships are established in lore. Complete mechanical sheets, spell repertoires and location staging cards must be prepared before they cast, fight or appear in a scene. No NPC receives automatic success, no ritual bypasses the rules, and no new fact becomes a PC's knowledge merely because the player helped design the setting.
