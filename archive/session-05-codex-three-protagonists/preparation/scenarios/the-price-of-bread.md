# The Price of Bread

GM ONLY. Live follow-up authorised 2026-10-21. Starts d3/night, session 5.
Use TypeDB for state; this document preserves construction and contingent play.

## Halla's Engine

Halla needs his honour to earn an honest soldier's place, yet obeying a lawful
superior can require abandoning the very people his honour obliges him to defend.
His asset is his good name: his word binds him more effectively than a chain.
Love of his sister and freedom from Crowbill pull toward security; Honour pulls
toward acting. Influence 10, Deceit 16 and Insight 18 leave public appearances
dangerous even when he wins the physical fight. Never prescribe his response.

## Antagonists

Alderic Vantt is the principal antagonist. His ordinary grain-credit and transit
business converts seized cargo into respectable collateral. He needs to satisfy
his creditor Hanzo without publicly admitting dependence. Restoring his credit
at the expense of river settlements lets him present interference as robbery,
even when Halla has honestly recovered stolen goods. He can offer genuine relief,
pay workers properly, and still insist on a ruinous priority over grain. He is
partly right: some cargo genuinely is his, loans fed people, and cancelling every
claim destroys livelihoods too. He remains warm and frightened, not a new hidden
mastermind. He has no knowledge of Halla's citadel history or secret plans.

Vask is the physical antagonist, acting to move Drell and inconvenient cargo out
of the reach of the promised Watch interview. His existing sheet stands: style
82, AP2, Stealth82, +1d2, no magic and no Luck. Do not inflate him after combat
starts. He can kill Halla through surprise, bow support and a sluice crossing;
he does not volunteer a fair arena duel. His command of the original frame is
NOT established. Removing a witness now is a new action, not retroactive proof.

Hanzo retains his locket and ambitions. His audience route failed because Halla
won. Vantt's shipments provide a fallback source of money and a route back toward
his border estate, not an automatic replacement possession. His departure and
the champion's recovery require live resolution. No forced second heist.

## Act VI: Seven Sequences

FOR: Halla recovers people and provisions through an active river adventure,
and decides what his newly legitimate name is worth when property and justice
diverge. TAKES: the anonymity in which he could oppose the Knights as casual
muscle. A public intervention, endorsement or refusal creates a lasting stance;
the particular losses follow choices and dice, never a compulsory tragedy.

### 1. The Boat Behind The Mill (d3/night)
Question: Can Halla reach the disputed lighter before it departs?
Moves first: Vantt buys transport; Vask's failed concealment lets Marda trace it.
Reversal: Drell is moving with the cargo, while some grain is genuinely Vantt's.
Cost: Acting commits Halla's remaining rest and exposes his involvement; waiting
spends the tide and moves the problem upriver. Reporting to Ward is viable and
does not consume an entire watch by fiat. Marda brings a cut boat-name board and
specific landing, not a demand that Halla accept another guard shift.

### 2. Across The Sluice (d4/dawn, earlier on arrival)
Question: Can Halla get aboard without the lighter or crew being lost?
Moves first: Escort hauls the boat into the mill's loading channel.
Reversal: Closing the gate traps the vessel but drives it onto the mill race;
opening it permits escape. The release winch is reachable along wet gearing.
Cost: Secure access spends equipment, time or concealment; pursuit sacrifices
the easy city return. No arbitrary gate collapse. Telegraph current, armour
weight and crossbows before commitment; use Boating, Mechanisms and Brawn.

### 3. The Sergeant's Passage (d4/day)
Question: Can Drell be brought to the interview the Baron promised?
Moves first: Vask's escort separates him from the cargo and takes a bank path.
Reversal: Drell offers testimony for protection, but his claim against higher
command must be tested and can be self-serving. He need not confess on cue.
Cost: Pursuit abandons an unattended load unless allies cover it; keeping the
cargo lets the escort gain distance. Rescue or arrest risks real combat.

### 4. A Generous Settlement (d4/dusk)
Question: Can Halla prevent hunger without endorsing an unjust seizure?
Moves first: Vantt offers full wages, a limited return of goods and a public
thank-you to the champion if the remaining shipment is left to him.
Reversal: His lawful share is real, and the boats serve forty upriver households
whose immediate food reserve the remaining mixed cargo includes.
Cost: Accepting surrenders a defined portion; refusing loses Vantt's credit and
backing. Clever negotiation can improve terms. One conversation, not hearings.
Concrete evidence: guild brands, split grain sacks, mill tallies, crew testimony.

### 5. The Broken Towpath (d4/night)
Question: Can Halla get the disputed grain and willing witnesses out?
Moves first: An escort cuts the towline if authorised to resist recovery.
Reversal: Saving the load at the race risks those aboard; saving them may mean
cutting it loose. The mill bypass offers a hard third route through the works.
Cost: Gear, provisions, fatigue or the secure route; resolved honestly rather
than requiring a death. If the escort was stopped, replace sabotage with an
ordinary dangerous transit, or compress it if the players made it safe.

### 6. Vask At The Crossing (d5/dawn)
Question: Can Halla deny Vask the people and cargo he came to remove?
Moves first: Vask tries to cut off escape at the bank crossing with soldiers.
Reversal: He wants Drell and the cargo, not Halla personally; civilians may be
allowed past while Halla must decide what to hold. He can withdraw if beaten.
Cost: Staying risks death; yielding loses tangible evidence or provisions.
Bowmen have actual ammunition and lines of sight; create sheets before rolling.
No immunity for Vask, free escapes or surprise class powers. A successful earlier
interception prevents this counter; do not restore his force to force a fight.

### 7. Bread And A Name (d5/day)
Question: Can Halla put the grain in the right hands and stand by his actions?
Moves first: Surviving creditors or crews present their claims where unloading
actually occurs, not in another office.
Reversal: Holding the grain is not enough: it must cross the damaged landing and
reach the settlement. Halla's practical dwarven skills can make this possible.
Cost: Distribution makes his stance public and spends time owed to his new
service; reconciliation with Ward depends on what he actually did and reported.
Success saves lives, preserves evidence and earns real allies; do not erase it
with automatic punishment. Failure creates shortages and displacement; deaths
are possible only through established conditions and resolution, never fiat.

Five or six sequences involve physical danger or recovery. Vask, crossbow fire,
moving boats and the mill race can kill Halla. The top stakes are lives through
hunger and an armed confrontation, not a bad review of a maintenance report.

## Branch Integrity

Ignoring the hook advances the shipment and provides a changed upriver adventure,
not a mandatory failure of the Crown trial. Obtaining Ward's permission makes
legitimate recovery possible; service is not a compulsory moral trap. Violence
against bystanders is never necessary. Keeping quiet tonight preserves the
requested family evening at the cost of the first interception window. No plot
requires Halla to break his word, lose his sister, or aid Crowbill.
