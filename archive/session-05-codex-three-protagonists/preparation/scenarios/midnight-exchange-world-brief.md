Selected by the player, 24 October 2026. This is OOC preparation, not an opening
scene. The live clock stays d7/day; Nimras is at his fellowship home outside
Purewater after successfully completing Glass Hounds. No return journey,

## Accepted player direction

The campaign should reveal the kingdom and the wider world beyond Purewater.
Use Purewater as an anchor with journeys outward; confirm any later preference
for a wholly itinerant campaign rather than imposing permanent employment.
Reuse magic-heavy fantasy-agent play and occasional dangerous combat.

Purewater follows the player's original thieves-world conception: a city that
presents itself as holy while its actual population, inequalities, livelihoods
and ambitions sustain a dark underbelly. The sacred waters and religion are not
therefore fake. Holy reputation, genuine devotion, private vice, exploitation,
survival and profitable crime can occupy the same street and household.

The wider kingdom is divided by dragon sympathisers who seek a return to the
old empire. Their movement uses revisionist history and powerful anti-elven
racism; its hidden programme advances demonology. These are connected: animists
from the deep forest, principally its wild elves, possess the knowledge needed
to truly counter the Baron's kind of art. Their persecution serves an actual
struggle over knowledge and magical power, as well as ideology and dispossession.
Racist claims are the movement's propaganda, not the world's narrative truth.

These points are worldbuilding supplied by the player. They are not automatic
knowledge for Nimras, accusations against particular existing NPCs, or a claim
that the Baron's thwarted Purewater operation has resumed.

## Political and commercial layers

Use these layers to design particular actors and jobs; they do not imply every
member of a profession belongs to a faction.

1. **Ordinary economy:** pilgrims, dock workers, sailors, craftspeople, sex workers,
   refugees, healers and shopkeepers have material needs and independent lives.
   Poverty and insecurity create opportunities for coercion and for solidarity.
2. **Respectable demand:** wealthy collectors, estate owners, antiquarians, temple
   donors and scholarly patrons can buy valuable objects without knowing every
   crime behind their acquisition. Particular buyers may know and choose to pay.
3. **Criminal supply:** theft, smuggling, protection, false provenance, intimidation
   and hired violence connect demand to goods. Prepare identifiable crews and
   brokers with finite assets, information routes and competing interests.
4. **Restorationist politics:** idealised accounts of the old empire, selective
   histories and anti-elven prejudice make some confiscations or expeditions
   publicly acceptable. Belief, profit, complicity and membership are distinct.
5. **Demonological acquisition:** specific committed actors can commission bindings,
   captured spiritual anchors or forbidden research for their own programme.
   Establish actual object, use, buyer, transport and witnesses. The historical
   Baron's own possession project required no supply line; do not rewrite that
   fact to justify these new, separately prepared operations.

The existing Ember Society is a useful model, not a list of guilty names. Most
subscribers do not know the hidden programme; genuine scholarship and public
dinners remain. Preserve Maro Quist's established innocence and the actual
information held by Ivo Calder and Cassian Bree. Their old case details become
Nimras's knowledge only through a credible discovery in this campaign.

The Crown, temples and Assay are neither universally captured nor a single
solution to everything. Particular honest officials can secure evidence, protect
witnesses or arrest criminals. Particular obstructors require motives and means.
No automatic noble immunity or secret complicity for King Ostmar, Prince Emeric,
Conall, Hesper, the independent masters or Nimras's fellowship is established.

## How this enters the adventures

These are situation seeds for the selected campaign, not incidents that have
already happened or mandatory links to the principal antagonist:

- A catalogue describes a forest community's funerary objects as the legitimate
  spoils of an imperial civilising war; a living claimant can contest that story.
- A recovery contract calls a spiritual anchor an antiquarian keepsake. Different
  people know its ownership, its magical use and who commissioned the purchase.
- A stolen case contains evidence of provenance, a useful magical object and a
  clue to its next buyer. Recovering it can genuinely finish that paid job.
- A patron's public nostalgia differs from the dangerous work commissioned by a
  particular agent; Nimras can investigate the connection rather than infer guilt.
- A borderland victim or animist is being pursued because of what they can undo.
  Give them their own goal, expertise, leverage and ability to refuse a bargain.
- A legitimate buyer, an ordinary thief and a committed demonologist want the same
  object for different purposes. Do not collapse them into one secret organisation.

The Exchange remains a market with many participants, not the Movement's universal
front. Some fortunes come from ordinary crime or independent research. Varied
motives preserve surprising alliances and opportunities for actual heists,
recoveries and magical fieldwork rather than a series of political briefings.

## Magic and the protagonist's role

Distinguish **interrupting a working** from **truly undoing its spiritual binding**.
Nimras's counter-magic, disguises, investigation, protection and battle magic
remain useful under CFI. He can capture a practitioner, take an anchor, disrupt a
site, save a witness or prevent completion if the actual rules and situation
allow. Do not retroactively give every demonic working blanket arcane immunity.

Animists have the spiritual relationships and specialist knowledge for the deeper
remedy: understanding the bound spirit and addressing the binding itself. Build
any specific rite and its limits using the current system before it is used.
Do not mix an unprepared base-Mythras Animism subsystem into a CFI encounter or
award Nimras a free new profession. Existing old lore already permits particular
anchors to be stolen or disabled; this distinction preserves those interventions
without treating them as a universal cure.

An animist ally needs a full character, independent motives and real decisions;
forest elves are neither a single political mind nor a collection of magical
quest keys. Nimras should have consequential work during any joint operation,
not spend the campaign escorting the person who performs every resolution.
