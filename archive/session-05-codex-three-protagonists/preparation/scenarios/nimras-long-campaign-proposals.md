# Nimras: longer campaign proposals

OOC brainstorming, 24 October 2026. All new people, organisations, places,
objectives and magical premises below are proposals, not established canon or
character knowledge. No world-clock advancement, accepted contract or active
agenda. Glass Hounds remains resolved; this does not reopen Senn's operation.

## Starting position and experience brief

Live TypeDB read: d7/day, session 6. Nimras and willing Rennic have returned to
their unnamed independent fellowship outside Purewater. Manuscript restored;
8GP paid; purse13GP4SP. Rank2 human Magic-User; Arcane Casting71, Arcane
Knowledge70, Stealth57, Deceit60, Influence/Insight/Perception/Streetwise70,
Survival60. Passions: independence70, fellowship59, respect for Rennic50.
Two experience rolls spent. Home region/fellowship proper name remain unchosen.
No rest or resource refresh occurred during planning.

Reuse the player's preferences: much consequential spellcasting, disguise,
infiltration and investigation; occasional dangerous combat; independent fantasy
secret-agent work. Interesting, seriously powerful wizards and connections to
city power are welcome. Whether to return to Purewater or roam remains open.

A standing campaign should provide an ongoing occupation or cause, a useful
base, recurring allies and opponents, money and access to magical learning,
and a sequence of adventures with endings that actually stand. Build chapters
of roughly 2–4 sessions, a first substantial arc of about 6–10, and room to
continue if desired. These are planning estimates, not minimum lengths.
Rank advancement, new spells, specialised abilities and research still use
actual CFI requirements; proposed rewards are not automatically granted.

## Twelve seeds considered

1. Independent arcane field agent among Purewater's great mages — retained.
2. Underworld recovery work against a murderous magical auction network — retained.
3. Expedition into drowned royal sites contested by rival claimants — retained.
4. Independent battle-mage missions in a developing border war — retained.
5. Mobile pursuit of a sorcerous fugitive through a chain of affected communities — retained.
6. A travelling magical practice in towns that cannot afford resident wizards —
   strong episodic frame, weaker principal opposition; retain as a possible mode.
7. Establishing a mage fellowship of Nimras's own — appealing long-term ambition,
   but too much institutional setup before adventurous work.
8. Recovering a vanished expedition beyond an unstable magical passage — good
   expedition, insufficiently distinct from the drowned-realm option here.
9. Bodyguard and counterspell work for a disputed royal heir — strong relationships,
   risks making the employer the protagonist unless carefully rebuilt.
10. Conall's full-time Assay recruit — rejected as default because Nimras values
    independence; official cooperation remains available in suitable options.
11. A prison break for enslaved spellcasters — powerful adventure but too close
    to the recently resolved mage-hunting conflict to be a distinct campaign pitch.
12. Retaliation by Senn's hidden superior — rejected; cancels the earned victory
    and invents an unseen replacement antagonist.

## Five candidates: provisional GM development

The public pitches withhold these preparatory details. None of the following
secret intentions are live facts; revise or discard them after selection.

### 1. The Free Hand — city-based arcane espionage

**Standing frame:** Nimras returns voluntarily to Purewater and establishes an
independent practice for discreet magical fieldwork. Several clients and personal
projects can generate jobs; no compulsory allegiance or criminal past is imposed.
An ordinary rented room can grow into a secure workshop and a network of contacts.

**Opposition:** proposed master Calven Drahl wants to secure an ancient binding
engine before rivals can use it against his household and dependants. He employs
retrieval crews and contract magicians, intimidates witnesses and will kill to
keep its location from hostile hands. His stated defence does not excuse using
others as expendable assets. Separately, independent operator Ilyra Voss sells
recovered workings and information; she wants control of the engine's trade and
may compete, ally or withdraw. Her means are buyers, paid agents and one safehouse,
not a private army. Neither knows Nimras's private plans automatically.

**Provisional new cast/places:** Drahl; Voss; Teren Lusk, a ward-breaker who needs
field protection. Lantern Court lodging/workroom; the Obsidian Conservatory;
Floodgate Vaults. Add their connections to actual Purewater geography only in prep.
Existing merfolk powers, Hesper and Conall can be approached through their actual
interests; none is secretly complicit or automatically a patron.

**Opening:** a client offers to retrieve a missing ward-breaker and their sealed
case from a closed conservatory; Nimras can inspect the last lodging, seek the
client's rivals or watch the building. No immediate revelation of the campaign's
largest prize. A separate offer to protect the ward-breaker's colleague provides
another entrance.

**Three acts:** establish practice and recover a person/object → field operations
expose competing claims and dangers → decide who can secure or disable the actual
surviving engine. Other jobs remain independently soluble.

**Why it lasts / rewards:** repeat clients, rival field agents, research access,
workshop security and growing leverage. Authorities can secure an exposed crime;
that does not appoint Nimras to a job or reveal unexplored magical sites. An early
victory over Drahl stands; assess whether remaining independent projects merit
continuation rather than adding a replacement secret master.

### 2. The Midnight Exchange — crime, theft and infiltration

**Standing frame:** voluntary recovery and protection contracts among a magical
black market whose auctions move between ports. A broker and travelling contacts
provide leads; Nimras can take legitimate recovery work or choose darker clients.

**Opposition:** proposed auction mistress Seressa Venn wants a unique succession
relic that lets its wielder pass a particular hereditary ward. Ownership gives
access to a hidden treasury; she has already bought an expedition's stolen notes
and hired a recovery crew. She will pay for abduction or murder to obtain the relic.
Rival collector Olt Marek seeks the same treasury for his claimants and wants the
relic intact; his money and hired guards are finite, and he may accept a division
or restitution. No ability to erase identities or defeat every ward is assumed.

**Provisional cast/places:** Venn; Marek; Nessa Rill, an appraiser demanding rescue
of a detained apprentice in exchange for useful introductions. The Velvet Hold
auction barge; an abandoned embassy; the Black Stair counting-house.

**Opening:** recover an appraiser's stolen case before an advertised auction.
The appraiser can hire Nimras directly; a legitimate owner can instead offer an
independent recovery commission. Magical authentication, disguises, burglary and
an occasional violent interception support different approaches.

**Three acts:** penetrate the trade and recover the first consignment → expeditions
and thefts contest the relic and its keys → resolve the actual attempt on the
sealed treasury, whether through seizure, recovery, negotiated access or exposure.

**Why it lasts / rewards:** rotating venues, proprietors with competing claims,
repeat recovery work, magical salvage and information. The Watch can close an
identified local auction; surviving sites and rightful claims are separate matters,
not an immunity shield or an automatically escaping villain.

### 3. The Drowned Crown — magical expedition and contested realms

**Standing frame:** a series of well-equipped, paid expeditions into drowned
royal sites, with a shore base and merfolk partners whose goals must be negotiated.
Underwater access and dangers need actual CFI equipment, spells and local aid;
Rank2 Nimras is never presumed able to breathe water or defeat a sea army.

**Opposition:** proposed merfolk claimant Princess Eshara seeks the crown that can
command one ancient royal defence network; she believes control protects her
people against surface incursions. Rival surface claimant Lord Rovan Sile hires
salvagers to seize it and secure the drowned treasury. Both would kill for the
strategic weapon and succession claim, but each has followers, limited diving
access and reasons to negotiate. Their claims and violence require investigation;
no existing merfolk faction becomes villainous by default.

**Provisional cast/places:** Eshara; Sile; Meren Glass, an expedition mage seeking
a missing research partner. The Bell Shoals camp; the submerged Court of Tides;
the partially air-filled King's Stair.

**Opening:** recover a missing expedition from a bell tower exposed at low tide;
a survivor's patron can commission Nimras, or a salvage crew can request magical
help. A genuine rescue is possible without first settling the throne.

**Three acts:** establish access and recover the expedition → contested sites and
magical defences reveal the crown's capabilities → settle the reachable royal
network and the surviving claimants' attempt to control it.

**Why it lasts / rewards:** discrete ruins, partners, maps, salvage, discoveries
and deeper access as the character develops. Official help protects the shore;
it cannot replace underwater expertise. Negotiating peace or disabling the network
can finish the central conflict early. No compelled coronation or war.

### 4. The Ash Marches — a battle mage's irregular war

**Standing frame:** independent magical field contracts along a contested frontier:
rescue, sabotage, reconnaissance, counter-magic and recovery. Nimras can choose
clients and causes; he is not automatically enlisted. A border town offers a
recurring base, healers, intelligence and people with lives to protect.

**Opposition:** proposed sorcerer-lord Vaust Ren is assembling a binding forge
that can rebuild his finite cadre of stone soldiers from rare material. He wants
the mountain passes and the wealth they carry, and uses coercive levies and raids.
Captain Edra Keln leads an independent raiding company seeking the same supply
routes and spoils; she can oppose either side and will bargain rather than die
for Vaust. Both will kill for actual military control and its profits.

**Provisional cast/places:** Vaust; Keln; Jessa Thorn, a displaced field mage
seeking her captured apprentices. The town of Harrowbridge; the ruined Sun Fort;
the Cinder Pass forge. No connection to Hanzo or his completed Purewater campaign.

**Opening:** extract trapped apprentices from a border fort before a known raid,
with routes through its old passages or a negotiated escort. Alternatively take a
paid counter-magic job protecting a retreat through the same region.

**Three acts:** protect a local community and learn the war's means → raids,
alliances and expeditions weaken the forge's supplies → decide the surviving
contest for the passes and the forge. No mandatory battle won by one Rank2 mage.

**Why it lasts / rewards:** recurring communities and changing military geography,
paid fieldwork, recovered magical teaching and stronger allies. Authorities are
already involved; Nimras supplies a limited, useful specialty, not all their work.
Fewer, significant fights remain possible, but this is the most combat-oriented
option and needs explicit confirmation against the player's earlier preference.

### 5. The Ashen Pilgrim — pursuit and supernatural hunting

**Standing frame:** investigate the wake of an itinerant sorcerer and take related
local contracts, building a travelling network of safe houses, colleagues and
survivors. Pursuit includes learning where to go next, rather than automatic
tracking or a target who always escapes on cue.

**Opposition:** proposed fugitive master Halren Veyr is gathering funerary workings
to bring back his dead household. He will rob protected sites and sacrifice others
if convinced that is necessary; what his rites can actually do is a discovery.
Independent relic-company captain Damar Kesh wants to capture Veyr for a reward
and sell the assembled research, with no interest in preserving affected places.
They have different information, finite travelling assets and failure conditions.

**Provisional cast/places:** Veyr; Kesh; Sister Anwen Tarl, a ward-keeper protecting
survivors. A silent wayside hospice; the Glass Heath battlefield; the Monastery
of Last Embers. Unusual manifestations need setting-compatible rules during prep.

**Opening:** a hospice pays Nimras to find its missing ward-keeper after funeral
lamps begin burning unattended. He can take that local job or accept a separate
investigation from a rival fellowship. No presumed death or attack on Rennic.

**Three acts:** resolve local harm and identify a pursuable route → investigate
several communities and intercept real acquisition attempts → confront the actual
surviving rite or capture the sorcerer wherever earned play makes that possible.

**Why it lasts / rewards:** travel, new magical traditions, research and discrete
rescues/hunts. Authorities help within their reach and can receive a captured
fugitive. An early capture is genuine; aftermath and separately motivated relic
buyers may be offered as another chapter, never imposed to undo it.

Outstanding design questions are deliberate: Free Hand/Exchange need a selected
first client and a stronger act-turn discovery; Drowned Crown needs practical
water access; Ash Marches needs appetite for combat checked; Pilgrim needs the
player's appetite for a primarily travelling campaign checked. None is ready to
run before selection, brief interview and full construction/model verification.

Counterfactuals applied to all five: refusing the first employer leaves a distinct
entrance, or the PC can decline the premise entirely; authorities can win a case
without erasing inaccessible or unrelated objectives; negotiation can change
alliances and secure an ending; allies supply bounded real help; investigating
out of order changes knowledge and enemy opportunities; early antagonist defeat
removes actual capacity and may finish the campaign. No planned sequel overrides
these outcomes. Never change the previous commission's success or make the
fellowship secretly treacherous to provide a new hook.

the magic-heavy fantasy-agent brief. Ask which appeals, and then clarify only
remaining choices such as a city base versus travel and emphasis/tone. No need
to re-interview established preferences or advance time.
