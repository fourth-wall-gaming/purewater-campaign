# GM Development Notes: Five Alternatives

2026-10-22. Speculative, noncanonical. Do not activate all five. Use
brainstorming-method.md; the public pitches deliberately omit these details.
Three-act transitions depend on play, never scripted PC decisions.

## 1. The Quiet War

World problem: an outside contraband concern wants dependable control of an
inland port. Crowbill's local accommodation cannot survive its exclusive system.
It operates independently of Halla and before it learns his name.

New cast: Sella Marr, ambitious freight broker seeking monopoly through exclusive
contracts; Captain Oth Bren, her disciplined mercenary partner seeking permanent
power; Neri Pell, independent boat organiser whose family business serves both
networks and who will not sacrifice her crews for either. These are adults.
New places: abandoned chainworks, workers' floating market, customs cisterns.

Act I: Crown duties intersect an actual Crowbill consignment. Crowbill asks for
information or deliberate inaction. Establish the real cargo, witnesses and
evidence of Halla's past before drafting any threat. Debt payment must work;
blackmail is fallible and exposing it may hurt Crowbill too. Initial seizure or
refusal reveals competition for more than this shipment.
Act II: Halla can use Crown backing, allies, interception, infiltration or open
opposition to take away faction resources. Physical opportunities include a
canal convoy interception, captive-witness extraction and chainworks raid.
Rivals can counter actual actions, not read the PC's mind. Crowbill can be an
enemy, uneasy ally, or defeated former patron. Do not mandate corrupt guards.
Act III: a struggle over dock access and armed control, shaped by which assets
remain. Halla can expose, defeat, divide or bargain with the powers. His status
and the waterfront change. No automatic dismissal or compulsory forgiveness.

Tension persists after involving Ward because two mobile networks have
independent assets, witnesses and bases; Crown support gives real force and
authority. Guards can win. New criminal foes must have actual combat sheets.

## 2. The Drowned Crown

World problem: changing water levels expose an old city's functioning flood
works. Explorers, current inhabitants and investors want incompatible uses.

New cast: Delra Flint, dwarf surveyor seeking secure access for her workers;
Eren Voss, rival expedition captain who values discovery but will seize a route
by force; Tavil, adult representative of a concealed amphibious community whose
homes depend on the flood system. New places: suspended stair, bell foundry,
submerged civic vaults, pump halls. No tie to the Baron's locket or Halla's past.

Act I: an expedition contract or independent venture, entry, securing a base.
Halla's practical abilities matter alongside martial leadership. Act II: a
connected map with multiple routes, rival footholds and machinery affecting
water levels; discoveries establish what treasure removal changes. Creature
territories and rival escorts are physical threats. Act III: a contest over
access to the central works, with different resolutions for trade, flood safety,
treasure and inhabitants. Never require drowning a community as the only solution.

Reporting to Ward can obtain help but cannot explore or secure the ruins by
itself. Early victory over Voss leaves environmental and community objectives.

## 3. The Siege of Lantern Isle

World problem: an outer harbour's fort and signal station control passage that
two rival powers require. Occupation has already cut normal trade and escape.

New cast: Captain Maelin Rook, occupying commander whose employer abandoned her
troops and who now demands a permanent holding; Orsa Fenwick, island shipwright
organising survival; Lieutenant Dain Sorrell, Crown relief officer willing to
destroy the harbour to deny the occupiers. No secret relation to Halla.
New places: Lantern Isle fort, salt gardens and ridge tunnels, wreck harbour.

Act I: a genuine relief mission, interrupted landing, securing allies or a base.
Act II: several tangible objectives permit alternate strategies: food stores,
signal fire, tunnel access, hostile artillery, transport for civilians. Halla
can earn command through action, never gain an officer commission by fiat.
Act III: a relief, breakout, negotiated surrender or successful defence depending
on resources and allies. Enemy surrender is possible and must count as victory.

Ward already supports the mission; limited boats, hostile control and physical
distance prevent immediate rescue, not arbitrary refusal. Enemy fire and a lake
crossing are dangerous; civilian losses are contingent, never a required scene.

## 4. The Night Ferry

World problem: an old compact permits travel between Purewater and a separate
lake shore with uneven time. A new attempt to make passage permanent has begun.

New cast: Captain Ysra Mere, returned ferry captain seeking her missing crew;
Hadrin Vale, a human trader seeking control of the passage; the Reed Advocate,
an otherworldly collector enforcing the compact literally. Invent the compact's
specific terms and physical mechanism before play; do not shift them to defeat
player solutions. New places: empty tollhouse, drowned orchard, other shore's
lantern market, ferrymen's causeway.

Act I: investigate physical evidence and attempt a crossing; do not imprison
Halla by a scripted abduction. Act II: competing routes and purposes, rescued
passengers, hostile crossing crews and material tests of the compact. Each clue
must enable action. Act III: secure, renegotiate, sever or redistribute access
through a risky final passage. Returned people and lost time depend on choices.

The Prince can supply a boat and people; mundane authority does not command the
other shore. No guaranteed sacrifice, dead-relative manipulation, or invented
knowledge of the PC's private memories. Include brave, strange and warm moments.

## 5. The Ashen Hunt

World problem: an independently operating captive-creature trade has lost an
adult magical predator. Its hunters seek the valuable living specimen; the
city wants safety. Establish its ecology, powers and tracks before play.

New cast: Mara Quen, reservoir guide seeking her missing adult hunting partner;
Ser Joric Hest, celebrated hunter protecting a reputation and his employer's
investment; Vessa Coil, adult creature trafficker who can abandon one specimen
but wants to conceal her active holding sites. New places: dry reservoirs,
reed-island nesting grounds, abandoned furnace, concealed transport pens.

Act I: a public bounty, physical tracking and a dangerous initial encounter.
Act II: Halla may kill, capture, redirect or investigate the quarry; the trade
and rival hunters continue to exist if he kills it early. Pursuit opens new
territory and further practical objectives rather than resurrecting the beast.
Act III: close the source of repeated danger by an assault, interception,
exposure with seizure, or other player-led operation at the surviving trade
assets. A tame monster or villain confession is not the only resolution.

Mythras play: stalking, concealment, traps, terrain, monster anatomy, fighting
and extraction. Show credible warnings; never assign an unbeatable creature
merely to keep the story alive. Crown involvement can protect districts and
support raids, but someone must find and reach the moving targets.

## Candidate review

All five have a world problem independent of Halla, three new consequential
NPCs, several new places, physical objectives, potential rewards and alternate
solutions. Only Quiet War requires a returning antagonist. None requires that
Halla lose his sister, job, reputation, weapons or a fixed fight. All allow
institutional support and effective early victories. The first is the closest
match to the player's explicit Crowbill request; the second adds the most
traditional expedition play. These are proposals, not evaluated playtests.

## Activation

After selection, prepare the actual geography, opponent sheets, known facts,
routes, resources and conditional next moves. Add only the selected situation
to the database. Draft the opening to reach a substantial player decision
within one exchange. Follow up on the player's chosen pursuits, including
paths outside the outline. Do not require six to eight sequences per act.
