## 3–4. Five independent candidate situations and arcs

### A. Glass Hounds — pursuit and counter-magic

Independent situation: stolen research has enabled a small, finite pack of arcane hunting constructs. Their maker wants them recalled; a rival recovery company wants living examples to sell. Vaur's retained manuscript is sought because it concerns the work. Why now: a specimen and a buyer are both in Purewater. Without intervention, the company attempts to seize the surviving pack and its controlling materials and transport them to a purchaser. Dangerous urban hunts and coercive use against casters become credible consequences, not automatic events.

Opponents: **Sivren Kest**, a renegade artificer/hunter seeking to destroy his compromised research and regain independence; and **Captain Mara Senn**, a professional recovery commander seeking saleable specimens and a profitable contract. Different wants permit bargains and a split. Initial design limits: four field hounds, two inert unfinished bodies, one workshop, a small team of six hunters; no effortless replacements or omniscient tracking. Exact sheets and tracking rules required only if selected.

New consequential NPCs: Kest, Senn, **Doria Flint**, a glassworker who supplied the bodies and can help interrupt fabrication without controlling the constructs. New locations: **Furnace Court**, **the Glasswalk roofs**, **the Drowned Kiln**. Fantasy element: spell-hunting glass constructs with explicit, counterable detection rules and finite bodies. No human possession or compulsory stolen identity.

Entrance: the Vey lead places Vaur's trail at Furnace Court; a hunt is physically underway there. Second entrance: a glassworker seeks protection from a recovered specimen. Nimras can track, negotiate, counter or destroy workings, use disguise and decide how to fulfil a real recovery. Reporting to the Assay or Ward can secure a workshop or civilians; it cannot instantly identify and intercept mobile quarry. Early capture or destruction stands. Rewards: recovery fee, rare research or safe study access, reputation and useful allies. Stakes: lives, coercive magical power, custody of the manuscript and teacher.

Act I: intercept the opening hunt and gain a substantial lead, specimen or prisoner. Act II: attack workshop, field team, transport and control dependencies by chosen approaches; rivals respond to actual losses. Act III: prevent or control a transfer using whichever assets and routes survive. The teacher can be recovered early; the independent trade can continue, be resolved by allies or be left behind if Nimras ends his commission.

### B. The Drowned Observatory — expedition and competing discoveries

Independent situation: a newly accessible observatory contains dangerous astronomical and passage research. An expedition financier seeks exclusive salvage while a merfolk guardian seeks to keep particular chambers sealed. Vaur's manuscript helps interpret the place; it is not an automatic master key. Why now: a limited physical access window identified by ordinary surveys and observation, with weather and tides described before action. Without intervention, rivals attempt incompatible excavations; loss of salvage, damaged chambers or a dangerous release follow only actual breaches and failed opposition.

Opponents: **Dame Corsel Ardent**, a salvage financier with two boats, eight paid expedition hands and a contracted caster; and **Malkor Dane**, a rival scholar willing to sever access to prevent exploitation, at risk of stranding the people inside. They are neither omniscient nor able to conjure replacement ships. **Asha Tern**, a merfolk guide, has her own interest in inhabitants and preserved routes; she is not ordered by a human patron.

New locations: **the Blackreef Steps**, **the Tide Bell Tower**, **the Understar Observatory**. Fantasy element: an ancient lens room whose prepared effects and physical controls must be specified before play. No undefined world-destroying button. Entrance: follow Vaur's expedition. Second: intercept its supplies or negotiate an alternative merfolk approach. Nimras matters through exploration, counter-magic and independent judgement; Crown help can secure surface departures while local knowledge and physical access still matter. Rewards include recovered texts, treasure, merfolk relationships and bounded research access.

Act I: reach the site and secure a way in or out. Act II: explore several connected chambers and compete over their distinct assets. Act III: control a specific extraction or closure using what survived. Negotiated sharing, a successful limited salvage, prevention of breach, defeat or withdrawal are possible.

### C. The Red Crossing — wizard feud and tactical survival

Independent situation: rival private companies dispute a valuable outer-island stronghold and the safe landing beside it. Vaur has taken refuge or made a bargain there; his choice is not necessarily the cause of the feud. Why now: an employer's withdrawal makes a paid garrison's position unstable. Without intervention, each company attempts to seize a crossing and force the other to surrender; civilians and ferry crews can be trapped if their own resistance and escape efforts fail.

Opponents: **Captain Edras Kerr**, seeking possession and an enforceable commission, with one launch, twelve soldiers and two battle mages; **Vala Sorn**, a powerful independent mage defending a family stronghold with six retainers, prepared wards and limited supplies. Their goals are incompatible over the stronghold, but ceasefire or evacuation is possible. **Kess Pry**, ferry captain, controls a genuine route out and prioritises her crew.

New locations: **the Red Causeway**, **the Broken Beacon**, **Cinder House**. Distinctive conflict: finite private military forces, with both patrons claiming legitimate interests. Entrance: pursue Vaur to a crossing under attack. Second: hire or aid Pry's ferry and approach from another side. Nimras can counter spells, secure terrain, disguise a crossing, break a siege asset or negotiate. Ward's reinforcements can matter but require boats, information and travel; neither side becomes immune to the Crown. Rewards: recovery, negotiated professional standing, a grateful crew and actual salvage; stakes include people and control of a strategically useful place.

Act I: secure a landing or evacuate an immediate danger. Act II: raid, negotiate, infiltrate or undermine finite military dependencies. Act III: seize, surrender, neutralise or evacuate the stronghold under conditions created by play. No required last stand, casualty quota or unavoidable death of Vaur.

### D. The Bell That Answers — supernatural mystery with physical counterplay

Independent situation: an old magical apparatus is reproducing dangerous fragments of historical battle workings. A private collector wants the intact apparatus; a funerary practitioner wants it destroyed before further manifestations. Vaur's manuscript contains relevant evidence, not the only solution. Why now: somebody has brought a separated component back into reach of its counterpart. Without intervention, limited stored effects can be triggered at inhabited places while the rivals seek the apparatus. Future effects require finite charges, actual triggers and visible warning.

Opponents: **Jorek Naul**, collector, with purchased access to a belfry, three agents and a guarded transport; **Sister Orva**, a funerary magician with four volunteers and prepared ways to destroy the apparatus, willing to abandon useful evidence. **Linn Seret**, bellfounder, can distinguish a mechanical casting flaw from an enchantment and wants her workers protected. New locations: **the Silent Belfry**, **Salt Bell Foundry**, **the Grey Supper House**. Fantasy element: replayed battle magic with explicitly listed effects and interrupters, not a power that can do anything to anyone.

Entrance: a manifestation interrupts pursuit of Vaur. Second: seek Seret's damaged commission or a witness at the foundry. Nimras can recognise, counter and track magic while making social choices about dangerous preservation. Officials can evacuate a marked street and hold a perimeter; they still need actionable locations and an intervention before the next actual trigger. Rewards: protected people, knowledge, a valuable recovered device or its destruction, and standing among practitioners.

Act I: survive or prevent a manifestation and obtain an independent route to the cause. Act II: investigate physical components and interrupt competing retrieval plans. Act III: settle the apparatus and its transport with the resources left. Destruction can be a complete early solution; no surprise indestructible core or mandatory hidden second machine.

### E. The Black Lantern Exchange — criminal recovery and magical heist

Independent situation: a broker is arranging a private sale of rare spell material, including a manuscript claimed by the fellowship. A rival bidder wants to seize rather than buy the relevant lot. Vaur's involvement creates the existing recovery entrance without predetermining his guilt or allegiance. Why now: buyers and goods are arriving for one finite sale. Without intervention, the broker attempts to complete purchases and move lots to distinct purchasers, making later recovery harder without making it impossible by fiat.

Opponents: **Silar Tern**, broker, with a floating saleroom, six hired guards, a ward specialist and three service staff; **Vessa Cairn**, rival buyer and capable mage, with two agents, a fast skiff and a limited cash reserve. Tern wants payment and reputation; Cairn wants custody of one specific work, even at the expense of the sale. **Ossa Flint**, porter and former copyist, knows loading arrangements and wants paid safety rather than heroics. New locations: **the Black Lantern Saleroom**, **the Maskmakers' Roof**, **the Pearl Vault Jetty**. Fantasy element: a finite collection of prepared enchanted lots and wards, each with defined effects and limits.

Entrance: trace Vaur to a sale invitation or goods arrival. Second: deal with a bidder, porter or concerned owner of a different lot. Nimras's Change Appearance, Invisibility, Deceit and counter-magic matter alongside open negotiation and force. Crown assistance can act on a concrete recovery claim and known venue; it creates a real alternative to a heist, with mobile goods and resistant actors resolved honestly. Rewards: recovered commission, legal fee, leverage, purchased or properly claimed research. No free appropriation of every owner's goods.

Act I: gain access, an ally or a target's location. Act II: prepare and execute an approach amid distinct buyers, wards and loading dependencies. Act III: secure custody or intercept departure according to what the earlier operation changed. A clean negotiated purchase can resolve the job; no compulsory alarm or betrayal restores a chase.

- **A:** Refuse client: Kest/Senn still pursue research, optional glassworker entrance remains. Report: authorities can secure a known asset. Capture Vaur early: recovery stands. - **B:** Refuse financier: merfolk and rival give independent access. Report: surface safety improves. Recover teacher early: can depart without a compulsory ruin visit. Defeat one rival: surviving expedition or guardian interests respond to that actual loss. Negotiate: bounded access and shared salvage possible. Allies: consume real boat capacity and supplies, add skills. Out of order: chambers and routes remain connected, no clue locks.
- **C:** Refuse both companies: ferry captain can offer evacuation work. Report: Crown approach and ceasefire remain possible. Recover teacher early: no recapture. Defeat commander: troops act on pay, orders and morale rather than fight forever. Negotiate: exchanges and withdrawal meaningful. Allies: alter the balance and can win. Out of order: raid stores or boats first; no invulnerable central castle.
- **D:** Refuse patron: visible manifestations and bellfounder offer other doors. Report: evacuation/perimeter matter. Recover teacher: secure him without denying the apparatus's independent existence. Destroy source early: end manifestations using actual dependencies. Negotiate: controlled study or supervised destruction possible. Allies: distribute containment work. Out of order: fragments, workshop evidence and witnesses provide redundant routes.
- **E:** Refuse infiltration: buy, report, intercept goods or end the contract. Report: official action is valid with usable evidence. Recover teacher/manuscript early: commission complete. Defeat broker: bidder plans may collapse or change, no immortal auctioneer. Negotiate: real sale or exchange possible. Allies: help doors, custody and transport. Out of order: loading jetty and bidder contact can bypass saleroom entirely.

All five work without returning antagonists. Vaur and Vey are existing personal connections, not compulsory old villains. No option requires Hanzo, a restored possession scheme, a secret crime by Nimras or a threat to Halla's family.
