## Established premise and private situation

Nimras's independent fellowship commissioned him to find Rennic Vaur alive and recover an entrusted manuscript. They have alleged unauthorised removal, not issued a Crown warrant. Their institutional name, Nimras's home region and any precise contract fee remain unassigned. His purse remains 6GP. His introduction directs him to Oren Vey. No new offstage payment or previous PC dialogue is invented.

Vaur is a Rank 3 mage hunter and Nimras's former teacher. He retained the fellowship's manuscript beyond his permission to study it, then travelled to Purewater to compare its old surveys with actual submerged structures. The manuscript describes freshwater intakes and maintenance passages, with annotations about rights of access; it grants no control of water, magical ownership or new spells. Vaur believes comparison could prevent useful public infrastructure disappearing into private ownership. His conviction does not make every annotation accurate, and the fellowship has a reasonable concern about circulation of plans that could assist interference with waterworks.

Vaur is not secretly possessed or an agent of the departed Baron. He can negotiate, return the manuscript, accept an escort, copy permissible portions or refuse to cooperate. No blood price, betrayal, dead teacher or compulsory battle is required. He has his own judgement and can be mistaken. The fellowship need not be secretly wicked to disagree with him.

At the initial scene Vaur is working from a rented bench at Lantern Float Market. This is starting setting preparation, not a rolled escape. His subsequent movements require time, motivation and resolution. He knows he has overrun his authorisation and that someone may be sent; he does not know the fellowship chose Nimras.

Vey is the established independent Arch Mage at the House of Small Windows on High Isle. Vaur consulted him and left a sealed cedar case for anyone who came from their fellowship. The case contains a cracked brass-mounted surveying lens and a short note: 'Before you call this theft, look at what they are closing. Lantern Float Market. Ask for Rennic.' This is a claim and an invitation, not proof of innocence. A folded sketch labels a public landing at the market. No roll is needed to read a legible note. Examining the lens may establish its history or current condition; do not invent magical powers for it. Nimras has not opened it yet.

Vey knows that Vaur consulted him about the survey and left the case. He does not know Vaur's present location or the whole commission. The unopened message gives the player a direct route without Vey needing an explanatory monologue. Other routes include the market crews' ordinary recollection of a paying customer and the fellowship's correspondence delivered through Vey. No single failed check destroys the trail.
