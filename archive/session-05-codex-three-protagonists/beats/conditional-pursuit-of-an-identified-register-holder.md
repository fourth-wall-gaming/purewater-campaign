## Conflict, object and finite resources

The Ash Register is a hand-sized brass reliquary, dead oath-keeper's name-tablet,
folded estate survey, and old seizure receipt. It is inert at this opening:
no host, active possession, contagious curse, damage pulse or completed binding.
Old occult manufacture can be detected; it supplies no present hostile spell to
dispel. Read Magic can render magical script, not translate every language or
reveal moral truth. Arcane Knowledge and physical comparison can interpret
construction. No magic is required to see the seizure seal, receipt or survey.
Appraise is known but not currently prepared; no free preparation change.
Any new preparation/casting uses actual CFI costs/time. No spell is awarded.

**Clarification of the unplayed outline:** Rusk disposed of this object through
estate clearance before Malrec recognised its use. Venn now holds it under a
completed clearance purchase; Rusk cannot simply withdraw someone else's lot.
He can genuinely buy it. His two agents are travelling with30GP from his80GP
venture purse and arrive d10/day. Remaining50GP, four retainers, cart and two
horses stay at the Ashwick depot; nobody occupies both places. No additional
secret bidder or unlimited credit. This fixes the draft contradiction between
Rusk selling the necessary anchor and simultaneously needing to recover it.

Rusk wants the complete authentic anchor for a future spirit-binding sale that
would buy wealth and patronage. Exposure threatens his freedom and the venture.
His first approach is purchase. If outbid, his agents may follow the actual
buyer and attempt coercion/theft; force depends on knowledge, access and survival.
He will order killing of a witness who can expose unlawful confiscation or
binding, but has no knowledge of Nimras at opening. No killing over an ordinary
fee or indiscriminate attack on customers. With asset/control loss or credible
arrest risk, bargain, surrender or retreat are valid. Taking the Register away
really blocks later demonstration. No replacement anchor or boss appears.

Venn owns Velvet Hold,4 guards, captain,2 crew, one ledger and the consignment.
These people protect her business, not Rusk. Marek has20GP and2 escorts; buys
for a claimant, independently of Rusk. Ordinary crew/guests are not combat

## Three acts and outcome ranges

I — Examine: voluntary paid appraisal, preview access, comparison of script,
construction and physical receipt. Objectives: earn fee, identify object and
establish actual ownership question. Without PC, preview continues. No attack
for examining. Turn: an earned examination or claimant evidence reveals the
estate-confiscation chain; refusing/finishing report can end this contract.

II — Contest custody: choose evidence, bargain, purchase, official custody,
copying or an explicitly new illicit recovery. Agent arrival and bidding create
competing control. Warning: bidder visibly matches the Register sketch; ownership
claims and ledger can be checked. Turn: real custody or exposure changes what
Rusk can attempt. Victory need not be theft; no obligation to spend own purse.

III — Get clear/settle: deliver report, settle agreed pay and any separately
accepted recovery. If agents lost a bid and learned holder/location, a finite
pursuit or demand can occur; otherwise departure may be quiet. Defeating them,
securing official custody, paying a bargain or keeping anonymity can end it.
Failure leaves specific owner/custody and available routes, not mandatory death.
Later estate work requires a new payer, consent and refreshed mechanical prep.
