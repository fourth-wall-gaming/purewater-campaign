Two Knights take what they want off a cookshop counter and tell the woman the
Baron will settle. She says something. One of them puts a hand flat on her chest
and pushes her down onto the stall behind her, not hard, the way you would move a
gate.

**And then the watch arrives**, in the person of a heavy, tired sergeant in
blue-grey with a book under his arm, and here is the beat: **he takes it all
down.** Names of the Knights if he can get them, the woman's name, the hour, what
was taken, two witnesses. He is thorough and he is unhurried and he is *good at
it*. Then he closes the book, tells her it has been recorded and will be referred,
and walks away, and the Knights watch him do it and laugh.

**This is the scene that explains the city.** Not a corrupt watch -- a *documented*
one. Every player who sees it understands the standing order without being told
what a standing order is.

**[[Abel Weir]] will talk** to anyone who follows him and does not sneer at him.
Not about opinions -- about the book. Thirty-one entries in a week. Emmit Rill's
jaw is number 31. Two boatmen who did not come back are 24 and 25. He is looking
for somebody the referral could be made *to* who is not the council, and he cannot
be the one to suggest it.

**If a PC produces a warrant, a seal, an Order's badge, or a Sword of the Lake's
name**, he will open the book on the spot.

Advance *Hold the streets* by 1. Advance *Make them pay for one of ours* by 1 if
Marda's people hear of it, which they will.
