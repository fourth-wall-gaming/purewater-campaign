Two miles east of the walls, where the ground rises out of the marsh: a good stone
house, stables for forty, orchards, and eight acres of water-meadow running down to
the channel. It belongs to **[[Alderic Vantt]]**, merchant prince, councillor, and
the Baron's host.

**The Dragon Knights are camped in the meadow.** Twenty-five of them, their horses,
the farriers and armourers, the servants, the tourney gear and the pavilions, from
eight days before the Tourney until whenever they choose to leave. [[Tat Atarer]]
runs the camp; the Baron himself sleeps aboard [[The DragonBarge]] and comes out
perhaps once a day.

**Why this matters more than it looks.** A foreign lord's armed company is quartered
on the private land of a sitting member of the Governor's council -- which means it
is not the city's business, no permission was required, and no one had to be asked.
It is also *why* Vantt wanted the standing order. There are twenty-five armed men on
his grass and he would like the week to pass without an incident that has his name
on it.

**What a party finds here**

- **Local labour, daily.** The camp eats. Bread, beer, fodder, water, laundry,
  and a farrier's boy from the village who is in and out of the horse lines all
  day and is not searched by anybody.
- **Men who talk.** Twenty-five bored mercenaries three days from a tournament,
  drinking Vantt's beer. Far looser than the six aboard the barge.
- **The gear.** Tourney harness, the champion's spare kit, the litter that carried
  something covered from the borderlands, and whatever came off it.
- **Vantt's household**, who did not ask for this and are frightened of it: a
  steward, a housekeeper, grooms, and a daughter of nineteen who has been sent to
  her aunt's in the city for the duration and is furious about it.
- **Tat Atarer**, findable, approachable, and increasingly unhappy about what a
  camp with nothing to do is doing to his company's discipline.

**It is not a fortress.** It is a rich man's farm with tents on it. There are no
walls, four gates, a lane, a river frontage, and a great deal of dark.
