# Kag, as an NPC

She was a player character for one run and she is canon now. This is how to run
her when somebody else is holding the dice.

Sheet: `characters/npcs/kag.json`. Agenda: `agendas/the-one-who-works-it-out.md`.
She has no beats. **She has no direct bearing on the plot** -- she runs errands
for Crowbill, manages his business on the streets, and runs a hustle or two of
her own. She is colour and a door, not a thread.

---

## The one rule

**How you talk to her decides what she is**, and it is not an Influence roll.

Everyone who meets Kag sees seven feet of half-orc and addresses the muscle.
She has been addressed as the muscle her whole life, she hates it at **75**, and
she has never once said so.

| what the party does | what they get |
|---|---|
| Give her an order. Talk past her. Ask her to fetch somebody. Be surprised she reads. | Correct, unhelpful, exactly as much use as they paid for. No sabotage. She simply never volunteers the fact that would have saved them two days, and they never learn it was there. |
| Ask her what a thing **costs**. Ask her to price it, read it, count it, work out who benefits. | The best analyst in Purewater, for nothing, permanently — and she goes on giving them things they did not ask for. |

Play the difference physically, not in dialogue. She does not sulk and she does
not explain. She just answers the question she was asked and stops.

---

## What she is doing, day by day

Crowbill's business, in an occupied week. None of it is the party's plot; all of
it is somewhere they can walk into her.

| when | what she does |
|---|---|
| `d-3` | Walks the collections round on the Shoals and finds three accounts short on one bridge, because the Knights take what they like and nobody bills them. Does sums out loud at a rope stall. |
| `d-2` | Moves cartage for Crowbill around the Knights' bridges. Buys up lamp oil on a hunch. |
| `d-1` | If the stairs stop, she is on the Shoals stair pricing the strike by the hour, and selling her lamp oil. |
| `d0` | Lays odds on the Archery and takes the city's money on the elf. |
| `d1–d2` | Wherever Crowbill needs her. If the Lullwater burns, she is in it, getting people into boats. |

---

## Three doors to her

1. **Crowbill.** Anyone who gets to him is handed her, because she is what he
   sends when the job needs reading rather than breaking.
2. **A bridge.** Anybody working the Shoals for a week hears the thieves story,
   and it is told with the teller's own improvements.
3. **A child.** She keeps two or three quay children in food, and they are
   everywhere, and they talk about her.

---

## What she trades, and what she will not

**Freely, to anyone who asks properly:** numbers. The camp, the tents, the
bills, what any cargo in this city is worth and who
profits if it moves.

**For a price:** anything that belongs to Crowbill — boats, watchers, the
canal routes. The price is never money. It is a piece of
information she does not have, and she will name it exactly.

**Never:** killing to order. Informing on Crowbill. Any job with a child in it
— and if she sees somebody else's child put in front of a knife she gets in the
way of it without being asked and does not mention it afterwards.

---

## What happened the one time a player had her

Kept because it is canon and because a GM should know what she is capable of
when somebody is actually driving.

She found the short accounts on day one and called four Dragon Knights thieves
on the Fishmarket bridge in front of forty people. She counted the camp from
under a water butt. She asked *who mended her* instead of *who cut her*. She
took a charcoal rubbing of a soul-knife in situ rather than lift it, and used it
to find the workshop and the fourth of June. She refused, to her own boss's
face, the arithmetic that said settle.

Then she ran a snatch on a water-stair that took the Baron's son alive,
interrogated him, killed him herself at first light because the alternative was
a reprisal on four thousand people, and fed him to the eel pots — and took a new
passion out of it.

She turned one of the Baron's own paid watchers in about ninety seconds. She
handed a King's officer the case of her life for nothing and asked for nothing
back. And she beat a man who has no breaking point by sending him an **itemised
invoice** with his employer priced at four shillings a head, specifically so
that the correction would tell her who was getting into the boats.

She finished the week stood on a pier with a cut ring in her hand saying *this
is Crowbill's town* in front of twenty-six people, five of whom were his.

**None of that came off her combat style.** All of it came off Commerce,
Insight, and being the only person in the room who asked what a thing cost.
