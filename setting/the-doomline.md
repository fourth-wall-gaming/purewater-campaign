# The Doomline — Purewater, start to finish

**Not the plot. The default** — what happens if nobody interferes. Every beat in
it is an *attempt* that gets rolled, and `revise-beat` and `cascade` are how it
keeps up. A doomline that survives contact with the players unchanged was never
a doomline; it was a rail.

It exists for two reasons. It makes the living world cheap to run: author the
default once, then play only the deviations. And after two runs that reached the
end of Act II with fourteen allies and no losses, it exists to enforce **shape**.

## The two rules above all the others

> **Every act takes something.** Not a setback — a loss that does not come back:
> a person, a place, a standing, a route. An act in which the party only gains
> has not happened. If the dice will not produce it, the Baron's playbook must.

> **Most people say no the first time.** An ally is a scene, not a conversation.
> Everyone wants something, fears something, or is committed elsewhere, and the
> default answer to a stranger asking for help in an occupied city is *no*.

# ACT I — THE COLUMN
### `d-3` · the city as it is, and then a girl dies in it

The di Teufel column comes up the Anminster road with a covered litter in the
middle of it that the outriders let nobody near. Three Dragon Knights hold the
gate bridge and take a toll they have no right to, and the watch stands twenty
feet away and looks at the water. **A knight greets Randall as "Lord Santo."**

That evening Santo offers to buy Emmeralda's contract and Constantine refuses
him. He takes a room anyway. In the night he attempts his father's working,
**fails it**, and with a girl on the floor and a channel half-opened in her he
uses the knife.

**Blau collects him** — waiting at the bottom of the back stair with a folded
cloth, unsurprised, and he *never goes up*. Which is why a knife, a crested coat
and a spent scroll sit in that room for two days.

# ACT II — THE CITY CLOSES
### `d-2` to `d-1` · they find out who did it, and that it does not matter

The look-alikes are taken — Hesper lifts Randall off the street, Marisette's
people take whoever else wears that face. The catacombs. Marisette works out
that neither did it and lets them go.

The Pearl goes dark. Four hundred people out of work on one woman's word,
because a boycott is the only weapon her trade has ever had.

And then the wall: **Santo did it, and nothing can be done.** The watch is under
a written standing order. The magistrates will not hear it. A baron's son with a
knife is weather, and you do not sue the weather.

**The presentation of the entrants.** Every competitor bare-headed for the space
of a bow. The helm comes off the Baron's champion and it is a thin boy with
dragon-scale growing out of the skin of his throat.

> **MIDPOINT.** She knows her brother is alive and inside that man's hold, and
> can do nothing at all — and the household becomes aware of *her*.

# ACT III — THE PLAN, AND THE COUNTER
### `d0` to `d1/day` · they build something; he breaks part of it

The party assembles a plan and **the Baron's playbook starts running on
triggers, not on a timetable.** Every step needs somebody who does not want to
help, and the first real success is answered within one watch.

The three jurisdictions become the game here (see below): the standing order
protects him from the *city's* law and does not touch the King's Assay, maritime
law, or the Order's sacred sites.

# ACT IV — THE UNFORGIVING CITY

# ACT V — THE SAND, THE WATER, AND THE ROOM
### `d2` · everything in one place at once

The lists. Four thousand people. The Order in the shallows at the foot of the
steps. The Knights loose in the streets behind. The champion walked out in
harness for the only time he ever leaves that ship.

**1 — The sand.** Cailan fights. Watching a thousand-year-old knight win in your
brother's body, unable to want either of them to lose.

**2 — The room.** By a form older than the Governor's office the champion is
presented **alone and unarmed** to the Crown's representative, behind a shut
door, immediately after the bout. Four generations bought their way toward that
room. If Temerach spent her standing she is standing in it.

Behind the door: **Draw Forth** on Cailan, **Seat the Bound** on Emeric. No
swords in this scene matter at all.

**3 — THE STANDOFF.** However the room ends, Hanzo comes out of it and into the
open, in front of the whole city — and on the far side of the arena is an elf
with a bow and nothing left to lose.

It is a standoff and not an ambush because **both of them know**. She does not
shoot from cover. She is a King's officer and she will make it look like a
lawful act even when it is not: she names him, across eighty yards, in front of
four thousand people, and gives him the chance to answer — which is the one
thing he has spent the entire week never having to do.

Then she shoots.

> **PIVOT — the shot.** `kills-him` / `wounds-him` / `misses`. All three are
> playable and none of them ends the war, because the credit line is not his.

**4 — The flight.** She is an outlaw from the instant the arrow leaves the
string: assassination at a sacred festival, in public, on the Crown's guest.
Thirty years of service gone in one second, deliberately, with her eyes open.
Whether the party helps her off the island is theirs to decide, and it costs.

**5 — The boy.** In the noise, in the water, or in the wreck of the room —
Gardwen gets her brother's body into her own hands, alive.

**And he is still not her brother.** The locket only ever held Thuban to *Hanzo*.
What holds him in the world is a box of bones in a tower on the border estate,
four days' hard riding, behind his own men. The rite cannot be said. The words
that were never said over a knight of the Sixth Wing still cannot be said.

**The last beat of the adventure is Thuban telling her where they are.**

# THE PAYOFF, AND WHAT IT DELIBERATELY WITHHOLDS

Epic here is not size. It is **everything happening at once in one place**, with
the smallest thing at the centre mattering most: the crowd, the shallows, the
burning streets, a shut room with a prince in it — and a woman holding a
seventeen-year-old's head out of the water.

**What is won:** the Prince is not taken. The house is broken in public. Bird is
out of that hold and breathing. The watch, if they were turned, chose. The city
saw all of it.

**What is not won, on purpose:** the boy is not free. Thuban is not released. The
words are not said. And the man who could have told them where the bones are is
either dead on the sand or riding for the border.

That ache is the hook for **the second adventure: the fortress, and the bones.**

# THE BARON'S PLAYBOOK

He is a man with more resources than the party and a strong preference for never
touching anything himself. **Triggered, not timed** — within one watch.

| They do this | He does this |
|---|---|
| Ask in public about his money | A price on the face. Men out by dawn — to *name* first, take later. |
| Reach his son | Blau moves Santo, and whoever carried the message stops being findable. |
| Get evidence to an official | **He accuses back.** He is the Crown's guest; they are an unlicensed foreign sorcerer, a thief with a murderer's face, an Assay officer outside his warrant. The watch helps him *lawfully*. |
| Put a hand on the champion | The reinforcement goes deeper that night and the boy is moved. |
| Take something off him | The Knights come off the standing order. Public reprisals on people who cannot answer. |
| Threaten the timetable | He offers a **deal** — warm, generous, sincere in the moment — to whoever in the party is most tired of losing. |
| Corner him | The warmth goes **up**. More attention, more hospitality, more delight in you. He has never raised his voice and will not start. |

**His assets in order:** the law, the Prince's affection, twenty-five armed men
the watch has been told to go easy on. **His money is not his, and that is the
thread that unravels him.**

## The Dragon Knights — the ladder

Worse each act; the watch does nothing each time, which is the point. **Never
skip a rung.**

1. Tolls and insults. A woman's basket tipped into the canal.
2. A beating in daylight, over nothing, and the watch walks away.
3. **A death nobody answers for** — a dredger, a boy off a boat.
4. **Reprisal**: after a loss, they burn something belonging to people who had
   nothing to do with it.
5. Open war in the streets in Act IV, with the city fighting back badly.

Mail, shields, trained combat styles, and **they fight in threes**. A PC who
takes one alone should lose.

## The three jurisdictions

The standing order shields him from *the city's* law only. It does not touch:

- **The King's Assay** — a Crown warrant, neither the city's nor the army's.
- **Maritime law** — boats, berths, cargo. The Knights have been commandeering
  boats and it is eating Velen Deepkeel alive.
- **The Order's sacred sites** — the water, the steps, the rite.

The game is not gathering evidence. It is **finding the one court his protection
cannot reach**.

## The watch — ninety people ordered to be useless

In writing, over Emeric's signature: *complaints against the Baron's people are
to be recorded and referred, and not acted upon, until the Tourney is concluded.*
Sergeants route patrols away from the Merchant's Quarter rather than be seen not
acting. The referral book is four inches thick and a week old.

**The same three councillors who advised that order later have him sign the
instruction silencing the temple's water alarm.** The watch thread and the
fouling thread are one thread and nobody in the fiction knows it.

**Abel Weir** is the crack: thirty-one entries from memory, none answered, and
one want — the book in front of somebody with standing, without being the man
who disobeyed. **Turning the watch is a victory condition**, available from Act
I to anybody who talks to him.

# SET PIECES

**The gate toll.** Small, early, and the best scene of the first run: exposed, in
front of witnesses, over a coin.

**The riot at the water-stairs.** A crowd, a flashpoint, Marda holding three
hundred people while somebody in mail decides how bored he is.

**The house defence.** They come for Constantine's at night, with girls and a
thirteen-year-old inside, and nowhere to fall back to.

**The lawful arrest.** No violence available. The warrant is correctly issued and
fighting turns them into what he has already said they are.

**Santo cornered.** Pathetic and dangerous at once. He does not run when he
panics — he **takes hold of whoever is nearest**, because it has always worked.

**Santo's bargain.** He sells the house, and the price is protecting Emmeralda's
killer from his own family. Put Constantine and Marisette in that room.

**The barge.** An invitation problem, not a stealth one. Twenty aboard, everyone
knows every face.

**The catacombs.** Hunted underground in the dark by men who do not know them.

**The Washing.** Forty seconds, hands near her brother, inside a rope no armed
man may cross, his father eleven feet away.

**The lift at the rail.** A theft under four thousand pairs of eyes in the one
hour that man is reachable.

**The sand.** See above.

**The standoff and the shot.** See above. The best image in the adventure.

**The bones.** Four days' ride and a border tower. Not this adventure.
