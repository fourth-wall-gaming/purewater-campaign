# Orrin Sculle, and the left-handed cup
*Gardwen's run · CH.10 · the DragonBarge after deck, d0/dusk · Act III*

**What happened.** Gardwen was caught on the after deck by a man in his fifties
coming up out of the hold with an empty basin — heavy forearms, sleeves rolled
past the elbow, liniment and lamp oil. He did not shout. He set the basin down
and told her exactly what was supposed to happen next: he calls out, six men in
mail come up the forward hatch, and nobody ashore ever hears about her again.

Then: *"So before I do — what are you."*

She told him the truth. *"I'm the sister of the boy in there. I just want to talk
to him."*

He sat down on the coaming like a man whose legs had stopped agreeing with him.

> *"Gabriel. Nobody has used that name on this ship in ten years. I don't use it.
> I use the other one, because if I slip once in front of the wrong man they take
> him off me and give him to somebody who'll manage him instead."*
>
> *"He was seven. He had a tooth coming through and it was bothering him, and I
> sat up with him the first night because nobody else was going to, and I have
> sat up with him most nights since."*

And then the thing nobody could invent twice:

> *"He holds a cup with his left hand. Always did. And that thing is
> right-handed, and every single time it puts a cup down and he comes up for a
> second he moves it across. Ten years. Nobody else has ever noticed and I have
> never told a soul, because who in God's name would I tell."*

He stepped aside. *"Twenty minutes. Boots off — the third step complains."*

**Why it worked.** The truth was the only thing he had not been offered in ten
years, and it cost nothing to say. The scene was decided **by his own passions
rather than by anything the PC rolled** — and the dice gave love a critical.

And the cup is proof. Not of a fact the plot needed, but that this man has
watched a boy for a decade closely enough to know which hand he reaches with —
which is the entire character in one observation.

**The dice.** Nothing on Gardwen's side. Orrin's own **Love (the boy) 70 against
Fear (the Baron) 65**, rolled openly: love came up **06, a critical**, fear made
its 64. Love took it, and not narrowly.

**Carrying it over.** The most portable thing here is the *procedure*: when an
NPC's choice is the scene, **roll their passions against each other in front of
the player** rather than rolling the PC's persuasion. The keeper existed as one
line of setting — *"Cailan and his keeper: below the waterline aft, a lamp that is
never put out, the keeper sleeping across the door"* — and everything else was
written in the ten minutes before he opened the hatch.

Keep the left-handed cup. Keep "who in God's name would I tell." Keep the third
step.
