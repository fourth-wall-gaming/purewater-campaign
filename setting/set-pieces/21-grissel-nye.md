# Grissel Nye
*Gardwen's run · CH.13 · two rooms over the tallow-yard, Fisher's Lane, d1/dusk · Act III*

**What happened.** The financial thread — the dull, important one — resolved into
an old woman in two rooms over a tallow-yard.

**Grissel Nye has carried out the ashes and the grates for all six counting-houses
on Chandler's Row for forty years.** In before light, out before the clerks
arrive. She is deaf. She cannot read. And in forty years **nobody has ever once
asked her what she has seen.**

She has a shelf of small things she carried out of those houses and never
mentioned.

**Why it worked.** It solves the problem the money thread always has, which is
that ledgers are boring. The evidence is not in a ledger — it is in the memory
and on the mantelpiece of somebody the entire city has looked straight through
for four decades, precisely *because* she cannot read and cannot hear, which is
why they were careless in front of her.

It is also the warmest scene in the record, and it arrives in the middle of the
driest thread.

**The dice.** Constantine's knock got the door open — an introduction, not a roll.

**Carrying it over.** Keep her whole. The transferable principle: **put the
decisive evidence in the hands of the least regarded person in the setting**, and
make the reason they have it the same reason nobody asked. The shelf of small
things is the image.
