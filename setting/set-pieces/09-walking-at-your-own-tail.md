# Walking straight at your own tail
*Conall's run · CH.2 · the Moist Oyster · Act I*

**What happened.** Conall spotted the man following him, crossed the room, and
sat down opposite him. Kerrin turned out to be Crowbill's, had been on him since
midday, was not a robber and was not the Watch.

Conall answered honestly — *"a flavour of the law, but not the city's and not the
king's"* — and then the good part: **Kerrin could not read him at all.** So he
refused to judge it himself and referred it upward, which is how the party got
an introduction to Crowbill instead of a knife.

**Why it worked.** The aggressive move is the safe one, and honesty is the
aggressive move. And the outcome came from a *failed* roll on the NPC's side
being interpreted in character: a professional who cannot get a read does not
guess, he escalates to somebody senior.

**The dice.** Opposed Insight 25/88 against Deceit 67/62 to take the tail off
him. Then Kerrin's Insight 98/55 — a clean failure, and the most useful roll of
the session.

**Carrying it over.** Portable anywhere. The principle to keep: **a watcher who
fails to read a PC does not shrug, he reports.** Failure on the NPC side should
generate plot, not nothing.
