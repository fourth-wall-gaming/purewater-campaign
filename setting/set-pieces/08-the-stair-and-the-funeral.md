# The stair, and the funeral
*Magda's run · CH.13 · the water-stair · Act V*

**What happened.** With the bones, the door and the latch all in one place for
the first time in a century, Magda walked two steps down into the black water
**where nine men had drowned a bound knight**, and told his tale out loud. She
named the murder. She gave him to her god.

The locket opened by itself.

And something went down past her legs in the water — unhurried — and looked at
her once on the way.

**Why it worked.** It is the quietest scene in the entire record and the best
one. No fight. No roll to save anybody. A woman standing in cold water telling a
thousand-year-old story to nobody, correctly, at last — and the world answering
in the smallest possible way.

The locket opening **by itself** is the detail. Nobody forced it. The binding
stopped applying, because the thing it was binding had been given what it was
owed.

**The dice.** 28/66 on the litany. One roll, and everything before it was setup.

**Carrying it over.** This is the emotional resolution the whole campaign is
built toward, and it must be **earned by knowing his story** rather than by
winning a fight. Keep the unhurried thing in the water. Keep the locket opening
on its own. Do not explain either.
