# Four hundred years old and not mine to bend
*Gardwen's run · CH.5 and CH.12 · Temple Isle · Act II–III*

**What happened.** Gardwen opened with *"The di Teufel clan."* Nerissa answered:

> *"You understand that I cannot discuss a guest of the Crown's representative.
> That is temple law. It is four hundred years old and it is not mine to bend.
> So I won't."*

And then told her something else entirely, which was worth more.

Later, asked to extend the Order's protection to a thief, she agreed — **on
conditions**, and counted aloud what it would cost her: that Lilura would have
grounds to put her out for it. When Gardwen withdrew the request rather than
meet the conditions, Nerissa's parting line was *"I am not a shop."*

**Why it worked.** An ally who says no, precisely, with a reason, and then gives
you something adjacent — and who will not be haggled with. She is not obstructive
and she is not a vending machine. The refusal is what makes the help mean
anything when it comes.

**The dice.** None. This is a card being played properly, not a roll.

**Carrying it over.** The single most portable thing in this file. **Most people
say no the first time, and the good ones say no with a reason and then hand you
something sideways.** "I am not a shop" should be pinned above the table.
