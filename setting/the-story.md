# Purewater — the plan

**This file is the plan, not the script.** It says what order things go in, what
each act is *for*, and what each beat is doing in the story. It deliberately does
not describe any scene — every description lives in the beat, in
[`beats/`](../beats/), which is the catalog.

That split is the point. The catalog is detailed and slow to change; this file is
thin and rewritten whenever the shape needs to change. If a description appears
in both, one of them is already wrong.

**Rewrite this file freely.** Reorder acts, move beats between them, cut beats,
write new ones. What must stay true is that every beat in the catalog appears
here exactly once, so the story is a plan rather than something that emerges by
accident from whatever happened to be scheduled.

## The three rules

**Every act takes something.** Not a setback — a loss that does not come back. A
person, a place, a standing, a route. An act in which the party only gains has
not happened. If the dice will not produce the loss, the Baron will.

**Most people say no the first time.** An ally is a scene, not a conversation.
Everyone here wants something, fears something, or is promised elsewhere, and
the honest answer to a stranger asking for help during an occupation is no.

**Conditions accelerate; time backstops.** A beat's `when` is the *latest* it
happens. Conditions on it can bring it forward when the party earns it. So a
thread can be accelerated but never stalled, and nothing waits forever.

## The clock

The party arrive `d-3`. **The Tourney runs three days**, and each is a different
event in a different part of the city — which is what paces the acts.

| | | |
|---|---|---|
| `d-1` | **The presentation** | every entrant before the Prince, bare-headed for one bow |
| `d0` | **The Archery** | the open event; no colours, no fee, anyone may shoot |
| `d1` | **The Single Combat** | the sand, and the only day the champion leaves that ship |
| `d2` | **The Run**, then the closing | across the whole city, then the audiences and the rewards |

Negative days are read at the table as what they are — *two days before* — so the
numbering has to keep meaning that.

# ACT III — `d-1/dusk to d0` · THE PLAN AND THE COUNTER

They stop reacting and start building. He stops running a timetable and starts answering, within a watch, every time they touch something real. The act's real question is jurisdiction, not evidence.
| *opportunity* | [He asks her, in front of them](../beats/kag-he-asks-her-in-front-of-them.md) | Crowbill treats her as a principal with his own men standing there. Once, ever. Makes the later collision expensive |
| `d0/dusk` | [Nus asks her if it can be done](../beats/kag-nus-asks-her-if-it-can-be-done.md) | THE FUSE. The hinge of the adventure is handed to her to light or not, and nobody warns her what it costs |


**The act's real question is jurisdiction.** The standing order shields him from
the *city's* law and touches nothing else. There are three courts it cannot
reach — the King's Assay, maritime law, and the Order's sacred ground — and the
middle game is finding the one that fits and getting somebody with standing to
walk into it. His money is the thread that unravels him, because it is not his.

**Takes:** A route, and somebody's freedom. The city stops being a place they can move around in.

# ACT IV — `d1` · THE UNFORGIVING CITY — **PLAYED, AND IT ENDED EARLY**

**Rewritten d1/night of run 4, because play went through this act in a single day and out the other side.**

The closing was run on the evening of the single combat rather than on `d2`, so
Act IV and the front half of Act V collapsed into one day. What this act was
*for* still happened — whatever they built got used against them, and the city
stopped being a place they could move around in — but its **Takes** did not
land where the plan put it.

**Takes, as played:** not an ally. It took the *plan*. Every route the party had
— the ransom, the case, the summons, the quiet exit — was spent or made
impossible inside four hours, and what they have instead is a boy, a locket, a
prisoner, and a corpse nobody can explain.

# ACT V — `d2` · **TO BE REWRITTEN. THIS IS THE REAL ONE.**

The old Act V — the run, the private audience, the standoff, the flight — was
built for a living Baron and a locket still on his neck. None of that exists.

**What the last act is now for:** the party won, in one night, harder and faster
than the plan allowed, and every single thing they won is a problem that has to
be answered before Thursday morning.

**The clock that replaces the Tourney:** **FOUR MORNINGS.** A seat ten years
deep is an open hole in a seventeen-year-old, it was reinforced every morning
for a decade, and nobody will reinforce it on Wednesday. Blau is selling the
procedure for a boat, a poler and the sea. Thursday is the wall.

# Kag, if she is an NPC in your game

She was the player character for run 4 and she is canon now. If somebody else is
holding the dice, she runs on her own track underneath the whole scenario —
sheet at `../characters/npcs/kag.json`, agenda at
`../agendas/the-one-who-works-it-out.md`, and the day-by-day and the one rule
for running her at [`kag-as-an-npc.md`](kag-as-an-npc.md).

The one rule, because it is the whole of her: **how you talk to her decides what
she is.** Address the muscle and she is exactly as much use as you paid for.
Ask her what a thing costs and you have the best analyst in Purewater for
nothing, permanently.

# What is not built yet

Honest gaps, kept here so they are visible rather than discovered mid-session.

- **The Baron's playbook is still prose, not beats.** Seven triggered counters —
  ask about his money and there is a price on the face by nightfall; reach his
  son and Blau moves him; get evidence to an official and he accuses back,
  lawfully. Each of those is a beat with a condition on it, and only one of them
  (*A price on the face*) exists. Until they do, his reactions depend on me
  remembering, which is exactly what this system is supposed to stop.
- **The jurisdiction thread is thin.** Act III's real question is which court his
  protection cannot reach — the King's Assay, maritime law, the Order's sacred
  ground. Only maritime law has a beat seeding it.
- **`d0` carries four beats** for a whole act. Act III is player-driven so some
  thinness is right, but four is low for "they build something and he breaks
  part of it."
- **No beat covers the watch turning.** It is named as a victory condition and
  has no event.
