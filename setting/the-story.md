# Purewater — the whole story, start to finish

This is the shape of the game, not a script. It is written to be **rewritten**:
every scene here is a likely event with a couple of good ways it could go, and
the moment play contradicts it, play wins and this gets edited.

It exists so that no scene has to be invented at the table under pressure. When
I am making things up on the fly I reach for the same move every time — somebody
new, somebody helpful, somebody who explains. This document is the antidote to
that.

## How to read it

Each scene says **where it happens**, **what is under way**, and **what the dice
are actually deciding**. Where a roll matters, the outcomes are written out as
possibilities in plain language. Where no roll is named, nothing is being
decided by chance and the scene turns on what the players do.

**Beats.** Each scene below links the beats that carry it, with the watch each
is scheduled for. The story is the authority and the beats are a projection of
it: a beat's `when` is **the latest it happens**, not the only time it can, and
conditions on a beat can bring it forward when the party earns it. A scene with
no beat has not been built yet; a beat linked from nowhere is one the story has
forgotten.

**The clock is a countdown to the Tourney's first day.** `d-3` is three days
before it starts; `d0`, `d1` and `d2` are the Tourney itself. Negative days are
meant to be read at the table as what they are — *two days before* — so the
numbering has to keep meaning that.

Two rules sit above everything else:

- **Every act takes something.** Not a setback — a loss that does not come back.
  A person, a place, a standing, a route. An act in which the party only gains
  has not happened. If the dice will not produce the loss, the Baron will.
- **Most people say no the first time.** An ally is a scene, not a conversation.
  Everyone in this city wants something, fears something, or is already promised
  elsewhere, and the honest answer to a stranger asking for help during an
  occupation is no.

## The clock

Six days, and the whole city is counting them down to the same week.

**The Tourney of the Lake runs three days**, and each of them is a different
event in a different part of the city:

| | | |
|---|---|---|
| `d-1` | **The presentation** | every entrant before the Prince, bare-headed for the space of one bow |
| `d0` | **The Archery** | the open event, on the long butts, anyone may shoot |
| `d1` | **The Single Combat** | the sand, the champions, and the only day the Baron's champion leaves that ship |
| `d2` | **The Run**, then the closing | across the whole city, and then the audiences, the rewards, and the shut room |

The party arrive on `d-3`, two days before any of it starts.

The Governor, **Prince Emeric** — the King's nephew, young, decent, and entirely
unsuited to this city — presides over all four. Every inn is full and every price
has doubled.

That is why the Baron is here, why the watch has been ordered to be patient
until it is over, and why nobody in authority wants a murder investigated this
week. The festival is not a backdrop. It is the reason the law has stopped
working, and it has a date on it — four of them.

# ACT I — `d-3` · THE COLUMN
### The city as it is, and then a girl dies in it

## The road, and the ford

The Anminster road comes down out of dry country and meets the lake at a
gravel ford where carts queue three deep in tourney week. It is hot, it smells
of horses and crushed fennel, and everyone has been standing in it for an hour.

Ahead in the queue is a di Teufel column — outriders in dragon-scale, a
baggage train, and in the middle of it **a covered litter that the outriders
let nobody walk beside**. Whatever is in it does not get out at the ford and
does not get out at the gate.

- This is where a party that does not know each other can see each other.
- Magda has dreamed the same dream eleven nights and one of these faces is in
  it. Whether she says so is hers.
- The litter is the first fact of the campaign and it is offered as an image,
  never explained. Anybody who pushes for a look gets an outrider's arm across
  their chest and a very polite refusal.

**Beats:** [The old woman at the ford](../beats/the-old-woman-at-the-ford.md) `d-3/dawn`

## Randall is greeted as Lord Santo

Four hundred yards further on, at the **gate bridge** — a stone span over the
outflow with a customs house at the far end and the lake opening up green and
enormous behind it — a Dragon Knight takes Randall by the arm, calls him **my
lord**, and offers a hand down off a cart he is in the middle of robbing.

**What the dice decide:** whether Randall plays it or runs. Deceit to hold the
part, Stealth to be gone before anybody senior looks up.

- Playing it well gets him a name and a piece of the household's business, which
  is worth more than the escape. A really good roll and the knight tells him
  where "his lordship" is expected this evening.
- Failing it puts him in front of somebody who has seen Santo close up — not
  fatal, but now two households are looking for a face.
- Running clean costs him nothing and teaches him nothing, which is a legitimate
  outcome and should be allowed to be dull.

Either way the city now contains a face that opens doors and gets throats cut,
and Randall is wearing it.

**Beats:** [A knight calls Randall 'my lord'](../beats/a-knight-calls-randall-my-lord.md) `d-3/day`

## Temerach on the road, and the joke

Somewhere out on the same road, a day short of the city: four knights, bored,
who stop a King's officer riding in plain harness with no house on her shield —
**because she is an elf, and because they can.**

This is not a fight. It is the slow, public, entirely deniable humiliation of
somebody who outranks them and cannot use it. They go through her saddlebags.
They ask her to say something in elvish. It goes on far longer than is
comfortable to play, and that is the point.

**She takes it.** Completely still, no answer, eyes front.

- If Conall is with her, the roll is his Willpower not to intervene — she has
  told him not to. Failing it is not a disaster; it is the first time he
  discovers what a warrant is worth out here, which is the education he came for.
- If he does intervene and it goes badly, one of the knights is now a face with
  a grievance, and grievances in this city get collected later.
- **Do not let her win this scene.** Her not winning it on the road is what buys
  the archery in Act III and the shot in Act V. Those two scenes are the same
  scene, three days apart.

**Beats:** [Four knights and a King's officer](../beats/four-knights-and-a-kings-officer.md) `d-3/day`

## The gate toll

Three Dragon Knights hold the gate bridge and take a toll they have no legal
right to take. Twenty feet away a pair of city watchmen stand looking at the
water with the particular stillness of men who have been given an order they
are ashamed of.

Small scene, early, and the best one in the act: exposed, in public, over a
coin. Nobody's life is at risk and everybody's dignity is.

- Paying is the cheap option and it is the correct one and it should sting.
- Arguing gets the party turned back rather than arrested, which is worse: now
  they need another way in and it is getting dark.
- Fighting here is available and a very bad idea. They fight in threes, in mail,
  with trained styles, and a lone PC who takes one on should lose.

**Beats:** [The Baron's column enters Purewater](../beats/the-baron-s-column-enters-purewater.md) `d-3/day` · [Three of them on the gate bridge](../beats/three-of-them-on-the-gate-bridge.md) `d-3/day`

## The Sylph's Embrace, and the offer for Emmeralda

**The place:** a tall lacquered house on the Fishmarket canal with a green door
and a first-floor gallery over the water. Constantine runs it. It is the good
house — quiet, expensive, well-lit, and the girls' contracts are their own,
which is the entire argument of her life.

**Emmeralda** is the finest performer on the water and knows it without being
tiresome about it. Santo di Teufel — nineteen, his father's least successful
project — offers to buy her contract outright. Constantine refuses him on her
own step in front of the room.

He takes a room in the house anyway.

**Beats:** [An offer for the girl](../beats/an-offer-for-the-girl.md) `d-3/dusk`

## The room at the Sylph's Embrace

The hinge of the act, and **entirely Santo's own doing.** Blau is not there and
would never have permitted it. There is a young man, a girl, a barbed knife he
has been told is necessary, and a scroll in his father's hand that he has read
four times and does not understand.

He attempts the working. **It fails.** There is a channel half-opened in her and
no way to finish it and no way to close it, and he panics and uses the knife.

This is where being in the building matters more than anywhere else in the game,
so the possibilities are worth laying out properly:

- **If nobody is there.** She dies. Santo walks out into the dark, and by
  morning the city has decided that the man with that face did it — which means
  Randall. This is the default and it is a good default: it costs the party a
  person they had met, and it frames one of them.
- **If Gardwen is in the house.** The wound is not a wound; it is an opening,
  and it reopens from the inside as fast as it is closed. Spirit sight first
  shows her why, and that knowledge is what makes the healing possible at all
  rather than merely hard. Saving Emmeralda should cost her everything she has
  and leave her on the floor — and it should be genuinely in doubt.
- **If Magda is there.** Santo can be taken or killed. He is a panicking
  fire-caster in an enclosed wooden building with people asleep upstairs, so the
  fight has a second clock running on it. Taking him alive is the hardest and
  best outcome: the whole middle of the campaign changes, because the party
  holds the Baron's son.
- **If Randall is there.** He knows every back stair in that building and can
  get people out of it. He can also be found standing over her, which is the
  worst single moment available to any PC in Act I.
- **If Conall arrives after.** He is the only one of them with a warrant and the
  habit of writing things down, and what he records in the first hour is what an
  official will look at in Act III.

**Act I takes:** Emmeralda, on most paths. And on every path, the assumption
that this city has laws that apply to everyone.

**Beats:** [Santo carves Emmeralda](../beats/santo-carves-emmeralda.md) `d-3/night`

# ACT II — `d-2` to `d-1` · THE CITY CLOSES
### They find out who did it, and discover that it does not matter

## The catacombs

By dawn there are two men in this city with the same face and somebody's people
have taken both of them.

**The place:** the old burial galleries under the Hill quarter — dry, cold,
chalk-white, stacked with three centuries of the respectable dead. The houses
use them because nobody official goes down there and sound does not carry up.

Two men tied to chairs in the dark, with the same face, who have never met.

- This is where the twins find out what they are, and where somebody says the
  word *brother* out loud for the first time.
- **Marisette** — madame of the Mother of Pearl, the senior woman of the trade,
  and the person the houses actually answer to — is working out whether to kill
  them. Her Insight decides whether she believes them quickly or slowly; a slow
  belief means hours in the dark first, and she is still right in the end.
- A witness from the house shortcuts it entirely. She will believe a person who
  was there over a face every time.
- If Santo was taken alive in Act I, he is down here too, and that is a
  completely different scene: three men, one of whom is guilty, in front of a
  woman deciding what to do about a Baron's son.

**Beats:** [Hesper takes the look-alike](../beats/hesper-takes-the-look-alike.md) `d-2/dawn` · [Two men with one face](../beats/the-catacombs-two-men-one-face.md) `d-2/dawn`

## The Pearl goes dark

Marisette closes the houses. Four hundred people out of work on one woman's
word, because a boycott is the only weapon her trade has ever had and she has
been saving it for twenty years.

It is a real threat for about a day and a half. After that it is a wasting
asset, and everyone involved knows it — which is what makes her dangerous to be
allied with. **She will spend it once.** The party's choice, eventually, is what
she spends it on.

**Beats:** [The houses close to the di Teufels](../beats/the-houses-close-to-the-di-teufels.md) `d-2/day` · [The Pearl goes dark](../beats/the-pearl-goes-dark.md) `d-1/dawn`

## The wall

The act's real business. They learn who did it almost immediately and then
spend two days finding out that knowing is worth nothing.

- The watch is under a **written standing order**, over Prince Emeric's own
  signature: complaints against the Baron's people are to be recorded and
  referred, not acted upon, until the Tourney is concluded. Ninety people who
  live in these districts have been ordered to be useless in them.
- The magistrates will not hear it. **Maro Quist** will read anything put in
  front of him, all the way to the end, at his own pace, while you wait — and
  then tell you accurately why it is not his to act on.
- The houses have no standing. The Order has no civic power. The Governor is
  hosting the man.

A baron's son with a knife is weather, and you do not sue the weather.

**Beats:** [Maro Quist reads it to the end](../beats/quist-reads-it-to-the-end.md) `d-1/day`

## Santo comes back for what he left

He cannot report the loss to his father, so he handles it himself. He pays
**three Dragon Knights** out of his own pocket, walks them to the green door in
daylight, and demands his property back. Constantine refuses him a second time,
on the same step, in front of a bigger crowd.

He is nineteen and humiliated in front of hired men, and he uses fire on an
inhabited wooden building on a crowded canal.

**Then Blau.** A thin white man walks into the middle of a street fight with
empty hands, and it stops. He takes Santo away — publicly, one flat hand between
the boy's shoulder blades, in front of thirty witnesses.

That is the moment the household stops having a disgraceful son and starts
having **covered for one**, and the whole street saw it happen.

- This should be a real fight with a fire clock running through it. Three
  professionals fighting as a trio, a panicking caster, girls and a
  thirteen-year-old inside, and nowhere to fall back to.
- Whoever chooses the fire over the fight saves the house and loses the man,
  and should have to make that choice out loud.

**Beats:** [Santo comes back for her](../beats/santo-comes-back-for-her.md) `d-2/day` · [Santo's tantrum on the Pearl street](../beats/santo-s-tantrum-on-the-pearl-street.md) `d-2/dusk`

## The presentation of the entrants

**The set piece of Act II.**

**The place:** the tourney ground on the island — raked sand, tiered timber
stands going up on three sides, the Governor's box hung with lake-green cloth,
and beyond the stands the water and the temple steps. It is late afternoon,
it is packed, and it is entirely ceremonial: every competitor comes forward
bare-headed for the space of one bow, so that the city can see who is fighting
under whose colours.

The Baron's champion comes forward last.

The helm comes off, and it is **a thin boy of seventeen with dragon-scale
growing out of the skin of his throat and jaw** — no strap, no edge, the flesh
around it raised and weeping, and a fresh dressing inside his right elbow over
a decade of older ones.

For Gardwen that is her brother, alive, eleven feet away and not there at all.

- Nothing can be done. That is the scene. Four thousand people, a rope line, and
  the household standing between.
- A roll here is only ever about **whether she keeps her face**, and whether the
  people in the box notice her keeping it. Failing it is more interesting than
  making it: the household becoming aware of her is the engine of Act III.
- Play the boy surfacing for a second — the eyes changing, the lips parting,
  half a word — and then going back under. It happens when he is exhausted or
  when somebody says his own name.

**Act II takes:** the belief that evidence is worth anything, and the Pearl's
livelihood, now burning down to nothing whether it is spent or not.

# ACT III — `d-1/dusk` to `d0` · THE PLAN AND THE COUNTER
### They build something, and he breaks part of it

The party stops reacting and starts working. The Baron stops running a timetable
and starts **answering**, within a watch, every time they touch something that
matters.

## The question the act is actually about

Not evidence. **Jurisdiction.** The standing order shields him from the *city's*
law and touches nothing else, and there are three courts it cannot reach:

- **The King's Assay.** A Crown warrant, neither the city's nor the army's, and
  Conall holds one. The Assay cares about coin and metal, which is exactly where
  the Baron is weakest, because **his money is not his**.
- **Maritime law.** Boats, berths, cargo, and the Knights have been
  commandeering hulls off the water-stairs for a week. It is eating Harbormaster
  **Velen Deepkeel** alive, and every one of those seizures is written down in a
  register nobody has ever asked to see.
- **The Order's sacred sites.** The water, the steps, the rite. Armed men have
  no standing there at all, and for forty seconds a year, neither does a Baron.

The whole middle game is finding the one court his protection does not cover,
and getting somebody with standing to walk into it.

**Beats:** [A boat taken off the water-stair](../beats/a-boat-taken-off-the-water-stair.md) `d-2/day` · [The stairs stop](../beats/the-stairs-stop.md) `d-1/dawn`

## The money

The thread that unravels him, and it needs to be made playable rather than
merely true.

He is not rich. He is **funded** — by a creditor whose name is not on anything,
through the Vantt house, and **Alderic Vantt** is a warm, generous, genuinely
good-company man who has not said out loud even at home how frightened he is.
Two self-deprecating jokes in a row from Alderic means you have found the seam.

The paper trail runs through the Assay, where **Ivo Calder** signed an
assessment he should not have and has not destroyed his original notes. He has
moved them twice. He wants very badly to be told that what he signed was
reasonable, and he will fold completely and at once the moment an independent
reading appears — there is no middle with him.

**What it buys:** a name. Not a conviction. Knowing who is paying the Baron is
what makes Act V's ending mean something, because the man who is paying is not
in this city and does not die on this sand.

**Beats:** [Alderic Vantt jokes about his cellar](../beats/alderic-vantt-jokes-about-his-cellar.md) `d-1/dusk` · [Ivo Calder folds](../beats/ivo-calder-folds.md) `d0/day`

## The thieves, and the locket

**Crowbill** runs what passes for organisation among the canal thieves — a man
who watches for a long time before deciding you exist. He does not help people.
He trades, and he will want something the party will not enjoy giving.

What he can do that nobody else can: **the Baron wears a chain**, and on it a
locket, and he has never once been seen without it. It holds part of the binding
that keeps Thuban Eta seated in Cailan, and the Baron would turn this city
inside out to get it back.

There is exactly one hour in the week when that man is reachable in a crowd, and
it is at the tourney rail on the morning of the sand.

**Nus** will take the job — young, quick-handed, entirely out of his depth, and
hiding it by raising the stakes out loud. That is the tragedy set up here and
paid off in Act IV, and the party choose it with their eyes open.

**Beats:** [Crowbill names his price](../beats/crowbill-names-his-price.md) `d0/dawn` · [Orrin Sculle decides](../beats/orrin-sculle-decides.md) `on contact`

## The archery

**The set piece of Act III.**

**The place:** the long butts on the north shore, marked out on the grass
between the water and the stands, two hundred paces of it, with the Governor's
box at the near end and half the city lying on the slope behind. Late morning,
flat light, a crosswind coming off the lake that everybody is complaining about.

The archery is the one open event — no house colours required, no fee, anyone
may shoot. That is why an elf in plain harness is standing in it.

**Temerach wins.** Let her win: three days of being stopped on the road, spoken
to in front of her own escort, searched — and then two hundred paces of absolute
silence and she puts them where nobody else in the field can.

**What the dice decide** is not whether she wins. It is what she does with the
ninety seconds afterwards, standing in front of the Governor's box with four
thousand people looking at her and a prize she has to be handed.

- **She spends it.** She says, out loud, in the open, whose men stopped a King's
  officer on the Anminster road and what has been done in this city this week.
  Thirty years of standing converted into one public accusation the Governor
  cannot pretend not to have heard. It buys the party a hearing they could not
  otherwise get — and it makes her a marked woman four days before she needs to
  be able to move freely.
- **She holds it.** She takes the prize, says the correct words, and walks. She
  is still a King's officer with a clean record and a door she can open once.
  Colder, and much more useful later.
- **She loses.** Rarer and better than it sounds. Somebody wins who should not
  have, and the household's archer takes the wreath, and the scene becomes about
  the party watching the one person with standing lose her chance to use it.

Whatever happens here, **this is the scene the standoff in Act V answers.**

**Beats:** [PIVOT: the Archery](../beats/pivot-the-archery.md) `d0/day`

## What he does about it

The Baron's counters are triggered, not scheduled, and they land within a watch.
He is a man with more resources than the party and a strong preference for
never touching anything himself.

- **Ask in public about his money** and there is a price on the face by
  nightfall. Men out to *name* first and take later.
- **Reach his son** and Blau moves Santo, and whoever carried the message stops
  being findable.
- **Get evidence in front of an official** and he accuses back, lawfully. He is
  the Crown's guest; they are an unlicensed foreign sorcerer, a thief wearing a
  murderer's face, and an Assay officer operating outside his warrant. The watch
  will help him, correctly, by the book.
- **Put a hand near the champion** and the reinforcement goes deeper that night
  and the boy is moved.
- **Take something off him** and the Knights come off the standing order
  entirely, and the reprisals fall on people who had nothing to do with it.
- **Corner him** and the warmth goes *up*. More hospitality, more delight in
  you, more attention. He has never raised his voice and is not going to start.

And at the point where somebody in the party is most tired of losing, he offers
them a deal — warm, generous, and sincere in the moment, because he means it
when he says it.

**Act III takes:** a route and somebody's freedom. The city stops being a place
they can move around in.

# ACT IV — `d1` · THE UNFORGIVING CITY
### The worst night

Whatever they built gets used against them. The Knights come off the leash. And
every ally is somewhere else for reasons already established in play — not
because the GM needs them gone.

## The lift at the rail

The morning crowd at the tourney rail, six deep, everybody pressed forward to
see the entrants walked past. One hour, one chance, four thousand witnesses.

- **It works.** Nus comes away with the locket and the whole city pays for it.
- **It fails.** He is taken at the rail in front of everyone, and what happens to
  him happens anyway — just sooner, and with the party watching.
- **It half works.** The chain comes away and the locket does not, or he gets it
  and cannot get clear, and somebody has to decide in about four seconds whether
  to go in after him.

**Beats:** [Nus lifts the binding locket](../beats/nus-lifts-the-binding-locket.md) `d1/dawn`

## The sand

Cailan fights, and wins, because Thuban Eta has been winning fights since before
this city had walls.

It should be magnificent and unbearable at the same time: a thousand-year-old
knight of the Sixth Wing doing beautiful work with arms too thin to do it, and
Gardwen in the stands unable to want either of them to lose.

- If the party have interfered with the reinforcement, the boy is closer to the
  surface and the fighting is worse — and there is a real chance Cailan is badly
  hurt on the sand, which is a disaster and an opportunity at once.
- If they have done nothing, he wins clean and the crowd loves him, and that is
  its own kind of horror.

**Beats:** [The champion takes the sand -- and wins](../beats/the-champion-takes-the-sand-and-wins.md) `d1/day`

## The purge of the canals

**The set piece of Act IV, and it only happens if the locket is taken.**

**The place:** the low canals below the Fishmarket — a district of plank
walkways, rope bridges, boat-houses and stilts, where four thousand people live
on water and every family has a punt. It is the one part of Purewater the
Knights have never gone into, because horses are useless in it and the locals
know every roof.

They go in at dawn, on foot and in boats, with hooks.

- They take the bridges first. That is the cruelty of it — cut six rope bridges
  and the district stops being a neighbourhood and becomes forty islands, each
  with a few frightened people on it who cannot help each other.
- They are not searching. They are making it **unbearable to keep** the thing
  that was stolen. Boats stove in, houses fired, people dragged out onto the
  planking in front of their own families.
- **Nus is found**, and his body is hung at the Fishmarket stair where the whole
  district walks past it, and left there. Blau arranged it and does not appear
  to have enjoyed it.
- Crowbill's people scatter. The one organisation that could move anything
  through this city quietly is gone in a morning, and the party did that.

**The choice at the centre of it:** the only thing that stops the purge is
giving the locket back, and they have been told that, clearly, by somebody with
no reason to lie. Every hour they hold onto it has a name attached to it.

Play it so they can hear it from where they are. Do not cut away.

**Beats:** [The purge of the canals](../beats/the-purge-of-the-canals.md) `d1/night`

## The other losses of that night

Pick from these according to what the party actually built:

- The house they defended in Act II is taken while they are elsewhere, and it is
  not burned — it is **occupied**, which is worse, because the people in it are
  still in it.
- Somebody they recruited by name, because they asked, is arrested lawfully, on
  a correct warrant, and fighting the arrest turns them into exactly what the
  Baron has already publicly said they are.
- Santo comes for the evidence himself, at night, and he does not run when he
  panics — he **takes hold of whoever is nearest**, because that has always
  worked for him.
- The Order's water alarm is stood down for the second time by an instruction
  the same three councillors advised, and the temple's own people are the last
  to know. Nobody in the fiction understands yet that the watch thread and the
  fouling thread are the same thread.

**Act IV takes:** somebody they recruited, by name, and because they asked.

The act ends at dawn on the day of the sand, with the party hunted and the thing
they needed most either lost or in the wrong hands.

# ACT V — `d2` · THE RUN, THE ROOM, AND THE SHOT
### Everything in one place at once

Epic here is not scale. It is **simultaneity** — the crowd, the shallows, the
burning district behind, a shut room with a prince in it — with the smallest
thing at the centre mattering most.

**The place:** the island. Four thousand people in the stands. The Order in the
shallows at the foot of the temple steps for the Washing, waist-deep in
lake-water, singing. The Knights loose in the streets on the mainland behind,
visible as smoke. And on the sand, in harness, for the only time he ever leaves
that ship: the champion.

## The Run across the town

The last event, and the only one that leaves the island. From the Lists out
through the water-gate, across Caravan Square, down the Merchant's Quarter, over
the Lullwater by whatever means a competitor can find, and back along the Shoals
to the temple steps. On foot and by boat, no route laid down, no help forbidden.

**For about two hours Purewater has no crowd control at all.** The watch is
holding a line on a route instead of patrolling districts. The Knights are
strung out along it with nothing to do. Every bridge and stair has people packed
onto it, cheering, and nobody can move against the flow.

That is either the best cover the party will ever get or the reason they cannot
reach somewhere in time, and it should be both in the same hour.

**It is the last normal thing that happens.** Whatever they have built by the
morning of `d2`, the Run is the window to use it in — because at dusk the
competitors come back to the steps, the Prince takes his place, and the closing
begins.

**Beats:** [The Run across the town](../beats/the-run-across-the-town.md) `d2/day`

## The shut room

By a form older than the Governor's office, the winning champion is presented
**alone and unarmed** to the Crown's representative, behind a closed door,
immediately after the bout. Four generations of di Teufels bought their way
toward that room and this is the week they reach it.

Behind the door, the plan the whole campaign has been about: **draw Thuban out
of Cailan, and seat him in Prince Emeric.**

No swords in this scene matter at all.

- **Who is in the room** is the question the party has been answering since Act
  III without knowing it. Temerach can be in there if she spent her standing at
  the archery. A priestess of the Order can be in there if the rite gives her
  standing. Conall can be in there on a Crown warrant if the Assay thread ran.
  Randall can be in there wearing the wrong face, which is the funniest and
  most dangerous option available.
- **If nobody is in the room**, the working is attempted and only the locket,
  or its absence, decides it. Holding the locket may be the whole of the party's
  contribution, and it may be enough.
- **If it succeeds**, the Prince walks out of that room with a dead knight in
  him and the campaign has a different second half — and that is a legitimate
  ending, not a failure state.

**Beats:** [The private audience](../beats/the-private-audience.md) `d2/dusk`

## The standoff

However the room ends, Hanzo comes out of it into the open, in front of the
entire city. And on the far side of the arena is an elf with a bow and nothing
left to lose.

It is a standoff and not an assassination **because both of them know**. She
does not shoot from cover. She is a King's officer and she will make it look
like a lawful act even when it is not: she names him, across eighty yards, in
front of four thousand people, and gives him the chance to answer — which is
the one thing he has spent the whole week never having to do.

Then she shoots.

- **She kills him.** The house is broken in public and the war is not over,
  because the credit line was never his.
- **She wounds him.** Arguably the best outcome: he is alive, humiliated, and
  has to be carried out through a crowd that watched him refuse to answer.
- **She misses.** Also playable. He answers her, in that warm reasonable voice,
  in front of everyone, and for about four seconds he wins — and then somebody
  in the crowd throws something, and then everybody does.

**Beats:** [THE STANDOFF](../beats/the-standoff.md) `d2/dusk`

## The flight

She is an outlaw from the instant the arrow leaves the string. Assassination at
a sacred festival, in public, on the Crown's guest. Thirty years of service gone
in one second, deliberately, with her eyes open.

Whether the party gets her off the island is theirs to decide and it costs them
something. She will not thank them and will not explain.

**Beats:** [The flight](../beats/the-flight.md) `d2/dusk`

## The boy

In the noise, or in the water, or in the wreck of that room, Gardwen gets her
brother's body into her own hands, alive.

**And he is still not her brother.** The locket only ever tied Thuban to *Hanzo*.
What holds him in the world is a box of bones in a tower on the border estate,
four days' hard riding, behind his own men. The rite cannot be said. The words
that were never said over a knight of the Sixth Wing still cannot be said.

**The last beat of the adventure is Thuban telling her where they are** — with
perfect, thousand-year-old courtesy, in a seventeen-year-old's voice, because
under pressure his manners get better rather than worse.

# WHAT IS WON, AND WHAT IS DELIBERATELY WITHHELD

**Won:** the Prince is not taken. The house is broken in public. Bird is out of
that hold and breathing. The city saw all of it happen.

**Not won, on purpose:** the boy is not free. Thuban is not released. The burial
words are not said. And the man who was paying for all of it is not in this
city and never was.

That ache is the hook for the next adventure — **the fortress, and the bones.**

# THE PEOPLE WHO DECIDE THINGS

- **Baron Hanzo di Teufel.** Warm, hospitable, never raises his voice, and has
  not personally done a single thing he could be charged with. His assets, in
  order: the law, the Prince's affection, and twenty-five armed men the watch
  has been told to go easy on.
- **Blau.** The thin white man with empty hands who arrives after things have
  gone wrong and makes them stop. He is not a fighter and does not need to be.
- **Santo.** Nineteen, his father's failure, and the most dangerous person in the
  first two acts precisely because he is not competent. Pathetic and lethal at
  the same time.
- **Marisette.** Runs the trade. One weapon, spendable once, and she knows
  exactly how fast it is losing value.
- **Constantine.** Refuses the same man twice on her own step. The house with the
  green door is hers and the contracts belong to the girls, and she will not
  trade either.
- **Crowbill.** Watches. Trades. Does not help.
- **Temerach.** A King's officer who has been swallowing this for thirty years,
  and has decided — quietly, some time before Act I — that she is going to stop.
- **Prince Emeric.** Decent, intelligent, and wrong about everything, because
  nobody has ever told him the truth about this city and his signature is on the
  order that made it worse.
- **Nerissa and the Order.** Real power in the water and none at all on land,
  and a goddess who has been quiet for fourteen months, which the High Priestess
  has told nobody.

## The Dragon Knights — never skip a rung

They get worse every act, and the watch does nothing every time, which is the
point of them.

1. Tolls and insults. A woman's basket tipped into the canal.
2. A beating in daylight, over nothing, and the watch walks away.
3. **A death nobody answers for** — a dredger, a boy off a boat.
4. **Reprisal.** After a loss, they burn something belonging to people who had
   no part in it.
5. Open war in the streets, with the city fighting back badly.

## The watch — ninety people ordered to be useless

They are not villains and they are not a wall. They are tradesmen with families
in the districts being preyed on, holding a written order that says to record
and refer and not to act. Sergeants route patrols away from the Merchant's
Quarter rather than be seen standing there doing nothing.

**They cannot be talked round, and they can be given a reason.** Not evidence —
they already believe it. What they lack is a lawful basis to act that does not
end their careers, and that is the same problem as the jurisdiction problem.
Bring them the Assay, or maritime law, or the Order's ground, and they will move
— fast, and in numbers, and with a great deal of stored-up feeling.

**Turning the watch is a victory condition**, available from Act I onward, and it
changes Act IV completely.
