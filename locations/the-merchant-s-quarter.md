The South Quarter of [[High Isle]], south across the Grand Canal, where the
**Triumph Bridge** from [[Caravan Square]] brings the city's trade ashore. At its
centre is the walled **Market Square**, ringed by the **Guildhall of Merchants**,
the **Guildhall of Coopers** and the **Guildhall of Dyers**; around them stand the
counting-houses, the **Watercrafters' Guild**, and the palazzi of the **merchant
princes** who handle Purewater's day-to-day governance
from the city council. Wealthy, busy, and politically slippery -- this is where the
city's money is made, weighed, and quietly steered. South of it, across a
narrow cut spanned by the Cooper's, Dyers' and Tidegate Bridges, lie the docks of
the Seaward Quarter. (Formerly described as a separate "Merchant's Isle"; it is a
quarter of High Isle, an island of its own, divided from the rest by the Grand
Canal and its branches.)

**The lie of it.** Market Square fills the middle of the island, with stalls in
rows around a market cross. The **Guildhall of Merchants** is to its north, the
**Guildhall of Coopers** to its north-east, and the **Guildhall of Dyers** to its
south. The **Watercrafters' Guild** stands on the west shore just below the Triumph
Bridge, with its own boathouse and slipway on the channel. The merchant princes'
palazzi line the north shore along the Grand Canal, each with its own water-gate.
Three of them belong to the councillors who carry the Governor's instructions:
**Palazzo Vantt** and **Palazzo Pyle** at the west end, and **Palazzo Quist**, the
oldest, further east. Four bridges cross the cut to the Seaward Quarter: the
**Cooper's Bridge** at the west, an unnamed footbridge, the **Dyers' Bridge** below
the Guildhall of Dyers, and the **Tidegate Bridge** at the east. These are marked
on the Merchant's Quarter sheet, `maps/merchants/`.

## Taverns, amenities and the watch

- **The Golden Scales** -- the factors' wine house; more business is done at its tables than in the Guildhall.
- **The Moneychangers' Arcade** -- under the arcade on the square's west side; refusing di Teufel credit notes, loudly.
- **The Notary** -- contracts, deeds and public notices; the notice board outside draws a crowd.
- **The Auction Rooms** -- a dead man's stock sold every week, sometimes with his widow present.
- **The Cistern Baths** -- bathhouse for the clerks and apprentices.
- **The Market Post** (watch) -- the watch post on the square, whose patrols now route round the quarter rather than be seen not acting.
