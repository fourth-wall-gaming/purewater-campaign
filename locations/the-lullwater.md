Off to the north-east, a separate island across open water from [[High Isle]]'s
Tournament Ground and the Forges, with the docks of [[The Shoals]] to its south,
lies the **Lullwater** -- a low,
crowded, water-logged quarter of the **narrowest, most crooked canals** in the city
and tenements leaning over them until they almost touch overhead. Its name sounds
gentle -- a lull, a calm, still water -- and that is the joke of it: the Lullwater
is where Purewater's **underworld and its working poor live cheek by jowl**, thieves
and dredgers and smugglers and dockhands all crammed into the same drowned maze.

No bridge reaches it: everything and everyone comes and goes by boat, and the
quarter likes it that way. The city map letters it "(Thieves' Quarter)", and so
does everyone who doesn't live there.

It is easy to get lost here, and dangerous to *look* lost -- [[Conall]] learns as
much when he is cornered within an hour of wandering in. [[Crowbill]]'s people move
the canals by hidden boat-paths; the honest [[The Dredgers]] keep their **Dredgers'
Hall** and safe houses here; and the storyteller [[Thorne]] holds court most
evenings at the **[[The Moist Oyster]]** tavern. The quarter's nearness to the
smuggling jetties of the Shoals is no accident -- contraband flows from one straight
into the other.

**Inside it.** One straight canal, **the Gutter**, runs down the island's spine
from the north shore almost to the southern tip, crossed by a handful of plank
bridges. It is the only water in the quarter wide enough for two boats, and it does
not reach the sea at either end. Just west of it, at the quarter's heart, is **the Still**, a small round pool of dead
water ringed by tenements, which the old women say gave the Lullwater its name.
**[[The Moist Oyster]]** stands on its north-western edge, one wing of it built out over the water. The **Dredgers' Hall**
is on the southern shore, looking across the water to the Shoals. Boats from High
Isle put in at **Shilling Stairs** on the west shore, opposite the Tournament
Ground; every crossing costs a shilling, and somebody always sees who paid it. To
the north-east the island trails off into **the Hook**, a reedy spit of sheds and
drying-racks. The deep bay on the south-eastern shore is **Eel Bay**, where the eel-women sell off
the boats.

**Whose it is.** [[Crowbill]] keeps no house. He has sat for thirty years on the
**flat lead roof over the cookshop ovens** beside the Gutter, the warmest place in
Purewater in October, eating out of a paper and not looking up. Two of his men stand
at the stair-head, and the street boys he runs sleep in the gap under the roof.
Late at night he is more often across the water, in the back of an ice-house on the
Shoals. His books are kept by **Hessa**, who has run the tallies for the whole of
that water for twenty years from a back room behind a **chandlery** on the Gutter's
west bank, forty yards down the waterfront from the Oyster. Her street boys
report to her, and when Crowbill needs to move fast he goes there. [[Kag]] lives on the west shore of Eel Bay: a room over a chandler's, up an
outside stair, with one window on the water and a door that opens outward. When
[[Conall]] came to the city he took a room in a plain inn near Shilling Stairs, the
kind that wouldn't attract attention, and he was cornered within an hour of walking
out of it.
