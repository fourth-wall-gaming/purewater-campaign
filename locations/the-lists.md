Raked sand inside a rope, tiered timber going up on three sides, and the
Governor's box at the western end hung with lake-green cloth. Four thousand
people fit in the stands and on the slope behind them, and on the day of the
Tourney every one of those places is taken.

What makes it the right place for an ending is that **everything is visible from
everywhere.** The shallows at the foot of the temple steps, where the Order
stands waist-deep for the Washing, are in plain sight of the stands. So is the
mainland across the water, which on the last day is showing smoke.

Behind the Governor's box is the small room where the winning champion is
presented to the Crown's representative, alone and unarmed, by a form older than
the office itself.
