# The Cellar and the Name

*Two days before the Tourney.* The face is arrested twice before dawn, and two men who have never met wake up looking at each other. By noon the four characters are a party, and by nightfall the face has a name and every door on the Pearl is shut to the Baron's men. They also learn something they will spend the rest of the week relearning: in this city, knowing who did it is worth almost nothing.

## Hesper Comes Before Dawn

<!-- covers: hesper-takes-the-look-alike -->

Marisette does not wait for the watch. By midnight the house has given her a description, and by the small hours her people have found the face twice. A thief who answers to it is fencing a morning's take on the Pearl. A young man-at-arms who answers to it has a room at a plain inn near Shilling Stairs. She does not know which one did it, so she takes both.

Hesper walks up to each of them in turn before dawn and puts them down without a fight worth the name: a word, a Sleep, a hand on the shoulder, and two men from the Mother of Pearl with a blanket and a boat. If both twins are characters, play the two takings as two short scenes, cutting between them. If only one is, the other twin is already in his chair when the character wakes. **Nobody gets away from Hesper; that is not what this scene is for.** A character who fights is put down harder and wakes with a worse headache. If neither twin is a character, both are taken anyway, and the party hears of it from Magda and Gardwen, who are about to be sent for as witnesses.

## Two Men with One Face

<!-- covers: the-catacombs-two-men-one-face -->

**This is the scene the whole opening has been building toward, and the one where the party is made.** Give it room.

The burial galleries under the Hill quarter are dry, cold and chalk-white, with three centuries of the respectable dead stacked in them, and sound does not carry up. Two men sit tied to chairs side by side in the lamplight, each looking at his own face on a stranger. Marisette stands in front of them with the lamp. Hesper stands behind them, where they cannot see.

She questions them **together**, which is cruel and clever: each one hears the other's answers, and each one looks at his own face while he gives them. She wants to know where they were at the turn of the night and who the girl is to them. She does not say what happened to the girl. Somewhere in it, somebody says the word *brother* out loud. Let the twins get there themselves if they can. Randall arrives carrying a guard who called him *my lord*. Conall arrives carrying a canal-rat who called him *Randall* and wanted an apology for something he never did. And here is Randall.

Then Marisette sends for the two women from the house, or they come on their own, because Gardwen saw the man on the stair and wants to know whom Marisette has taken. **Magda and Gardwen clear them on what they saw.** The man on the stair was cradling a burnt right hand, and neither of these two has a mark on him. He was dressed better than either of them, and the coat he wore is still over a chair in that room, with a crest under the collar. If either twin can be placed elsewhere at the turn of the night, a witness who was there makes it stick. Marisette believes a person who was there over a face, every time. If you want a roll, it is her Insight against the witnesses' Influence, and with the burn and the coat it is Easy.

**If the witnesses do not carry it,** because the dice go badly or a character refuses to cooperate, other people tip the scales, each with a price attached. A folded note arrives by the hand of Temerach's squire. Temerach does not come herself, and whatever is in the note, Marisette unties Conall without another question and looks at him differently afterwards. Crowbill sends word that Randall is a known man of the Lullwater and he would like him back. Or Emmeralda, if she is alive and herself, is carried down to look at them and shakes her head. Marisette is right in the end either way; the slow way just means hours in the dark first.

**Play the moment the ropes come off.** It is the first time the four characters are in one room: two women who saw it, two men who were blamed for it, and a madame who now owes the twins an apology. It is the party's founding image, and the players will remember it.

Then Marisette looks at the face in front of her for a long moment. If the coat has been brought from the house, she turns the collar over and reads the crest. **She names the di Teufels.** She does not know which di Teufel, and she knows exactly what it means that two strangers in her city wear their face.

## The Assayer in the Doorway

<!-- covers: conall-reads-the-room -->

From Marisette's questions, Conall has just heard what was done upstairs at the Sylph's Embrace, and **the moment he hears it, he investigates.** It is not curiosity. Seating a spirit in an unwilling person is a hanging matter under the King's Assay's charter, and he is the only assayer in Purewater. In the natural course of things he walks out of the cellar and straight to the Pearl. If he hears sooner, on the night, from the street or from a note of Temerach's, let him go sooner.

Constantine does not let strangers into that room, but the warrant opens it: a thumb-ring of assay silver, a steel punch, and a man who says *the Crown's Assay* in a voice that expects to be obeyed. She has never seen an assayer, though she has heard of them. She lets him in and stands in the doorway to watch.

Whatever Santo left and nobody has taken is waiting for him. The **chalk seat** under the rug is still live. Detect Magic finds it, and Read Magic, or the Assay's training, reads what it was for: a seating, not a summoning. Somebody tried to put something *into* her, and whether the seat is spent or broken tells him whether it took. The **spent scroll** holds nine lines in an unregistered hand, which is an offence before anybody asks whose hand it is, and Conall can say, accurately, that whoever wrote it has never been on the Register and is very, very good. The **barbed knife** is behind the armoire if the working failed, and the Assay knows the mark of Asmodeus on its pommel. The **coat** is here, if Marisette has not already taken it. And **Emmeralda** is here, cut open and tainted if the working failed, or, if it took and she is still in the house, bright and wrong and asking for the window to be opened, with Conall's Detect Magic showing him what is sitting inside her.

**This is the first lawful lever anybody has had on the di Teufels all week.** The Assay's doctrine is *establish, record, refer*, and Conall is good at it: a written finding struck with his own punch is evidence no council can wave away, and the basis of a capital charge, in a court the standing order cannot touch. And it costs. A finding that names a great lord, laid by a junior assayer who cannot make it stand, is a finding he answers for himself. Temerach will want to know what he has written before he seals it. The doctrine says send it to Caldreth, where it will arrive after everything has happened. His brother, whom he has had for about four hours, says do something now.

## Santo Wants It Back

<!-- covers: santo-comes-back-for-her -->

Santo has two problems this morning, and he is going to try to solve both at once.

**The room** comes first. He wants his coat and his scroll, which are errands he could explain to his father if he had to. He sends a man up for them by water between the noon rest and the evening trade. In the natural course of things Conall has been through the room first, straight from the cellar, and Santo's man finds bare boards. That enrages Santo and sends him after whoever has them. If the characters dawdle, the boat arrives while they are still on the stairs, and the scene becomes a fight in a doorway over a coat.

**The girl** comes second, and only if the working took and he failed to get her out on the night. (If he already has her, she is aboard the ship. If the working failed, he does not want a girl with a wound the length of her body, and he goes straight to his temper.) She is still in Constantine's house, and she still wants to go with him. That is the horror of it: the house has to keep her in while she is trying to get out, and the thing inside her is patient and charming and keeps asking for the window to be opened. So he comes back with a boatman hired off the Shoals, two men who ask no questions and a covered skiff at the water-stair behind the house. He does not have to carry her. He only has to be there when she reaches the door.

Onscreen, this is a fight, a chase over water, or a negotiation the party wins simply by being present and awake. The hard part is Emmeralda, who is on his side. Offscreen, if nobody is watching that stair, **he gets her**, and from dusk she is aboard the Baron's ship, willing, nobody's prisoner but her own. Gardwen will find her there in two nights' time. Do not soften this. It is the cost of not being there.

## Boats on the Water-Stair

<!-- covers: a-boat-taken-off-the-water-stair -->

At some point in the middle of the day, on a water-stair on the Pearl, four Knights step into a working skiff that already has a fare in it. The fare gets out. The boatman, a Shoals man named Perrin Cale, forty, who has rowed this stair for twenty years, stays with his boat, because it is his livelihood and he has decided, wrongly, that he can. They let him row. The two boatmen taken before him are numbers 24 and 25 in Abel Weir's referral book, and they have not come back. Nobody has found a body, and the Dredgers have decided that they were rowed out through the Mouth and left there.

**If a character is anywhere near the water, this is the easiest intervention in the scenario and the most instructive one.** Four armoured men in a small boat on deep water, in a city where every local can swim and none of them can. A party that thinks about the water rather than steel wins this without a fight, and makes three hundred dockworkers extremely interested in them. Offscreen, roll for Perrin Cale: he comes back at midnight with a broken hand, or he does not come back at all. Either way Marda Blackwater hears about it before breakfast. Mark one for the occupation, and move the Dredgers one step closer to the stairs.

## The Lie on Every Stair

<!-- covers: sinnit-tells-the-pearl-it-was-randall -->

By mid-morning the whole Pearl knows Marisette's people took somebody in the night for what was done at the Sylph's Embrace, and Sinnit makes sure it knows who. He tells it on every stair and in every wine-shop between Pearl Quay and the Shoals: *Randall.* Randall, raised in the Promise of Heaven, who turned on the women who raised him. Randall, whom Hesper herself came for. Sinnit was in the Lullwater last night with a man wearing that face, and he has a bruise to show for it.

It does not matter that Marisette cleared him. The clearing happened in a cellar in front of four people; the accusation happens in the open, forty times, in a voice that carries. By evening the street believes, falsely, that the man who carved Emmeralda was Randall the Pearl thief, and it costs Randall on the one ground where he is rich. The Promise of Heaven's door opens to him a little more slowly, and Asphodel's face holds a question she is ashamed to ask. A fence he has used for six years is out when he calls. Somebody spits on the stair behind him. Ila, at the Sylph's Embrace, looks at him and runs. None of it is permanent, and all of it is this week, which is the week he needs it. Marisette can end it with one sentence on the quay, and will if somebody asks her, at a price. Emmeralda, if she is alive and herself, can end it by being seen with him. Or Randall can go and find Sinnit, which is what Sinnit wants.

And Sinnit has seen the real Randall today, walking the Pearl free and unbruised. He is slow, but he is starting to understand that there are two of that face. Mark two for Sinnit.

## Fire on the Quay

<!-- covers: santo-s-tantrum-on-the-pearl-street, the-houses-close-to-the-di-teufels -->

If Santo does not have her, because the working failed or the boat did, he comes back to the Pearl at dusk with his right hand bandaged and nobody with him, because he has run out of people to send. He stands in front of the Sylph's Embrace and demands her. Then he demands his coat. Then, in front of the evening crowd, he says, *"I'll burn your house with your girls inside it."*

**And then he does something about it.** A cantrip his father let him learn sends a gout of fire into the doorway, the hangings and the painted shutters. It is not big, and it does not need to be: on a street of timber and lamp-oil it is the worst thing anybody has done on the Pearl in twenty years, and people are running with buckets before he has finished. Blau arrives inside a minute (he has been looking for the boy since noon), takes him by the collar and by the burnt wrist, and walks him off the Pearl without a word, through thirty witnesses who now know exactly what that face is called. The name follows them down the quay: *di Teufel. The son.* **This is the moment the face gets a name**, and anybody who was in the cellar this morning can put the burn, the name and the threat together.

(If Santo has the girl safe aboard his father's ship, he has no reason to make a scene, and none of this happens. Constantine finds an empty room instead, with a coat in it.)

That night Constantine comes to the Mother of Pearl, with smoke still in her hair or an empty room behind her, and tells Marisette what a di Teufel has done under one of her roofs. **Marisette does not close the Pearl.** That would put four hundred of her own people out of work to punish twenty-five men. **She closes it to them.** Every house on the island, at once, to anybody in di Teufel colours or the Baron's pay: no rooms, no drink, no music, no girls, and a doorman on every stair who knows the dragon when he sees it. Crowbill's people enforce it, for a fee Marisette pays without blinking. By morning, a Knight who walks onto the Pearl walks into a street where every door is shut in his face and every woman on it is watching him go.

It costs the di Teufels nothing material and a great deal of face, and the whole city reads it as the Pearl taking a side. It costs the Pearl almost nothing, which is why it holds. The Baron does nothing to Marisette directly. He leans on the Prince's councillors instead, and gives Blau a sharp word about the boy, and the Knights take it out on the streets. Mark one for the occupation.

## Whose Son He Is

<!-- covers: temerach-tells-conall-whose-son-he-is -->

Late that night, in a stable, out of earshot of anybody, Temerach tells Conall the truth she has held for years.

She has not been waiting for courage. She has been waiting for the moment the truth becomes more useful told than kept: the moment he would find it out anyway, from somebody she does not control. Today he woke in a chair next to a thief wearing his face, who had been called *my lord* by a guard at the Baron's stirrup. His mother was an elven mage the Baron seduced and broke. She fled to this city and died here. Temerach has known since before she recruited him.

**Play it as a handler briefing an asset, and let the warmth leak through anyway.** She tells him in about six sentences. She does not apologise. She tells him what it means for the next two days and what she needs him to do about it, and then, once, she says something that is not a task. If Conall is not a character, this still happens, and the party meets a Conall who is suddenly a different man. If the players have already worked it out, bring the scene forward. There is nothing worse than a Non-Player Character withholding a fact the table already has.
