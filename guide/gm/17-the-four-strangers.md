# The Four Strangers

Purewater is written for four pregenerated characters, and each of them is wired into the plot by somebody else's mistake. This chapter is the Games Master's view of them: what each brings, what each does not know, and how the first day pulls them into the same night without letting them meet. The players' view, with their sheets and their opening scenes, is in the *Players' Guide*. Any of the four the players do not choose stays in the world as a Non-Player Character, does exactly what this chapter says, and meets the party in the cellar on the second morning.

## Gardwen

A feral-raised elf of the deep woods and a druid of the old quiet powers: healing, green things and pure water. A she-wolf called Ruhi walks at her heel, neither a pet nor tame. Ten years ago the Dragon Knights burned her family's steading at night and took her little brother, and his body was never among the dead. She has followed that colour across a great deal of country ever since, on the strength of one belief that nobody else shares. Three weeks ago she picked up the Baron's column on the north road.

**What she does not know:** that her brother is alive, that he is the champion in the litter, and that something else is wearing him. She learns the first two at the presentation of the entrants, when the helm comes off for four seconds, and the third on the first night of the Tourney, when she goes aboard the Baron's ship and hears her brother's voice speak with somebody else's manners.

**What she brings:** healing on the night it is needed most, and the scenario's emotional centre. She is the single most motivated person in Purewater. Every scene she is in should have that weight under it.

## Magda

Sixty-odd, built like a gatepost, ringmail under a travelling cloak, a sworn servant of Hel the death-goddess, who does not let her age. She has outlived every single person she started with. For eleven nights Hel has sent her the same dream: a young woman with a wolf, a burning house, and a knight in dragon-scale who is already dead and does not know it. The dream said *lay the dragon warrior to rest*, which is not the same as kill, and she has been turning that over for three weeks of walking.

**What she does not know:** what the dream means. She will find out piece by piece, and the scenario ends by telling her where the dragon warrior's bones are. Magda knows Purewater a little from years ago, enough to know that Constantine at the Sylph's Embrace rents rooms to women travelling alone.

**What she brings:** the oldest, steadiest head in the party, the recognition of possession when she sees it (she has served Hel long enough to know a soul that is in the wrong body), and in a fight, terror out of all proportion to her age. Outside a fight, she is the most reliable adult in the story.

## Randall

A Pearl thief, raised by the women of the Promise of Heaven, who knows every roof, ledge, water-stair and back door on that island and most of the city besides. He pays the Pearl back because the Pearl raised him, and he does not discuss his mother. Nus is his friend and second pair of hands. Sinnit has hated him for seven years over a purse handed back with a bow.

**What he does not know:** whose face he has. He is the Baron's son and Conall's twin, and people have been telling him all his life that he looks like somebody without his ever asking whom. He gets the first hint before noon on the first day, when a guard at the Baron's stirrup calls him *my lord*, and the answer in the catacombs at dawn on the second.

**What he brings:** the city itself, which is his character sheet, and favours nobody else can call in. He is the only one of the four who is home, which means he has the most to lose. On the second night of the Tourney he is the one thief in Purewater who can stand on the Baron's deck for three seconds without anybody shouting.

## Conall Bjornlasch

A militiaman's son from Ashwick, pulled out of a ditch at nineteen by Temerach Nebulo, taught to read, and then taught a good deal more. The King's Assay trained him and gave him a warrant, a silver thumb-ring and a steel punch with his own mark. He is an arcane investigator (find-magic, illusion, a borrowed face when the work needs one) and a serious fighter, which surprises people who have decided he is a clerk. He rides into Purewater beside Temerach on an errand she has not explained.

**What he does not know:** almost everything that matters about himself. Temerach has known for years whose son he is. He gets the first hint at dusk on the first day, when a canal-rat in the Lullwater calls him *Randall*, and the truth from Temerach herself on the second night, once it has become more useful to her told than kept.

**What he brings:** standing. A warrant opens doors that stay shut for anybody else, and seating a spirit in an unwilling person is squarely within his charter, so when he hears what was done at the Sylph's Embrace, he goes to investigate it. He also brings the scenario's central dilemma: the Assay's doctrine is *establish, record, refer*, and his brother, whom he has had for about four hours, says do something now.

## How the First Day Pulls Them Together

Each character starts in their own entry point, and the first day exists to put all four into the same night without their meeting.

- **Magda and Gardwen** meet in the queue at the ford, behind the Baron's column, and Magda takes Gardwen to the Sylph's Embrace for beds. They are in the house when Santo works his scroll, and they see the man on the stair.
- **Randall** is working the carts in Caravan Square when the column comes through, and a terrified guard calls him *my lord*.
- **Conall** rides in with Temerach, and at dusk she sends him into the Lullwater with a note, where Sinnit corners him and calls him *Randall*.

Before dawn on the second day, Marisette's enforcer takes both men who wear the attacker's face, and Magda and Gardwen are the witnesses who clear them. That cellar is where the party is made. Everything before it is a reason for the four of them to be in it.

## Characters of the Players' Own

A player who would rather build somebody can, and it may be the better choice. Roll the characteristics first and decide who the character is afterwards: a CHA of 5 or a SIZ of 20 is a character concept arriving unbidden. Then ask the only question that matters: *why are you in Purewater this week, and who would notice if you left?* Put them in somebody's debt, or somebody in theirs: a guild boss short of answers, a madame short of law, a harbourmaster, a temple, a company of unpaid soldiers. Build a collision into their Passions on purpose, a loyalty that will one day disagree with an ambition.

**Then find where they already are.** Do not invent a new corner of the city; every corner is already in this book. Their way in is the place where their ordinary work breaks this week: a boatman whose boat is commandeered, a clerk who sees Vantt's accounts, a temple novice who notices the water, a fence who is offered a marked knife. Give them at least one scene each day that only they would notice, and aim it at a decision only they can make. If a player's character replaces one of the four, the four's scenes still happen. Give the replaced character's hook to whoever is nearest it.
