# What Is Really Going On

This chapter tells the whole secret, in the order it was built.

## Betrayal and Death a Thousand Years Ago

A thousand years ago the Dragon Kings ruled Thornmere, and their armies were led by dragon-knights, warriors bound in fire who fought like nothing living. There were never any dragons. The Kings were men, and each of them wore a demon; the dragon on their banners was the image of the greatest of those demons, and *What the Dragons Were* tells the rest.

Thuban Eta was a knight-commander of the Sixth Wing, and he carried no demon at all. He was a good enough soldier without one, and that is why he held command: the bound men went mad in the end, and somebody sane had to lead them. When his company was ordered to burn a valley with the people still in it, he refused. His own comrades drowned him for it, in his mail, with his hands tied, and buried him without the rites a knight of his order was owed.

The Dragon Kings fell soon afterwards in the great uprising, and their heraldry has been an unwelcome sight on a lord's banner ever since. But Thuban was never given the rites, and so he never went anywhere. He has been awake near his bones the whole time. The betrayal and dishonour of his killing has kept him here as a ghost, waiting to be laid to rest in peace as he should have been, all those years ago.

## Dark Plans from the Borderlands

The di Teufels are a border family, and for four generations they have pursued one forbidden ambition: to rule as the Dragon Kings ruled. The first of them found the texts, written in the old court tongue of the Kings themselves, and read in them what the rest of the realm had forgotten: there had never been any dragons, and the source of the Kings' power was demonology. The second found Thuban's bones and carried them home to **Emberhold**, the family's keep of black stone and a single tall tower above a burned valley on the border. The third worked out the school of binding, the old art of holding a spirit in a living body. The fourth is **Baron Hanzo di Teufel**, a warm, unhurried man in his fifties who has never once been refused anything he asked for pleasantly, and he is the one who made it work.

Hanzo is the most charming man most people will ever meet, and a pathological narcissist. He is warm, attentive and delighted by other people; he remembers your name, your mother's illness and the joke you made a year ago, and he makes everybody in the room feel like the one he came to see. He has practised it all his life, and he feels none of it. He needs to be admired the way other men need to eat, he cannot bear to be refused, and he never forgives anybody who humiliates him or sees through him. The only person who ever saw him clearly, he broke.

He is also the first of the four with no belief left, only appetite. He believes in his own greatness and in power, and in nothing else. The Dragon Kings, the old faith and the fire he binds are tools to him, and so is everybody who serves him, and he holds all of them in contempt, smiling. He means to depose the King and rule Thornmere as a man, because he cannot imagine anybody else fit to.

Ten years ago, his Dragon Knights burned a steading on the border at night and took a seven-year-old boy named Gabriel. His sister Gardwen was small enough for the woods to hide, and she survived. The Baron opened the boy and seated Thuban Eta inside him.

Thuban has been there ever since. The boy is seventeen now, and he is called Cailan. Dragon-scale grows out of the skin of his throat and jaw with no strap or edge, the flesh around it raised and weeping. He fights like a knight of the dragon wars because one does the fighting for him.

And Gabriel is still in there. For ten years, below the waterline of the Baron's ship, the knight has kept the boy company. He took the fighting and the rites so that Gabriel need not be present for them, and he taught him in the hours between. Thuban raised him. Gabriel surfaces when the Baron is not watching, and he has learned to hide it.

The **Awake the Dragon Movement** belongs to the Baron. Its believers want the Dragon Kings back in the flesh and think he is their herald. He lets them think so, and holds them in contempt. Under him:

- **Lady Ysolde Marr**, the Steward of the Ember House at Caldreth, makes friends for him at court.
- **Cassian Bree** keeps the Ember Society in Purewater and decides who goes further in.
- **Caldus Vane** raises sworn companies in the provinces.
- **Severin Kade** works the fire.
- **Alderic Vantt** supplies the money, frightened and in debt.

Each of them knows only their own part, and the believers do not know what the Baron is really for. That gap is the Movement's weakness.

## What Holds Thuban

Three things hold Thuban in the boy, and each of them can fail separately.

**The bones** lie in a box in the tower at Emberhold, four days' hard riding from Purewater. They hold Thuban in the world. Nothing in this scenario can reach them, and the scenario ends by pointing at them.

**The locket** hangs on a chain round the Baron's neck, and it ties Thuban to Hanzo. It is the leash. With it in his hand the Baron can command Thuban, draw him out of the boy and seat him somewhere else. Without it he can do none of those things, and the spirit he has held for ten years becomes a dragon-knight who owes him nothing but a reckoning. The locket is a strong working, and only a powerful Dispel Magic or an Exorcism would break it. Holding it gives nobody else any power over Thuban. It only takes him away from the Baron.

**The nightly rite** renews the seat. Every night, behind sealed doors on his ship, the Baron makes a cut, fills a bowl and speaks for an hour with the locket in his hand. Then he takes the chain off, lays the locket on the table beside his bed and goes to sleep. **That is the only time the locket is ever off his neck.**

## The Room

The boy was never the goal. Cailan is the jar: ten years of proof that a dragon-knight's spirit can be kept in a living body without destroying it. The Baron means to put that spirit into somebody else.

The Lake Lady's Tourney is older than Purewater's government. By its oldest form, the champion of the Single Combat is presented **alone and unarmed** to the Crown's representative, behind a closed door, in a small stone room behind the Governor's box. Because it is a rite and not a privilege anyone asks for, nobody has ever thought to guard it. The Crown's representative in Purewater is **Prince Emeric**, the King's nephew: two-and-twenty, decent, earnest and badly advised.

In that room, the Baron means to draw Thuban out of the worn-out boy and seat him in the Prince. A baron with the King's nephew on his leash does not need a barony. He has the court, and after the court, the crown. King Ostmar is decisive and strong, and his nephew has been a disappointment to him. A nephew who comes home courteous, steady and formidable is the heir Ostmar always wanted, and the Baron is that heir's oldest friend.

So the champion must win the Single Combat, and must not be recognised before he does. Everything else the Baron does this week serves those two needs: the curtained litter, the ship he never leaves at night, the champion nobody is allowed to speak to.

## A Chink in the Great Man's Armour: His Heir

As a young man Hanzo had many passions, and he fathered more sons than he knows about. One of them is his legitimate heir, **Santo**, who is nineteen, has never been told no by anybody who mattered, and shares his father's appetite for power and for lust. Santo has no part in his father's plan and no idea what it is. He knows only that his father can put a spirit in a living body and be obeyed by it, because he has watched it done for ten years.

Santo wants a girl. **Emmeralda** is the finest dancer on the Pearl, and he wants her kept on his father's barge and glad to be there. So he has stolen one scroll of Seat the Bound from his father's locked case, and a barbed ritual knife, and on the first night of the scenario he tries to put a small, hungry spirit into her to make her want what he wants. He has no training, and the dice decide whether it works. Either way, the scroll he burns out is written in his father's own hand, and his squalid little crime on the Pearl is the first thread that leads to the real one.

## Two More Sons

The Baron does not know he has three sons. Long before his open descent he seduced and broke a young elven mage, who fled to Purewater and died there when her children were small. She was the only person who ever saw what he was, and *What the Dragons Were* tells what she saw.

She bore twins. **Randall** was raised by the women of a pleasure house on the Pearl and became a thief. **Conall** was taken to Ashwick, pulled out of a ditch at nineteen by a knight of the Swords of the Lake, and trained by the King's Assay as an arcane investigator. Both of them wear the di Teufel face, and so does Santo.

On the night Santo goes upstairs with Emmeralda, nobody on the Pearl knows the heir's face, because he has never needed to show it. When the city goes looking for the man who did it, all it has is the face, and three men in Purewater wear it.

## What the Characters Can Do

Nobody can arrest a baron, and the characters cannot fight twenty-five Knights. But the Baron's plan has three weak points, and the characters can reach all of them:

- Stop the champion winning, or have the match stopped on sacred ground.
- Keep the Baron out of the room.
- Take the locket on the one night it lies on a table instead of round his neck, and keep it.

The story leans toward the third, and the third costs the most. A stolen locket turns a patient, smiling lord into a man who will burn a quarter of the city to get it back, and what the characters do with that day decides how the scenario ends.
