# What Is Really Going On

Here is the whole secret, in the order it was built.

## A Knight Who Was Buried Wrong

A thousand years ago the Dragon Kings ruled Thornmere, and their armies were led by dragon-knights: warriors bound in fire, who rode beside the great beasts and fought like nothing living. One of them was Thuban Eta, a knight of the Sixth Wing. When his company was ordered to burn a valley with the people still in it, he refused. His own comrades drowned him for it, in his mail, with his hands tied, and buried him without the rites a knight of his order was owed.

The Dragon Kings fell soon afterwards in the great uprising. Their heraldry has been an unwelcome thing to see on a lord's banner ever since. But Thuban was not given the rites, and so he never went anywhere. He has been awake, somewhere near his bones, for the whole of it. He does not think of what was done to him as cruelty. He thinks of it as a breach of law, which is worse, because law can be argued.

## A Family That Wanted Him

The di Teufels are a border family, and for four generations they have pursued one forbidden ambition: to become what the Dragon Kings were. The first of them found the texts. The second found Thuban's bones and carried them home to a tower on their border estate. The third worked out the school of binding, the old art of holding a spirit in a living body. The fourth is **Baron Hanzo di Teufel**, a warm, unhurried man in his fifties who has never once been refused anything he asked for pleasantly, and he is the one who did it.

Ten years ago, his Dragon Knights burned a steading on the border at night and took a seven-year-old boy named Gabriel. His sister Gardwen, who was small enough for the woods to hide, survived. The Baron opened the boy and seated Thuban Eta inside him, and Thuban has been there ever since. The boy is seventeen now. He is called Cailan. Dragon-scale grows out of the skin of his throat and jaw with no strap or edge, the flesh around it raised and weeping. He fights like a knight of the dragon wars because one does the fighting for him. Gabriel is still in there, intermittently and briefly, at enormous cost.

Behind the Baron stands the **Awake the Dragon Movement**: a patient, distant circle of men who believe the age of the Dragon Kings can be restarted, in a body of their choosing. They are not his masters, exactly. He is their most promising experiment, and they are paying for it.

## What Holds Thuban

Three things hold Thuban in the boy, and each is a separate point of failure.

**The bones** lie in a box in a tower on the Baron's border estate, four days' hard riding from Purewater. They hold Thuban in the world. Nothing in this scenario can reach them, and the scenario ends by pointing at them.

**The locket** hangs on a chain round the Baron's neck. It ties Thuban to *Hanzo*: it is the leash. With it in his hand, the Baron can command Thuban, draw him out of the boy, and seat him somewhere else. Without it he can do none of those things, and the spirit he has held for ten years is a dragon-knight who owes him nothing but a reckoning. The locket is a robust working; only a powerful Dispel Magic would break it. And, crucially, holding it gives nobody else any power over Thuban. It only takes him away from the Baron.

**The nightly rite** renews the seat. Every night, behind sealed doors on his ship, the Baron makes a cut, fills a bowl and speaks for an hour with the locket in his hand. Then he takes the chain off, lays the locket on the table beside his bed, and goes to sleep. That is the only time the locket is ever off his neck, and it is the most important thing in this book.

## The Room

The boy was never the point. Cailan is the jar: ten years of proof that a dragon-knight's spirit can be kept in a living body without destroying it. The destination is somebody else.

The Lake Lady's Tourney is older than Purewater's government, and by its oldest form, the champion of the Single Combat is presented **alone and unarmed** to the Crown's representative, behind a closed door, in a small stone room behind the Governor's box. It is a rite, not a favour, which is exactly why nobody has ever thought to guard it. The Crown's representative in Purewater is **Prince Emeric**, the King's nephew: two-and-twenty, decent, earnest and badly advised.

In that room, the Baron means to draw Thuban out of the worn-out boy and seat him in the Prince. A baron with the King's nephew wearing his leash does not need a barony. He has the court.

So the champion must win the Single Combat, and must not be recognised before he does. The Baron arranges everything else around those two facts: the curtained litter, the ship he never leaves at night, the champion nobody is allowed to speak to.

## The Son Who Ruined It

The Baron has a legitimate heir, **Santo**, who is nineteen and has never been told no by anybody who mattered. Santo is no part of his father's plan and has no idea what it is. He knows only that his father can put a spirit in a living body and be obeyed by it, and he has watched it done for ten years.

Santo wants a girl: **Emmeralda**, the finest dancer on the Pearl. He wants her kept on his father's barge and glad to be there. So he has stolen one scroll of Seat the Bound from his father's locked case, and a barbed ritual knife, and on the first night of the scenario he tries to put a small, hungry spirit into her to make her want what he wants. He has no training. The dice decide whether it works. Either way, the scroll he burns out is written in his father's own hand, and the squalid little crime he commits on the Pearl is the door that opens on the real one.

## Two More Sons

The Baron does not know he has three sons. Long before his open descent he seduced and broke a young elven mage, who fled to Purewater and died there when her children were small. She bore twins. **Randall** was raised by the women of a pleasure house on the Pearl and became a thief. **Conall** was taken to Ashwick, pulled out of a ditch at nineteen by a knight of the Swords of the Lake, and trained by the King's Assay as an arcane investigator. Both of them wear the di Teufel face. It is Santo's face, too.

On the night Santo goes upstairs with Emmeralda, nobody on the Pearl knows the heir's face, because he has never needed to show it. So when the city goes looking for the man who did it, all it has is the face. Three men in Purewater wear it.

## What the Characters Can Do

Nobody can arrest a baron, and the characters cannot fight twenty-five Knights. But the Baron's plan has exactly three weak points, and the characters can reach every one of them:

- **Stop the champion winning,** or have the match stopped on sacred ground.
- **Keep the Baron out of the room.**
- **Take the locket,** on the one night it lies on a table instead of round his neck, and keep it.

The third is the one the story leans toward, and the one that costs the most. A stolen locket turns a patient, smiling lord into a man who will burn a quarter of the city to get it back. What the characters do with that day decides how the scenario ends.
