# A Week Under the Dragon

Nobody in Purewater calls it an occupation. Everybody goes round.

Eight days before the Tourney, an advance detachment of a dozen Dragon Knights came into the city to open the Baron's camp and provision his ship. On the day the characters arrive, the column comes in behind them and doubles their number. The characters do not walk into a city bracing for an occupation. They walk into one that has been living with a small one for a week and is watching it treble in an afternoon.

## What It Looks Like

The **Dragon Knights** are a mercenary company of twenty-five, borderland raiders who have terrorised the Baron's marches for thirty years. They wear dragon-blazoned harness, and many carry fire-touched blades. The city calls them *the dragonshits*, quietly. They camp on a councillor's water-meadow two miles east of the walls, but they are not kept there.

They walk the Merchant's Quarter and the Forges in threes and fours. They take the middle of a bridge and let the city go round them in single file against the parapet. They eat and drink on the promise that *the Baron will settle*, and he settles late and short, so the stallholders have started pricing it in and everybody else's bread has gone up. They take boats off the water-stairs with the boatmen still in them, rowed wherever they like and left wherever they finish. Two boatmen have not come back. Six days before the Tourney, a dockhand named **Emmit Rill** was slow moving a barrow and had his jaw broken by a mailed fist in front of forty people. He is still not working.

Nobody is dead yet. Everybody expects somebody to be, and that expectation is the mood of the whole city.

Play the Knights as weather. They are in most outdoor scenes, mostly not fighting, always making the city smaller. A party that never draws a blade on them still spends the week watching people step off the pavement.

## The Standing Order

Seven days ago, the morning after the first complaints about the advance detachment reached the watch, the city watch received a written instruction from the Governor's council: the Baron's people are guests of the Crown's representative, and complaints against them are to be **recorded and referred, and not acted upon**, until the Tourney is over. It went out over Prince Emeric's signature, and three of his councillors got it there, none of them for the same reason.

- **Alderic Vantt** drafted it. He is warm, funny and the best host in the city, and two winters ago the Baron quietly covered four thousand of his debts and asked for nothing. The Dragon Knights are camped on his grass, and he would like the week to pass without an incident that has his name on it. He is bought, and he has not said so even to his wife.
- **Hesketh Pyle** seconded it. Two years ago the Baron's people made his son's killing of a man go away, and kept the deposition. He has agreed with everything ever since. He is frightened.
- **Maro Quist** carried the chamber for it, on the merits. He is seventy-one, incorruptible and formidable, and he was given true facts in exactly the right order. He is sincere, and he is wrong.

None of them is a cultist. None of them knows what the Baron is actually doing. That matters, because a party that comes at the council expecting a conspiracy will find three ordinary men and nothing to arrest.

**The watch is not corrupt.** It has been ordered, in writing, and every sergeant in the city knows exactly how that will read afterwards. **Sergeant Abel Weir** of the Isle post keeps the referral book: thirty-one entries and counting, four inches of complaints he has been ordered to record and not to act on. He can recite every one from memory. He is looking for somebody who can use the book without making him the man who disobeyed.

## What It Does to the City

The occupation presses on everybody differently, and each of them is a fuse.

**The Dredgers** work the water-stairs the Knights keep commandeering, and their leader, **Marda Blackwater**, has three hundred dock labourers who are angrier than she is, and no lawful way to settle it. She wants Rill paid, the two boatmen accounted for, and one Knight made to answer for something in front of a magistrate. If nobody gives her any of that, she has one lever left. On the eve of the Tourney she can stop the water-stairs, and with them every boat, barrel and competitor's kit in the city.

**Crowbill's** code is *no killing locals, no harming children, no touching the Lady's sites*, and the Knights are breaking the spirit of all three. He cannot be seen to enforce a code against armoured men without starting a war he would lose, and it is costing him standing every day. His collections come up short on the bridges the Knights stand on, because if the Knights do not have to pay, why should anybody?

**The Pearl** takes the worst of the nights and is the least able to complain, because complaining is bad for trade.

**The Swords of the Lake** answer to their order and not to the council, which makes **Temerach Nebulo** the only armed person in the city who could act against the Baron's men without disobeying anybody. She knows it. She is also holding an Archery entry she has spent four years earning.

## How It Ends, If Nobody Ends It

Left alone, the occupation tightens. Every unanswered incident teaches the city a little more about what it is now. Stallholders price in theft, and boatmen work the far stairs. Two Knights beat a man in front of a watch patrol that walks away. A woman is taken off the Pearl street, and the house that objects is burned. Then a local is killed in the open and nothing is done, and on that day Purewater stops being a city and starts being an occupied one, and the Dredgers move whether Marda wants them to or not.

What stops it is not force. It stops when an incident lands somewhere the standing order does not reach: the Prince in person, the Order, a Sword of the Lake, or the Tourney ground itself, where a di Teufel cannot afford a scene. The Baron does not want a riot two days before he walks into a room with the Crown's nephew. Push the city hard enough, in public, and the Baron himself has to rein in his own men. That is the characters' first real victory over him, and it does not require a single sword.
