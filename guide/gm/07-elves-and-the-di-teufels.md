# Elves and the di Teufels

The Baron's men stop elves on the bridges of Purewater and nobody else. That is not a habit of rough soldiers. Hatred of elves is a defining feature of di Teufel rule, and it has a reason underneath it that the family has never said aloud.

## Two Kinds of Elf

**Most elves are the elves everybody knows**: long-lived, graceful, at home in cities and on the border, and as ordinary a sight in Thornmere as dwarves. Temerach, Emmeralda, Ila and Gardwen are all elves of this kind, and so are the borderland elves the Knights have raided for thirty years. Any elf a player makes is one of them.

**The wild elves of the Deep Glades are something else.** They are made of growing things, more plant than flesh. They have almost no dealings with anybody outside the wood, and they are alien, strange and, in the end, far more powerful: they are shamans, and they walk among spirits as easily as other people walk down a street. At Drakesfall they were the only power in the world able to pull demons out of men at the scale of an army, and they broke the Dragon Kings with it.

**That is what the Movement fears.** Anybody who wants the old age back has to fear the people who ended it.

## Why the di Teufels Hate Elves

The persecution falls on ordinary elves, because ordinary elves are within reach. The family's propaganda blurs the two kinds on purpose, so that a seamstress in Ashwick answers for Drakesfall. It is also strategy. The old knowledge of how to pull a demon out of a living body, the Wild Line that Gardwen learned, came out of the deep wood through the borderland elves. Thirty years of raids on the border have cut that road.

- **To the Baron**, it is useful and it is personal. It gives him land, everything north and west of Emberhold; a scapegoat for every crowd he needs to move; the counter-knowledge broken; and revenge on the elven mage who saw him and got away.
- **On the Baron's march**, elves are outlawed and hunted, and the raids are policy.
- **In Purewater this week**, the Knights single elves out, and the watch writes it down and does nothing.
- **If the Baron wins**, a royal edict in the Prince's name goes out against elves and against the Swords of the Lake.
- **The twins are half-elven**, which is one more reason the Baron cannot let their claim stand.
- **Gabriel was chosen on purpose.** Elves hold a seated spirit better than anybody else, which is also why they make the best shamans.

Show it through acts, laws and who gets stopped on a bridge. Never through speeches.

## People Who Carry It Out

- **Dame Hesra Kell** is the Baron's Warden of the March. She wants order on the border and the Baron's thanks, and she keeps the bounty rolls. She was at the raid on Gardwen's steading, and she holds the road north in the first act of the Bones.
- **Master Edric Thale** wants to be the historian of the restored age, and he is a believer who has no idea what the Baron is really for. He writes the addresses Cassian Bree prints, and at the Readings he names the elves as the people who broke the age. Thorne knew him, years ago.
- **Sir Aldren Vaust** wants a lasting estate of his own, and the Wind Stair pass cleared of the elven teaching house that stands in it. He carries a stolen imperial funerary standard to a collector who wants the old age back.
- **Severin Rusk** wants a binding venture of his own, and buys elven grave goods, and the elven dead, to use as anchors.

## People Who Resist It

- **Temerach Nebulo**, the Swords' spymaster in Purewater, wants the Baron brought down, and has carried her right to accuse him for four years.
- **Gleinanion Othal** keeps the Remade Grove, and wants the Wild Line kept alive by teaching it. They taught Gardwen, they walk among spirits, and they are one of the very few people who still know the way into the deep wood.
- **Talarin Reedwake** wants the stolen dead returned and the families of the dead kept out of Rusk's reach, and knows exactly what binding does to a dead elf.
- **Serevan Ashleaf** keeps the Wind Stair teaching house that Vaust wants cleared, and wants his pupils safe and their stolen heritage back.

## And One Who Keeps His Head Down

**Corran Feyle** wants to be safe, and believes the way to be safe is to be quiet. He pays his subscription to the Ember Society so as to look harmless, and inside the elven community he argues against every kind of resistance. That makes him dangerous to his own people, and it makes him turnable.

## Reaching the Wild Elves

Nobody comes out of the deep wood to treat with anybody, and there is no envoy to find. The way in is through the bridge the raids have nearly destroyed, a borderland elf like Gleinanion who still knows the road, or through the spirits themselves. Going into the Deep Glades is an adventure of its own, and an alarming one: the wild elves and their spirits are the likeliest things in the realm to take a visitor and keep them.
