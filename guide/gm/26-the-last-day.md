# The Last Day

*The third day of the Tourney.* The Run, the closing, and one of three endings, chosen by where a small warm locket is at dawn.

**Read the board before you play a scene.** Last night's theft decides which road this day takes, and the three roads share almost nothing:

| Road | If | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| The Quiet Room | The locket was never stolen, or the theft failed | The Run and the closing. The Baron walks his champion into the room by the old rite and takes the Prince, and nobody in Purewater knows |
| The Burning | The locket was stolen, and Blau gets it back | The Baron burns the Lullwater to find it. Nus is betrayed and dies. The Prince summons the Baron to answer and is taken in that same room. Temerach makes the shot of her life, and it is not enough |
| The Breakout | The locket was stolen, Nus lives, and the characters keep it from Blau | The Lullwater burns, but the Baron goes into the evening without his leash, and the thing in the boy stands up |

Table: The Three Roads

## The Lullwater Burns

<!-- covers: the-lullwater-burns -->

*The Burning and the Breakout: only if the locket was taken.*

The Baron wakes at first light to an empty table, and the warm, reasonable, unhurried man the city has watched all week is gone. Without the locket he cannot walk into the room tonight, and four generations of work end at dusk unless he gets it back. He has no idea who has it, only that it went over the stern in the dark toward the north-east, where the thieves live. So he goes after it himself.

He comes off the ship at dawn with Blau and every Knight aboard, and the camp is roused behind him. He does not ask questions. He takes boats at Shilling Stairs at swordpoint and crosses to the Lullwater, and the first people he meets on the stair are killed for being on it. **He is a demonologist and a fire-mage, and he has stopped pretending not to be.** Doors burst inward. A man who will not say where the boy-thieves sleep is burned where he stands. He is not searching; he is making it unbearable for anybody to keep the thing that was stolen.

**The quarter fights back.** The Lullwater has no bridges, no horse can go into it, and four thousand people who know every roof live in it. The Dredgers come out of their hall with boathooks and grabs. Crowbill's people go up onto the roofs with slings. Women drop roof-tiles into the Gutter, and punts ram the Knights' boats on open water. There is blood on the planking, and running battles go up and down the Gutter and around the Still. If the stairs stopped on the eve, Marda's three hundred are already angry and close. A character in the Lullwater this morning is in a battle: run the Knights in threes, with the Dragon Knight Soldier statistics, and give the locals the roofs, the water and the numbers.

**And when the quarter will not give up the thief, the Baron burns it.** He calls fire into the tenements along the Gutter, and in a city of timber leaning over water it goes from roof to roof faster than anybody can row. Name what burns, and let the players see which places they know are gone: the Moist Oyster, the Dredgers' Hall, the drying-sheds on the Hook, Eel Bay. By noon there is a column of smoke over the north-east that the Prince can see from the Governor's box. **Play it so the characters can hear it from wherever they are, and do not cut away.**

**The Run is abandoned.** Nobody races through a city on fire.

## The Man in the Smoke at Shilling Stairs

<!-- covers: sinnit-sells-nus -->

*The Burning and the Breakout.*

The whole Lullwater knows somebody went over the Baron's stern last night and is hiding in it, and Sinnit knows who. Nus has been Randall's second pair of hands for years, and Sinnit has watched them both for seven. He knows Nus's boat, his sister's loft over Eel Bay, and the drying-shed on the Hook where he sleeps when he does not want to be found.

**If nobody has turned him,** he finds Blau in the smoke at Shilling Stairs and tells him: because it hurts Randall, because he is frightened, and because he thinks being useful to the men with fire will keep the fire off him. Blau goes straight there. He does not panic and is not fooled by much, and he finds Nus and kills him, quickly, without appearing to enjoy it, and brings the Baron his locket back. That is the road to the Burning. **This is the scene to intercept.** A party that has been watching Sinnit, or guesses who would sell Nus, has one chance to get between him and Blau, or to move Nus before Blau arrives.

**If Randall apologised to him,** then when Blau comes to the drying-shed, Sinnit is the one standing in the doorway, big and loud and announcing exactly what he is going to do: *"Nobody does that to our people."* Then he does it. He will not win. He buys Nus the minutes it takes to get into a boat, and that may be all the characters need. Whether he lives through it is up to the dice.

**This is a consequence, not a scheduled tragedy.** If the characters hid Nus, got him out of the city, drew the hunt onto themselves, or are holding the locket somewhere Blau cannot follow, honour it. The burning still happens, but Blau comes back empty-handed, and the Baron goes into the evening without his leash. That is the road to the Breakout.

## The Run

<!-- covers: the-run-across-the-town -->

*The Quiet Room only.*

If nothing is burning, the last event of the Tourney goes out through the Lists' water-gate at noon, and for two hours the whole city is the course. Competitors run the length of the North Quarter, over the Triumph Bridge to Caravan Square and back, down the Merchant's Quarter, along the Seaward docks, over the Fishmarket Bridge into the Shoals, across to the bridgeless Lullwater by whatever means they can find, and back over open water to the temple steps, on foot and by boat. No route is laid down and no help is forbidden: half the city is a course and the other half is a crowd.

**For two hours, Purewater has no crowd control at all.** The watch is holding lines along a route, not patrolling districts. The Knights are strung out along it with nothing to do. Every bridge and stair is packed with cheering people, and nobody can move against the flow. That is the best cover the characters will ever get, or the reason they cannot reach somewhere in time, and it should be both in the same hour. This is the Quiet Room's last chance: the locket is on the Baron's chain, the champion has won, and nothing stands between Hanzo and that door except what the characters do in these two hours. If nobody uses it, it is a magnificent afternoon that the party watched happen, and the Baron's people move freely under exactly the same cover. At dusk the competitors come back to the steps, the Prince takes his place, and the closing begins.

## The Room

<!-- covers: the-private-audience -->

*The Quiet Room and the Burning: only if the Baron has the locket at dusk.*

This is the point of the entire hundred years. Prince Emeric is two-and-twenty, well-meaning and badly advised, and he has already been used once this week, when three of his councillors got his name onto the order that silenced the watch. Cailan is the jar, and Emeric is the destination. In that small stone room behind the Governor's box, the Baron casts Draw Forth on the boy and Seat the Bound on the Prince, and the anchor hangs round his neck.

**By the old rite**, on the Quiet Room's road, the champion won the Single Combat yesterday, and tonight, when the Run comes back to the temple steps and the Prince takes his place, the Baron walks his champion through that door, alone and unarmed, by a form older than the Governor's office. It is quiet, and nobody in the city knows anything has happened.

**Summoned to answer**, on the Burning's road, the Prince could see the smoke from the Governor's box by noon, and by mid-afternoon the reports came in: a foreign lord is burning a quarter of the city on the last day of a sacred festival. Emeric does the brave, decent and stupid thing. He summons the Baron to answer to him in private, and the Baron comes with his champion at his side, smoke on his clothes and the locket back round his neck, while the councillors stand outside the door. It is the same room, reached the other way, and Hanzo could not have arranged it better.

**The Prince comes out.** He is gracious and composed, and right-handed in a way nobody thinks to notice. Thuban Eta is wearing the Crown's nephew. Cailan is carried out behind him, emptied and barely breathing. After the old rite, the Baron goes home with a prince in his pocket and begins to move on the throne, and the scenario ends with the characters knowing exactly what has happened and nobody in the kingdom who would believe them. After the summons, the Prince steps out onto the steps above the burning city and sides with the Baron in front of everybody (*the thieves' quarter rose, and the Baron restored order*), and across the arena, an elf has a bow in her hand.

## The Impossible Shot

<!-- covers: the-impossible-shot -->

*The Burning; and the Quiet Room only if Temerach has learned what the champion is.*

The Prince on the steps, siding with the Baron above a city that is still burning. The Baron beside him, smoke on his coat and the locket back on his chain. Four thousand people on the slopes and in the stands. For Temerach, the calculation inverts. A bound spirit in a stolen boy, and now in the Crown's own nephew, is desecration, and the goddess's own business, and a Sword may act on that without asking anybody.

She names him across the whole width of the arena, in front of the city, as the King's officer's last lawful act. Then she draws.

**It is a shot nobody else alive could make**: distance, crosswind, smoke, the angle of the steps and a crowd in between. Roll it, at Formidable, and it hits, because she is who she is. And it is not enough. The arrow takes him high, through the shoulder or the side; he goes down on the steps, and gets up again, and he is alive. Then the Knights are coming across the arena for her, and the crowd breaks.

She is an outlaw from the instant the arrow leaves the string, and she knew it before she drew. **She flees with the characters**, or the characters flee with her, over the water, through the smoke, and out of the city by whatever route is left: Crowbill's canals, a Dredger's boat out through the Mouth, or the open road. It costs them something to get out, and it should. She does not thank them, and she does not explain.

## The Thing in the Boy Stands Up

<!-- covers: thuban-breaks-out -->

*The Breakout: only if the characters kept Nus alive through the burning and the locket is still out of the Baron's hands at dusk.* It is the ending the players have to earn.

The Baron has spent the day burning the Lullwater and has not found it. At dusk he has no leash, and for the first time in ten years nobody renewed the seat last night with the anchor in hand. **The characters cannot command Thuban**, because they are not demonologists, and they cannot break the locket. What they can do is keep it away from the Baron, and that is enough, because Thuban is no longer obeying anybody.

Somewhere on the ship, or on the competitors' ground where the Baron has dragged his champion to keep him close, the thing in Cailan stands up: a knight of the Sixth Wing with a thousand years of courtesy and ten years of grievance, in a seventeen-year-old's body, with the leash off. **He fights his way out through the Dragon Knights.** Play it as the dragon wars come back for one evening: unarmed at first, then with a Knight's own sword, through the deck, over the side and up the pier. He is looking for one person, the woman who came aboard two nights ago and spoke to him in her brother's name, and when he finds the characters he falls in beside them as though he has always been there. Temerach covers the retreat from the far side of the water with a bow, shot after shot. She has seen what came out of that ship, and she knows now what was under the helm, and whether she meant to or not, she is leaving with them. They get out of Purewater. It should cost them a boat, a route or a friend holding a stair, but they get out.

**And he is still not her brother.** The locket only ever tied Thuban to the Baron. What holds him in the world is a box of bones in a tower on the border estate, four days' hard riding away, behind the Baron's own men. Until those bones are found and the rite is said, Thuban stays where he is, and Gabriel stays under him.

**The last scene of the adventure** is Thuban telling Gardwen where the bones are, with perfect thousand-year-old courtesy, in her brother's voice, because under pressure his manners get better rather than worse, and asking, very politely, whether she would be so kind as to help him die. Do not resolve it, and do not let anybody say the words tonight. Magda's dream said *lay the dragon warrior to rest*, and now she knows where.

## The Captain's Arithmetic

<!-- covers: tat-counts-the-cost -->

That night, whatever has happened, Tat Atarer does arithmetic in the open: men lost, a contract that keeps expanding, and a growing certainty about what was behind the inner doors. Only a character who has been cultivating him sees it. His soldiers are hired, not sworn, and that is the Baron's structural weakness. Every time the characters have cost the Dragon Knights something this week (a man in the water, a broken contract, a burning quarter that will get a company hanged), Tat has added it up. If the sum has grown long enough, he will not fight the Baron. He will march his company home and leave the Baron with six men on a ship, and on the road out of the city, a captain who does not pursue is worth a great deal.
