# The City, Island by Island

The map below is canon: where a description and the map disagree, the map wins. Each district sheet that follows enlarges part of it, with a numbered key. Crosses mark places, discs mark taverns and amenities, and squares mark posts of the city watch. **Nothing secret is lettered on any district sheet,** so you can put them on the table, and you should. "How do we get there?" is a real question in Purewater, and the answer is always a bridge, a stair, a ferry or a fare.

## Getting About

Three facts shape every journey, and players learn them fastest by running into them.

**No bridge crosses the Grand Canal.** The North Quarter and the Merchant's Quarter meet only by ferry: the Palazzo Ferry and the Guild Ferry.

**No bridge reaches the Lullwater or Temple Isle.** The first is reached by boat from Shilling Stairs, at a shilling a crossing, and somebody always sees who paid. The second is reached only by the temple's own ferries, deliberately, so that the profane city cannot simply walk onto holy ground.

**The Triumph Bridge is the only way in by land.** Everything else comes by water.

A character who needs to cross water hires a boatman, finds a ferry, steals a punt, or swims. Make the choice matter. A boatman is a witness; a stolen punt is a crime in a city where everybody knows whose boat that is; a swimmer arrives wet, and in the eastern yards near the Baron's ship the water is warm in a way it should not be. Every local can swim. Almost no Dragon Knight can.

## The Pearl

The pleasure island lies off the North Quarter, lamplit and lovely over dark water: a long island with a lobe at each end, its sheltered south shore one long waterfront called **Pearl Quay**. The houses string their lamps along it like pearls. The **Siren's Call**, grandest of them, stands at the foot of the **Lantern Bridge**, the island's front door. The **Sylph's Embrace** is next along, then **Marisette's** house, the **Mother of Pearl**, plain and signless in the middle of the quay. The poorer **Promise of Heaven**, where Randall was raised, sits at the south-western end by the Old Span and the Hush Bridge, and the **Lily Garden** stands among the trees of the western lobe. To the east, the **Widow's Bridge** crosses to a walled islet and a house that has been shuttered for two hundred years.

At dusk the doorman of the Mother of Pearl carries a flame from the Widow's lamp down the quay, every house lights its lamps from it, and then the Lantern Bridge is lit, post by post, and must not go dark before dawn. Old Nan sells eel pies on the quay and knows the name of everybody who passes and says all of them. The watch box at the North Quarter end of the Lantern Bridge is unmanned after dark: the Pearl keeps its own order.

**This week,** the scenario opens here. Santo buys Emmeralda's evening at the Sylph's Embrace on the first night. On the second day he comes back for her by boat if the working took, Sinnit spreads his poison from stair to wine-shop, and at dusk Santo loses his temper on the quay with fire. By that night, every door on the island is shut to the Dragon Knights.

## The Lullwater

Off to the north-east, across open water from the Tournament Ground, reached by no bridge, lies the city's poor-and-criminal quarter: the narrowest, most crooked canals in Purewater, and tenements leaning over them until they almost touch overhead. Its name sounds gentle, a lull and a still water, and that is the joke of it. Thieves, dredgers, smugglers and dockhands all live crammed into the same drowned maze, and the quarter likes being hard to reach.

One straight canal, **the Gutter**, runs down the island's spine, crossed by a handful of plank bridges. It is the only water in the quarter wide enough for two boats. Just west of it, at the heart of the island, lies **the Still**, a small round pool of dead water ringed by tenements, with the **Moist Oyster** tavern on its north-western edge. Boats from High Isle put in at **Shilling Stairs** on the west shore, where the boatmen drink at the Lamprey while you do your business. The **Dredgers' Hall** stands on the south shore. **Crowbill** keeps no house: for thirty years he has sat on the flat lead roof over the cookshop ovens beside the Gutter, the warmest place in Purewater in October, eating out of a paper. The watch post here has its door nailed shut. The watch comes over only to fish out the dead.

It is easy to get lost here and dangerous to look lost, as Conall learns on his first evening. If the locket is stolen, this is where the Baron comes looking for it, and on the last day this is the quarter that fights back, and burns.

## The Shoals

The seaward jaw of the Mouth: a low, ragged island at the city's south-east corner, facing the open sea and taking its weather. The middle is the **Fishmarket**: stalls, fish-sheds, barrels and boats hauled up on the stones. At the foot of the **Fishmarket Bridge** from the Forges is the **Fishmarket Stair**, the busiest water-stair in the city. Along the south-western arm run the **Low Canals**, four thousand people living on plank walkways and stilt-houses, and at its tip, the smugglers' jetties. The net-lofts and ice-houses stand on the east side; Crowbill sits in the back of Marn's ice-house with a lamp at two in the morning. The Dredgers are strongest here, and the Harbor Masters' tower across the water can see the front jetties and not the back ones.

**This week,** the Knights' commandeered boats come off these stairs, and the boatmen who do not come back are Shoals men. If nobody answers the Dredgers' grievance, it is at the head of the Fishmarket Stair that Marda Blackwater stands at dawn on the eve of the Tourney, entirely reasonable, while not one boat moves in the city behind her.

## The Lists and Temple Isle

At the east end of the North Quarter stands the walled **Tournament Ground**: a six-sided list of raked sand inside a rope, ringed by tiered timber stands for four thousand people, with the **Governor's box** built into the western stand and hung with lake-green cloth. Behind the box is a small stone room with one door from the box stair and one to the outside: the room where the champion is presented, alone and unarmed. The **Long Butts**, where the Archery is shot, run along the ground's north side. The **water-gate** in the east wall, with broad steps down into the lake, is where the Run goes out. The bookmakers outside the east wall have the Baron's champion odds-on, and nobody likes it.

Just north, across a narrow water and in plain sight of the stands, lies **Temple Isle**: one walled precinct around the **Sacred Confluence**, the great round pool where the purest lake-water wells up. The temple stands at the north end, cloisters run down both sides, and broad **water-steps** go down into the lake at the south, facing the Lists between two towers. The Lady's blessing and the Washing are held on those steps.

What makes this the right place for an ending is that **everything is visible from everywhere.** The steps, the box, the sand and the Lullwater's skyline across the water can all be seen from every seat. The presentation, the Archery, the Single Combat and the closing all happen here, in front of the whole city.

## Caravan Square

Purewater's only land approach, on the western shore: a walled waterfront of staging-yards and warehouses where the Caravan Road ends. Caravans unload in the **wagon park**, beasts stand in the **beast yard**, and everything is broken down into boats, in public, slowly, one crate at a time. The road ends at the **Gate of Triumph**, which everybody calls the Land Gate, and the **Triumph Bridge** crosses the landward channel to the customs house on the Merchant's Quarter shore. A **Ferry Stage** at the square's southern end serves everything that goes by water.

**This week,** the Baron's column comes through here on the first day, with the litter in the middle of it, and three Knights stand in the middle of the bridge eating while the city edges round them.

## The Forges and the Docks

High Isle's industrial island: foundries and boatyards, many of them dwarf-run, coal and ore heaped in open yards, chimneys trailing smoke over the water day and night. The **Forge Tower** stands over the south shore, and below it, at a gated private pier, lies the **DragonBarge**, steaming where its hull meets the water. The corruption is worst here, and the heat is felt most in these eastern yards. A few streets west of the Foundries, **Orsk & Daughter**, goldsmiths, cut the mark on Santo's knife to a servant's drawing and kept the drawing, as they keep all of them. The Seaward Quarter's docks run along the south shore, a comb of piers with the round **Harbour Master's Tower** at the tip of the longest mole. The watch's busiest post has quietly stopped sending patrols anywhere near the barge pier.

## The North Quarter

The long northern island facing the open lake. Its western half is houses, rich toward the Pearl and middling eastward; its eastern half is the official quarter, with the **Governor's Palazzo**, the **Hall of Justice** and the **Barracks** around Palace Square. **The Processional**, a wide paved avenue lined with the bigger houses, sweeps down from the Pearl's bridges toward the Grand Canal. Competitors' families stay at the Gilded Swan. Two ferries cross the canal: the Palazzo Ferry and the Guild Ferry.

## The Merchant's Quarter

The South Quarter, where the Triumph Bridge brings the caravan trade ashore and the city's money is made, weighed and quietly steered. The walled **Market Square** fills the middle, ringed by the guildhalls of the Merchants, Coopers and Dyers. The **Watercrafters' Guild** stands on the west shore with its own slipway. The palazzi of the merchant princes line the Grand Canal, each with its own water-gate, and three of them belong to the councillors who carried the standing order: **Palazzo Vantt** and **Palazzo Pyle** at the west end, and **Palazzo Quist**, the oldest, further east. Under the arcade on the square, the moneychangers are refusing di Teufel credit notes, loudly.

## The Realm Beyond

Purewater is one city in a larger kingdom. King Ostmar rules from Caldreth, far away and content, surrounded by men who have been the Baron's friends for thirty years. The Baron's border estate, and the tower where Thuban's bones lie, is four days' hard riding from the city. Every ending of this scenario points there.
