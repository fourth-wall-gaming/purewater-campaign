# The House of di Teufel

The Baron's household is small, and everybody in it is afraid of something different. Full statistics for every person in this chapter are in *Statistics*.

## Baron Hanzo di Teufel

*Fifties, well made, silver at the temples, black cloth that cost more than a fishing boat. Nothing about him is ornamented.*

Hanzo di Teufel is charming, and that is what makes him frightening. He is warm, interested in people, good company, and delighted when somebody says something clever. He remembers what your mother was ill with and asks after her a year later. He was a younger son of nothing, and he learned very early that the world opens for men who are loved. Some of that he learned from the thing his father seated in him at twelve (see *What the Dragons Were*). Thousands of people have loved him, and none of them has ever known what he is.

He believes in one thing, and that is himself. The Dragon Kings, the Movement's faith and the fire he binds are tools, and so is everybody who serves him. He means to depose the King and rule Thornmere because he cannot imagine anyone else fit to. He wants everybody in the room to like him, because nobody searches a man they are fond of, and because he cannot live without admiration.

**Play him as the nicest man the characters meet all week.** He never raises his voice and never threatens. When he is cornered he does not feel fear. He feels that his hospitality has failed, and he corrects it by becoming more generous. When he offers a character a chair, a drink, an introduction or a purse, their position is getting worse, and the kinder he is, the worse it gets. He has three generations of patience behind him, and never once in his life has he been seen doing anything he would need to explain.

> "You must forgive my son. He was born after I had stopped being poor, and it shows."

**This week** he wants the champion to win and the room to be his, and he wants nobody close enough to Cailan to notice that the boy does not answer to his own name. He keeps his champion aboard his ship, in a laced litter, behind six inner guards, and renews the binding every night. He leaves the street to Blau and Tat Atarer. Cost and scandal do not deter him, because he can survive both. If anybody comes close, he would rather move the boy somewhere worse than let the questions continue. If the Pearl shuts its doors on his men, he leans on the Prince's councillors rather than on Marisette.

**The locket changes him.** If he loses it, the patient, smiling lord the city has watched all week is gone, and in his place is a demonologist and fire-mage who will burn a quarter of the city by noon to get it back. That is the only time anybody in Purewater sees the real man, and it should frighten your players.

**His weaknesses all come from the same place:**

- He holds the Movement's believers in contempt and cannot do without them. If they ever see the contempt, they turn on him.
- People flatter him instead of briefing him, so bad news reaches him late or never.
- He is certain he can master a dragon-knight. That is pride, and he has no plan for being wrong.
- Vanity moves him. Bribes and threats do not.
- He cannot let a humiliation stand. If he loses the Tourney, he remembers every face that watched.

He is a magic-user of a forbidden school, with Charm Person, Hold Person and Sleep among his ordinary spells and the whole binding art besides: Open the Channel, Seat the Bound, Anchor, Reinforce the Seat and Draw Forth. He has never fought anybody himself, and in a straight fight he is a nobleman with a broadsword. He never has to be in a straight fight.

## Santo di Teufel

*Nineteen, dressed beautifully and always slightly too warm for it. Rings. A good sword he cannot really use.*

The heir is enormous fun for about an hour. He is loud, generous and funny when things are going his way, and he spends money on people so that he can watch their faces. In his own account of himself he is not wicked. He is wronged, constantly, by everybody. When he is refused, the vagueness burns off him and he becomes horribly precise, listing exactly what will happen to your house, your girls and your boat, the details getting smaller as the panic gets bigger.

> "Do you know, I have never once had to ask for anything twice. Not once. It is a very strange feeling."

**This week** he wants Emmeralda, kept on the barge and glad of it, and he goes about getting her the way a spoiled young man does:

1. He buys her evening, giving no name.
2. He tries to make her willing with his stolen scroll.
3. If the working takes, he tries to walk her out of the house that night.
4. If he is stopped, he comes back for her by boat the next day.
5. If anything stops him then, he loses his temper in the street.

Fear works on him: exposure, a blade at his own throat, or his father learning what he has done before he can dress it up as a success. Taking the knife away from him does not make him give up the girl. It makes him frantic, which is far more dangerous.

He has a few cantrips of fire, Burning Hands and Magic Missile among them, and his rapier. When he panics, he does not run. He takes hold of whoever is nearest.

## Blau

*Thin, white-skinned, red-eyed, old burn-scarring down one side. Plain harness, kept well, no colours.*

The ranks call him **Commander Vask**; everybody else calls him Blau. He runs the Dragon Knights day to day and handles the Baron's dirty work, and his real job is keeping the heir's appetites from costing the family anything. He is so unhurried that other people find it physically uncomfortable to stand near him. He does not drink, boast, complain or take pleasure in winning. He is patient the way stone is patient, and he is never bored. He notices lies, he sees through most disguises, and he does not panic. He pays people off and hauls Santo home by the collar, and when containment fails, he escalates fast and brings Knights.

> "Come away now, my lord. You are being watched by people who can count."

**Nothing has ever been seen to corner him,** and your players should learn what that costs them. Whatever put the burns on him also took away any belief that noise achieves anything. His loyalty is to finishing things, and the Baron happens to be the job. For years he has been studying his employer's work with the settled intention of walking away with it. He has told nobody, because telling is how people lose things.

**This week** he cleans up after Santo. At the presentation he watches the crowd, sees a woman in it stop breathing, and doubles the watch on the ship. He buys a canal-rat's secret on the eve of the Tourney, and when the locket goes, he is the one who gets it back. He is good at all of it and enjoys none of it.

**Evidence that the mess is bigger than Santo turns him.** Show him that the young master has been dealing with Hell in a way that endangers the Baron's own plans, and his loyalty tilts toward the father and away from the son. **If Santo dies,** his job becomes vengeance, and he becomes the characters' most persistent pursuer.

## Tat Atarer

*Hard-faced professional in harness that fits properly. Fifties. Unhurried.*

The captain of the Dragon Knights is a mercenary of thirty years' standing whose whole reputation rests on bringing companies home. He fights for pay, and his view of the Baron is that *the Baron pays for our swords, not our opinions.* He is flat, courteous and without any theatre at all. He deals with his own men in front of you and does not apologise for the interruption. He tells you at once which part of what you want is not his to give, and people take that for hostility when it is only efficiency. He has lost two companies in his life, neither of them in a battle; both were lost to a bad month in a town exactly like this one. He can see the standing order rotting the discipline he has spent his life building, and he has said so, once, to the wrong person.

> "I have never lost a company to a battle. I have lost two to a bad month in a town."

**He is the weak point in the Baron's wall,** and the players will not notice him unless you let his reluctance show. Show him that the contract is going to cost him men or reputation, or that he is guarding something that will get the company hanged, and he becomes negotiable in a way no other Dragon Knight is. He will not fight the Baron. He will march his company home and leave the Baron with six men on a ship. On the last day, a captain who does not pursue is worth a great deal to people on the road out of the city.

## Cailan, Who Is Gabriel

*Seventeen and far too thin for the harness. Dragon-scale growing out of the skin of his throat and jaw with no strap or edge, the flesh around it raised and weeping. A fresh dressing inside the right elbow, over years of older scarring.*

He was seven when he was taken, and since then he has been a room somebody else lives in. Most of the time the characters meet the knight wearing his face and doing what he is told. But Gabriel has survived. He is stubborn and funny and fond of his sister, whom he used to call Bird, and he was never alone down there.

For ten years Thuban kept him company. The knight took the fighting and the rites so that the boy need not be present for them, and in the hours between he taught him:

- the code of the Sixth Wing, above all that law outranks orders;
- the old court tongue, spoken and written, so that Gabriel can read the first di Teufel's texts;
- every form of the sword, in a body that has never trained;
- and patience.

The knight is his father in every way that counts. Gabriel surfaces when the Baron is not watching, and he has had ten years to learn to let the Baron see nothing. In front of anybody else, his own name or exhaustion brings him up the way a swimmer comes up under ice, for a second or two, and he goes back down before anyone can notice.

> "I'm still —"

That is all he gets out, and it is the most valuable line in any scene he is in. Do not give him a whole sentence until the end.

## Thuban Eta

*Not a body. A weight behind a boy's eyes, and a fighting style a thousand years out of date, executed perfectly by arms too thin to do it.*

Thuban was a knight of the Sixth Wing of a court that no longer exists, drowned by his own company and buried without his rites, and awake through all of it. In life he carried no demon. He was an unbound knight-commander, sane where the bound men were not. He is courtly, formal, unhurried and very tired. Ten years of having nothing to do but listen have made him the best listener alive. He is ancient, and he is owed something, and he is neither evil nor friendly. He considers what has been done to him and to the boy a breach of law, which to him is worse than cruelty, because law can be argued and cruelty can only be resisted. He holds a cup right-handed.

The one thing he has chosen in a thousand years is the boy. He raised Gabriel below the waterline, and he loves him. Laying Thuban to rest gives Gardwen her brother back and takes away Gabriel's father, and both of them know it.

> "You will want to sit. What I have to tell you is long and I have had some time to arrange it."

**Under pressure, his manners get better.** When things are worst, his courtesy becomes older and more elaborate, a vanished court speaking through a seventeen-year-old, and it is unbearable to be in the room for. He wants to be heard by somebody who knows what a burial rite is for. He cannot leave while the bones hold him, and he cannot disobey while the Baron holds the locket. If the leash ever comes off, he will not serve the people who took it either. He serves the old codes, and the first of them is that he is owed a proper death.

## Orrin Sculle

*Fifties, heavy in the forearms, sleeves permanently rolled. Smells of liniment and lamp oil. Moves around a small dark space without needing to look at it.*

The champion's keeper has fed, dressed, washed and walked the boy for ten years, and talked to him every night while he did it, softly and without stopping, the way you talk to a horse or to somebody very ill. He worked out long ago that the boy is still in there, and he has seen Gabriel surface when the Baron was not watching. He has never told a living soul, because the man who says that out loud gets replaced by a man who does not care. He knows which hand Gabriel reaches with. He is not stupid, not cruel and not at all brave, and he knows perfectly well that ten years of kindness in that hold make him complicit.

> "I have had him since he was seven. Whatever you think I am, I am the one who knows how he takes his water."

He will never help anybody openly. But he sleeps across the champion's door, and on the right night he may sleep less soundly than usual. The first character who gets aboard the ship, or near the champion by any other route, meets him, and what happens next is his decision. See *Arrows and the Ship*.

## The Dragon Knights

The Dragon Knights are twenty-five borderland raiders who have terrorised the Baron's marches for thirty years, in dragon-blazoned harness, many with fire-touched blades. They keep the order's own ranks:

- Ember Recruits, Flame Soldiers, Smoke Lieutenants and Charred Captains;
- the **Ash Lords**, each of whom carries a seated Wanting of rage and loses himself to it a little more every year;
- and the **Dragon Circle**, the Baron's six-man inner guard, who serve aboard the ship and are never seen in the streets.

They are disciplined and thoroughly violent, and they have been told the watch will not touch them. Their worst enemies are the survivors of their old raids, and Gardwen is one of those. The raids were policy: on the Baron's march, elves are outlawed and hunted, and thirty years of burning has cut the old road between the border elves and the deep wood. Binding a man into an Ash Lord takes years. Severin Kade's fire-in-flesh would make one in a night, and that is Kade's purpose: a man who means to hold a capital needs an army.

**Run them in threes and fours.** They take bridges, bottles and boats, and they mostly do not fight, because nobody makes them. Every Knight in Purewater can fight, and almost none of them can swim. Use the Dragon Knight Soldier statistics for the rank and file, and the Ash Lord for anybody the Baron trusts.
