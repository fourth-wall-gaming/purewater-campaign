# The House of di Teufel

The Baron's household is small, and everybody in it is afraid of something different. Full statistics for every person in this chapter are in *Statistics*.

## Baron Hanzo di Teufel

*Fifties, well made, silver at the temples, black cloth that cost more than a fishing boat. Nothing about him is ornamented.*

The horror of Hanzo di Teufel is that he is genuinely nice. He is warm, and not faking it. He is interested in people, good company, and delighted when somebody says something clever. He remembers what your mother was ill with and asks after her a year later, and he means it. He was a younger son of nothing, and he learned very early that the world opens for men who are loved. He has been loved by thousands of people, none of whom has ever known what he is, and he wants to be liked by everybody in the room, because nobody searches a man they are fond of.

**Play him as the nicest man the characters meet all week.** He never raises his voice and never threatens. He does not experience being cornered as fear; he experiences it as a failure of hospitality, and corrects it by becoming more generous. When he offers a character a chair, a drink, an introduction, a purse, their position is getting worse, and the kinder he is, the worse it gets. He has a hundred years of patience behind him, three generations of it inherited, and never once in his life has he been seen doing anything he would need to explain.

> "You must forgive my son. He was born after I had stopped being poor, and it shows."

**This week** he wants the champion to win and the room to be his, and he wants nobody close enough to Cailan to notice that the boy does not answer to his own name. He keeps his champion aboard his ship, in a laced litter, behind six inner guards, and renews the binding every night. He leaves the street to Blau and Tat Atarer. He is not deterred by cost or by scandal; both are survivable. If anybody comes close, he would rather move the boy somewhere worse than let the questions continue. If the Pearl shuts its doors on his men, he leans on the Prince's councillors rather than on Marisette.

**What changes him** is the locket. Lose it, and the patient, smiling lord the city has watched all week simply vanishes, and what comes out instead is a demonologist and a fire-mage who will burn a quarter of the city by noon to get it back. That is the only time anybody in Purewater sees the real man, and it should frighten the table.

He is a magic-user of a forbidden school, with Charm Person, Hold Person and Sleep among his ordinary spells and the whole binding art besides: Open the Channel, Seat the Bound, Anchor, Reinforce the Seat and Draw Forth. He has never personally fought anybody, and in a straight fight he is a nobleman with a broadsword. He never has to be in a straight fight.

## Santo di Teufel

*Nineteen, dressed beautifully and always slightly too warm for it. Rings. A good sword he cannot really use.*

The heir is enormous fun for about an hour. He is loud, generous and funny when things are going his way, and spends money on people to watch their faces. He is not wicked in his own account of himself; he is wronged, constantly, by everybody. When he is refused, the vagueness burns off him and he becomes horribly precise: an itemised account of exactly what will happen to your house, your girls and your boat, the details getting smaller as the panic gets bigger.

> "Do you know, I have never once had to ask for anything twice. Not once. It is a very strange feeling."

**This week** he wants Emmeralda, kept on the barge and glad of it, and he goes about getting her in the order a spoiled young man does these things. First he buys her evening, with no name. Then he tries to make her willing with his stolen scroll. If the working takes, he tries to walk her out of the house that night, and if he is stopped, he comes back for her by boat the next day. If anything stops him, he loses his temper in the street. Fear works on him: exposure, a blade at his own throat, or his father learning what he has done before he can dress it up as a success. Taking the knife away from him does not make him give up the girl. It makes him frantic, which is far more dangerous.

He has a few cantrips of fire, Burning Hands and Magic Missile among them, and his rapier. When he panics, he does not run; he takes hold of whoever is nearest.

## Blau

*Thin, white-skinned, red-eyed, old burn-scarring down one side. Plain harness, kept well, no colours.*

The ranks call him **Commander Vask**; everybody else calls him Blau. He runs the Dragon Knights day to day and handles the Baron's dirty work, and his real job is keeping the heir's appetites from costing the family anything. He is unhurried to a degree other people find physically uncomfortable. He does not drink, boast, complain, or take pleasure in winning. He is patient the way stone is patient, and he is never bored. He notices lies. He is not easily fooled by a disguise, and he does not panic. He pays people off, hauls Santo home by the collar, and when containment fails, he escalates fast and brings Knights.

> "Come away now, my lord. You are being watched by people who can count."

**Play him as a man with no breaking point,** and let the table find out what that costs them. Nothing has ever been observed to corner him. Whatever put the burns on him also took away his belief that noise achieves anything. He is not loyal to the Baron; he is loyal to finishing things. Secretly, he has been studying his employer's work for years with the settled intention of walking away with it, and he has told nobody, because telling is how people lose things.

**This week,** he cleans up after Santo. He watches the crowd at the presentation, where he sees a woman in it stop breathing, and he doubles the watch on the ship. He buys a canal-rat's secret on the eve of the Tourney, and when the locket goes, he is the one who gets it back. He is good at all of it and enjoys none of it. **What turns him** is evidence that the mess is bigger than Santo: show him that the young master has been dealing with Hell in a way that endangers the Baron's own plans, and his loyalty tilts toward the father and away from the son. **If Santo dies,** his job becomes vengeance, and he becomes the characters' most persistent pursuer.

## Tat Atarer

*Hard-faced professional in harness that fits properly. Fifties. Unhurried.*

The captain of the Dragon Knights is a mercenary of thirty years' standing whose whole reputation rests on bringing companies home. He fights for pay, not for the Baron: *the Baron pays for our swords, not our opinions.* He is flat, courteous and entirely without theatre. He deals with his own men in front of you and does not apologise for the interruption. He tells you at once which part of what you want is not his to give, which people mistake for hostility and is actually efficiency. He has lost two companies in his life, and neither to a battle; both to a bad month in a town exactly like this one. He can see the standing order rotting the discipline he has spent his life building, and he has said so, once, to the wrong person.

> "I have never lost a company to a battle. I have lost two to a bad month in a town."

**He is the crack in the Baron's wall,** and the players will not notice him unless you let his reluctance show. Show him that the contract is going to cost him men or reputation, or that he is guarding something that will get the company hanged, and he becomes negotiable in a way no other Dragon Knight is. He will not fight the Baron. He will march his company home and leave the Baron with six men on a ship, and on the last day, a captain who does not pursue is worth a great deal to people on the road out of the city.

## Cailan, Who Is Gabriel

*Seventeen and far too thin for the harness. Dragon-scale growing out of the skin of his throat and jaw with no strap or edge, the flesh around it raised and weeping. A fresh dressing inside the right elbow, over years of older scarring.*

He was seven when he was taken, and he has been a room somebody else lives in ever since. What you mostly meet is an absence wearing his face and doing what it is told. But what is left of Gabriel is stubborn and funny and fond of his sister, whom he used to call Bird, and none of those things has had anywhere to go in ten years. Exhaustion surfaces him, or his own name: he comes up the way a swimmer comes up under ice, for a second or two, with no time to do anything with it, and is pulled back down.

> "I'm still —"

That is all he gets out, and it is the most valuable line in any scene he is in. Never give him a whole sentence until the end.

## Thuban Eta

*Not a body. A weight behind a boy's eyes, and a fighting style a thousand years out of date, executed perfectly by arms too thin to do it.*

A knight of the Sixth Wing of a court that no longer exists, drowned by his own company and buried without his rites, and awake for the whole of it. He is courtly, formal, unhurried and profoundly tired. Ten years of having nothing to do but listen have made him the best listener alive. He is not evil and he is not friendly. He is ancient, and he is owed something. He considers what has been done to him and to the boy a breach of law rather than a cruelty, which is worse, because law can be argued and cruelty can only be resisted. He holds a cup right-handed.

> "You will want to sit. What I have to tell you is long and I have had some time to arrange it."

**Play his manners getting better under pressure.** When things are worst, his courtesy becomes older and more elaborate, a vanished court speaking through a seventeen-year-old, and it is unbearable to be in the room for. He wants to be heard by somebody who knows what a burial rite is for. He cannot leave while the bones hold him, and he cannot disobey while the Baron holds the locket. If the leash ever comes off, he will not serve the people who took it, either. He serves the old codes, and the first of them is that he is owed a proper death.

## Orrin Sculle

*Fifties, heavy in the forearms, sleeves permanently rolled. Smells of liniment and lamp oil. Moves around a small dark space without needing to look at it.*

The champion's keeper has fed, dressed, washed and walked the boy for ten years, and talked to him every single night while he did it, quietly and continuously, the way you talk to a horse or to somebody very ill. He worked out long ago that the boy is still in there. He has never said so to a living soul, because the man who says that out loud gets replaced by a man who does not care. He knows which hand Gabriel reaches with. He is not stupid, not cruel and not remotely brave, and he knows perfectly well that ten years of kindness in that hold make him complicit rather than kind.

> "I have had him since he was seven. Whatever you think I am, I am the one who knows how he takes his water."

He is the door to the boy. The first character who gets aboard the ship, or near the champion by any other route, meets him, and what happens next is decided by his own heart, not theirs. See *Arrows and the Ship*.

## The Dragon Knights

Twenty-five borderland raiders who have terrorised the Baron's marches for thirty years, in dragon-blazoned harness, many with fire-touched blades. They keep the order's own ranks: Ember Recruits, Flame Soldiers, Smoke Lieutenants, Charred Captains, the **Ash Lords** who have undergone partial fire-binding, and the **Dragon Circle**, the Baron's six-man inner guard, who serve aboard the ship and are never seen in the streets. They are disciplined and thoroughly violent, and they have been told the watch will not touch them. Their worst enemies are the survivors of their old raids, and one of those is Gardwen.

**Run them in threes and fours,** as weather. They take bridges, bottles and boats, and they mostly do not fight, because nobody makes them. Every Knight in Purewater can fight, and almost none of them can swim. Use the Dragon Knight Soldier statistics for the rank and file, and the Ash Lord for anybody the Baron trusts.
