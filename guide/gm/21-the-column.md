# The Column

*Three days before the Tourney.* Four strangers arrive in Purewater on the same day as the Baron, none of them knowing about the others, and before the night is out a man whose face three men in this city share has done something unforgivable on the Pearl.

Run the four openings separately and cut between them, a scene or two each, and do not hurry them together. This day puts all four characters into the same night without letting them meet. By the time the lamps are lit on the Pearl, Magda and Gardwen should be lodged at the Sylph's Embrace, Randall somewhere on the Pearl or in the city with a strange story to tell, and Conall in the Lullwater, wishing he had never accepted the note.

## The Ford and the Road

<!-- covers: the-old-woman-at-the-ford, four-knights-and-a-kings-officer -->

The day begins a few miles outside the city, in two places at once.

**The ford.** At the gravel ford on the landward road, carts stand three deep in the heat, among horse-smell and crushed fennel, and everybody has been in the queue for an hour. At the front of it is the di Teufel column, and in the middle of the column is a covered litter with its curtains laced shut, which the outriders let nobody walk beside. Gardwen is in the queue behind it, watching the dragon-scale disappear toward the city gate. Three places back, an old woman in ringmail is looking straight at her and not pretending otherwise. That is Magda, who has dreamed Gardwen's face for eleven nights, and every dream ends in Magda's own death. She is not good at explaining, and she will not try twice.

**The litter is the first fact of the scenario.** Describe it and do not explain it. Anybody who pushes for a look gets an outrider's arm across the chest and a very polite refusal. What matters is that the two women find each other, and that by evening Magda has taken Gardwen to the one madame she knows who rents beds to women travelling alone: Constantine, at the Sylph's Embrace. If either woman is a Non-Player Character, the other still meets her, and they still go to that house. The scenario needs both of them under that roof tonight.

**The road.** An hour up the Ashwick road, four bored Dragon Knights stop a King's officer riding in plain harness with no device on her shield, because she is an elf and because they can. The officer is Temerach Nebulo, and Conall is riding beside her. Nobody draws a weapon. The Knights humiliate her slowly, publicly and deniably, because she outranks them and cannot use it. They go through her saddlebags, they ask her to say something in elvish, and it goes on far longer than is comfortable to play. Let it. She takes it, completely still, with no answer and her eyes front. She has told Conall not to intervene, and if he is a player character, he rolls Willpower not to.

**Do not let her win this scene.** Losing it here is what buys her the Archery, and the shot at the end of the week.

## The Gate

<!-- covers: the-baron-s-column-enters-purewater, a-knight-calls-randall-my-lord, three-of-them-on-the-gate-bridge -->

It is mid-morning in Caravan Square, and the crowd is thick enough to work. Randall has had a decent hour. Then the crowd turns, the way a crowd does, and the Dragon Knights come through the Land Gate: twenty-odd riders with their banners cased against the damp, the covered litter in the middle, and at their head a lord riding bare-headed and unhurried, letting Purewater look at him. A character in the crowd who watches the litter can see how carefully it is screened, and with a successful Perception roll, that the guards face inward as much as out.

Every eye in the square goes to the litter, so nobody is watching their purse, and Randall is in the middle of robbing a cart when a guard at the Baron's stirrup turns his head and sees him.

**The guard goes white.** He breaks step. He says *"My lord —"* in the voice of a servant who has just seen the heir standing in the street where the heir is not supposed to be, in a thief's clothes, with his hand in somebody's cargo, and who will be blamed for it. He offers Randall a hand down off the cart, because he cannot think of anything else to do. Then the column moves, and he has to move with it, and he looks back twice.

Let Randall play it.

- **A Deceit roll** to carry it off is worth more than escaping. On a success, Randall gets a name and a piece of the household's business. On a critical, he learns where his lordship is expected this evening. A failure puts Randall in front of somebody who has seen Santo close up.
- **A Stealth roll** to vanish costs nothing and teaches nothing. That is a fair outcome, and you should let it be dull.

**The guard reports nothing.** He thinks he has seen Santo slipping his leash again, and a guard who tells the Baron about the young master's absences does not stay a guard. Nobody in that column has seen a second face, only the first one in the wrong place, so the Baron learns nothing today.

The column crosses the Triumph Bridge to the customs house, makes for the Forges, and carries the litter aboard the steaming ship at the private pier. The city talks about it for the rest of the day.

**The bridge is the first lesson Purewater gives anybody who comes in by the gate.** Three men in dragon-blazoned harness stand in the middle of it, doing nothing much but eating. It is the only way in from Caravan Square, and the whole morning's traffic (carters, a fish-cart, two temple girls, an old man with a goat) goes round them in single file against the parapet, and nobody says anything. A watchman in blue-grey stands at the far end, and he is not doing anything either.

Describe it and let the players notice it. Do not point at it; everything they need to know is in what the locals do with their feet. If a character intervenes, and somebody usually will, the Knights are bored rather than spoiling for a fight. They give way sooner to a well-dressed stranger with a confident manner than to a carter, which teaches the characters something too. The watchman will not back them, but afterwards he asks their name in a low voice, and does not write it down where anybody can see. These three are the advance men; by dusk there are twenty-five. If nobody interferes, mark one for the occupation.

## A Rich Young Man with No Name

<!-- covers: santo-buys-her-evening -->

At the start of the evening trade, Emmeralda dances for the front hall of the Sylph's Embrace. A young man in a good coat with no device on it watches her the whole way through and does not drink. Then he goes to the desk and buys her evening, the way a hundred men a week do, with new silver and over the odds, and asks for a room on the second floor.

**He does not say *di Teufel*.** Nobody on the Pearl knows the heir's face, but everybody there knows Randall's, and the hall sees Randall the Pearl thief in a rich man's coat, playing the gentleman. It gets a laugh at the bar. The next two days turn on that mistake. When the house goes looking for the man who went upstairs with Emmeralda, it goes looking for Randall, and three men in the city wear his face.

Magda and Gardwen are in the hall, taking their rooms. A player who is watching the room can notice him with a Perception roll: young, rich, a soft right hand, and the coat, which is better than anything else he has on and which he is going to leave behind. Constantine takes him for Randall at a glance, takes his money and thinks nothing of it. She has known Randall since he was a boy, and a proper look would have shown her a human face, a year or two younger than his. She will spend the next day wishing she had looked harder.

## Randall, *There* You Are

<!-- covers: sinnit-corners-the-wrong-man -->

At the same hour, on the other side of the city, Conall is about to be called by his brother's name for the first time.

Temerach sends him over to the Lullwater by Shilling Stairs with a sealed note for the storyteller at the Moist Oyster. She does not explain it, because she never does. He dresses down for the errand, which attracts no attention anywhere except the Lullwater. Within the hour he is lost in the narrow canals north of the Still, and cornered by three men in a wet passage between two tenements, with water at both ends. The one in front fills the passage: big, canal-rat, permanently aggrieved. He says the name with enormous satisfaction.

> "Randall. *There* you are."

**This is Sinnit**, and he thinks he has finally caught Randall off the Pearl, alone, on Sinnit's own ground. He does not notice the clothes, the accent or the King's Assay ring. He sees the face he has hated for seven years. And he announces exactly what is going to happen, because that is the one mercy in him. He wants a reckoning before he wants a beating:

> "Pearl Quay. Seven years back. The purse. The bow. Say it. Say you're sorry, in front of my lads, and we're square."

Conall has no idea what he is talking about, and the scene runs on that. Every honest denial sounds like Randall being clever, which is exactly what Randall did last time, and Sinnit gets louder with each one. Every way out is fine:

- **Fight.** Sinnit hits hard and fights dirty, and his two lads (use the Canal Cutpurse) are worse than he is. Three on one in a wet passage is still three on one.
- **Magic.** It works, and it leaves Sinnit very frightened and very sure that Randall has learned something new.
- **Say it.** Conall can apologise for something he never did. It does not count, because the wrong man said it, but Sinnit does not know that and goes away shaken.
- **The Lullwater takes a hand.** A door opens, an old woman shouts from a window, a punt comes under the passage. Sometimes the quarter looks after strangers who are being picked on.

Conall leaves with something to turn over all night: a thief called Randall who, as far as the Lullwater is concerned, wears his face. It is the first sign of the twins on his side, and it comes the same day Randall is called *my lord*. Sinnit leaves with a story. Tomorrow, when he sees the real Randall walking the Pearl free and unbruised, he begins to understand something that will be worth money.

## The Night at the Sylph's Embrace

<!-- covers: santo-s-working -->

Santo has a room on the second floor, the girl's evening, and his father's scroll in his coat.

He thinks he is making her *willing*. For ten years he has watched his father seat a spirit in a living body and be obeyed by it, and he means to put something small and hungry into this girl, a passion spirit, so that she wants to come to the barge. He has one use of a stolen scroll and no training whatsoever, and he is nineteen and frightened. **The dice decide which version of the scenario you are running**, so roll at the table where the players can hear it, even if none of them is in the room.

**The working.** The scroll does most of the work, and Santo does the rest badly. A scroll lets somebody with the core of the art perform one step of it once, one grade harder, so Santo rolls his Willpower at Hard (46) against Emmeralda's Willpower (58). An untrained binder does not get what he called. He gets whatever answers, and tonight what answers is a Wanting of Imbra. Whatever the result, the scroll burns out in his hand and leaves a fresh burn across the palm and fingers of his right hand that will not heal by morning. That burn is the clue that clears the twins.

**If it takes,** the channel opens, the seat holds, and the thing settles into her. She stops being afraid of him. She wants what he wants, and she is bright and pliant and wrong in a way anybody who knew her would see in a minute. Now he has to get her out of the house: a Stealth roll down the back stair to the water-door and the skiff, made one grade easier because she is helping.

- If he succeeds, he keeps her, and she is aboard the Baron's ship by midnight.
- If he fails, somebody stops them on the stair. He lets go and runs, the girl is left on the landing laughing at nothing, and he tries again by boat tomorrow.

**If it fails,** the channel opens and the seat does not hold. Whatever answered will not go where he tells it, and he finishes the working the only way his panic can think of, with the barbed knife: a long ritual cut that opens her the length of her body. She screams. He runs, and as he runs he kicks the knife behind the armoire. (If your table likes the Widow's legend, this is the night the women of the quay decide that a binding cannot hold under the Pearl's lamps.)

**Magda and Gardwen lodge at the top of the back stair, which is why Magda brought her here.**

- **If the working failed,** they hear the screams and are first through the door. Gardwen's healing, or anybody's First Aid, saves Emmeralda's life; without it she dies before dawn. On the back stair they see the man going the other way: young, rich, bare-headed, cradling his right hand.
- **If it took,** they see a couple on the back stair in the small hours, and a girl who was frightened of him at dusk now hanging on his arm. Magda has served Hel long enough to know what she is looking at. If they step in, Santo's getaway becomes an opposed roll against theirs.

**He leaves everything in the room**, because he left in a panic either way:

- the barbed knife behind the armoire, if the working failed, with a mark on the pommel: a ring with a line through it and three strokes off the line;
- the spent scroll on the floor, burnt out but legible, in the Baron's own hand;
- a chalk seat drawn on the boards under the rug, still live until somebody sweeps it;
- and his coat over a chair, with a Purewater tailor's chit in the pocket and the di Teufel crest under the collar.

Nobody in the house is going to search a room while a girl is bleeding in it, so all of it is still there in the morning, and so is the man who wants it back.

Either way, before midnight Marisette has heard, and the hunt for a man with that face has begun.
