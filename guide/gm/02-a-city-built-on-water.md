# A City Built on Water

Purewater stands across the Mouth, the narrow strait where a great sacred lake drains south into the open sea. It is rich, strategic and rotten, and its own people call it Pisswater, for the smell of the canals and the smell of its politics alike.

There is no ground. Houses come straight up out of the channels with no bank, their doors opening onto steps that go down under the surface and keep going. Everything that matters moves by boat, poled, rowed and shouted about. The rest goes over a bridge or along a ledge a body or two wide with no rail, and every journey costs a fare. Anybody raised here handles a boat the way they walk, and nobody says out loud that outsiders cannot. A stranger in Purewater cannot move a sack of flour across the city without hiring somebody born here. There are no horses at all. The land stops at Caravan Square on the western shore and does not start again.

The heart of the city is High Isle, which is really four islands cut apart by water:

- the **North Quarter** of fine houses, the Governor's Palazzo and the Tournament Ground;
- the **Merchant's Quarter** of guilds and counting-houses;
- the **Forges**, all smoke and foundries;
- and the **Seaward docks**.

North of them, among the scattered Northern Isles, lies the **Pearl**, the pleasure island. North-east, across open water and reached by no bridge at all, lies the **Lullwater**, where the thieves and the working poor live together in a drowned maze. The **Shoals** guard the seaward side with their fish-markets and smugglers' jetties. Just north of the Tournament Ground, alone, unbridged and holy, sits **Temple Isle**. *People and Places in Purewater* walks every one of them.

## The Water Is the Goddess

The priestesses of the Lake Lady say the water *is* the goddess, and they mean it plainly. Her holy water wells up in the Sacred Confluence on Temple Isle and runs out through the temple into the city's channels, and by ancient writ the Order of the Lake Lady owns every channel and pool. The city council owns the isles. The most sacred ground in Purewater is also its traffic system, which gives the Order a standing no council can overrule and no baron can buy.

Two houses serve the goddess, and neither can give the other an order.

**The Order of the Lake Lady** is the priesthood. It keeps the temples, pools, rites, tithes, calendar and Tourney. It is a civic power, and it is bound by temple law, which forbids the clergy to intervene in the city's affairs unless the sacred sites themselves are threatened.

**The Swords of the Lake** are a knightly order sworn to the goddess herself rather than to her clergy. They are austere, own no property, serve across the whole realm and hold the oldest battle honours from the uprising against the Dragon Kings. They take no tithes, keep no temples and do not answer to the Governor's council.

The two houses love each other like two branches of one family that cannot spend a week under the same roof, and in four hundred years neither has found a polite way to ask the other a favour.

**The water is going wrong.** Since Candlemas the pools have been fouling, worst in the eastern yards near the Forges and creeping north toward the lake. The cause is the Baron's ship, whose hull steams wherever it meets the water as its fire magic fights the holy channel. The Order raised the alarm. Six weeks ago the Watercrafters' Tide Master, Ivo Calder, signed it off as a *false reading* at one polite request from a man he respected, and an instruction over the Prince's signature then silenced it. High Priestess Nerissa knows the reading was right. She can feel the fouling in her own body, and she drinks the water anyway. But a corrupted channel is not yet, in law, a threatened site, so temple law ties her hands.

Only one person knows the worst secret in the Lady's house. **Lilura Deepcurrent**, the Lady's voice for thirty years, has heard nothing from the goddess for fourteen months. She has told nobody, because a faith whose goddess has gone silent has no civic power at all. Whether the silence comes from the fouling, from the bound dragon-knight in the harbour or from something older, nobody in this scenario will find out. If you want the Lady to stir at the end of the week, when the Lullwater burns or the Baron is named on her steps, you have a reason ready.

## The Rule of the Pearl

Every child on the Pearl can tell the story of the Widow. Two hundred years ago a merchant prince bought a fifteen-year-old girl outright, with a bill of sale witnessed by two councillors, and kept her behind a wall on a little islet. For eleven years she lit a lamp in her window every dusk and talked to the water. Then she asked the basin for her husband, and one calm night it took him. She bought the biggest house on the Pearl's quay with his money, opened its doors to women who had been sold as she had been, and laid down the rule every house on the island still keeps: **a night is for sale; a person is not.** No bill of sale for a woman is honoured on the Pearl, and no man who brings one is let in anywhere on the island again.

At dusk every evening the doorman of her old house, the Mother of Pearl, lights a taper from a lamp that is said not to have gone out in two hundred years. Every house on the quay lights its own lamps from that taper, and so do the posts of the Lantern Bridge. The Pearl's women believe that no one under that flame can be owned. A bill of sale signed under the Pearl's lamps fades off the page by morning, they say, and a binding of any kind fails.

None of that is confirmed, and nothing in the scenario depends on it. But when a working fails on the Pearl, everybody on the quay knows why.

## The Lake Lady's Tourney

The Tourney is the year's holy business: three days of contests in the goddess's honour, older than the Governor's office, drawing competitors from across the realm. For that week the city fills, everybody makes a year's money in six days, and the law goes quiet.

On the **eve of the Tourney**, every entrant in every event is presented to the Crown's representative by long form: names read, colours shown, and each competitor bare-headed for the space of a bow. It is tedious, and nobody can skip it without forfeiting the entry. It is also the only time before the sand that the Baron's champion must show his face in daylight.

The **first day** is the **Archery**, shot on the Long Butts beside the Lists: two hundred paces of grass, a crosswind off the lake that everybody complains about, and the Governor's box at the near end. It is the one open event. Anybody may shoot, with no colours and no fee, so it is the only place all week where a person with no standing can stand in front of the Governor and be owed a hearing for as long as the crowd is watching.

The **second day** is the **Single Combat**, the only martial event: one-on-one bouts on the raked sand of the Lists in front of four thousand people.

The **third day** is the **Run**, the only event that leaves the Tournament Ground. Competitors go out through the water-gate and race the whole city on foot and by boat, by any route and with any help, and come back over open water to the temple steps. For two hours Purewater has no crowd control at all. At dusk the Prince takes his place and the **closing** begins: the prizes, the speeches and, behind a closed door, the champion's presentation.

## Who Runs Purewater

Nobody runs all of it, and that is the trouble.

- The **Prince** governs in the King's name. He is two-and-twenty and has learned to sign things rather than decide them.
- A **council of merchant princes** runs the city's daily business, and three of them got his signature onto the order that silenced the watch.
- **Marisette** rules every house on the Pearl, on nothing but the settled expectation that she will still be there afterwards.
- **Crowbill** owns the canals nobody maps, and keeps a code: no killing canal folk, no harming their children, no touching the Lady's water where the low water would feel it.
- **Marda Blackwater** speaks for three hundred dock labourers and, through them, for every boat on the water-stairs.
- The **Order** owns the water and may not act. The **Swords** may act and may not speak for the water.
- The **merfolk** live in the deep water of the lake and come into the city by their own paths under the canals. Since the Baron's ship arrived they keep away from it, and their paths near it are closing.
- The **Mermaid's Court** keeps faith with them. It is a hidden society of water-mages, humans and half-merfolk, meeting in drowned chambers under Lost Isle, and it means to take the city back one generation at a time. Seven leaders, the **Deep Council**, give its orders, and above them is a figure known only as **the Nacre**, whom few have met and some call a merfolk princess. The Court hates the Baron's fire and would rather wait him out than fight him.
- The **watch** has been ordered, in writing, to do nothing.

Any one of them could stop the Baron if they were willing to spend themselves doing it, and none of them will go first. The characters arrive into that.
