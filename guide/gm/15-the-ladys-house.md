# The Lady's House and Her Knights

Two houses serve the goddess. The Order keeps her water and may not act. The Swords keep her road and may not speak for the water. Between them are the people with the most standing in Purewater and the least freedom to use it.

## Lilura Deepcurrent

*Elderly, piercing blue eyes, very still. Holds her breath unnaturally long without noticing she is doing it.*

The head of the Order of the Lake Lady has been the goddess's voice for thirty years, and for the last fourteen months there has been nobody on the other end. She has told no living soul. A faith whose goddess has gone silent has no civic power at all, and at present civic power is the only thing protecting her people. She is spare and slow, with no performance left in her; she has stopped doing the things a High Priestess does for effect. She is kind in a remote way, like weather clearing, and when she speaks it is one sentence and the matter is closed. People say there is merfolk blood in her.

> "I have been the Lady's voice for thirty years. I would like to be told what she is saying."

**Under pressure she talks about water**: the cistern, the tide, which pool you drank from and what it tasted like. She is looking for the answer somewhere other than the place she can no longer look. She runs the practical half of the Order, the gate, the rota and the correspondence, so that Nerissa can run the sacred half. She will decide whether the Order can ever be moved, and nothing less than the goddess, or something that sounds like her, will move her.

## High Priestess Nerissa

*Fifties. Blue-green robes worked so heavily with silver that the light does something strange on the cloth.*

The High Priestess of the temple on Temple Isle has been right, alone, for two years, about something enormous, and every person she told has managed her, kindly and patiently. It has made her careful, which is worse than bitter. She can feel what is happening to the water in her own body. The Order's answer was to have a younger priestess sit with her. She drinks the fouled water herself and has told nobody why. She is grave, dry and physically fearless. Her manner is cool, and her actions are unusually kind: when somebody is in trouble in front of her, she deals with it, without commentary.

> "I am permitted to be wrong about this. That is the whole of what I am permitted."

**This week** she wants to hold the Tourney without the goddess's water being profaned, and she wants somebody with standing to say out loud what she is not permitted to say. Temple law binds her until a sacred site itself is threatened, and she cannot act on a rumour carried by strangers. She can act, decisively and in public, on evidence in the open: a demon-marked blade laid on her altar in front of witnesses, Ivo Calder's original notes, or an Assay finding with Conall's punch on it. Give her that, and she can deny the Baron the Lady's ground, refuse his champion the blessing, or stop a bout on sacred sand, with the city watching. When she stops being careful, the hedging falls away and she states things flatly and publicly, and everybody who knows her goes very quiet, because they have all been afraid of this.

Her power is the rites, and the rites need the temple, the pool, the Order and time. In her own house she is formidable. On a dredge-boat in the Lullwater she is an ordinary woman in her fifties. She has no possession magic of any kind, and the Order never has. That province belongs to the demonologists and the wild-elf druids.

## Sana

*Nineteen, dark red hair chopped ragged down one side, temple blue-green worn like a coat that belongs to somebody else.*

Sana is Nerissa's niece, six weeks a novice on Temple Isle, and cleverer than the job she has been given. She is fast, funny, tactless and wildly curious. She interrupts, asks the question everybody else was too polite to ask, apologises, and asks it again. Her family sent her here instead of keeping her at home, and nobody has told her whether that was a chance or a punishment. She is terrified that the adults have already worked out she is not really one of them.

> "Nobody tells me anything, which means I have to work it out, which means I am usually right and never believed."

She notices what nobody else notices, including, at the presentation of the entrants, a boy too thin for the armour he is in. She wants one adult to take her seriously, once, out loud, in front of somebody else. If a character does that, the party has a friend inside the temple who will carry a message to the High Priestess that would never reach her through the front door. When Sana goes completely quiet, anybody who knows her takes it at once as an emergency, because she never does.

## Temerach Nebulo

*Elf. Plain full plate without a single ornament, kept perfectly. No colours, no house, no badge. Never seen arriving; already there.*

Temerach is a knight of the Swords of the Lake, called the Lakelady's Javelin for what she can do with a bow. She was admitted on merit to a human order thirty years ago and she is still the outsider in every room. She does not belong to the Purewater chapter. She came from the capital, Caldreth, as an officer of the Crown's own intelligence service, and she answers to a royal spymaster at court.

She is the Baron's most dangerous enemy on the King's side. She knows about the Movement and how far it reaches: the friends at court, the money, the sworn companies in the provinces and the fire. She knows what a threat he is to the Crown, and that is why she took Conall, a King's assayer with the Baron's own face. What she does not know is the Baron's plan. Nothing she has gathered points at the Tourney or the room behind the Governor's box, and she cannot make the pieces fit.

For four years she has run a network around Baron Hanzo that nobody has ever seen whole: ostlers, temple acolytes, a lamp-man on the Forge wharf, two clerks on the Governor's council, a boatwoman at the Mouth, and men she has had swear over still water and keeps sworn. None of them knows the others, and most of them do not know whom they report to.

> "You were followed from the catacombs by two of Blau's and one of mine. Mine is the only one who reported."

**Keep her out of reach.** If the characters can visit her, confide in her or win her over, she stops working. She is an implacable spymaster, and from the moment the catacombs make the four of them interesting, she is running them, whether they know it or not. Play her by these rules:

- **She cannot be found.** Nobody walks up to her. Her squire, Murgeroch, is the only way to her, and he takes people to her when she says so. She arrives: on a stair, in the back of a boat, at the end of a table nobody saw her sit down at.
- **She always knows more.** Open every scene with her by having her tell the characters something about their own last day that they did not know she knew. Prepare it in advance. She is never surprised in front of them.
- **She gives tasks, and never asks favours.** A door watched, a man followed, a message carried to Nerissa: each one precise, paid for exactly and never explained. She does not owe anybody, and she does not let anybody owe her.
- **She tells them only what makes them useful,** never early and never the whole picture. She does not lie, because she is a King's officer and that matters to her, but withholding is her trade, and a direct question gets a better question back.
- **Pleading, charm, anger, threats and liking do not move her.** Only proof does, and only proof she can check through her own people.
- **If the characters push her, she cuts them loose.** If they try to force her accusation before she is ready, she stops using them, and they discover how much of the last two days she was covering for them.

**She has waited four years for a reason.** She has the standing to call the Baron to answer, and she has carried it unused, because a lord called to answer may offer trial by his champion's sword, and she does not know what is under that helm. She is very probably the one blade in Thornmere who could beat the champion, and she cannot enter the Single Combat, because her Archery entry is what buys her a quarter of an hour in front of the Prince. She brought Conall to Purewater knowing whose son he is, and she has handled him like a piece on a board for four years. She would do it again, and she cannot forgive herself for that.

**Two things change her.** Danger to Conall overrides everything, including her own caution and her order's protocol. And learning what is inside the champion changes what she is allowed to do: the moment she knows it is a bound spirit in a stolen boy, it is desecration and the goddess's own business, and a Sword may act on that without asking anybody.

**The players never win her friendship.** Their reward comes late in the week, when she hands them a task with the *reason* attached, once, and they understand they have gone from being her assets to being people she trusts.

## Murgeroch

*A steady young fighter, kit well kept, who stands slightly in front of whoever he is screening without making a production of it.*

Temerach's squire and go-between carries her messages, screens her meetings and tends her gear, and he is usually the first of her people the characters deal with. He has made being reliable his whole identity, and he is right to. He repeats a message back to confirm it and tells you when it will be delivered. He wants promotion more than he would ever say out loud, because saying it would be unprofessional. When he decides a character is not worth his time, the warmth switches off, and nothing said afterwards moves him an inch.

> "I'll carry it word for word. I'd rather you said it twice than I guessed once."

## Corvin

*Twenty-two, a Sword of the Lake in white, open honest face. Rows extremely well and would like that mentioned.*

Corvin is a young Sword who took his vows for sincere reasons and is terrified that when the moment comes he will not be equal to it. Nothing has happened to him yet, so he has no evidence about himself either way, and he badly needs some. He is eager, warm, a bit too loud and good-hearted. He picks up the heavy end of things before anybody asks, sometimes before anybody wants him to, and he agrees to things he has no authority to agree to. He believes the best of people, and he is right more often than cynics expect.

> "I'll come. I should probably ask first. I'll come."

He is the one Sword the characters can recruit, which makes him valuable and dangerous in equal measure. He acts first, steps in, takes the weight and gives away the thing he was told to keep, and then you can watch him realise, a moment late, what he has just done.
