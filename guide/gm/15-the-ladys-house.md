# The Lady's House and Her Knights

Two houses serve the goddess. The Order keeps her water and may not act; the Swords keep her road and may not speak for the water. Between them stand the people with the most standing in Purewater and the least freedom to use it.

## Lilura Deepcurrent

*Elderly, piercing blue eyes, very still. Holds her breath unnaturally long without noticing she is doing it.*

The head of the Order of the Lake Lady has been the goddess's voice for thirty years, and for the last fourteen months there has been nobody on the other end. She has told no living soul. A faith whose goddess has gone silent has no civic power at all, and civic power is currently the only thing protecting her people. She is spare and slow, with no performance left in her; she has stopped doing the things a High Priestess does for effect. She is kind in a remote way, like weather clearing, and when she speaks it is one sentence and the matter is closed. People say there is merfolk blood in her.

> "I have been the Lady's voice for thirty years. I would like to be told what she is saying."

**Play her turning to water.** Under pressure she talks about the cistern, the tide, which pool you drank from and what it tasted like. It is never small talk. It is always her looking for the answer somewhere other than the place she has stopped being able to look. She runs the practical half of the Order so that Nerissa can run the sacred half: the gate, the rota, the correspondence. She is the one who will decide whether the Order can ever be moved, and she will not be moved by anything less than the goddess, or something that sounds like her.

## High Priestess Nerissa

*Fifties. Blue-green robes worked so heavily with silver that the light does something strange on the cloth.*

The High Priestess of the temple on Temple Isle has been right, alone, for two years, about something enormous, and has been kindly and patiently managed by every person she told. She is not bitter about it; she is worse than bitter, because she has become careful. She can feel what is happening to the water in her own body. The Order's answer was to have a younger priestess sit with her. She drinks the fouled water herself and has told nobody why. She is grave, dry and physically fearless, not warm in manner but unusually kind in practice: when somebody is in trouble in front of her, she simply deals with it, without commentary.

> "I am permitted to be wrong about this. That is the whole of what I am permitted."

**This week** she wants to hold the Tourney without the goddess's water being profaned, and she wants somebody with standing to say out loud the thing she is not permitted to say. Temple law binds her until a sacred site itself is threatened, and she cannot act on a rumour carried by strangers. She can act, decisively and in public, on evidence in the open: a demon-marked blade laid on her altar in front of witnesses, Ivo Calder's original notes, or an Assay finding with Conall's punch on it. Give her that, and she can deny the Baron the Lady's ground, refuse his champion the blessing, or stop a bout on sacred sand, and the city will watch her do it. When she stops being careful, the hedging falls away and she starts stating things flatly and publicly, and everybody who knows her goes very quiet, because this is what they have all been afraid of.

Her weight is the rites, and the rites need the temple, the pool, the Order and time. In her own house she is formidable. On a dredge-boat in the Lullwater she is an ordinary woman in her fifties. She has no possession magic of any kind, and neither has the Order, ever. That province belongs to the demonologists and the wild-elf druids.

## Sana

*Nineteen, dark red hair chopped ragged down one side, temple blue-green worn like a coat that belongs to somebody else.*

Nerissa's niece, six weeks a novice on Temple Isle, and cleverer than the job she has been given. She is fast, funny, tactless and wildly curious. She interrupts, asks the question everybody else was too polite to ask, apologises, and asks it again. She was sent here rather than kept at home, and has never been told which of those it was, and she is terrified that the adults have already worked out she is not really one of them.

> "Nobody tells me anything, which means I have to work it out, which means I am usually right and never believed."

She notices what nobody else notices, including, at the presentation of the entrants, a boy too thin for the armour he is in. She wants one adult to take her seriously, once, out loud, in front of somebody else. Do that, and the characters have a friend inside the temple who will carry a message to the High Priestess that would never reach her through the front door. When Sana goes properly quiet, anybody who knows her reads it at once as an emergency, because it is the one thing she never does.

## Temerach Nebulo

*Elf. Plain full plate without a single ornament, kept perfectly. No colours, no house, no badge. Never seen arriving; already there.*

A knight of the Swords of the Lake, called the Lakelady's Javelin for what she can do with a bow, admitted on merit to a human order thirty years ago and still the outsider in every room. She is also the order's spymaster in Purewater. For four years she has run a network around Baron Hanzo that nobody has ever seen whole: ostlers, temple acolytes, a lamp-man on the Forge wharf, two clerks on the Governor's council, a boatwoman at the Mouth, and men she has had swear over still water and keeps sworn. None of them knows the others. Most of them do not know whom they report to.

> "You were followed from the catacombs by two of Blau's and one of mine. Mine is the only one who reported."

**In earlier tellings she was far too approachable, and it broke her.** She is not a patron the characters can visit, confide in or win over. She is an implacable spymaster, and from the moment the catacombs make the four of them interesting, she is running them, whether they know it or not. Play her by these rules:

- **She cannot be found.** Nobody walks up to her. Her squire, Murgeroch, is the only door to her, and he opens it when she says. She arrives: on a stair, in the back of a boat, at the end of a table nobody saw her sit down at.
- **She always knows more.** Every scene with her opens with her telling the characters something about their own last day that they did not know she knew. Prepare it. She is never surprised in front of them.
- **She does not ask; she tasks.** A door watched, a man followed, a message carried to Nerissa. Precise, paid for exactly, and never explained. She does not owe and does not let herself be owed.
- **She gives them only what makes them useful,** never early and never the whole picture. She does not lie, because she is a King's officer and that matters to her, but she withholds as a profession, and a direct question gets a better question back.
- **She is not moved** by pleading, charm, anger, threats or being liked. Persuasion does not work on her. Proof does, and only proof she can verify through her own people.
- **Push her, and she cuts them loose.** If the characters try to force her accusation before she is ready, she simply stops using them, and they discover how much of the last two days she was quietly covering for them.

**Why she has waited four years.** She has the standing to call the Baron to answer, and she has carried it unused, because a lord called to answer may offer trial by his champion's sword, and she does not know what is under that helm. She is very probably the one blade in Thornmere who could beat the champion, and she cannot enter the Single Combat, because her Archery entry is what buys her a quarter of an hour in front of the Prince. She brought Conall to Purewater knowing whose son he is, and she has handled him like a piece on a board for four years. She would do it again. That is the part she cannot forgive.

**What changes her** is danger to Conall, which overrides everything, including her own caution and her order's protocol; and learning what is actually inside the champion. The moment she knows it is a bound spirit in a stolen boy, the calculation inverts, because that is desecration and the goddess's own business, and a Sword may act on that without asking anybody. **The reward for the players** is not her friendship. It is the moment, late in the week, when she hands them a task with the *reason* attached, once, and they understand they have been promoted from asset to somebody she trusts.

## Murgeroch

*A steady young fighter, kit well kept, who stands slightly in front of whoever he is screening without making a production of it.*

Temerach's squire and go-between carries her messages, screens her meetings and tends her gear, and he is usually the first of her people the characters deal with. He has decided that being reliable is a whole identity, and he is quietly right. He repeats a message back to confirm it and tells you when it will be delivered. He wants promotion more than he would ever say out loud, because wanting it out loud would be unprofessional. When he decides you are not worth his time, the warmth simply switches off, not cold but finished, and nothing said afterwards moves him an inch.

> "I'll carry it word for word. I'd rather you said it twice than I guessed once."

## Corvin

*Twenty-two, a Sword of the Lake in white, open honest face. Rows extremely well and would like that mentioned.*

A young Sword who took his vows for entirely sincere reasons and is privately terrified that when the moment comes he will turn out not to be equal to it. Nothing has happened to him yet, and that is the wound: he has no evidence about himself, and he needs some. He is eager, warm, a bit too loud and genuinely good-hearted. He picks up the heavy end of things before anybody asks, and occasionally before anybody wants him to, and he agrees to things he has no authority to agree to. He believes the best of people, and is right more often than the cynical expect.

> "I'll come. I should probably ask first. I'll come."

He is the Sword the characters can actually recruit, which makes him precious and dangerous in equal measure. He acts first, steps in, takes the weight, gives away the thing he was told to keep, and you can watch him arrive, a beat late, at what he has just done.
