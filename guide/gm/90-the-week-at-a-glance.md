# The Week at a Glance

Everything in this book that happens on its own, in order. Each time is the latest the event happens; the characters can bring it sooner. Read down this table each time you move the clock, and ask, for each event, whether a character is there.

<!-- timeline -->

## Running the Living World

Every Non-Player Character and faction with a stake in the week holds an **agenda**: something they want, pursued on a **clock** of four to ten segments. Fill a segment when the fiction moves the holder toward their goal; *Events and Story Structure* tells you when the main ones move. When a clock fills, the holder has what they wanted and the world changes. Nothing empties a clock. When the characters genuinely beat an agenda, mark it thwarted, and then let the holder react: a thwarted agenda should produce a new one, not a quiet re-run of the old. Some clocks start partly full, because the week began before the characters arrived. The Dragon Knights, for instance, have been holding the streets for eight days.

One agenda sleeps until something wakes it. *A di Teufel who is not his* waits until somebody in the Baron's service sees two of the di Teufel face, or is sold the fact. Once it wakes, it never sleeps again.

### Conditions to Watch For

- **The water-stairs stop** at dawn on the eve of the Tourney if nobody has helped Marda Blackwater.
- **The Pearl closes** to the Dragon Knights after Santo's tantrum, or after Constantine finds the room empty.
- **Sinnit sells the face** at three steps if Randall has not apologised to him, and a price on the face follows the next dusk.
- **The theft** decides the last day: read the board at dawn, and play only the road it chose.

### Who Knows What

Some events depend on who knows what. Keep a short list of the facts that matter and who holds each one, and add a name whenever a character tells somebody something. A Non-Player Character can only act on what they know.

- The twins are the Baron's sons: Temerach knows; the Baron learns it only from Sinnit, or from seeing two of the face.
- The champion is Gardwen's brother: the Baron and Orrin know; Gardwen learns it at the presentation.
- The locket is the leash and comes off at night: Crowbill knows, and the characters learn it from him.
- The man who carved Emmeralda was Randall the Pearl thief: false, and believed on the street after Sinnit's lie.
