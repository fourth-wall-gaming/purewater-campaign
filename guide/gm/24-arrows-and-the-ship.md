# Arrows and the Ship

*The first day of the Tourney.* The city fills the stands and the slopes, the bookmakers chalk their odds, and an elf in plain harness walks onto the Long Butts. By the end of the night the characters know what the champion is, what holds him, and where that thing lies when the Baron sleeps.

**By now the characters have proof, and today's problem is jurisdiction.** They need the court that fits (the Assay, the port or the goddess's ground) and somebody with standing who is willing to walk into it. The thread that unravels the Baron is his money, because it is not his.

## A Price on a Rooftop

<!-- covers: crowbill-names-his-price -->

At first light Crowbill collects a character off a ledge, as he has collected people in this city for thirty years, and puts them on a cookshop roof to talk. He already knows what they did last night: the hour, the tide and how many of them there were. He does not ask what they found. That unsettles them more than a threat would, and it shows them exactly how much of the city he sees.

Crowbill trades; he does not help. He wants something they will not enjoy giving: intelligence on the Baron's plan, a route into the camp, or control of a story the street is already telling. He has a line, and demon-craft worked on a helpless girl beside the Lady's water crosses it. If the characters bring him proof rather than a grievance, he opens his canals, safe houses and men to them.

**The thing he can give that nobody else can is the locket.** The Baron wears a chain with a locket on it and has never been seen in daylight without it. Crowbill keeps a lamp-man on the Forge wharf who watches that ship for a living, and the lamp-man has seen that it comes off at night. The Baron sleeps aft with the locket on the table beside his bed, on a chain with a clasp rather than a collar, and Blau sleeps across the door. By day it hangs round his neck on that same chain, which is an ordinary lift for a good pickpocket who can get close to him. Crowbill does not say *steal it*. He says what it is, where it lies, and that he knows a boy who would.

Nus is in the next room and has heard every word.

If no character goes to Crowbill, he says the same thing over breakfast to Nus anyway, as a joke. Nus does not hear it as a joke. Either way, the whole scenario has just started to turn, and nobody in the room has noticed.

## The Tide Master's Notes

<!-- covers: ivo-calder-folds -->

Somewhere in the middle of the week, and the first day of the Tourney is the natural place for it, a clever party goes looking for the reading that was signed off as false. That leads them to Ivo Calder, the Watercrafters' Tide Master. He is best approached after the characters have found the weak place in Alderic Vantt and know whose money it is. He is fifty, distinguished, in guild indigo, with his instruments kept beautifully, and he has not slept properly in six weeks.

He has his whole defence prepared and wants to deliver it, and a question he has not anticipated stops him dead. He wants to be told that what he signed was reasonable.

**He holds out completely, and then all at once he gives way.** He mounts a vigorous defence until an independent reading appears, from Nerissa's people, from Gardwen's own senses at the water's edge or from the Assay. Then everything comes out in one breath, with something close to relief. He has not destroyed the original notes. He has moved them twice.

His confession buys a name, and no conviction: the name of who is really paying the Baron, a man who is not in this city and will not die on the sand. His notes also reopen the alarm, which puts the Order officially onto the Baron and gives Nerissa exactly the cause she needs to act on her own ground.

## The Archery

<!-- covers: pivot-the-archery -->

**The Archery is the set piece of the day.** It is shot over two hundred paces of marked grass on the Long Butts, with a crosswind off the lake that everybody complains about, the Governor's box at the near end, and half the city lying on the slope behind. It is the one open event, with no colours and no fee, which is why an elf in plain harness is standing in it.

**Temerach wins.** Let her. After three days of being stopped on the road and spoken to in front of her own escort, she puts two hundred paces of arrows where nobody else in the field can, in absolute silence. Then she has to walk up to the Governor's box to be handed a prize, with four thousand people watching, and **the dice decide what she does with the ninety seconds she has there.** Roll her Passion to see the Baron called to answer against her Loyalty to the Swords.

- **If her Passion wins, she spends it.** She names, out loud, whose men stopped a King's officer on the Ashwick road and what has been done in this city this week. She turns thirty years of standing into one public accusation the Governor cannot pretend not to have heard. It buys a hearing, and it makes her a marked woman two days early.
- **If her Loyalty wins, she holds it.** She takes the prize, says the correct words and walks away. She remains a King's officer with a clean record and a door she can open once, colder now, and far more useful later.
- **Very rarely, if the dice say so, she loses.** The household's archer takes the wreath, and the characters watch the one person with standing lose her chance to use it.

Whichever way it falls, the shot on the last day answers this scene.

## A Price on the Face

<!-- covers: a-price-on-the-face -->

If the Baron has learned of the twins, and if nothing has intervened he has, because Sinnit sold them last night, then at dusk Blau puts money on the street: a purse for a name, and a bigger one for delivery. By the next dawn every dockhand, courtesan and cutpurse in Purewater has the description.

From now on the twins cannot move openly. Randall's friends on the Pearl look at him and start counting what he is worth. Conall's warrant stops opening doors and starts announcing him. Blau will not announce a bastard to the city. He takes one off a street at night, and he is patient. A twin taken now goes to the ship, because the Baron wants the tool before he extinguishes the claim.

**From here on, the scenario is a pursuit**, and the characters brought it on themselves.

## Gardwen Goes Aboard

<!-- covers: gardwen-goes-aboard, orrin-sculle-decides -->

She has seen her brother's face for four seconds after ten years, and she is not going to wait for the sand to see it again.

**This is the most important night of the scenario, and it should be quiet.** Twenty people are aboard the Baron's ship, at a private pier on the Forges' seaward side, its hull steaming where it meets the water. A stranger on that deck is noticed inside a minute, and since the presentation Blau has doubled the watch and stopped changing it at a fixed hour. Getting aboard is a whole operation: Crowbill's canal routes, Ruhi's nose, the dark under the pier, the rest of the party making noise at the far end of the wharf. Run it as a real infiltration, Stealth against the Dragon Circle's Perception, and let it cost something.

Below the waterline aft she finds a lamp that is never put out, and a keeper asleep across a door. Then a man in his fifties comes up out of the after hold carrying an empty basin: heavy forearms, sleeves rolled past the elbow, a smell of liniment and lamp oil. This is **Orrin Sculle**, and he has been aboard longer than everybody on deck except the shipmaster. He does not shout. He sets the basin down without looking at it and tells her exactly what is supposed to happen next: he calls out, six men in mail come up the forward hatch, and nobody ashore ever hears of her again. Then he asks, *"So before I do — what are you?"*

**Nothing she can say is cleverer than the truth**, and nobody has offered him the truth in ten years. A character who lies to him gets the six men. If she tells him the truth, she makes no roll. **His own heart decides it, rolled in front of the player: his Love for the boy, 70%, against his Fear of the Baron, 65%.**

- **If Love wins,** he does not step aside. He sits down on the coaming like a man whose legs have stopped agreeing with him. He gives her knowledge, though not access. He tells her the boy's real name, which nobody has said aboard that ship in ten years. He tells her the thing he has never told a living soul: Gabriel holds a cup left-handed, the thing wearing him is right-handed, and every time it puts a cup down and the boy surfaces for a second, he moves it across. Then he gives her twenty minutes, boots off, and a warning: the third step complains, and the watch changes at the turn of the glass.
- **If Fear wins,** he does not betray her either, because he is not a cruel man. He tells her to get off his ship, and he means it, and he will be at the top of that companionway every time she comes back. A second approach should be harder, and still possible.
- **If both rolls are criticals or both are fumbles,** he asks her to take the boy with her tonight. She cannot, and he will never forgive her for refusing.

Everything she learns tonight, she learns from him or from the boy:

- **What is in her brother:** a dragon-knight called Thuban Eta, a thousand years dead, held in a living body by a torment and a binding.
- **That Gabriel is still in there,** and the cup proves it.
- **What holds the binding:** the bones in the tower at Emberhold, which hold Thuban in the world, and the locket on the Baron's chain, which is the leash, renewed every night. Without the locket, the Baron can neither command Thuban nor move him.
- **What the Baron wants:** if Orrin's Love won, he tells her what he has worked out and never said. The Baron does not want a champion. He wants the room after the bout.
- **The ship's routines:** the step, the glass, and the Baron's cabin aft beside the working cabin. Two thieves will use that map tomorrow night.

**Thuban sometimes surfaces in the small hours.** If he does, he speaks to her in her brother's voice with perfect, thousand-year-old courtesy. He does not want to be there. He cannot leave while the bones hold him, and he cannot disobey while the locket does. If she asks about the boy, he speaks of Gabriel the way a father does, and she will not be ready for that. Let the conversation be long and quiet, and let the player ask him anything. Nothing else in the book is as good.

**She cannot take her brother tonight.** Orrin will not help, and he is right: taking the body frees nobody while the anchors hold. She leaves knowing exactly what has to be taken, and from whom.

- **If Emmeralda is aboard,** she is forward in the good cabins, bright and wrong, and calls out to anybody she sees.
- **If Gardwen is caught,** she is not killed. Blau wants to know who she is. A captured Gardwen aboard the ship means a rescue for the party and a very bad night for her.

Orrin opens the way to the boy for the first person who gets near him by any route: a delivery, an invitation, a disguise, somebody else's errand. If the characters reach him before tonight, play him then. If nothing changes, Gardwen is the one who finds him.
