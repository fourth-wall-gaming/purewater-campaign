# Arrows and the Ship

*The first day of the Tourney.* The city fills the stands and the slopes, the bookmakers chalk their odds, and an elf in plain harness walks onto the Long Butts. By the end of the night the characters know what the champion is, what holds him, and where that thing lies when the Baron sleeps.

**The question of the day is jurisdiction, not evidence.** By now the characters have proof. What they need is the court that fits (the Assay, the port or the goddess's ground) and somebody with standing who is willing to walk into it. The Baron's money is the thread that unravels him, because it is not his.

## A Price on a Rooftop

<!-- covers: crowbill-names-his-price -->

At first light, Crowbill collects a character off a ledge, as he has collected people in this city for thirty years, and puts them on a cookshop roof to talk. He already knows what they did last night: the hour, the tide and how many. He does not ask what they found. That is more unsettling than any threat, and it establishes exactly how much of the city he sees.

He does not help people. He trades, and he wants something they will not enjoy giving: intelligence on the Baron's plan, a route into the camp, or control of a story the street is already telling. He has a line, and demon-craft worked on a helpless girl beside the Lady's water crosses it. Bring him proof rather than a grievance, and he opens canals, safe houses and men.

**What he can give that nobody else can is the locket.** The Baron wears a chain with a locket on it and has never been seen in daylight without it. Crowbill keeps a lamp-man on the Forge wharf who watches that ship for a living, and what the lamp-man has seen is that it comes off at night. The Baron sleeps aft with the locket on the table beside his bed, on a chain with a clasp, not a collar, and Blau sleeps across the door. Crowbill does not say *steal it*. He says what it is, where it lies, and that he knows a boy who would.

Nus is in the next room and has heard every word.

If no character goes to Crowbill, he says the same thing over breakfast to Nus anyway, as a joke. Nus does not hear it as a joke. Either way, the hinge of the whole scenario has just been set swinging, and nobody in the room has noticed.

## The Tide Master's Notes

<!-- covers: ivo-calder-folds -->

Somewhere in the middle of the week, and the first day of the Tourney is the natural place for it, a clever party goes looking for the reading that was signed off as false. That leads to Ivo Calder, the Watercrafters' Tide Master, best reached after the characters have seen the seam in Alderic Vantt and know whose money it is. He is fifty, distinguished, in guild indigo, with his instruments kept beautifully, and he has not slept properly in six weeks.

He has his whole defence prepared and wants to deliver it, and a question he has not anticipated stops him dead. What he wants is to be told that what he signed was reasonable. **There is no middle with him**: a vigorous defence, and then, the moment an independent reading appears (from Nerissa's people, from Gardwen's own senses at the water's edge, or from the Assay), everything, in one breath, with something close to relief. He has not destroyed the original notes. He has moved them twice. What his confession buys is a name, not a conviction: who is actually paying the Baron, a man who is not in this city and will not die on the sand. And his notes reopen the alarm, which puts the Order officially onto the Baron, and that is exactly the cause Nerissa needs to act on her own ground.

## The Archery

<!-- covers: pivot-the-archery -->

**This is the set piece of the day.** Two hundred paces of marked grass on the Long Butts, a crosswind off the lake that everybody complains about, the Governor's box at the near end, and half the city lying on the slope behind. It is the one open event, with no colours and no fee, which is why an elf in plain harness is standing in it.

**Temerach wins.** Let her. After three days of being stopped on the road and spoken to in front of her own escort, she puts two hundred paces of arrows where nobody else in the field can, in absolute silence. Then she has to walk up to the Governor's box, with four thousand people watching, to be handed a prize, and **the dice decide what she does with the ninety seconds she has there.** Roll her Passion to see the Baron called to answer against her Loyalty to the Swords. If her Passion wins, **she spends it**: she names, out loud, whose men stopped a King's officer on the Ashwick road and what has been done in this city this week. Thirty years of standing are converted into one public accusation the Governor cannot pretend not to have heard. It buys a hearing, and it makes her a marked woman two days early. If her Loyalty wins, **she holds it**: she takes the prize, says the correct words and walks. She is still a King's officer with a clean record and a door she can open once, colder, and far more useful later. And very rarely, if the dice say so, **she loses**: the household's archer takes the wreath, and the scene becomes the characters watching the one person with standing lose her chance to use it.

Whichever way it falls, this is the scene the last day's shot answers.

## A Price on the Face

<!-- covers: a-price-on-the-face -->

If the Baron has learned of the twins, and in the natural course of things he has (Sinnit sold them last night), then at dusk Blau puts money on the street: a purse for a name, and a bigger one for delivery. By the next dawn every dockhand, courtesan and cutpurse in Purewater has the description.

From now on the twins cannot move openly. Randall's friends on the Pearl look at him and do arithmetic. Conall's warrant stops opening doors and starts announcing him. Blau will not announce a bastard to the city; he takes one off a street at night, and he is patient. A twin taken now goes to the ship, because the Baron wants the tool before he extinguishes the claim. **This is the moment the back half of the scenario turns into a pursuit**, and it is entirely the characters' doing, which is the point.

## Gardwen Goes Aboard

<!-- covers: gardwen-goes-aboard, orrin-sculle-decides -->

She has seen her brother's face for four seconds after ten years, and she is not going to wait for the sand to see it again.

**This is the most important night of the scenario, and it should be quiet.** Twenty people are aboard the Baron's ship, at a private pier on the Forges' seaward side, its hull steaming where it meets the water. A stranger on that deck is noticed inside a minute, and since the presentation Blau has doubled the watch and stopped changing it at a fixed hour. Getting aboard is a whole operation: Crowbill's canal routes, Ruhi's nose, the dark under the pier, a party making noise at the far end of the wharf. Make it a real infiltration, Stealth against the Dragon Circle's Perception, and let it cost something.

Below the waterline aft she finds a lamp that is never put out, and a keeper asleep across a door. And then a man in his fifties comes up out of the after hold carrying an empty basin: heavy forearms, sleeves rolled past the elbow, a smell of liniment and lamp oil. This is **Orrin Sculle**, and he has been aboard longer than everybody on deck except the shipmaster. He does not shout. He sets the basin down without looking at it and tells her exactly what is supposed to happen next: he calls out, six men in mail come up the forward hatch, and nobody ashore ever hears of her again. Then he asks, *"So before I do — what are you?"*

**Nothing she can say is cleverer than the truth**, and the truth is the one thing nobody has offered him in ten years. A character who lies to him gets the six men. What decides it is not a roll on her side but **his own heart, rolled in front of the player: his Love for the boy, 70%, against his Fear of the Baron, 65%.** If Love wins, he does not move out of the way; he sits down on the coaming like a man whose legs have stopped agreeing with him. What he gives her is not access but knowledge. He tells her the boy's real name, which nobody has said aboard that ship in ten years. He tells her the thing he has never told a living soul: Gabriel holds a cup left-handed, the thing wearing him is right-handed, and every time it puts a cup down and the boy surfaces for a second, he moves it across. Then twenty minutes, boots off, and a warning: the third step complains, and the watch changes at the turn of the glass. If Fear wins, he does not betray her either, because he is not a cruel man. He tells her to get off his ship, and he means it, and he will be at the top of that companionway every time she comes back; a second approach should be harder, and possible. If both rolls are criticals or both are fumbles, he asks her to take the boy with her tonight, which she cannot do, and he will never forgive her for refusing.

Everything she learns tonight, she learns from him or from the boy. **What is in her brother:** a dragon-knight called Thuban Eta, a thousand years dead, held in a living body by a torment and a binding. **That Gabriel is still in there,** and the cup proves it. **What holds the binding:** the bones in a tower on the Baron's border estate, which hold Thuban in the world, and the locket on the Baron's chain, which is the leash, renewed every night; without it, the Baron can neither command Thuban nor move him. **What the Baron wants:** if Orrin's Love won, he tells her what he has worked out and never said, that the Baron does not want a champion, he wants the room after the bout. **And the ship's routines**: the step, the glass, and the Baron's cabin aft beside the working cabin. That is the map two thieves will use tomorrow night.

**If Thuban surfaces**, as he sometimes does in the small hours, he speaks to her with perfect, thousand-year-old courtesy in her brother's voice. He does not want to be there. He cannot leave while the bones hold him, and he cannot disobey while the locket does. Let that conversation be long, and quiet, and let the player ask him anything. It is the best scene in the book.

**She cannot take her brother tonight.** Orrin will not help, and he is right: taking the body frees nobody while the anchors hold. She leaves knowing exactly what has to be taken, and from whom. If Emmeralda is aboard, she is forward in the good cabins, bright and wrong, and calls out to anybody she sees. If Gardwen is caught, she is not killed; Blau wants to know who she is, and a captured Gardwen aboard the ship is a rescue for the party and a very bad night for her.

Orrin is a door that opens the first time anybody gets near the boy, by any route: a delivery, an invitation, a disguise, somebody else's errand. If the characters reach him before tonight, play him then. In the natural course of things, it is Gardwen who finds him.
