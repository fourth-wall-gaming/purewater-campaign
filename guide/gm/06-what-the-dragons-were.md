# What the Dragons Were

Everybody in Thornmere knows the story of the Dragon Kings, and everybody has it wrong. The history in the *Players' Guide* is what the realm believes, and it stays true as belief. This chapter is what actually happened, and it is for the Games Master alone.

## Men with Banners

**The Dragon Kings were men.** They had heraldry, legend and demonology, and nothing else. They never rode dragons, and nobody has ever found the skeleton of one, on any battlefield, because there were none to find. Each King was a man wearing one of the greatest demons there are, and the bound soldiers under them each carried a lesser one. The lesser demons ate their hosts slowly, and the bound soldiers went mad over the years, so command was held by unbound knights who were sane. Thuban Eta was one of those. He carried nothing, and he refused the order to burn the valley as a man.

**Real dragons exist.** They are so rare that the appearance of one is an event the whole realm talks about for a generation. None of them ever served a King. A real one coming out of the Hoarfells to take back its kind's name, in front of the Movement's believers, would set the record straight in a single afternoon. That is a seed for some later year, not for this week.

**The Movement's believers are wrong twice**: about the dragons, and about the Baron, who knows perfectly well that it was always demons and does not care.

## Demons

Demons are spirits from outside the world, older than it and hostile to it. Seated in living flesh they burn the host's life as fuel. That heat is where the whole costume of fire came from: the scale on Cailan's throat, the steaming hull of the Baron's ship, and the bodies in *The Drowned Trade*, which burn from inside.

| Rank | What it is | What it gives a host |
|---|---|---|
| **A Wanting** | Nameless; one hunger; it does not speak beyond that hunger | One gift. Over months or years it eats the host's self, and what is left is madness |
| **A Named** | A Wanting that has fed through many hosts and grown a name. It speaks, bargains, lies and remembers | Several gifts and a will of its own. Knowing its name makes binding it one grade easier |
| **A Crowned** | One of the six greatest, each with a following of lesser demons | Rule. The host becomes its face |

**Damnation is real.** Whoever dies with a demon seated in them does not go to rest. They go into that demon's Crowned, which keeps them. That is the true price of being an Ash Lord, and none of the Ash Lords has been told it.

## The Six Crowned

There were six Kings, and each wore one of these. Each King's Wing served his Crowned.

- **Aun, the Dragon**: elemental fire, fire that is alive and has intent. The dragon of the legends was Aun's image on its King's banner, and the whole myth begins there. The Sixth Wing served Aun, which is why its order was to burn the valley.
- **Imbra, the Never-Sated**: passions and drives, satiation and lust. The thing Santo's scroll put into Emmeralda, if it took, is one of Imbra's Wanting.
- **The Pale Tallyman**: pain, suffering, death and war. The rage the Ash Lords carry is his.
- **Ishet, the Broken Choir**: madness and incoherence.
- **Vell of the Second Face**: lies, deceit and subterfuge. Its servants are the hardest of all to find.
- **Corrow, Who Wears the Crowd**: power over others, enslavement and subjugation, and the end of the individual.

**They went into the Lake.** At Drakesfall the wild elves of the Deep Glades tore the Crowned out of the Kings and drowned them, which is what happened to Thuban done at the scale of an army, and why everybody says the age ended *at the Lake*. The Lady's water still holds them. One of them may be what is stirring in the Old Water beneath the Forges.

**Kade's ember-bone** is a relic of Aun: a finger of the Sixth Wing's King, which still holds a little of Aun's fire. It is why Kade's work in the Warm Pool burns people from inside, and why it nearly works.

## Why the Baron Chose a Ghost

His ancestors wanted to become Kings by wearing a Crowned. Hanzo will not. A Crowned in the Prince would eat him and rule in its own name, and Hanzo allows nothing above himself. A ghost obeys its anchors. So he chose a dead knight who can be leashed, and that is the difference between him and the four generations before him.

His own idea of rule, everybody subjugated and himself on top, sits very close to Corrow's domain. Corrow would erase him along with everybody else. He does not see it.

## The Thing His Father Put in Him

When Hanzo was about twelve, his father, the one who worked out the binding school, seated a **Named of Corrow** in him: a test of the rite, and an heir who could move men. Hanzo was opened as a child, as he later opened Gabriel, and he thinks of it as his making. For more than ten years it taught him how people are made to move as one, and his Influence and his warmth are partly learned from it.

At about twenty-five, he seduced a young elven mage who saw the thing with Spirit Sight and drew it out of him, and it went back where it came from. Without it, he was exactly the same man. The narcissism was always his; the demon only fed it. She was the only person who ever saw that, and he broke her for seeing him. She fled to Purewater, and her sons are the twins. Spirit Sight still shows an old closed seat on him, like a healed scar, to anybody who knows what one looks like.

## Demons, the Dead and the Lady's Magic

**Demons and the dead are different.** A demon driven out goes back where it came from, or is loose in the room. The unquiet dead have nowhere to go. Driven out of the boy, Thuban is loose and still held by his bones, which is why only the rite at Emberhold can end him.

*Classic Fantasy Imperative* has no demonology of its own, and the binding art in this book is a set of powers rather than spells. Divine magic still meets spirits in the ordinary way:

- **Spiritshield** (Cleric 0): a spirit must beat the caster's Piety with its Willpower to enter.
- **Protection from Evil** (Cleric 1): possession automatically fails.
- **Detect Evil** (Cleric 1): shows a seated demon.
- **Dispel Magic** ends spells, and does nothing to powers or to spirits.
- **Exorcism** (Cleric 3) drives out a possession whose Intensity is no more than half the spell's. Treat a seat's Intensity as the binder's POW divided by three, plus one for each anchor. Santo's seat in Emmeralda is weak, and an ordinary priest can break it. Hanzo's seat of Thuban, with the bones and the locket, is beyond any priest in the city. A spirit that wins the duel may possess the loser.

The Order of the Lake Lady has no possession magic of any kind, by doctrine.
