# Afterwards

None of the three roads ends with the Baron beaten. The best of them takes his champion away from him and puts the characters on the road to the bones. The worst leaves the Crown's nephew carrying a dragon-knight, with nobody in the kingdom willing to believe the people who know it. Purewater is the first act of a longer campaign, and every ending leaves the characters with somewhere to go next.

## After the Quiet Room

Nothing burns and nobody dies in public. The Tourney closes with its prizes, the Dragon Knights march home the next morning, and the standing order lapses without anybody quite remembering who signed it. The characters hold whatever they gathered (the scroll in the Baron's hand, Conall's finding, the Tide Master's notes) against a man who now has a prince to vouch for him.

Cailan walked out of the room on his own feet, and there is nobody in him now but Gabriel. He is seventeen and weak, in a body that has never trained, and his father has just been taken out of him and put into the Prince. He wants to go after him. That is the most hopeful thing in this ending, and also the most dangerous.

What follows is a pursuit and a conspiracy. The Prince goes home to the King's court at Caldreth wearing Thuban, among men who have been the Baron's friends for thirty years. The bones at Emberhold still hold Thuban in the world, now in a far more dangerous body.

## After the Burning

The Lullwater is ash. Nus is dead at Blau's hand, unless the characters changed who paid. The Prince was taken in the room he summoned the Baron to, and Temerach's shot wounded the Baron in front of four thousand people. Then she left the city an outlaw, with the characters.

What they carry out of Purewater:

- a wounded enemy whom the whole city saw refuse to answer;
- a King's officer who spent thirty years of standing on one arrow;
- the knowledge of what is now wearing the Prince;
- and Gabriel, if he came out of that room. He is with them, or trying to be.

The Lullwater will not forget who burned it, and Marda's three hundred, Crowbill's people and the women of the Pearl have all now seen the Baron's true face. In Purewater, at least, nobody will doubt the characters again. Ahead lie the road and the tower, and Temerach knows the border country and has nothing left to lose.

## After the Breakout

The characters kept a reckless young thief alive, and kept a locket from the most patient man in the kingdom through a day of fire, and so the Baron went into the evening without his leash. Thuban broke a decade of bondage and cut his way out through the Dragon Knights with Gabriel's body and Gabriel's voice, and left Purewater beside the woman who came aboard and spoke to him in her brother's name. The Prince is untouched. The Baron has lost his champion, his proof and his room.

What they carry:

- the locket, which they cannot use and cannot easily destroy, and which the Baron wants back more than anything in the world;
- Thuban, a courteous, grieving and terrifying ally who wants only to die properly;
- and Gabriel, two in one body with him, who knows what that death will cost him.

Ahead lies the hardest ride of the campaign, to Emberhold, with the Baron and everything he has left behind them.

## Two Worlds

The Baron came to Purewater for two things, and the Tourney decides both: the locket, which is his leash on Thuban, and the room, where his champion is presented alone and unarmed to the Prince. **If he gets both**, as on the roads of the Quiet Room and the Burning, one world follows. **If he loses either**, as in the Breakout, another does.

### The Road to the Crown

In the world where the Baron wins, he does not need a rebellion. He needs four steps, and each one is an adventure of its own.

1. **Favour.** King Ostmar names the Prince his heir and gives him command, of the March or of the royal army. Ostmar is right to be proud. Thuban's courtesy is real, and unleashed, Thuban would make a very good heir. Ostmar loves his nephew at last, now that his nephew is somebody else. The Baron rides on that trust as the Prince's oldest friend.
2. **The court.** Lady Marr's people fill the Prince's household, and Tobias Wrenn drafts what he signs. The edict against elves goes out under the King's own seal, at the Prince's urging. Vane's companies and the Ash Lords stand ready.
3. **The King dies.** A strong King cannot be deposed, so he is killed, in a way that looks natural, by a servant of Vell of the Second Face. The Prince is crowned, and makes the Baron Lord Protector.
4. **The Prince is used up,** and the Baron is crowned by a council his Ember House made.

## What Comes Next: the Bones

All three roads lead to the same place. **Emberhold** lies four days north by Ashwick and the Scorched Hills: a keep of black stone and a single tall tower above a burned valley. The valley is Thuban's. The Sixth Wing drowned him in its water and then burned it anyway, and the di Teufels built their seat over the place where their power begins. The bones are there, in a box in the tower. So is the place where his company stood, and the first di Teufel's library, which holds the rite that was never said.

Magda's dream told her to lay the dragon warrior to rest, and she has understood by now that it did not mean kill him. Gardwen came to Purewater to bring her brother home. Laying Thuban to rest is the only way to do both, and it is the next adventure. The characters already know what frees him. Somebody must stand where his company stood and say that what they did was wrong, and then a burial rite works in a moment. Unseating him from a body will not do it alone.

**The stakes depend on the world.**

- **If the Baron won,** laying Thuban to rest frees the Prince, and the whole plan falls with him. It is a counter-coup with the kingdom on it. The Baron is at court in Caldreth, and Emberhold is held by a castellan and Ash Lords. It also takes away the heir the King loves. Ostmar will fight the people trying to free his nephew, and the characters must make a strong King believe that the son he is proud of is a dead man on a leash.
- **If the Baron lost,** it is a race. He rides home behind them with everything he has left, and he wants the locket.

The adventure falls into three acts:

1. **The road north.** Ashwick and Sergeant Lysa Bell's post; Dame Hesra Kell's bounty riders; Vane's oath companies; the Scorched Hills.
2. **Emberhold.** The keep, the tower, the box of bones, and the first di Teufel's library.
3. **The water.** Standing where the Sixth Wing stood, saying it was wrong, and the rite, read and spoken by Gabriel in the old court tongue Thuban taught him. The Baron's people arrive first, or last.

**Allies:** Temerach, who knows the border; Thuban himself, after the Breakout; Gabriel; and the wild elves, who still hold the knowledge that broke the Kings. Drakesfall is a day's ride from the keep.

**Gabriel pays the price.** Laying Thuban to rest gives Gardwen her brother and takes away his father. He goes north anyway, because it is what Thuban wants.

## The Drowned Trade

*The Drowned Trade* is the other adventure that follows the Tourney, in either world. People are being taken out of the low water and sold to Severin Kade, who is trying to make Ash Lords in a night with fire worked in the Lady's own sacred water. The seller is Tide Lord Aurelie Stane, one of the Ember Society's Four, and the receipts carry Lady Marr's ember mark.

- **If the Baron lost,** it is covert, and an investigation.
- **If he won,** it is open. The "unregistered" are taken lawfully under royal warrant, Stane is the new regime's harbour officer, and the story is a resistance.

Halla is already working it, from his own side.

## Threads to Carry Forward

- **The Movement** belongs to the Baron, and the Tourney decides only how openly it moves. If he won, the Ember Society is his court. If he lost, it is where he rebuilds, and Cassian Bree is still writing his reports to Lady Marr.
- **The twins** are the Baron's only other blood. If he knows of them, he wants them: as tools first, and then with the claim extinguished.
- **Santo,** if he lives, is a frightened young man with a burnt hand and a father who has stopped forgiving him.
- **Emmeralda,** if the working took, still has a Wanting of Imbra seated in her. Santo's seat is weak, and an Exorcism or a powerful Dispel Magic will drive it out.
- **Blau** has spent a week watching the characters, and he does not forget faces. He has also spent years studying his employer's art, and a wounded or ruined Baron may be the opening he has waited for.
- **Tat Atarer** has counted the cost. A company that has seen what was behind the inner doors may not ride for the Baron again.
- **Halla, Hrill and Nimras** each have a thread running on (see *Three More Strangers*), and each thread touches the Baron's people somewhere.
- **The elves** of Purewater and the border are the Baron's chosen enemy in either world, and the people best placed to help the characters against him.
- **The Lady** has been silent for fourteen months, and Lilura still has not told anybody. You decide whether the goddess stirred when her Sword named the Baron on her steps.

## Experience

Award three Experience Rolls for completing the scenario, and one more for each of the following:

- Nus is alive at the end.
- The Lullwater did not burn.
- Marda's grievance was answered without bloodshed.
- The Pearl's rule held.
- Thuban was freed from the Baron.

Then review every character's Passions. Purewater is a story about what people love and fear, and most characters should leave it with at least one Passion changed by ten percentage points or more, and some with a new one. Ask the players which moment changed their character, and believe them.
