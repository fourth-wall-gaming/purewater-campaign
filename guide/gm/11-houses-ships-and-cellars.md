# Houses, Ships and Cellars

The scenario keeps coming back to a handful of buildings. Each has a plan, drawn so that the scenes set there can be played out on it: who is in which room, which stair is quiet, which door opens onto the water.

## The Sylph's Embrace

The Sylph's Embrace is Constantine's house on Pearl Quay: four storeys, narrow at the street and running deep back to the water, with walls thick enough to muffle the night's business. Its back rises straight out of the basin, with no path between the house and the water.

- **Ground floor.** The doorkeeper sits in the flagstoned front hall, and that is where a young man with no name buys Emmeralda's evening on the first night. Across the water side is the **salon**, with the little dancing floor where she dances. **Constantine's office** looks onto the street. In the water-side corner beside the salon is the **private water-stair**, a covered boat-slip open to the basin with a skiff kept in it. Santo means to take her out that way, and his covered skiff comes back for her there the next day.
- **First floor.** The best rooms, and Constantine's own chamber.
- **Second floor.** **Emmeralda's room** is the big room at the back, over the basin, with an armoire standing out from the wall in the corner and a big rug in the middle of the floor.
- **Attic.** The younger girls sleep in a dormitory. The cold top back room with the sticking shutter is where Magda and Gardwen lodge, with the wolf asleep by the door.

**Two stairs run through the house.** The grand stair goes from the hall to the second floor. The narrow **back stair** runs from the attic down past Emmeralda's floor to the water-stair. It is the quiet way through the whole house, and it is the one Santo runs down. Two women lodging at the top of it are bound to hear him.

## The Mother of Pearl

Marisette's house is the seat of her authority over the whole island. It is the oldest house on the quay and the only one that has never needed to advertise: a plain water-stair, a doorman who already knows who you are, and no sign of any kind. Nobody goes there until they are worth meeting. Marisette receives visitors; she does not make visits.

The doorman's lodge beside the deep front arch keeps the Widow's lamp burning in a niche. On the **first floor**, Marisette's **audience room** is small and comfortable, with one good chair by the fire for her and the others set back against the walls. She sits, and everybody else stands. Beside it are the **counting room**, where the ledgers of eleven houses are kept, and the small bare room of her enforcer, Hesper. In the **cellar**, among the vaulted storerooms, is a lock-up with a ring in the wall, where the house keeps somebody it has taken until it has decided what they are. At the far end, an old bricked arch holds a heavy door, and behind it a stair goes down into the old galleries under the city.

## The Moist Oyster

The Moist Oyster is a rough Lullwater tavern of four patched buildings around a yard on the north-western edge of the Still, under a sign of a half-open oyster shell. It offers cheap drink, loose tongues and the occasional knife fight. The big **taproom** on the street has a long bar and a hearth, and in the corner by the hearth, on a raised bench, the storyteller **Thorne** holds court most evenings. The **back room** is built out over the Still on piles, with shutters on every water side and a water-door onto a plank landing, and things go in and out of it by boat that never cross the bar. Upstairs, off a gallery round the yard, are cheap rooms for people who do not want to be found.

Conall sets out for the Oyster on his first evening with a note from Temerach, and never arrives. On the last day, if the Lullwater burns, the Oyster is one of the places the players should watch go.

## The Lists

The plan of the Tournament Ground shows the six-sided sand and its rope; the stands on all six sides, with gated gaps for the combatants east and west; the Governor's box in the western stand and the small stone presentation room behind it; the competitors' pavilions in the south-west; the horse-lines, the heralds' house and the infirmary; the Long Butts along the north wall; and the water-gate with its broad steps into the lake.

The presentation room has two doors, one from the box stair and one to the outside. Characters trying to keep the Baron out of that room, or to get into it, will want to know both.

## Temple Isle

The plan of the Lake Lady's precinct shows the temple at the north end, with its colonnade, altar dais and the channel running down the length of the floor; the cloisters, with the pilgrims' cells to the west and the High Priestess's rooms, chapter house, library and infirmary to the east; the ring of shrines around the Sacred Confluence; and the water-steps facing the Lists between two towers. The temple's ferries tie up at the steps and at a small landing on the west shore, which has a postern gate.

## The DragonBarge

The Baron's ship lies at its private pier below the Forge Tower. It is a large vessel worked all over with dragon motifs, steaming without pause where its hull meets the water. **Twenty people are aboard, and nobody else.**

- **Aft** are the Baron's quarters and his working cabin, with the locked case behind the border rolls. Blau is wherever the Baron is.
- **Forward**, Santo has the good cabins, with too much money and no duties.
- **Below the waterline aft**, by a lamp that is never put out, live the champion and his keeper, Orrin Sculle, who sleeps across the door.
- **Six of the Dragon Circle**, the Baron's inner guard, stand watch-and-watch.
- **The rest** are the shipmaster, four crew and four household: a cook, a body servant, a secretary, and a laundress who cannot get a grey residue out of the same linen every morning and has stopped mentioning it.

**A stranger on this deck is noticed inside a minute.** There is no crowd to get lost in, everybody aboard knows every face, and the ship is quiet enough at night to hear a man walk. By day, getting aboard depends on being invited. Provisions come out daily and the cook takes them at the forward gangway, letting no carrier past. A tailor, a petitioner or a woman brought out for an evening comes aboard escorted, and none of them goes aft. By night, the only way aboard is from the water: under the pier, up the side, or through the stern windows over steaming water. Gardwen does it on the first night of the Tourney, and Nus and Randall on the second.

## The Vantt Estate

Two miles east of the walls by the Causeway road, where the ground rises out of the marsh, stand Councillor Alderic Vantt's good stone house, his orchards, stables for forty, and eight acres of water-meadow running down to the channel. **The Dragon Knights are camped in the meadow**: twenty-five men, their horses, farriers and armourers, the pavilions and the tourney gear. Tat Atarer runs the camp. The Baron sleeps aboard his ship, comes out perhaps once a day, and dines in the house on the eve of the Tourney.

The estate is a rich man's farm with tents on it. It has no walls, four gates, a lane, a river frontage and a great deal of dark. Local labour goes in and out all day with bread, beer, fodder and laundry, and a farrier's boy from the village is in the horse lines constantly without anybody searching him. Twenty-five bored mercenaries three days from a tournament, drinking Vantt's beer, are far looser than the six aboard the ship. The champion's spare kit is here, and so is the litter that carried something covered from the borderlands. Vantt's household did not ask for any of this and is frightened of it. It is the soft target, and a clever party will work that out.

There is no sheet for the estate; describe it from these paragraphs.

## Beneath the City

Under the Hill quarter, and under a great deal more of the city than anybody official would like to know, run old burial galleries and older water-pools, dry, cold and chalk-white, with three centuries of the respectable dead stacked in them. Smugglers use them as hidden roads. The houses of the Pearl use them for questioning people, because nobody official goes down there and sound does not carry up. To be *taken to the catacombs* is to be in serious trouble. The untouched pools at the bottom have developed strange properties, and some believe they connect to waters older than the city.

Marisette's people bring the twins here on the second morning. **These sheets are for the Games Master only,** and nobody in the city above knows anything on them.
