# Houses, Ships and Cellars

The scenario keeps coming back to a handful of buildings. Each has a plan, and each plan is drawn so that the scenes set there can be played out on it: who is in which room, which stair is quiet, which door opens onto the water.

## The Sylph's Embrace

Constantine's house on Pearl Quay: four storeys, narrow at the street and running deep back to the water, with walls thick enough to muffle the night's business. Its back rises straight out of the basin, with no path between the house and the water.

On the **ground floor**, the flagstoned front hall is where the doorkeeper sits and where a young man with no name buys Emmeralda's evening on the first night. Across the water side, the **salon** with its little dancing floor is where she dances. **Constantine's office** looks onto the street. In the water-side corner beside the salon is the **private water-stair**, a covered boat-slip open to the basin with a skiff kept in it. That is where Santo means to take her out, and where his covered skiff comes back for her the next day. The best rooms and Constantine's own chamber are on the **first floor**. **Emmeralda's room** is the big room at the back of the **second floor**, over the basin, with the armoire standing out from the wall in the corner and a big rug in the middle of the floor. In the **attic**, the younger girls sleep in a dormitory, and the cold top back room with the sticking shutter is where Magda and Gardwen lodge, with the wolf asleep by the door.

**Two stairs run through the house.** The grand stair goes from the hall to the second floor. The narrow **back stair** runs from the attic down past Emmeralda's floor to the water-stair. It is the quiet way through the whole house, and the one Santo runs down. Two women lodging at the top of it are bound to hear him.

## The Mother of Pearl

Marisette's house, and through it the seat of her authority over the whole island: the oldest house on the quay and the only one that has never needed to advertise. It has a plain water-stair, a doorman who already knows who you are, and no sign of any kind. You do not go there until you are worth meeting. She receives; she does not travel.

The doorman's lodge beside the deep front arch keeps the Widow's lamp burning in a niche. On the **first floor**, Marisette's **audience room** is small and comfortable, with one good chair by the fire for her and the others set back against the walls. She sits; everybody else stands. Beside it are the **counting room**, where the ledgers of eleven houses are kept, and the small bare room of her enforcer, Hesper. In the **cellar**, among the vaulted storerooms, is a lock-up with a ring in the wall, where the house keeps somebody it has taken until it has decided what they are. At the far end, an old bricked arch holds a heavy door, and behind it a stair goes down into the old galleries under the city.

## The Moist Oyster

A rough Lullwater tavern of four patched buildings around a yard on the north-western edge of the Still, its sign a half-open oyster shell. Cheap drink, loose tongues and the occasional knife fight. The big **taproom** on the street has a long bar and a hearth, and in the corner by the hearth, on a raised bench, the storyteller **Thorne** holds court most evenings. The **back room** is built out over the Still on piles, with shutters on every water side and a water-door onto a plank landing, and things go in and out of it by boat that never cross the bar. Upstairs, off a gallery round the yard, are cheap rooms for people who do not want to be found.

Conall comes here on his first evening with a note from Temerach and never arrives. On the last day, if the Lullwater burns, the Oyster is one of the places the players should watch go.

## The Lists

The Tournament Ground in plan: the six-sided sand and its rope, the stands on all six sides with gated gaps for the combatants east and west, the Governor's box in the western stand and the small stone presentation room behind it, the competitors' pavilions in the south-west, the horse-lines, the heralds' house, the infirmary, the Long Butts along the north wall, and the water-gate with its broad steps into the lake. Note the presentation room's two doors, one from the box stair and one to the outside. Characters trying to keep the Baron out of that room, or get into it, will want to know both.

## Temple Isle

The precinct of the Lake Lady in plan: the temple at the north end with its colonnade, altar dais and channel running down the length of the floor; the cloisters, with the pilgrims' cells to the west and the High Priestess's rooms, the chapter house, library and infirmary to the east; the ring of shrines around the Sacred Confluence; and the water-steps facing the Lists between two towers. The temple's ferries tie up at the steps and at a small landing on the west shore, with a postern gate.

## The DragonBarge

The Baron's ship lies at its private pier below the Forge Tower, a large vessel worked all over with dragon motifs, steaming continuously where its hull meets the water. **Twenty people are aboard, and that is the whole of it.** The Baron's quarters and his working cabin are aft, with the locked case behind the border rolls. Santo has the good cabins forward, with too much money and no duties. Blau is wherever the Baron is. Below the waterline aft, by a lamp that is never put out, live the champion and his keeper, Orrin Sculle, who sleeps across the door. Six of the Dragon Circle, the Baron's inner guard, stand watch-and-watch, and the shipmaster, four crew and four household make up the rest: a cook, a body servant, a secretary, and a laundress who cannot get a grey residue out of the same linen every morning and has stopped mentioning it.

**A stranger on this deck is noticed inside a minute.** There is nowhere to be lost in a crowd, everybody aboard knows every face, and the ship is quiet enough at night to hear a man walk. Getting aboard by day is an invitation problem, not a stealth problem. Provisioning comes out daily and is taken at the forward gangway by the cook, who lets no carrier past. A tailor, a petitioner or a woman brought out for an evening comes aboard escorted, and none of them goes aft. Getting aboard by night means the water: under the pier, up the side, or through the stern windows over steaming water. Gardwen does it on the first night of the Tourney, and Nus and Randall on the second.

## The Vantt Estate

Two miles east of the walls by the Causeway road, where the ground rises out of the marsh, stand Councillor Alderic Vantt's good stone house, his orchards, stables for forty, and eight acres of water-meadow running down to the channel. **The Dragon Knights are camped in the meadow**: twenty-five men, their horses, farriers and armourers, the pavilions and the tourney gear. Tat Atarer runs the camp. The Baron sleeps aboard his ship and comes out perhaps once a day, and dines in the house on the eve of the Tourney.

It is a rich man's farm with tents on it, not a fortress. It has no walls, four gates, a lane, a river frontage and a great deal of dark. Local labour is in and out all day with bread, beer, fodder and laundry, and a farrier's boy from the village is in the horse lines constantly without anybody searching him. Twenty-five bored mercenaries three days from a tournament, drinking Vantt's beer, are far looser than the six aboard the ship. The champion's spare kit is here, and so is the litter that carried something covered from the borderlands. Vantt's household did not ask for any of this and are frightened of it. It is the soft target, and a clever party works that out.

There is no sheet for the estate; describe it from this paragraph.

## Beneath the City

Under the Hill quarter, and under a great deal more of the city than anybody official would like to know, run old burial galleries and older water-pools, dry, cold and chalk-white, with three centuries of the respectable dead stacked in them. Smugglers use them as hidden roads, and the houses of the Pearl use them for questions, because nobody official goes down there and sound does not carry up. To be *taken to the catacombs* is to be in serious trouble. The untouched pools at the bottom have developed strange properties, and some believe they connect to waters older than the city.

Marisette's people bring the twins here on the second morning. **These sheets are for the Games Master only,** and nothing on them is known to the city above.
