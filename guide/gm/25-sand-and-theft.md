# Sand and Theft

*The second day of the Tourney.* The champion comes out of the litter at last and wins, and the Baron, for the first time in four generations, goes to bed easy. That night the chain comes off his neck.

## The Champion Takes the Sand

<!-- covers: the-champion-takes-the-sand-and-wins -->

**This is what the whole week has been running toward, and it is a real fight, not a formality.**

The stands are full by mid-morning, four thousand people on the timber and the slope behind, with the Governor's box hung in lake-green and the Prince in it, and the bookmakers outside the east wall have the Baron's champion at odds nobody likes. The champion comes out of the competitors' ground in full harness with the helm down, and the helm stays down; nobody sees his face on the sand. He is seventeen and thin, and he fights like a knight of the dragon wars, because he is one. Thuban Eta does the fighting, and Gabriel is not there for it, which is the nearest thing to mercy in his life. He wins each bout in under a minute. In the final he breaks his opponent's leg in three places, and the crowd goes quiet instead of loud.

**He wins.** He is meant to win; everything depends on it. Run the bouts with Cailan's body and Thuban's Combat Style, using the host's hit locations and armour. Everything that can stop him is hard, which is as it should be:

- **Beat him on the sand.** Nobody entered can. Temerach could, but she is entered in the Archery, and the Single Combat closed its lists on the eve.
- **Have the match stopped.** Nerissa can halt a bout on sacred ground if she has cause she can defend in front of the city: a demon-marked blade on her altar, Ivo Calder's notes, Conall's finding. She stops it once, publicly, and the Baron must answer for it before the Prince. That does not end his plan, but it moves the room, and it puts every eye in Purewater on the champion's helm.
- **Reach Thuban.** Gardwen's voice at the rope, or Speak with the Bound, can make the thing in the harness hesitate for one Combat Round. Thuban does not want to win, but he cannot disobey.

When the last bout is over, **the Baron lets it show.** For the first time all week, a hand rests on the champion's shoulder, he bows to the Governor's box, and the warm, easy man goes home to sleep well. Tomorrow at the closing he walks into the room. That ease is what makes tonight possible.

## The Rite Behind the Sealed Door

<!-- covers: the-nightly-reinforcement -->

Every night, behind sealed doors in the working cabin aft of the Baron's ship, the Baron renews the binding: a cut, a bowl, the locket in his hand, and an hour of words. The laundress cannot get the grey residue out of the same linen every morning and has stopped mentioning it.

It is offscreen unless characters have got inside the ship, which is a whole operation of its own. Anybody close enough to hear it learns the two most important facts in the scenario. The champion is a prisoner, and the thing inside him does not want to be there: Thuban surfaces during the rite and is heard, once, speaking in another man's voice, a betrayed knight enslaved through the torment of that betrayal. **Tonight it matters twice over,** because when it is done, the Baron takes the chain off, lays the locket on the table beside his bed, and sleeps, and that is the hour two thieves go in over the stern.

## Two Thieves and a Table

<!-- covers: nus-lifts-the-binding-locket -->

**This is the hinge of the whole scenario.**

The champion has won, and the Baron has come back to his ship with victory in his grasp, renewed the seat, taken the chain off, laid the locket on the table beside his bed and gone to sleep. In the dead hour after the rite, **Nus and Randall go aboard.** Nus has wanted one big score since Crowbill named the locket yesterday morning. Randall is the only thief in the city who has been mistaken for a di Teufel, and can stand on that deck for three seconds without anybody shouting.

What they need is a way in, a way into the cabin, and a pair of hands. **The way in** is the map Gardwen brought back from her night aboard, if the party has it: the third step that complains, the turn of the glass, the Baron's cabin aft. Without it, the doubled watch makes every Stealth roll one grade harder. **The way into the cabin** is not the door, because Blau sleeps across it. It is the stern windows, from a boat, over steaming water. **The table** holds a locket on a chain with a clasp. It is not welded on, and it is not on the Baron's neck, and that is the whole point of doing it tonight.

**Roll it for real.** It takes Stealth against the watch, Athletics for the stern, Sleight for the table, and Perception for Blau if anything is fumbled. If it fails, they are seen and run with nothing, or are caught, and the locket stays where it is: the Baron walks into the room tomorrow, and the last day takes its first road. If it succeeds, they are over the side with the locket before anybody knows, and the Baron wakes at first light to an empty table.

Play the moment the locket is in Nus's hand. It is small, and warm, and the most valuable thing in the kingdom. Then remind the players what it is and is not. **It is the leash.** Without it in his hand, the Baron cannot command Thuban, cannot draw him out of the boy and cannot seat him in anybody else. It is a robust working, and destroying it would take a powerful Dispel Magic that nobody in the party can cast. It does not free Thuban from the world: the bones do that, and they are four days away. **And holding it gives nobody any power over Thuban.** It only takes him away from the Baron.

This only happens if somebody points them at it, and in the natural course of things Crowbill does. If the characters have deliberately stopped Nus, the theft does not happen. That is a legitimate story, and it leads to the first ending.

## Santo Comes Himself

<!-- covers: santo-comes-for-it-himself -->

If the characters are still holding Santo's evidence, whether the coat, the scroll or the knife, then tonight, while the thieves are on the water, Santo comes for it himself, because he cannot report the loss to his father twice. (If he recovered everything on the second day, there is nothing for him to come for, and this does not happen.)

He is pathetic and lethal at once. **He does not run when he panics.** He takes hold of whoever is nearest (a servant, a girl, a thirteen-year-old like Ila) because it has always worked for him and nobody has ever made it stop working. He has his rapier, his fire and nothing to lose that he understands. If he dies tonight, the Baron loses his only legitimate heir, and if anybody connects the twins' face to the dead heir, the Baron's hunt for them begins at once, if it has not already: with Santo gone, the twins are the only di Teufel blood walking. And Blau, whose whole job was the boy, stops cleaning up and starts hunting.
