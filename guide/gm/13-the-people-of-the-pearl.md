# The People of the Pearl

The Pearl is where the city's powerful come to be indiscreet, so it holds more of Purewater's secrets than anywhere else. It is also where the scenario opens, with a crime committed under one of its roofs. Eleven houses answer to one woman, and every one of them keeps the Widow's rule.

## Marisette

*Immaculate, ageless in the way money buys, never dressed louder than the room. Sits while others stand.*

Marisette governs eleven houses on nothing but the belief that she is inevitable, and she knows exactly how thin that is. She has no force behind her. Her authority is the settled expectation, held by everybody including her rivals, that she will still be there afterwards. She came up through the same rooms her girls work in now, and she does not discuss it. She is not ashamed of it; surviving those rooms is simply the one thing she has never been able to put to use. She is gracious, unhurried and privately amused by most things, and she forgives almost anything in somebody who is competent.

> "Sit down. You have been standing since you came in and it is making my girls nervous."

**Her hospitality is the danger signal.** When she offers a chair, a drink or another hour of her time, the warmer the room goes, the worse the character's position has become.

**This week** a woman has been wronged with demon-craft under a roof Marisette is responsible for, and she wants a name, and then a reckoning every house in the city hears about. She does not wait for the watch. She has Hesper, and the only intelligence network in Purewater that hears what men say when they are not being careful, and she grabs first and asks afterwards. That is how Randall, and a stranger with Randall's face, end up tied to chairs in the catacombs on the second morning.

She is fair in her way. Shown the truth, she acts on it, even against a baron. Her strongest weapon is withdrawal. On Constantine's complaint she closes every house on the Pearl to the di Teufel retinue, and only to them, so that the whole city reads it as the Pearl taking a side.

## Hesper

*Plain dark clothes, good boots, empty hands, a face you would lose in a crowd of four.* Hesper is neither man nor woman and uses they/them. Everyone on the Pearl knows it, and nobody remarks on it.

To Purewater, Hesper is Marisette's mage-enforcer: cold, quiet and dangerous, able to drop a strong man with a single spoken word. The menace is a costume they put on in the morning. Underneath, Hesper is an intelligencer for **the Quiet Hand**, a hidden fellowship centuries old that steers the world toward the good by knowing first and acting unseen, and has kept its power by never once being described.

Hesper is very calm, very quick and privately entertained by the world. They enjoy precision the way other people enjoy music. They are far kinder than the work requires, and they think of that kindness as their one professional weakness, because gratitude produces descriptions, and a described intelligencer is a dead one.

> "I am going to help you, and you are not going to tell anyone that I did."

**This week** Hesper takes both men with the face before dawn on the second day, as Marisette orders, without a fight worth the name. They are also working in secret against the Awake the Dragon Movement, and they are the best hidden patron the characters could find, if the characters ever see past the costume. They will never take credit or seize power, and they will spare whoever they can. Under pressure the warmth goes and only economy is left: shorter sentences, exact numbers, no adjectives at all. Then they will tell you the true and unpleasant thing rather than let you walk off comfortable.

## Constantine

*Forties, strong forearms, sleeves shoved past the elbow. Good cloth worn like working clothes.*

The proprietor of the Sylph's Embrace has run a house of other people's daughters for twenty years, and has concluded that everybody in this trade is sold by somebody in the end. She has decided, absolutely, that she will not be the one who does the selling. She is exact and dry, and warm once you are inside her door. She likes competence and dislikes charm. She feeds people without being asked, swears like the dock she grew up on, and does not moderate it for priests.

> "I have buried four girls out of this house. I did the books for every one of them."

**This week** she took Emmeralda's evening money from what she thought was Randall, the Pearl's own thief, playing the gentleman in a borrowed coat, and thought nothing of it. She has known Randall since he was a boy, and one proper look would have told her: this man was human where Randall is half-elven, and a year or two younger. She spends the rest of the week wishing she had taken it. When something goes wrong in her house she becomes precise about the record: what was actually said, on what day, in whose hearing, because the record is the one thing that has never failed her. She carries the complaint to Marisette. She wants justice and she is hiring protection, and she will want to know what the characters are going to cost her before she lets them any further in.

## Emmeralda

*Elven, poised, the finest performer on the Pearl, and vain about her hair.*

For fifteen years Emmeralda has been wanted by everybody and asked what she wants by almost nobody, and she has been counting down, privately, to the year she gets to stop. She is light, quick and charming for a living, and warmer than the living requires. She is kind to the younger girls and feeds every cat on the street. She cannot add up. She answers the question you actually came with rather than the one you asked, and she does it gently. When she stops performing, what is underneath is plain, flat and much younger.

> "Everyone in this room wants something off me. You are the first one all week who hasn't decided what yet."

**The dice on the first night decide what she is.**

- **If Santo's working failed,** she is cut open the length of her body and tainted by whatever answered his call. Without help she dies before dawn. With help she lives, frightened of a face three men wear.
- **If the working took,** she is bright and pliant and wrong, and she wants to go to the Baron's barge. The thing inside her is a passion spirit, a lesser demon of the Crowned called Imbra. It is patient and charming and keeps asking for the window to be opened.

Either way, if anybody asks her about Santo before that night, she says he is not dangerous the way the dockers are dangerous; he is dangerous the way a boy with a lit lamp in a stable is dangerous. Check which version of events you are in before you play her.

## Ila

*Thirteen, elven, thin. Sits where she can see the front door.*

Ila is a child at the Sylph's Embrace, devoted to Emmeralda. Adults who are managing their own grief badly have told her to be somewhere else for four days, and she has decided, entirely on her own, to do something about it. She has no social reflexes at all. She does not move aside, does not soften things, and does not pretend to be all right. She was the one who found Emmeralda, and nobody has asked her a single question about it.

> "You're all talking about who did it. I know which stair he went down."

Ila is a witness nobody thinks to ask, and a likely hostage. When Santo panics he takes hold of whoever is nearest, and a thirteen-year-old who will not stay out of the way is usually the nearest person in the house. Give her a job, however small, and she will be loyal to whoever gave it.

## Ravella

*Half-merfolk to anybody who looks properly: faint scales at the hairline, slightly webbed fingers, eyes that catch the water-light.*

The proprietor of the Siren's Call runs a pleasure house and an information market in one, and upstairs a house full of hidden waterfolk whose existence is illegal. She has decided that the safest cover in the world is to be the most visible, sociable and harmless creature in the district. She is delighted, exhaustingly so, by gossip, by new people, by a good glass and by the idea that you have come to see her. She is the most likeable person on the water, and about a third of it is armour.

> "Oh, that is a good question. Let me get you a better chair before you ask me the next one."

She wants to know everything before anybody else does, because knowing first is how her people stay ahead. Under pressure she becomes a better host: more wine, more attention, an introduction to somebody far more interesting. By the end of it the character has forgotten what they came to ask.

## Asphodel and Mariarta

The **Promise of Heaven** is the poorest house on the quay and the one that raised Randall, and two of its women are his family in all but blood.

**Asphodel** is warm, quick, bossy about small things, always slightly underdressed for the weather, and enormous fun. She touches people while she talks to them and feeds them within a minute of their arrival. She loves Randall like a brother. She knows exactly what he is, and she has accepted that knowing does not mean she can stop him. She has already worked out how this ends and has not let herself say it out loud. When she is frightened, the bustling stops dead and she holds on to a sleeve, and nothing anybody says helps.

> "Eat that. Then tell me the version you were going to tell me, and then the real one."

**Mariarta** is older and composed, and in a room full of soldiers nobody notices her, which is her whole skill. The house sends her up to entertain the Baron's men, and she brings back the hard intelligence on his people: who guards what, who drinks, who talks. She repeats a question back before answering it, so that she is sure she is answering the one that was asked. She does not hide what it has cost her to be in those rooms, and she does not offer it either.

> "He said it twice. The second time he was not talking to me, which is why I believed it."

**This week** the Promise of Heaven is Randall's safe house, and after Sinnit's lies on the second day, its door opens to him a little more slowly. How these two treat him is the best measure of what the week has cost him.
