# Playing Mythras

*Classic Fantasy Imperative* is built on *Mythras*, and *Mythras* is simple at heart. This is the short version, enough to play your first session. The Games Master will teach the rest as it comes up, and the full rules are free.

## Skills Are Percentages

Everything your character can do is a skill with a percentage, and to do something you roll percentile dice (two ten-sided dice, 1d100) and try to roll **equal to or under** the skill. An Athletics of 65% succeeds on a roll of 01 to 65.

- **A critical success** is a roll of one-tenth of the skill or less, rounded up. With Athletics 65%, that is 01 to 07. Something spectacular happens.
- **A fumble** is a roll of 99 or 00. Something goes badly wrong.

When a task is harder or easier than usual, the Games Master sets a **Difficulty Grade**. Easy adds half your skill again. Hard takes off a third of it. Formidable halves it, and Herculean leaves you a fifth. Very Easy doubles it.

When you are up against somebody, whether you are sneaking past a guard or arguing with a councillor, you make an **opposed roll**. Both of you roll, and the better level of success wins: a critical beats a success, which beats a failure. If both of you succeed, the higher roll that still succeeded wins.

## Passions

Your character has **Passions**: the people, causes and things they love, hate, fear or are loyal to, each with a percentage like a skill. They are the best part of *Mythras*, and they work in four ways:

- **They make you better.** When a Passion is truly at stake in what you are doing, it adds a fifth of its value to the skill you are using. A Love (Brother) of 80% adds 16% to anything you do for him.
- **They make you act.** When it matters, the Games Master may ask you to roll a Passion to see whether your character can hold back, or walk away.
- **They fight each other.** When two of your Passions pull in different directions, roll them against each other, and the winner decides what you do.
- **They protect you.** When somebody tries to bend your will by magic or manipulation into acting against a Passion, you may roll the Passion instead of your Willpower to resist.

Passions change in play. Expect yours to.

## Luck

Your character has a few **Luck Points**, which return at the start of each session. Spend one to re-roll any roll of your own, or to swap the two dice (a 75 becomes a 57), or to make an opponent re-roll a blow against you. You can also spend one to get an extra Action Point in a fight when you have run out, or to turn a Major Wound into a Serious one. Only one Luck Point may be spent on any one action. Use them. They are there to keep heroes alive.

## Fighting

*Mythras* fights are fast, brutal and decided in a few blows, so choose them carefully.

- **Initiative.** At the start of a fight everybody rolls 1d10 and adds their Initiative Bonus, and acts in order from highest to lowest.
- **Action Points.** Everybody gets a few Action Points each Combat Round of five seconds, two or three for beginning characters. An attack costs one. So does a reaction, such as a **Parry** with your weapon or shield, or an **Evade** to get out of the way. Spend them wisely: if you attack twice, you have nothing left to defend with.
- **Combat Style.** You fight with your Combat Style skill, which covers the weapons your character has trained in.
- **Special Effects.** When an attacker and a defender both roll, and one of them does better than the other, the winner gets one or more **Special Effects**: choosing the hit location, disarming, tripping, bleeding, impaling, and many more. This is where *Mythras* combat comes alive. A good parry can disarm the man who swung at you.
- **Hit Locations and Armour.** A blow strikes one of seven locations (the legs, abdomen, chest, arms or head), each with its own Hit Points and armour. Damage is reduced by a parry, and then by armour. A location taken to zero is a Serious Wound, and one taken below zero by its full Hit Points is a Major Wound, which can maim or kill.

## Magic

In *Classic Fantasy Imperative*, magic belongs to your class. **Magic-users** cast arcane spells with the Arcane Casting skill and need a spell book. **Clerics** cast divine spells with the Channel skill, by prayer and their holy symbol, and **druids** draw on the old powers of the wild in the same way. Casting spends **Magic Points**, which return with rest. Magic-users cannot cast effectively in armour.

## Getting Better

At the end of a session, or at the end of the scenario, the Games Master gives out **Experience Rolls**. For each one, pick a skill and roll 1d100. If you roll **over** your current skill, it improves, so the things you are worst at get better fastest. Passions change through play rather than through rolls.

## The Most Important Rule

Say what your character is trying to do, and why. The Games Master will tell you what to roll. In Purewater, the best tools are rarely swords: a well-placed question, the right person told the right thing, a boat at the right stair, and knowing when to swim.
