# Choosing Your Character

Four people are converging on Purewater in the three days before the Tourney, and none of them knows about the others. Read all four before you choose. Each comes with a line saying what kind of game they give you, and each comes with a full character sheet.

The three you do not choose are not gone. They stay in the world, played by the Games Master, and you will meet them.

## Gardwen, the Woman from the Woods

Gardwen is a feral-raised elf of the border woods, a long way from anything she recognises, in a city with no earth in it. Ten years ago Dragon Knights came through her family's steading at night, and she has been following those colours ever since: patiently, alone, across a great deal of country, on the strength of one thing she believes that nobody else does. Her little brother's body was never among the dead.

She is a divine caster of nature and pure water: healing, green things, the old quiet powers. A she-wolf called **Ruhi** walks beside her. Ruhi is not a pet and not tame, and goes where Gardwen goes. Gardwen is direct, and cold, dirt and long waits do not bother her. She has no small talk and no social armour whatsoever, and she helps without being asked and without announcing it. Nobody in Purewater wants anything as badly as she does, and in ten years nobody has believed her.

*Play her if you want the emotional centre of the story, and a reason to care before anybody has explained anything.*

<!-- box -->
**Your opening.** Three weeks ago you picked up a column of Dragon Knights on the north road, twenty or thirty riding under di Teufel colours toward a tournament in a canal city, and you have followed at a distance ever since. Now the column has crossed the ford ahead of you and you are stuck in the tourney crowd behind it, watching the last of the dragon-scale disappear toward the city gate. There is a covered litter in the middle of them that the outriders will not let anyone near. And an old woman leaning on a staff three places back in the queue has been staring at you for some time.

## Magda, the Old Woman Who Has Buried Everybody

Magda is sixty-odd and built like a gatepost, with grey hair cut short and badly with her own knife, and ringmail under a travelling cloak. She is a barbarian of the woodland faith and a sworn servant of **Hel**, the death-goddess, who does not let her age, and who sends her dreams she would rather not have.

She has outlived every single person she started with. She is blunt and unsentimental, rude to people she likes and unfailingly polite to people she is considering hitting, and a great deal funnier than she lets on. She has an enormous appetite for food, for sleep and for sitting down, and she takes a plain pleasure in doing a physical job properly. In a fight she is terrifying out of all proportion to her age. Outside one, she is the most reliable adult in the story.

*Play her if you want to be the one who is not frightened, and to find out what that costs.*

<!-- box -->
**Your opening.** For eleven nights the death-goddess has put the same dream behind your eyes: a young woman with a wolf, a burning house, and a knight in dragon-scale who is already dead and does not know it. Hel does not explain. Hel points. The dream said *lay the dragon warrior to rest*. It did not say *kill*, and you have spent three weeks of walking turning that over. This morning you are at the ford outside Purewater in a slow queue of tourney traffic, and there, three places ahead of you, is the woman from the dream, wolf and all. You know Purewater a little, from years ago: enough to know which madame on the Pearl rents rooms to women travelling alone.

## Randall, the Pearl Thief

Randall is Purewater-born, and he was raised in one of the pleasure houses of the Pearl by women who are still, all of them together, his family. He knows every roof, ledge, water-stair and back door on that island and most of the rest of the city besides, and he has never in his life been more than four minutes from somebody who would hide him.

He is a thief of the practical sort: locks, pockets, other people's routes, and a very good read of a room. He is quick and charming, and much softer-hearted than the trade rewards. He pays the Pearl back because the Pearl raised him, and he does not discuss his mother. A young pickpocket called Nus will do anything he asks, which Randall should probably think about more than he does.

He is the only one of the four who is **home**, so he has more to lose here than anybody, and he can call in favours nobody else can reach.

*Play him if you want the city itself to be your character sheet.*

<!-- box -->
**Your opening.** Tourney week is the best earning of the year: the city fills with strangers who carry their purses where you can reach them. It is mid-morning in Caravan Square, the crowd is thick enough to work, and you have had a decent hour. Then the crowd turns, the way a crowd does, and the Dragon Knights come through the gate: twenty-odd riders in scale, a covered litter in the middle of them, and at their head a lord who does not look at anybody. Every eye in the square goes to the litter. Nobody is watching their purse.

## Conall Bjornlasch, the Warranted Investigator

Conall is a militiaman's son out of Ashwick who should have died in a ditch at nineteen. Instead a knight of the Swords of the Lake pulled him out of it, taught him to read, and then taught him a good deal more. The **King's Assay** finished the job, and gave him a warrant, a thumb-ring and a steel punch with his own mark on it. He investigates things other people would rather write off as bad luck.

He is an arcane practitioner of the investigative sort, with find-magic, illusion and a borrowed face when the work needs one, and he is a serious fighter, which surprises people who have decided he is a clerk. He rides in beside his patron, the elf knight **Temerach Nebulo**, on an errand she has declined to explain, and she has been unusually quiet for two days.

He is the only one of the four who arrives with **standing**. A warrant opens doors that would stay shut for anybody else, and it also means he has something to lose by using it wrongly.

*Play him if you want to investigate, and to have the authority to make people answer.*

<!-- box -->
**Your opening.** You are an hour out of Purewater on the Ashwick road, riding beside Temerach, who has described this trip as "an errand" in a tone that did not invite questions. Then the Dragon Knights overtake you: twenty-odd riders in scale under di Teufel colours, moving at a pace that says they own the road, with a covered litter in the middle of the column and its curtains laced shut. The outriders wave you into the verge. Temerach watches them pass without a word, and you notice her hand does not leave her reins. Your patron is keeping something from you. You can look at the litter, look at her, or ask the question she has been avoiding.
