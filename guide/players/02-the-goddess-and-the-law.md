# The Goddess and the Law

## The Water Is the Goddess

The channel comes down out of the lake and through the temple on Temple Isle, and the priestesses of the **Lake Lady** say the water *is* the goddess, and mean it plainly. By ancient writ, the Order owns the water: every channel and pool is hers. The isles belong to the city council.

That matters more than it sounds. It means the most sacred ground in Purewater is also its traffic system, and it gives the Order a standing that no council can overrule and no lord can buy.

Two houses serve the goddess, and **neither can give the other an order**:

- **The Order of the Lake Lady** is the priesthood: temples, sacred pools, rites, tithes, the calendar, and the Tourney. It is a civic power with property and politics, and it is bound by **temple law**, which forbids its clergy to intervene directly in the affairs of the city unless the sacred sites themselves are threatened. Its priestesses wear blue-green worked with silver waves. Its High Priestess on Temple Isle is **Nerissa**.
- **The Swords of the Lake** are a knightly order sworn to the goddess herself, not to her clergy. They are austere, realm-wide, own nothing by rule, and hold the oldest battle honours of the uprising against the Dragon Kings. They take no tithes, keep no temples, and do not answer to the Governor's council.

The two houses are not enemies. They are more like two branches of one family who love each other and cannot spend a week under the same roof. They keep old courtesies: any Sword may claim bread, water and a bed at any of the Lady's temples, and it is never refused; any priestess travelling may claim a Sword's protection, and a Sword may not decline. But in four hundred years neither house has developed a polite way to ask the other a favour.

**People say the water has been wrong since Candlemas**: warm in the eastern yards, a colour in the channels near the Forges that it should not be. The Order raised an alarm about it, and the alarm was officially found to be a false reading.

## Once There Were Dragon Kings

They are gone, and every child knows it. A thousand years ago the Dragon Kings ruled Thornmere with fire and dominion, served by dragon-knights, and they were thrown down in a great uprising in which the Swords of the Lake won their oldest honours. The older powers of the water (the merfolk, the spirits of lake and sea, and the goddess the city calls the Lake Lady) stood against them then. What is left of the Dragon Kings now is old armour on border lords, a dragon on a baron's banner, and a small number of people who would like the old power back badly enough to pay for it.

## The King's Justice

Justice in Thornmere flows from **King Ostmar** at Caldreth, and nowhere else. In a city it flows through the King's representative, who in Purewater is **Prince Emeric**, the King's nephew and Governor, who may act in the King's name, here and now, on his own word. Everything else is administration: the watch, the guilds, the port, the Assay. Administration can record, refuse, impound, close a workshop and turn a man away from a berth. It cannot touch a lord.

**Not everybody may accuse a lord.** A dockhand's word against a baron is not a small case, it is no case. To call a lord to answer, a person needs **standing**: another lord, a knight of a recognised order, a Crown officer under warrant within their charter, a priest within her faith, or a guild master within his guild. And to accuse a great lord and fail is to answer for it yourself. A lord who is accused may also refuse the matter and offer trial by arms, his own or his champion's.

**The city watch** is about ninety men and women in blue-grey, competent at drunks, thieves and canal disputes. They answer to the Governor's council.

## The King's Assay

The **Assay of Thornmere**, universally *the Assay*, is the Crown's chartered body for licensing, inspecting and recording the practice of magic. It was founded after the uprising for the plainest of reasons: the realm had just finished a war against men who ruled by magic, and it never intended to be surprised by that again. Its assayers carry a warrant, a silver thumb-ring and a small steel punch bearing their own mark, which they strike into what they inspect. Every licensed practitioner and every made thing is written down in **the Register**. Working magic outside it is an offence, and some kinds of magic are hanging matters.

The Assay's doctrine is *establish, record, refer*: an assayer investigates and writes it down, and the Table at Caldreth decides what to do. Its warrant opens a great many doors. It is worth much more with a watch sergeant or a harbourmaster than with any criminal in Purewater.
