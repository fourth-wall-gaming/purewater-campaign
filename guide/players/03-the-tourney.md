# The Lake Lady's Tourney

The Tourney comes round once a year, and it is the year's holy business: three days of contests in the goddess's honour, older than the Governor's office, drawing competitors from across the realm. For the week of it the city fills, everybody makes a year's money in six days, and the law goes quiet.

It is three days away.

## Before the Tourney

**Arrival.** Competitors, their households and their hangers-on pour in through Caravan Square for days beforehand, and every bed in the city is let.

**The presentation of the entrants.** On the eve of the Tourney, by long form, every entrant in every event is presented to the Prince on the Tournament Ground: names read, colours shown, and each competitor bare-headed for the space of a bow. It is ceremony, it is tedious, and the whole city half-watches it from the stands. Nobody can skip it without forfeiting their entry.

**The blessing.** Before they fight, combatants and the heads of their houses go down to the Lady's water on the Temple Isle steps for her blessing.

## The Three Days

**The Archery** comes first: two hundred paces of marked grass on the Long Butts, with a crosswind off the lake that everybody complains about and nobody shoots well against, the Governor's box at the near end, and half the city lying on the slope behind. It is **the one open event.** Anybody may shoot, with no colours and no fee, which makes it the only place all week where a person of no standing can stand in front of the Governor, be handed something in public, and be owed a hearing for as long as the crowd is watching.

**The Single Combat** comes on the second day, and it is the only martial event: one-on-one bouts on the raked sand of the Lists, in front of four thousand people. Placed fighters win guild contracts. The champion wins the old honours of the Tourney, and is honoured by the Prince at the closing.

**The Run** comes on the third day, and it is the only event that leaves the Tournament Ground. Competitors go out through the water-gate and race the whole city, on foot and by boat: along the North Quarter, over the Triumph Bridge to Caravan Square and back, through the Merchant's Quarter and the docks, over the Fishmarket Bridge into the Shoals, across to the bridgeless Lullwater by any means they can find, and back over open water to the temple steps. No route is laid down and no help is forbidden. Half the city is a course and the other half is a crowd, and for two hours nobody can keep order anywhere. Cash and local fame go to the winner, and it is said that the city's criminals and captains watch the Run very carefully for talent.

**The closing** follows at dusk on the last day, on the temple steps and in the Governor's box: the prizes, the speeches and the champion's honours.

## The Odds

The bookmakers outside the Tournament Ground's east wall will tell anybody who asks that the favourite for the Single Combat is a champion entered by a visiting baron, whom nobody outside his household has ever seen fight, or seen at all. The odds are short, and nobody likes them.
