# This Week

**Baron Hanzo di Teufel** has come down the road for the Tourney with his Dragon Knights at his back, and he has entered a champion in the Single Combat whom nobody outside his household has been allowed to see. His advance men have been in the city for a week. The rest of his column arrives today, the same day you do.

## The Dragon Knights

The Baron's company is twenty-odd borderland soldiers in dragon-blazoned harness, many of them with fire-touched blades, camped on a councillor's water-meadow two miles east of the walls. The city calls them *the dragonshits*, quietly.

**This is what the last week has been like.** They walk the Merchant's Quarter and the Forges in threes and fours. They take the middle of a bridge and let the city go round them in single file. They eat and drink on the promise that *the Baron will settle*, and he settles late and short, so the stallholders have started pricing it in and everybody's bread has gone up. They take boats off the water-stairs with the boatmen still in them, rowed wherever they like and left wherever they finish, and two boatmen have not come back. Six days ago a dockhand named **Emmit Rill** was slow moving a barrow and had his jaw broken by a mailed fist in front of forty people. He still cannot work.

Nobody is dead yet. Everybody expects somebody to be.

## The Standing Order

**And the watch has been told to leave them alone.** A week ago the watch received a written instruction from the Governor's council: the Baron's people are guests of the Crown's representative, and complaints against them are to be recorded and referred, and not acted upon, until the Tourney is over. It went out over the Prince's own signature. Every sergeant in the city has a book of complaints and nothing to do with it.

So there is no office to complain to, and everybody in Purewater has worked that out privately, and nobody has said it out loud. Nobody calls it an occupation. Everybody goes round.

## Who Matters in Purewater

These are the people everybody in the city knows of, and what everybody says about them.

- **Prince Emeric,** the King's nephew and Governor: two-and-twenty, clever, idealistic and decent, and run rings around by his own council. People like him and do not expect anything of him.
- **The city council,** the merchant princes who run the city's daily business from their palazzi on the Grand Canal. Three of them carried the standing order. Everybody has an opinion about which three, and why.
- **Marisette,** who rules the Pearl, every house on it. She receives; she does not travel.
- **Crowbill,** who runs the city's other business, the business nobody writes down. He is said to keep a code: no killing locals, no harming children, no touching the Lady's sites. Some people find that comforting.
- **Marda Blackwater,** who speaks for the Dredgers, three hundred dock labourers who work the water-stairs, and who are angrier this week than she is.
- **The Order of the Lake Lady** and its High Priestess, **Nerissa**, who owns the water and is forbidden to meddle in the city.
- **The Harbor Masters,** who write down every hull on the water, including the Baron's ship, which lies at a private pier in the Forges, steaming where it meets the water.

## What People Are Saying

Ask around in any tavern in the city this week and you will hear some of these. Some of them are true.

- The Baron's champion travels in a litter laced shut, and the outriders face inward as much as out.
- The water by the Forges has been warm for weeks, and the Dredgers will not put a grab in it.
- The Order raised an alarm about the water and the council made them take it back.
- Two boatmen were rowed out through the Mouth by the Knights and left there.
- A councillor's daughter has been sent away for the week, and she is furious about it.
- Crowbill's collections are coming up short on every bridge the Knights stand on.
- An elf knight of the Swords of the Lake has entered the Archery, and nobody has ever seen one shoot.
- The Dredgers are talking about stopping the stairs if nobody pays Emmit Rill.
- The Baron is the most charming man anybody has ever met, and he remembers everybody's name.

That is the city you are walking into. Something is going to give this week, and the interesting question is not whether. It is who is standing nearest when it does.
