# Making Your Own Character

You do not have to play one of the four. A character of your own, built well, can be the best thing in a game like this, and the four will still be in the world for you to meet. This is how to build somebody who belongs in Purewater this week, without it taking an hour.

## Roll First, Decide Second

Roll your characteristics the way *Classic Fantasy Imperative* tells you to, and read the numbers out before you decide what kind of person this is. A CHA of 5 or a SIZ of 20 is a character concept arriving unbidden, and it is nearly always better than the one you walked in with. Then choose a species, a culture and a class (cleric, fighter, magic-user or rogue) that fit what the dice gave you.

## Answer One Question

Not "what is your backstory". Answer this:

> **Why are you in Purewater this week, and who would notice if you left?**

A character with an answer to that is in the story. A character without one is a tourist. The Tourney gives you a dozen reasons: competing, guarding somebody who is competing, selling to the crowds, picking their pockets, praying, collecting a debt, delivering a message, running from something on the mainland. The city gives you a dozen more.

## Owe Somebody

Choose one person or group in the city, and put your character in their debt, or them in yours. Purewater is full of people who already want things this week: a guild boss short of answers, a madame short of law, a harbourmaster, the temple, a company of soldiers who have not been paid, the dockers' guild. Pick one with the Games Master and write the tie down. It is the single most useful thing on this page.

## Passions, with a Collision in Them

Choose three Passions, and build a collision into them on purpose: a loyalty that will one day disagree with an ambition, or a love that will one day disagree with an oath. Five points of difference between them is plenty; it does not need to be a tragedy. Passions are where your character's voice comes from, so spend real time here, and say them out loud.

## Four Questions

Then answer these, and let the Games Master write the answers down:

1. **Where did you learn the thing you are good at, and from whom?** Name that person. They may still be alive.
2. **What did you lose?** Not a tragedy competition, but something specific and ordinary: a trade, a hand, a name, a city you cannot go back to.
3. **What do you carry?** One object with a history. It will come up.
4. **What would make you walk away from all this?** This is the one players have not thought about, and it is the one that makes a character a person.

Ask your Games Master to read it back to you as two short paragraphs, in the voice of the game, and fix anything that is wrong. Then the sheet: skills, kit and the rest, quickly. Nobody has ever enjoyed the shopping.
