# Background and Introduction

This chapter gives the Games Master everything that is true before the first scene. None of it is known to the characters at the start, and much of it is known to nobody in the city.

## The City and the Water

Purewater stands across the Mouth, the narrow strait where the sacred Lake drains south into the open Sea. Its heart is High Isle, which is really four islands cut apart by the Grand Canal and its branches: the North Quarter of houses, the Governor's Palazzo and the Tournament Ground; the Merchant's Quarter of guilds and counting-houses; the Forges; and the Seaward Quarter of docks. The Pearl, the pleasure island, lies off the North Quarter among the Northern Isles. The Lullwater, the thieves' quarter, lies across open water to the north-east, reached by no bridge. The Shoals, all fish-markets and smugglers' jetties, guard the seaward side. The only way in by land is Caravan Square on the western shore, through the Gate of Triumph and over the Triumph Bridge.

There is no ground worth the name. Nobody rides a horse in Purewater. Goods and people move by boat, by bridge or by water-stair, and every local can handle a boat while no outsider can. The locals call the place Pisswater, for its smell and for its politics.

The water is the goddess. The Lake Lady's holy water wells up on Temple Isle, a small, deliberately unbridged island just north of the Tournament Ground, and the Order of the Lake Lady tends it. The pools have been fouling since Candlemas. The Order's High Priestess, Nerissa, knows it; her assessor's alarm was signed off as a false reading six weeks ago by a Watercrafters' Tide Master, Ivo Calder, at the polite request of a man he respected. The corruption comes from the Baron's ship, which steams continuously where its hull meets the water.

The city's real powers are Prince Emeric, the King's nephew and Governor; a council of merchant princes who handle its day-to-day business; Marisette, who rules the Pearl; Crowbill, who owns the canals nobody maps; the Dredgers, who work the water-stairs; and the Order. They do not trust each other and they have never needed to.

## The Lake Lady's Tourney

The Tourney is the year's holy business: a sacred tournament older than the Governor's office, drawing competitors from across the realm. It runs for three days.

- **The Archery** on the first day is the one open event. Anybody may shoot, with no colours and no fee, on the Long Butts beside the Lists.
- **The Single Combat** on the second day is the only martial event: one-on-one bouts on the raked sand of the Lists, watched by four thousand people.
- **The Run** on the third day sends competitors through the whole city on foot and by boat, from the Lists' water-gate and back to the temple steps. For two hours the city has no crowd control at all. At dusk the Tourney closes, and the Prince takes his place to give the rewards.

On the eve of the Tourney every entrant in every event is presented to the Prince by long form: names read, colours shown, and each competitor bare-headed for the space of a bow. Before the Single Combat every combatant and the head of their house goes down to the Lady's water on the Temple Isle steps for the blessing.

**The rite that matters.** By a form older than the city's government, the champion of the Single Combat is presented to the Crown's representative alone and unarmed, behind a closed door, in a small stone room behind the Governor's box. It is a ceremony, not a favour, and that is exactly why nobody has ever thought to guard it.

## The Baron and the Dragon Knights

Baron Hanzo di Teufel is a borderland lord in his fifties: warm, unhurried, well made and never seen doing anything he would need to explain. He is the last complete practitioner of a school of binding that the realm outlawed after it overthrew the Dragon Kings, and four generations of his family have worked toward one thing. He means to become a Dragon King himself. He is funded by the Awake the Dragon Movement, a patient, distant circle who regard him as their most promising experiment. The money reaches him through Councillor Alderic Vantt, whose debts Hanzo quietly covered two winters ago.

His Dragon Knights are a mercenary company of twenty-five, raiders who have terrorised the border for thirty years, captained by Tat Atarer, a professional who fights for pay. The day-to-day command, and the Baron's dirty work, falls to Blau, the albino enforcer the ranks call Commander Vask. The company camps on Vantt's water-meadow two miles east of the walls. The Baron sleeps aboard the DragonBarge, at a private pier in the Forges, with his household and six of his inner guard.

**The occupation.** A dozen Knights came in eight days before the Tourney to open the camp and provision the ship. They walk the city in threes and fours, take the middle of the bridges, eat and drink on the promise that *the Baron will settle it*, and commandeer boats off the water-stairs with the boatmen still in them. Two boatmen have not come back. Six days ago a dockhand named Emmit Rill had his jaw broken with a mailed fist in front of forty people. Nobody is dead yet, and everybody expects somebody to be.

**The standing order.** Seven days ago the city watch received a written instruction from the Governor's council: the Baron's people are guests of the Crown's representative, and complaints against them are to be recorded, referred and not acted upon until the Tourney is over. It went out over Prince Emeric's signature, on the advice of three councillors: Alderic Vantt, who is bought; Hesketh Pyle, who is frightened; and Maro Quist, who is sincere and wrong. Sergeant Abel Weir keeps the referral book, which is four inches thick and growing. The watch is not corrupt. It has been ordered, in writing, and every sergeant in the city knows how that will read afterwards.

## What Is in the Litter

Ten years ago the Dragon Knights burned a steading on the border and took a small boy. His name was Gabriel, and his sister Gardwen survived because she was small and the woods hid her. The Baron opened the boy and seated in him the spirit of Thuban Eta, a knight of the Sixth Wing of the old dragon armies, who was drowned by his own company in his mail with his hands tied for refusing to burn a valley. The Baron has held Thuban in the boy ever since. The boy is now seventeen, thin, and called Cailan, and he fights like a knight of the dragon wars because one is doing the fighting.

**What holds Thuban.** Three things, and each is a separate point of failure.

- **The bones.** Thuban's bones lie in a box in a tower on the Baron's border estate, four days' hard riding away. They hold him in the world. They are out of reach for this whole scenario.
- **The locket.** The Baron wears it on a chain. It ties Thuban to *Hanzo*: it is the leash. Without it in his hand the Baron cannot command Thuban, cannot draw him out of the boy, and cannot seat him in anybody else. By day it never leaves the Baron's neck. At night he takes the chain off and lays the locket on the table beside his bed, and Blau sleeps across the door. The locket is a robust working, and destroying it takes a powerful Dispel Magic.
- **The nightly reinforcement.** Every night, behind sealed doors aboard the DragonBarge, the Baron renews the seat with a cut, a ritual and the locket in his hand. Anybody close enough to hear it learns that the champion is a prisoner and that the thing inside him does not want to be there.

**The real target.** Cailan was never the point. He is the jar, and ten years of keeping a spirit seated in a living body is the proof that it can be done. If the champion wins the Single Combat, he is presented alone and unarmed to Prince Emeric at the closing of the Tourney. In that room the Baron casts Draw Forth on the boy and Seat the Bound on the Prince. Take the Crown's nephew, and a baron no longer needs a barony.

**Thuban himself** is not the Baron's creature. He is held by torment, not by loyalty, and in the small hours he sometimes surfaces: perfectly courteous, a thousand years old, speaking in a seventeen-year-old's voice. He holds a cup right-handed. Gabriel, when he surfaces for a second, moves it to his left. The only person who has noticed is Orrin Sculle, the boy's keeper, who has told nobody in ten years.

## The Twins

The Baron does not know he has three sons. Long before his open descent he seduced and broke a young elven mage, who fled to Purewater and died there when her children were small. She bore twins. One, Randall, was raised by the women of the Promise of Heaven on the Pearl and grew up a thief. The other, Conall, was taken to Ashwick, adopted by a militiaman's family, pulled out of a ditch at nineteen by Temerach Nebulo of the Swords of the Lake, and trained by the King's Assay as an arcane investigator.

Both of them wear the di Teufel face, the same face as the Baron's legitimate heir, Santo. Nobody on the Pearl knows Santo's face, because he has never needed to show it. Temerach has known whose son Conall is for years. She brought him to Purewater deliberately and has not told him.

If anybody in the Baron's service ever sees two of that face at once, or is sold the fact, the Baron learns he has more sons than he thought. He wants the tool, and he wants the claim extinguished, in that order. This agenda, *A di Teufel who is not his*, sleeps until then, and once it wakes it never stops.

## Santo and Emmeralda

Santo is nineteen, the legitimate heir, a spoiled and frightened young man with a talent for fire and a little demonology he taught himself by tracing his father's work. He can open a channel. He cannot seat a binding. He has stolen exactly one scroll of Seat the Bound from his father's locked case, and a barbed ritual knife bearing the mark of Asmodeus.

He has seen Emmeralda dance on the Pearl and decided to take her home: kept, on the barge, and glad about it. He means to put a small, hungry spirit in her that will make her want what he wants. He is not part of his father's scheme and has no idea what it is. But the scroll he spends is written in his father's hand, and that squalid little crime is the door that opens on the real one.

## The Law, and Why Nobody Can Arrest a Baron

There is no arresting Baron Hanzo di Teufel. Justice in Thornmere flows from the King, King Ostmar at Caldreth, and in Purewater through his representative, Prince Emeric. The watch, the guilds and the port are administration: they can record, refuse, impound and turn a man away from a berth, and they cannot touch a lord.

To call a lord to answer at all, a person must have **standing**: another lord, a knight of a recognised order, a Crown officer under warrant within their charter, a priest within her faith, or a guild master within his guild. Temerach has standing as a Sword of the Lake. Conall has it as an assayer of the King's Assay, because seating a spirit in an unwilling person is a hanging matter under the Assay's charter. Nerissa has it over anything done to the water.

**Accusation is a wager.** To accuse a great lord and fail is to answer for it, with ruin, exile or the rope. And a lord who is called to answer may refuse the matter and offer trial by arms, or by his champion's arms. Hanzo has a champion nobody has seen fight. That is why Temerach has held her accusation for four years.

So the characters' real problem is never finding proof. It is finding somebody with standing who is willing to spend themselves, and giving them enough that they will. The standing order shields the Baron from the city's own law and touches nothing else. Three jurisdictions lie beyond its reach: the King's Assay, maritime law and the Order's sacred ground.

## Starting the Scenario

Purewater is written for four pregenerated characters, described in *The Characters*. Each has an entry point that opens the scenario for them. The characters a player does not choose remain in the world as Non-Player Characters and meet the party in the catacombs on the second morning. A player who would rather roll their own character can; give them a reason to be in Purewater this week, somebody who would notice if they left, and a debt.

### The Four Characters

- **Gardwen** is an elf druid, the sole survivor of the raid that took her brother. She has followed the Dragon Knights for three weeks with her wolf, Ruhi.
- **Magda** is an old warrior of Hel, sent by eleven nights of the same dream: a young woman with a wolf, a burning house, and a dragon warrior who is already dead and does not know it. The dream told her to lay him to rest.
- **Randall** is a thief of the Pearl, raised by the women of the Promise of Heaven. He is home, which means he has more to lose here than anybody.
- **Conall Bjornlasch** is a warranted investigator of the King's Assay, riding in beside Temerach on an errand she has not explained.

### How They Come Together

The opening day pulls the four into the same night without their meeting.

- Magda and Gardwen meet in the queue at the ford, and Magda takes Gardwen to the Sylph's Embrace for beds. They are in the house when Santo works his scroll, and they see the man on the stair.
- Randall is working the carts in Caravan Square when the column comes through, and a terrified guard at the Baron's stirrup calls him *my lord*.
- Conall rides in with Temerach. At dusk she sends him into the Lullwater with a note, and Randall's old enemy, Sinnit, corners him there and calls him *Randall*.

Before dawn on the second day, Marisette's enforcer takes both men who wear the attacker's face. They wake tied to chairs side by side in the catacombs, and Magda and Gardwen are the witnesses who clear them. That cellar is where the party is made, and everything before it is a reason for the four of them to be in it.
