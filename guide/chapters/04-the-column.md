# The Column

*Three days before the Tourney (d-3).* The Baron rides in, four strangers arrive without knowing about one another, and before the night is out a man with a face three men in this city share has done something unforgivable on the Pearl.

**What this day takes:** Emmeralda, cut open or carried off with something in her; and the assumption that the law applies to everyone.

Each character starts in their own entry point (see *The Characters*). Run the openings separately, cutting between them, and do not hurry them together: the day exists to put all four into the same night without their meeting. Magda and Gardwen should end it lodged at the Sylph's Embrace, Randall somewhere on the Pearl or in Caravan Square, and Conall in the Lullwater or at his inn by Shilling Stairs.

## The Old Woman at the Ford

<!-- beat: the-old-woman-at-the-ford -->

Carts stand three deep at the gravel ford outside the city, in heat, horse-smell and crushed fennel, and everybody has been standing in the queue for an hour. Ahead of it is the di Teufel column, with a covered litter in the middle that the outriders let nobody walk beside.

Three places back from Gardwen, an old woman in ringmail is looking straight at her and not pretending otherwise. This is Magda, who has dreamed that face for eleven nights, and every dream ends in her own death. She is not good at explaining and does not try twice. Magda knows Purewater a little, and knows which madame rents rooms to women travelling alone: Constantine, at the Sylph's Embrace.

**The litter is the first fact of the scenario.** Offer it as an image and never explain it. Anybody who pushes for a look gets an outrider's arm across the chest and a very polite refusal.

**If either woman is a Non-Player Character,** the other still meets her here, and Magda still takes Gardwen to the Sylph's Embrace. Their being in that house tonight is what the scenario needs.

## Four Knights and a King's Officer

<!-- beat: four-knights-and-a-kings-officer -->

A day short of the city, four bored Dragon Knights stop a King's officer riding in plain harness with no house on her shield, because she is an elf and because they can. Temerach Nebulo, with Conall beside her, is that officer.

This is not a fight. It is the slow, public and entirely deniable humiliation of somebody who outranks them and cannot use it. They go through her saddlebags. They ask her to say something in elvish. It goes on longer than is comfortable to play, and that is the point.

**She takes it.** Completely still, no answer, eyes front. She has told Conall not to intervene, and if he is a character, the roll is his Willpower not to. Do not let her win this scene. Her not winning it here is what buys the Archery and the shot.

## The Baron's Column Enters Purewater

<!-- beat: the-baron-s-column-enters-purewater -->

Twenty-odd Dragon Knights ride into Caravan Square with their banners cased against the damp, and behind them comes the covered litter that the outriders keep the crowd back from. The Baron rides uncovered and unhurried, letting Purewater look at him.

A character in the crowd sees the litter and how carefully it is screened. A successful Perception roll notices that the guards face *inward* as much as out. The column crosses the Triumph Bridge and makes for the Forges, where the litter is carried aboard the DragonBarge. The city talks about it for the rest of the day.

## A Knight Calls Randall "My Lord"

<!-- beat: a-knight-calls-randall-my-lord -->

Randall is in the middle of robbing a cart in Caravan Square, with every eye in the square on the litter, when a guard at the Baron's stirrup turns his head and sees him. The guard goes white, breaks step, and says "My lord —" in the voice of a servant who has just seen the heir standing in the street where the heir is not supposed to be, in a thief's clothes, with his hand in somebody's cargo. He offers Randall a hand down off the cart, because he cannot think what else to do. Then the column moves on, and he looks back twice.

**Deceit to play it, Stealth to be gone.** Playing it well is worth more than escaping. On a success Randall learns a name and a piece of the household's business; on a critical, where his lordship is expected this evening. A failure puts him in front of somebody who has seen Santo close up. Running clean costs nothing and teaches nothing, and that is a legitimate outcome that should be allowed to be dull.

**What the guard thinks he saw** is Santo, slipped out of the household again. He reports nothing, because telling the Baron about the young master's absences is how a guard stops being a guard. This does not wake the Baron's interest in the twins: nobody has seen a second face, only the first one in the wrong place.

**What Randall leaves with** is a stranger who was terrified of him and called him a lord, with the Baron three feet away. He knows the city well enough to vanish, and to start asking, quietly, whose face he has.

## Three of Them on the Gate Bridge

<!-- beat: three-of-them-on-the-gate-bridge -->

Three men in dragon-blazoned harness stand in the middle of the Triumph Bridge, doing nothing much but eating. The bridge is the only way in from Caravan Square, and the morning's traffic of carters, a fish-cart, two temple girls and an old man with a goat goes round them in single file against the parapet. Nobody says anything. A watchman in blue-grey stands at the far end, and he is not doing anything either.

**Play it as scenery and let it be noticed.** Do not point at it, and do not tell anybody what it means. The information is entirely in what the locals do with their feet.

**If a character intervenes,** and somebody will, the Knights are bored rather than spoiling for a fight. They give way sooner to a well-dressed stranger with a confident manner than to a carter, which is its own lesson. The watchman will not back them. Afterwards he wants their name, quietly, and does not write it down where anybody can see.

These three are the advance men. By dusk there are twenty-five. Advance *Hold the streets* by 1 if nobody interferes.

## Santo Buys Her Evening

<!-- beat: santo-buys-her-evening -->

At the start of the evening trade, Emmeralda dances for the front hall of the Sylph's Embrace. A young man in a good coat with no device on it watches her the whole way through and does not drink. Then he goes to the desk and buys her evening, as a hundred men a week do, with coin and no name. He pays in new silver and over the odds and asks for a room on the second floor.

**He does not say di Teufel.** Nobody on the Pearl knows the heir's face, and Santo likes it that way. That is the engine of the next two days: when the house goes looking for the man who went upstairs with Emmeralda, all it has is the face, and three men in the city wear it.

**Magda and Gardwen are in the hall,** taking rooms. A character who is watching the room can notice him with a Perception roll: young, rich, a soft right hand, and the coat, which is better than anything else he has on and which he will leave behind. Constantine takes his money and thinks nothing of it. She will spend the next day wishing she had looked harder.

Advance *Have her* by 1.

## Sinnit Corners the Wrong Man

<!-- beat: sinnit-corners-the-wrong-man -->

Conall's first evening in Purewater, and the first time anybody in the city calls him by his brother's name.

Temerach sends him over to the Lullwater by Shilling Stairs with a sealed note for the storyteller at the Moist Oyster, which she does not explain. He dresses down for it. Within the hour he is lost in the narrow canals north of the Still, and within the same hour he is cornered in a passage between two tenements, with water at both ends, by three men. The one in front fills the passage: big, canal-rat, permanently aggrieved. He says the name with enormous satisfaction.

> "Randall. *There* you are."

**Sinnit thinks he has finally caught Randall** alone, on Sinnit's own ground. He does not notice the clothes, the accent or the King's Assay ring; he sees the face he has hated for seven years. He announces exactly what is going to happen, because that is the only mercy in him, and it is not a beating, not at first:

> "Pearl Quay. Seven years back. The purse. The bow. Say it. Say you're sorry, in front of my lads, and we're square."

Conall has no idea what he is talking about. That is the whole scene: every honest denial sounds like Randall being clever, which is exactly what Randall did last time, and Sinnit gets louder with each one. Seven years ago, in front of the Promise of Heaven, Randall lifted Sinnit's purse and handed it back to him with a bow while the house's girls laughed.

**All the ways out are fine:**

- **Fight.** Sinnit hits hard and fights dirty, and his two lads are worse than he is. Conall is a capable fighter, but three on one in a wet passage is still three on one. Use Sinnit's statistics, and the Canal Cutpurse for his lads.
- **Magic.** A borrowed face, an illusion or a find-magic works, and it leaves Sinnit very frightened and very sure that Randall has learned something new.
- **Say it.** Conall can apologise for something he never did. It does not count, because the wrong man said it, but Sinnit does not know that yet and goes away shaken.
- **The Lullwater.** A door opens, an old woman in a window shouts, a punt comes under the passage. The quarter looks after strangers who are being picked on, sometimes.

**What Conall leaves with** is a thief called Randall who, as far as the Lullwater is concerned, wears his face. It is the first thread of the twins on his side, and it arrives the same day Randall is called *my lord*.

**What Sinnit leaves with** is a story. Tomorrow, when he sees the real Randall walking the Pearl free after the catacombs, with a different coat and no bruises, he begins to understand something that will be worth money. Advance *Make Randall say it* by 1, and by 1 more if Conall beat him in front of his lads.

## Santo's Working

<!-- beat: santo-s-working -->

The inciting event of the scenario, and the dice decide which event it is.

Santo has a room on the second floor, the girl's evening, and his father's scroll in his coat. He thinks he is making her *willing*. He has watched his father seat a spirit in a living body and be obeyed by it for ten years, and he means to put a lust spirit in this girl, a small and hungry thing, so that she wants to come to the barge. He has one use of a stolen scroll and no training whatsoever.

### Roll It Twice

**First, the working.** The scroll does most of the work and Santo does the rest badly. Roll against a poor chance: he is untrained, frightened and nineteen. Whatever the result, the scroll burns out in his hand and leaves a fresh burn across the palm and fingers of his right hand that will not heal by morning. That burn is the clue that clears the twins.

- **It takes.** The channel opens, the seat holds, and the thing settles into her. She stops being afraid of him. She wants what he wants, and she is bright, pliant and wrong in a way anybody who knew her would see in a minute. **Now roll the second thing:** getting her out. It is a Stealth roll down the back stair to the water-door and a skiff, made one grade easier because she is helping. On a success he keeps her: she is aboard the DragonBarge by midnight, and nobody saw her go except the people in the next section. On a failure somebody in the house stops them on the stair; he lets go and runs, and the girl is left on the landing laughing at nothing. He will try again by boat (see *Santo Comes Back for Her*).
- **It fails.** The channel opens and the seating does not. Whatever answered the invitation will not go where he tells it, and he finishes the working the only way panic knows, with the barbed knife: a long ritual cut that opens her the length of her body. She screams. He runs, and in running he kicks the knife behind the armoire.

### The Witnesses

Magda and Gardwen lodge in the top back room of this house, and that is why Magda brought her here. Whichever way it goes, they are close enough.

- **If it failed,** they hear the screams and are first through the door. Gardwen's healing, or anybody's First Aid, saves Emmeralda's life; without it she dies before dawn. On the back stair they see the man going the other way: young, rich, bare-headed, cradling his right hand.
- **If it took,** they see a couple on the back stair in the small hours, and a girl who was frightened of him at dusk now hanging on his arm. Magda, who has seen possession in Hel's service, knows what she is looking at. If they intervene, Santo's getaway roll is opposed by theirs.

What they see is what clears Randall and Conall in the catacombs at dawn: the burnt hand, the coat and the hour.

### What Is in the Room Afterwards

He leaves in a panic either way and takes nothing:

- **the barbed knife** behind the armoire, if the working failed, with a mark on the pommel: a ring with a line through it and three strokes off the line;
- **the spent scroll,** burnt out but legible, in the Baron's own hand;
- **a chalk seat** drawn on the boards under the rug, which stays live until somebody sweeps it;
- **his coat,** over a chair, with a Purewater tailor's chit in the pocket and the di Teufel crest under the collar.

Nobody in the house is going to search a room while a girl is bleeding in it. Whatever is not taken tonight is still there in the morning, and so is the man who wants it back. The first person with the standing to search it properly is Conall, with the King's Assay's warrant (see *Conall Reads the Room*).

**If no character is in the house,** Magda and Gardwen are there as Non-Player Characters and it goes the same way. Either way, Marisette's hunt for a man with that face begins tonight.

Advance *Have her* by 2 if Santo gets her out of the house.
