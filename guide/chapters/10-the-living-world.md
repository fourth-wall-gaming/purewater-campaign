# Running the Living World

Purewater does not wait for the characters. Every Non-Player Character and faction with a stake in the week holds an **agenda**: something they want, pursued on a clock, whether or not anybody is watching. The events in this book are what those agendas produce if nobody interferes. This chapter explains how to run them at a table with nothing but paper.

## Agendas and Clocks

An agenda has a holder, a goal and a **clock** of four to ten segments. When the fiction moves the holder toward their goal, fill a segment. When a clock fills, the holder has what they wanted, and the world changes. Some agendas start partly filled, because the week began before the characters arrived: the Dragon Knights have been holding the streets for eight days.

**What fills a clock.** An event in this book says when to advance an agenda. Beyond that, advance a clock when the fiction earns it: an unanswered incident in the streets, a rumour that spreads, a door that opens. Clocks are not timers. Nothing fills one just because time passes.

**What empties one.** Nothing empties a clock. When the characters genuinely beat an agenda, mark it thwarted, and then let the holder react. A thwarted agenda should produce a new one, not a quiet re-run of the old.

**Dormant agendas** wait for something to wake them. *A di Teufel who is not his* sleeps until somebody in the Baron's service sees two of the di Teufel face, or is sold the fact. Once it wakes, it never sleeps again.

## Onscreen and Offscreen

Every event happens whether the characters are there or not. Each scene card says when it happens at the latest, where, and who is involved, and the *Onscreen if* line says who has to be where for it to be a scene.

- **Onscreen:** a character is at the place, or is in the cast. Play it as a scene.
- **Offscreen:** nobody is there. It happens anyway, exactly as written. Note it, and let the characters discover it afterwards as a rumour, a body, a closed door, or a girl who was already bleeding when they got there.

The same event is a fight, an alibi or a piece of news depending only on where the players went. That is the whole design.

**When is the latest, not an appointment.** A scene card's *When* is the latest point at which the event happens. Its conditions can bring it forward when the characters earn it. So a thread can be accelerated, and it can never stall forever.

## Running the Clock on Paper

Keep one sheet for the week. Down the left side, write the six days, each with its four watches: dawn, day, dusk and night. Mark the current watch. Between scenes, move the mark on when the characters have spent a watch, and read down every event due in that watch from *The Week, If Nobody Interferes* in the Overview. For each one, ask whether a character is there. If so, it is onscreen; if not, it happened, so write one line about it.

Alongside, keep the agendas and their clocks as a row of boxes each. Tick them as events and the fiction advance them. When a box row fills, read the agenda's entry below and change the world.

**Conditions to watch for:**

- **The stairs stop** at dawn on the eve of the Tourney if nobody has helped Marda Blackwater.
- **Sinnit sells the face** if *Make Randall say it* reaches 3 and Randall has not apologised.
- **A price on the face** follows if the Baron has learned of the twins.
- **The Pearl closes** after Santo's tantrum, or after Constantine finds the room empty.
- **The theft** decides the last day: read the board at dawn and cancel the scenes of the endings that no longer apply.

## The Agendas

**Field the champion and take the city (the Baron).** Win the Single Combat, take the champion's private audience, and put Thuban into the Prince. Nothing deters him short of losing the locket, and losing the locket unhinges him. His pressure points are the locket, the bones (out of reach), Thuban's reluctance, and the fact that he has to win.

**Keep the champion unseen (the Baron).** Nobody close enough to Cailan to notice that he does not answer to his own name. Meals go in and no conversation comes out. Blau's eye on Gardwen at the presentation advances it.

**A di Teufel who is not his (the Baron).** Dormant until the twins are seen together or sold. Then: quietly first, a name and a purse; then Blau; then the Knights. He takes a bastard off a street at night.

**Wake a Dragon King (the Movement).** The horizon behind the story. It advances when the Movement learns something, whoever teaches it to them. Nothing available to the characters this week stops it.

**Have her (Santo).** One girl, kept on the barge and glad of it. Fear works on him, and so does exposure. Taking the knife off him makes him frantic, which is more dangerous.

**Clean up after the young master (Blau).** Contain Santo's messes before they reach the Baron or the watch. Shown that the mess endangers the Baron's own plans, Blau's loyalty tilts toward the father. When the locket goes, this is the agenda that gets it back. If Santo dies, it becomes vengeance.

**Get paid, keep the company whole (Tat Atarer).** The contract fee, his men alive, and no part of what is behind the inner doors. Show him the contract costs men or reputation and he becomes negotiable.

**Hold the streets as if we own them (the Dragon Knights).** Habit, not conquest. One segment for every unanswered public incident. At 4, two Knights beat a man in front of a watch patrol that walks away; at 5, a woman is taken off the Pearl street; at 6, a local is killed in the open and the Dredgers move whether Marda wants them to or not. It stops when an incident lands where the standing order does not reach: the Prince in person, the Order, a Sword of the Lake, or the Tourney ground.

**Make them pay for one of ours (Marda Blackwater).** Redress for the dockhands, done properly, in daylight, by somebody official. It advances with the Knights' clock. At 4 the stairs stop. Get Rill paid, the boatmen accounted for, or Marda in front of somebody who can act, and she holds her people another day every time.

**Find who carved Emmeralda (Marisette).** A name, and then a reckoning every house hears about. Her weapon is withdrawal, not violence. The catacombs, the tantrum and Constantine's complaint advance it.

**Keep the Lady's water clean (High Priestess Nerissa).** Hold the Tourney without profaning the water. She needs evidence in the open, not rumour from strangers; a demon-marked blade on her altar in front of witnesses moves her decisively.

**Keep the table level (Crowbill).** Keep business predictable. Demon-craft on a helpless girl crosses his line. His price is always something he can use.

**Shield the boy, unmask the house (Temerach Nebulo).** The Baron called to answer, and Conall alive through learning what he is. She is a handler, not an ally: she cannot be found, only reached through Murgeroch; she always knows more; she gives tasks and never asks for help; persuasion does not move her, only proof she can verify. Danger to Conall overrides everything, and so does learning what is inside the champion.

**One big score, and a name (Nus).** The job nobody sensible takes. Warning him off makes him keener. Crowbill names the locket in his hearing, and he goes.

**Make Randall say it (Sinnit).** An apology, out loud, with people watching, and until he gets it he takes everything from Randall that can be taken. Threats do not stop him and beating him in public advances the clock. Randall's public apology turns him into a loud, unreliable and brave ally.

**The one who works it out (Kag).** Crowbill's errands and his business on the streets, and a hustle or two. It never carries the plot. She is a door to Crowbill and a number when the characters need one. How the characters talk to her decides what she is: address the muscle and she is exactly as much use as they paid for; ask her what a thing costs and she is the best analyst in Purewater.

## The Knowledge Web

Some events depend on who knows what. Keep a short list of the facts that matter and who holds each one:

- the twins are the Baron's sons (Temerach knows; the Baron does not, until Sinnit);
- the champion is Gardwen's brother (Gardwen, after the presentation; Orrin; the Baron);
- the locket is the leash and comes off at night (Crowbill; the characters, after his price);
- the man who carved Emmeralda was Randall the Pearl thief (false, and believed on the street after Sinnit's rumour).

A Non-Player Character can only act on what they know. When a character tells somebody something, add them to the list.
