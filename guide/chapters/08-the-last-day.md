# The Last Day

*The third day of the Tourney (d2).* The Run, the closing, and whichever of three endings the week has earned.

**At dawn, read the board.** The theft last night decides which road the day takes. The scenes in this chapter do not all happen: each one says which ending it belongs to, and the Games Master cancels the ones that no longer apply.

| Ending | If | Then |
|---|---|---|
| A | The locket was never stolen, or the theft failed | The Run and the closing; the old rite; Thuban into the Prince; the Baron moves on the throne |
| B | The locket was stolen and Blau gets it back | The Lullwater burns; Sinnit sells Nus and Blau kills him; the Prince summons the Baron and is taken in that room; Temerach's impossible shot wounds and does not kill; she flees with the characters |
| C | The locket was stolen, Nus lives, and the characters keep it out of Blau's reach | The Lullwater burns, but the Baron goes into dusk without his leash; Thuban breaks out through the Dragon Knights and leaves the city with the characters and Temerach |

Table: The Three Endings

## The Lullwater Burns

<!-- beat: the-lullwater-burns -->

**Endings B and C: only if the locket was taken.**

The Baron wakes at first light to an empty table, and the warm, reasonable, unhurried man the city has watched all week is gone. Without the locket he cannot walk into the room tonight. Four generations of work end at dusk unless he gets it back, and he has no idea who has it, only that it went over the stern in the dark toward the north-east, where the thieves live.

So he goes after it himself.

### The Hunt

He comes off the barge at dawn with Blau and every Knight aboard, and the camp at the Vantt Estate is roused behind him. He does not ask questions. He takes boats at Shilling Stairs at swordpoint and crosses to the Lullwater, and the first people he meets on the stair are killed for being on it. He is a demonologist and a fire-mage, and he has stopped pretending otherwise. Doors burst inward. A man who will not say where the boy-thieves sleep is burned where he stands. He is not searching; he is making it unbearable to keep the thing that was stolen.

### The Quarter Fights Back

The Lullwater has no bridges, no horse can go into it, and four thousand people who know every roof live in it. **They resist.** The Dredgers come out of their hall with boathooks and grabs. Crowbill's people take to the roofs with slings. Women drop roof-tiles into the Gutter, and punts ram the Knights' boats on open water. There is blood on the planking and there are running battles up and down the Gutter and around the Still. If the stairs stopped, Marda Blackwater's three hundred are already angry and close.

**Play it so the characters can hear it from wherever they are. Do not cut away.** A character in the Lullwater is in a battle: use the Dragon Knight Soldier statistics for the Knights, in threes, and give the locals the roofs, the water and the numbers.

### The Fire

When the quarter will not give up the thief, the Baron burns it. He calls fire into the tenements along the Gutter, and in a city of timber leaning over water it goes from roof to roof faster than anybody can row. Name what burns, and let the players see which of the places they know are gone: the Moist Oyster, the Dredgers' Hall, the drying-sheds on the Hook, Eel Bay. By noon there is a column of smoke over the north-east that the Prince can see from the Governor's box.

### Nus

Blau tracks him down. Blau does not panic and is not fooled by much, and he has help: Sinnit, who knows every hole Nus sleeps in (see *Sinnit Sells Nus*). Blau goes straight there, finds Nus, kills him quickly, without appearing to enjoy it, and brings the Baron his locket back.

**This is a consequence, not a scheduled tragedy.** If the characters hid Nus, got him out of the city, turned Sinnit, got between Sinnit and Blau, drew the hunt onto themselves, or are holding the locket somewhere Blau cannot follow, honour it. The burning still happens, but Blau comes back empty-handed and the Baron goes into the evening without his leash. That is the road to *Thuban Breaks Out*. If Blau gets the locket back, the road is *The Private Audience*, summoned in the smoke.

The Run is abandoned. Nobody races through a city on fire. Fill *Hold the streets* and *Make them pay for one of ours*.

## Sinnit Sells Nus

<!-- beat: sinnit-sells-nus -->

**Endings B and C: only if the locket was taken.** This is how Blau finds Nus.

The quarter is fighting and burning, and the whole Lullwater knows somebody went over the Baron's stern last night and is hiding in it. Sinnit knows who. Nus has been Randall's second pair of hands for years, and Sinnit has watched them both for seven. He knows Nus's boat, his sister's loft over Eel Bay, and the drying-shed on the Hook where he sleeps when he does not want to be found.

**If nobody has turned him,** he finds Blau in the smoke at Shilling Stairs and tells him: because it hurts Randall, because he is frightened, and because he thinks being useful to the men with fire will keep the fire off him. Blau goes straight there, and that is how Nus dies. The characters can learn afterwards from anybody who saw Sinnit at the stairs. **This is the scene to intercept.** A party that has been watching Sinnit, or guesses who would sell Nus, has one chance to get between him and Blau, or to move Nus before Blau arrives.

**If Randall apologised to him,** then Sinnit is the one standing in the door of the drying-shed when Blau comes: big and loud, announcing exactly what he is going to do, *"Nobody does that to our people"*, and then doing it. He will not win. He buys Nus the minutes it takes to get into a boat, and that may be all the characters need. Whether he lives through it is up to the dice.

## The Run Across the Town

<!-- beat: the-run-across-the-town -->

**Ending A only.** If the Lullwater is burning, the Run is abandoned at dawn, and this scene does not happen.

The last event of the Tourney, and the only one that leaves the Tournament Ground. The Run goes out from the Lists' water-gate, along the North Quarter, over the Triumph Bridge to Caravan Square and back, down the Merchant's Quarter, along the Seaward docks, over the Fishmarket Bridge into the Shoals, across to the bridgeless Lullwater by whatever means a competitor can find, and back over open water to the temple steps. It is run on foot and by boat. No route is laid down and no help is forbidden: half the city is a course and the other half is a crowd.

**For about two hours Purewater has no crowd control whatsoever.** The watch is holding lines along a route rather than patrolling districts. The Dragon Knights are strung out along it with nothing to do. Every bridge and stair is packed with people cheering, and nobody can move against the flow. That is either the best cover the characters will ever get, or the reason they cannot reach somewhere in time, and it should be both in the same hour.

This is the first ending's road: the locket is on the Baron's chain, the champion has won, and nothing stands between Hanzo and the room except what the characters do in these two hours. If nobody uses it, it is a magnificent afternoon that the party watched happen, and the Baron's people move freely under exactly the same cover. At dusk the competitors come back to the steps, the Prince takes his place, and the closing begins.

## The Private Audience

<!-- beat: the-private-audience -->

**Endings A and B: only if the Baron has the locket at dusk.** If the characters still hold it, cancel this scene and run *Thuban Breaks Out*.

This is the point of the entire hundred years. Prince Emeric is two-and-twenty, well-meaning and badly advised, and he has already been used once this week, when three councillors had him sign the order that silenced the watch. Cailan is the jar. Emeric is the destination. In that room the Baron casts Draw Forth on the boy and Seat the Bound on the Prince, and he wears the anchor round his neck.

### Two Ways into the Room

**By the old rite (ending A).** By a form older than the Governor's office, the Single Combat's champion is presented to the Crown's representative alone and unarmed, at the closing, with the doors shut: a rite, not a favour, which is exactly why nobody guards it. The champion won yesterday. Tonight, when the Run comes back to the temple steps and the Prince takes his place, the Baron walks his champion through that door. Quietly. Nobody in the city knows anything has happened.

**Summoned to answer (ending B).** By noon the Prince can see the smoke from the Governor's box. By mid-afternoon the reports are coming in: a foreign lord is burning a quarter of the city on the last day of a sacred festival. Emeric does the brave, decent and stupid thing. He summons the Baron to answer to him, in private, and the Baron comes with his champion at his side, smoke on his clothes and the locket back on his neck. The councillors stand outside the door. It is the same room, reached the other way, and Hanzo could not have arranged it better.

### What Comes Out

The Prince comes out. He is gracious and composed, and right-handed in a way nobody thinks to notice. Thuban Eta is wearing the Crown's nephew. Cailan is carried out behind him, emptied and barely breathing.

**After the old rite,** the Baron goes home with a prince in his pocket and begins to move on the throne. Nobody in Purewater knows. The scenario ends with the characters knowing exactly what has happened and nobody in the kingdom who would believe them.

**After the summons,** the Prince steps out onto the steps above the burning city and sides with the Baron in front of everybody: the thieves' quarter rose, and the Baron restored order. Across the arena, an elf has a bow in her hand.

**How to break it, until dusk:** stop the champion winning; keep the Baron out of that room; or take his anchor and keep it.

## The Impossible Shot

<!-- beat: the-impossible-shot -->

**Ending B: after the Prince is summoned to the room and comes out wearing Thuban.** After a quiet old-rite audience nobody knows to shoot, so in ending A this happens only if Temerach has learned what the champion is.

The Prince stands on the steps, siding with the Baron above a city that is still burning. The Baron stands beside him, smoke on his coat and the locket back on his chain. Four thousand people are on the slopes and in the stands. For Temerach, the calculation inverts: a bound spirit in a stolen boy, and now in the Crown's own nephew, is desecration and the goddess's own business, and a Sword may act on that without asking anybody.

She names him across the whole width of the arena, in front of the city, as the King's officer's last lawful act. Then she draws.

**It is a shot nobody else alive could make:** distance, crosswind, smoke, the angle of the steps, and a crowd in between. Roll it, at Formidable, and it hits, because she is who she is. And it is not enough. The arrow takes him high, through the shoulder or the side. He goes down on the steps, gets up again, and is alive. Then the Knights are coming across the arena for her, and the crowd breaks.

### The Flight

She is an outlaw from the instant the arrow leaves the string, and she knew it before she drew. **She flees with the characters,** or the characters flee with her: over the water, through the smoke, and out of the city by whatever route is left, whether Crowbill's canals, a Dredger's boat out through the Mouth, or the open road. It costs them something to get out, and it should. She does not thank them and does not explain.

**The end of ending B.** The Baron is wounded and alive. The Prince is a vessel. The Lullwater is ash. The characters are on the road out of Purewater with a King's officer turned outlaw, and the only way left to undo any of it is four days away, in a tower on the border estate, in a box of bones.

## Thuban Breaks Out

<!-- beat: thuban-breaks-out -->

**Ending C, the one the players have to earn:** only if the characters kept Nus alive through the burning and the locket is still out of the Baron's hands at dusk.

The Baron has spent the day burning the Lullwater and has not found it. At dusk he has no leash, and for the first time in ten years nobody renewed the seat last night with the anchor in hand.

**The characters cannot command Thuban.** They are not demonologists. Holding the locket keeps it from Hanzo and does nothing else for them. They cannot break it either: destroying it would take a powerful Dispel Magic that nobody here can cast. What they can do is keep it away from him, and that is enough, because Thuban is no longer obeying anybody.

### The Breakout

Somewhere on the DragonBarge, or on the competitors' ground where the Baron has dragged his champion to keep him close, the thing in Cailan stands up: a knight of the Sixth Wing with a thousand years of courtesy and ten years of grievance, in a seventeen-year-old's body, with the leash off.

**He fights his way out through the Dragon Knights.** Play it as the dragon wars come back for an evening: unarmed at first, then with a Knight's own sword, through the deck, over the side and up the pier. He is looking for one person, the woman who came aboard two nights ago and spoke to him in her brother's name, and when he finds the characters he falls in beside them as though he has always been there.

**Temerach covers the retreat** from the far side of the water with a bow, shot after shot. She has seen what came out of that ship, and she knows now what was under the helm. Whether she meant to or not, she is leaving with them. They get out of Purewater. It should cost them a boat, a route, or a friend holding a stair, but they get out.

### And He Is Still Not Her Brother

The locket only ever tied Thuban to Hanzo. What holds him in the world is a box of bones in a tower on the border estate, four days' hard riding away, behind the Baron's own men. Until the bones are found and the rite is said, Thuban stays where he is, and Gabriel stays under him.

**The last scene of the adventure is Thuban telling Gardwen where the bones are,** with perfect thousand-year-old courtesy, in her brother's voice, because under pressure his manners get better rather than worse, and asking, very politely, whether she would be so kind as to help him die. Do not resolve it. Do not let anybody say the words tonight. Magda's dream said *lay the dragon warrior to rest*, and now she knows where.

## Tat Counts the Cost

<!-- beat: tat-counts-the-cost -->

At night, whatever has happened, Tat Atarer does arithmetic in the open: men lost, a contract that keeps expanding, and a growing certainty about what was behind the inner doors. His soldiers are hired, not sworn, and that is the Baron's structural weakness.

**This is visible only to a character who has been cultivating him.** Advance *Get paid, keep the company whole* whenever the characters cost the Dragon Knights something: a man in the water, a broken contract, a burning quarter that will get a company hanged. At 2 or more he becomes negotiable in a way no other Dragon Knight is. He will not fight the Baron. He will march his company home and leave the Baron with six men on a ship, and in ending B or C, a Knight who does not pursue is worth a great deal to people on the road out of the city.
