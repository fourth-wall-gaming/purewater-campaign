# The Sand and the Theft

*The second day of the Tourney (d1).* The champion wins. The Baron sees four generations of work within his grasp, goes to bed easy for the first time in his life, and takes the chain off his neck.

**What this day takes:** safety. Whatever happens tonight, the city pays for it tomorrow.

## The Champion Takes the Sand, and Wins

<!-- beat: the-champion-takes-the-sand-and-wins -->

**The climax the whole week has been running toward, and it is a real fight, not a formality.**

The champion comes out of the litter at last, in full harness with the helm down, and the helm stays down; nobody sees his face on the sand. He is seventeen and thin, and he fights like a knight of the dragon wars, because he is one. Thuban Eta does the fighting, and Cailan is not there for it, which is the nearest thing to mercy in his life. He wins each bout in under a minute. In the final he breaks his opponent's leg in three places. The crowd goes quiet instead of loud.

**He wins. He is meant to win, and everything depends on it.** Use Cailan's statistics with Thuban's Combat Style: Thuban fights through his host's body, with the host's hit locations and armour.

**Ways to stop him, all of them hard:**

- **Beat him on the sand.** Nobody entered can. Temerach could, but she is entered in the Archery, and the Single Combat closed its lists on the eve.
- **Have the match stopped.** Nerissa can halt a bout on sacred ground if she has cause she can defend in front of the city: a demon-marked blade on her altar, Ivo Calder's notes, or Conall's finding. She stops it once, publicly, and the Baron must answer for it before the Prince. This does not end his plan; it moves the room.
- **Reach Thuban.** Speak with the Bound, or Gardwen's voice at the rope, can make the thing in the harness hesitate for one Combat Round. Thuban does not want to win. He cannot disobey.

**And the Baron sees victory in his grasp.** For the first time all week he lets it show: a hand on the champion's shoulder, a bow to the Governor's box, the warm, easy man going home to sleep well. Tomorrow at the closing he walks into the room. That ease is what makes tonight possible.

## The Nightly Reinforcement

<!-- beat: the-nightly-reinforcement -->

Every night, behind sealed doors in the working cabin aft, the Baron renews the binding: a cut, a bowl, the locket in his hand, and an hour of words. The laundress cannot get the grey residue out of the same linen every morning and has stopped mentioning it.

**This is offscreen unless characters have got inside the ship,** which is a whole operation. Anybody close enough to hear it learns the two most important facts in the scenario: that the champion is a prisoner, and that the thing inside him does not want to be there. Thuban surfaces during the rite, and is heard, once, speaking in another man's voice. A betrayed knight, enslaved through the torment of that betrayal, and a possible ally if the characters can reach him rather than the boy.

**Tonight it matters twice over.** When it is done, the Baron takes the chain off and lays the locket on the table by his bed, and that is the hour Nus and Randall go in over the stern.

## Nus and Randall Lift the Binding Locket

<!-- beat: nus-lifts-the-binding-locket -->

**The hinge of the whole adventure.**

The champion has won. The Baron has come back to the barge with victory in his grasp, renewed the seat, taken the chain off, laid the locket on the table beside his bed, and gone to sleep. Nus and Randall go aboard after the reinforcement, in the dead hour. Nus has wanted one big score since Crowbill named the locket at dawn yesterday. Randall is the only thief in the city who has been mistaken for a di Teufel, and can stand on that deck for three seconds without anybody shouting.

What they need:

- **The way in:** Gardwen's map from last night, if the party has it: the third step, the turn of the glass, the cabin aft. Without it the doubled watch makes this very hard, with every Stealth roll one grade harder.
- **The cabin:** Blau is asleep across the door, which means the way in is not the door. It is the stern windows, from a boat, over steaming water.
- **The table:** a locket on a chain with a clasp, and Nus's hands. It is not welded on and it is not on the Baron's neck. That is the whole point of doing it tonight.

**Roll it for real:** Stealth against the watch, Athletics for the stern, Sleight for the table, and Perception for Blau if anything is fumbled. If it fails, they are seen and run with nothing, or are caught. Either way the locket stays with the Baron, and the original plan goes ahead: the private audience at the closing, and Thuban into the Prince (see *The Last Day*).

**If it succeeds,** they are over the side with it before anybody knows, and the Baron wakes at first light to an empty table. *The Lullwater Burns* follows at dawn.

**It happens only if somebody points them at it.** Crowbill does, so in the natural course it happens. If the characters have deliberately stopped Nus, it does not, and that is a legitimate campaign that leads to the first ending.

**What the locket is and is not.** It is the leash: without it in his hand, the Baron cannot command Thuban and cannot draw him out of the boy. It is a robust working, and destroying it takes a powerful Dispel Magic, which the characters cannot simply cast. It does not free Thuban from the world, either; the bones in the border tower do that, and they are four days away. **Holding it lets nobody command Thuban.** It only takes him from Hanzo.

Stall *Field the champion* at once, and fill *One big score, and a name*.

## Santo Comes for It Himself

<!-- beat: santo-comes-for-it-himself -->

**This happens if the characters hold Santo's evidence:** the coat, the scroll or the knife. If Santo recovered everything on the second day, there is nothing for him to come for, and this is cancelled.

At night, for the evidence, Santo comes himself, because he cannot report the loss to his father twice. He is pathetic and lethal at once. **He does not run when he panics.** He takes hold of whoever is nearest, a servant, a girl or a thirteen-year-old like Ila, because it has always worked for him and nobody has ever made it stop working. He has his rapier, his fire, and nothing to lose that he understands.

If Santo dies, the Baron loses his only legitimate heir. If anybody connects the twins' face to the dead heir, *A di Teufel who is not his* wakes at once, if it is not awake already: with Santo gone, the twins are the only di Teufel blood walking, and that is a succession dagger at Hanzo's name. And Blau, whose whole job was the boy, stops cleaning up and starts hunting.
