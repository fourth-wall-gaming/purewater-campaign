# The Archery

*The first day of the Tourney (d0).* The characters stop reacting and start learning what they are up against. By the end of the night they know what the champion is, what holds him, and where that thing lies when the Baron sleeps.

**What this day takes:** Gardwen's hope that her brother can simply be taken home.

**The question of the day is jurisdiction, not evidence.** The standing order shields the Baron from the city's law and touches nothing else. Three courts lie beyond it: the King's Assay, maritime law and the Order's sacred ground. And three people have the standing to walk into them: Conall, Temerach and Nerissa. The middle of the scenario is the characters finding the court that fits, and somebody with standing who is willing to spend themselves in it. His money is the thread that unravels him, because it is not his.

## Crowbill Names His Price

<!-- beat: crowbill-names-his-price -->

At first light Crowbill collects a character off a ledge, as he has collected people in this city for thirty years, and puts them on a cookshop roof to talk. He already knows what they did last night: the hour, the tide and how many. He does not ask what they found, which is more unsettling than a threat, and it establishes exactly how much of the city he sees.

He does not help people. He trades, and he wants something they will not enjoy giving: intelligence on the Baron's plan, a route into the camp, or control of a story the street is already telling. He has a line, and demon-craft worked on a helpless girl beside the Lady's water crosses it. Bring him proof rather than a grievance and he opens canals, safe houses and men.

**What he can give that nobody else can** is the locket. The Baron wears a chain with a locket on it and has never been seen in daylight without it. Crowbill has a lamp-man on the Forge wharf who watches that ship for a living, and what the lamp-man has seen is that it comes off at night. The Baron sleeps aft with the locket on the table beside his bed, on a chain with a clasp rather than a collar, and Blau sleeps across the door.

He does not say *steal it*. He says what it is, where it lies, and that he knows a boy who would. Nus is in the next room and has heard every word.

**If no character goes to Crowbill,** he says the same thing to Nus anyway, over breakfast, as a joke. Nus does not hear it as a joke.

## Ivo Calder Folds

<!-- beat: ivo-calder-folds -->

**An opportunity:** this scene happens when the characters reach Ivo Calder with an independent reading of the water, after they have seen the seam in Alderic Vantt.

Ivo Calder is the Watercrafters' Tide Master and the city's appointed assessor: fifty, distinguished, guild indigo, his instruments kept beautifully, and he has not slept properly in six weeks. Six weeks ago, at one polite request from a man he respects, he signed off the Order's fouling alarm as a false reading. He kept his original notes, and he has moved them twice.

He has his whole defence prepared and wants to deliver it. A question he has not anticipated stops him dead. What he wants is to be told that what he signed was reasonable.

**There is no middle with him.** He mounts a vigorous defence, and then, in one breath, gives up everything the moment an independent reading appears: from Nerissa's people, from Gardwen's own senses at the water's edge, or from the Assay. What this buys is a name, not a conviction: who is actually paying the Baron. The man who is paying is not in this city and does not die on the sand. And Calder's notes reopen the alarm, which puts the Order officially onto the Baron and gives Nerissa the cause she needs to act on her own ground.

## The Archery

<!-- beat: pivot-the-archery -->

**The set piece of the day.** Two hundred paces of marked grass on the Long Butts, a crosswind off the lake that everybody complains about, the Governor's box at the near end and half the city lying on the slope behind. It is the one open event: no colours, no fee, anybody may shoot. That is why an elf in plain harness is standing in it.

**Temerach wins.** Let her. After three days of being stopped on the road and spoken to in front of her own escort, she puts two hundred paces of arrows where nobody else in the field can.

**The dice decide what comes after,** as she stands in front of the box with four thousand people watching and a prize to be handed to her. Roll her Passion *See Hanzo di Teufel called to answer* against her Loyalty to the Swords:

- **She spends it.** She names, out loud, whose men stopped a King's officer on the Ashwick road and what has been done in this city this week. Thirty years of standing are converted into one public accusation that the Governor cannot pretend not to have heard. It buys a hearing, and it makes her a marked woman two days early.
- **She holds it.** She takes the prize, says the correct words and walks away. She is still a King's officer with a clean record and a door she can open once. Colder, and far more useful later.
- **She loses.** Rarer, and better than it sounds: the household's archer takes the wreath, and the scene becomes the characters watching the one person with standing lose her chance to use it.

Whichever way it goes, this is the scene the last day's shot answers.

## A Price on the Face

<!-- beat: a-price-on-the-face -->

**This happens only if *A di Teufel who is not his* is awake,** which in the natural course of things it is: Sinnit sold the faces to Blau last night.

Blau puts money on the street: a purse for a name, and a bigger one for delivery. By the next dawn every dockhand, courtesan and cutpurse in Purewater has the description. From this point the twins cannot move openly. Randall's friends on the Pearl look at him and do arithmetic. Conall's warrant stops opening doors and starts announcing him.

**This is the scene that turns the back half of the scenario into a pursuit.** Blau will not announce a bastard to the city; he will take one off a street at night, and he is patient. A twin taken now is brought to the barge, and the Baron wants the tool before he extinguishes the claim.

## Gardwen Goes Aboard

<!-- beat: gardwen-goes-aboard -->

She has seen her brother's face for four seconds after ten years, and she is not going to wait for the sand to see it again.

**The ship.** Twenty aboard, a private pier on the Forges' seaward side, and a hull steaming where it meets the water. A stranger on that deck is noticed inside a minute, and since the presentation Blau has doubled the watch and stopped changing it at a fixed hour. Getting aboard is a whole operation: Crowbill's canal routes, Ruhi's nose, the dark under the pier, or a party making noise at the far end of the wharf. Make it a real infiltration, with Stealth against the Dragon Circle's Perception, and let it cost something.

**Below the waterline aft** she finds a lamp that is never put out and a keeper asleep across a door, and *Orrin Sculle Decides* happens the moment she is seen. Play it in full. Everything she learns tonight she learns from him or from the boy:

- **What is inside her brother:** a dragon-knight called Thuban Eta, a thousand years dead, held in a living body by a torment and a binding. Her brother is still in there. Orrin can prove it: Gabriel holds a cup left-handed, and the thing wearing him is right-handed.
- **What holds the binding:** two anchors. The bones, in a tower on the Baron's border estate, hold Thuban in the world. The locket on the Baron's chain ties Thuban to Hanzo. It is the leash, renewed every night, and without it the Baron can neither command him nor move him.
- **What he is for:** if Orrin's Love wins, he tells her what he has worked out and never said. The Baron does not want a champion. He wants the room after the bout.
- **The ship's routines:** the third step that complains, the watch that changes at the turn of the glass, and the Baron's cabin aft beside the working cabin. This is the map Randall and Nus will use tomorrow night.

**If Thuban surfaces,** as he sometimes does in the small hours, he speaks to her with perfect, thousand-year-old courtesy in her brother's voice. He does not want to be there. He cannot leave while the bones hold him, and he cannot disobey while the locket does. That is the most important conversation in the scenario, and it should be quiet.

**She cannot take him tonight.** Orrin will not help, and he is right: taking the body frees nobody while the anchors hold. She leaves knowing exactly what has to be taken, and from whom. If Emmeralda is aboard, she is forward in the good cabins, bright and wrong, and calls out to anybody she sees.

**If she is caught,** she is not killed. Blau wants to know who she is. A captured Gardwen aboard the barge is a rescue for the party and a very bad night for her.

## Orrin Sculle Decides

<!-- beat: orrin-sculle-decides -->

**This scene has no time and never happens on its own.** It is a door that opens the first time a character gets aboard the ship, or gets near the boy by any route: a delivery, an invitation, a disguise, the Washing, or somebody else's errand. In the natural course of things Gardwen reaches it tonight.

A man in his fifties comes up out of the after hold carrying an empty basin: heavy forearms, sleeves rolled past the elbow, a smell of liniment and lamp oil. He is the last person on that ship anybody thinks about, and he has been aboard longer than everybody on deck except the shipmaster. He does not shout. He sets the basin down without looking at it and tells the intruder exactly what is supposed to happen next: he calls out, six men in mail come up the forward hatch, and nobody ashore ever hears of the intruder again. Then he asks, "So before I do — what are you?"

**What decides it is not a roll on the character's side.** Nothing a character can say is cleverer than the thing that actually works, which is the truth, and the truth is the one thing he has not been offered in ten years. A character who lies to him gets the six men. It is decided by his own Passions, rolled against each other in front of the player: **Love (the boy) 70% against Fear (the Baron) 65%**.

- **Love wins.** He does not move out of the way; he sits down on the coaming like a man whose legs have stopped agreeing with him. What he gives is not access but knowledge: the boy's real name, which nobody has said aboard that ship in ten years, and the cup moved from one hand to the other. Then twenty minutes, boots off, and a warning: the third step complains, and the watch changes at the turn of the glass.
- **Fear wins.** He does not betray them either, because he is not a cruel man. He tells them to get off his ship, and he means it, and he will be at the top of that companionway every time they come back. This is not a failure: he is now somebody they have met and could try again, and a second approach should be harder and possible.
- **Both critical, or both fumbled.** He asks them to take the boy with them tonight, which they cannot do, and he will not forgive them for refusing.

**He will not help them steal the boy.** Ask him and he says no, and he is right: taking the body frees nobody while the anchor holds. He is proof of something the plot does not need and the story does: somebody has watched this boy closely enough for ten years to know which hand he reaches with. Everything else in the scenario says Cailan is a weapon that used to be a person. Orrin is the only evidence that he is still in there.
