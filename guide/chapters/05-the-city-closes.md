# The City Closes

*Two days and one day before the Tourney (d-2 and d-1).* The face is arrested twice, two strangers meet as brothers in a cellar, and the four characters come together. Then the face gets a name, the Pearl draws a line, and the characters spend the rest of these two days discovering that knowing who did it is worth almost nothing. It ends at the presentation, where Gardwen sees her brother.

**What these days take:** the twins' anonymity, and the belief that evidence matters by itself.

**The shape of it.** The second morning is fixed: the catacombs. After that the characters choose where to go, and the scenes below happen whether they are there or not. Three threads run through these two days. Santo comes back for what he left. Sinnit turns Randall's home ground against him and then sells what he knows. And the occupation keeps tightening until somebody with standing does something about it, or until the Dredgers do.

## Hesper Takes the Look-Alikes

<!-- beat: hesper-takes-the-look-alike -->

Marisette does not wait for the watch. By midnight the house has given her a description, and by the small hours her people have found the face twice: a thief fencing a morning's take who answers to it, and a young man-at-arms lodged near Shilling Stairs who answers to it as well. She does not know which, so she takes both.

Hesper walks up to each of them in turn before dawn and puts them down without a fight worth the name: a Sleep or a Hold Person, and two men from the Mother of Pearl with a blanket and a boat. They wake bound to chairs side by side in the dark under the Hill quarter, looking at each other.

**Run the two takings as two short scenes** if both twins are characters, or one scene with the other twin already in his chair if only one is. Nobody gets away from Hesper; this is not what the scene is for. A character who fights is put down harder and wakes with a worse headache. If neither twin is a character, both are still taken, and the party hears of it from Magda or Gardwen, who are about to be sent for as witnesses.

## Two Men with One Face

<!-- beat: the-catacombs-two-men-one-face -->

**The pivotal scene of the opening, and the one where the party is made.**

The burial galleries are dry, cold and chalk-white, with three centuries of the respectable dead stacked in them. Two men with the same face, who have never met, sit tied to chairs in the lamplight. Marisette stands in front of them with the lamp. Hesper stands behind them, where they cannot see him.

### The Interview

Marisette questions them together, which is cruel and clever: each hears the other's answers, and each looks at his own face while he gives them. She wants to know where they were at the turn of the night and who the girl was to them. She does not say what happened to the girl.

Somewhere in it, somebody says the word *brother* out loud. Let the twins get there themselves if they can. Randall arrives already carrying a guard who called him *my lord*. Conall arrives carrying a canal-rat in the Lullwater who called him *Randall* and wanted an apology for something he never did, and here is Randall.

### The Witnesses

Marisette sends for the two women from the house, or they come on their own, because Gardwen saw the man on the stair and wants to know whom Marisette has taken. Magda and Gardwen clear the twins on what they saw:

- **The hand.** The man on the stair was cradling a burnt right hand. Neither of these two has a mark on him.
- **The coat.** He was dressed better than either of them, and the coat he wore is still over a chair in that room, with a crest under the collar.
- **The hour.** If either twin can be placed elsewhere at the turn of the night, a witness who was there makes it stick.

Marisette believes a person who was there over a face, every time. Use her Insight against the witnesses' Influence if a roll is wanted; with the burn and the coat, the roll is Easy.

**If the witnesses do not carry it,** because the dice go badly or a character will not cooperate, other influences tip the scales, each with a price. A folded note reaches Marisette by the hand of Temerach's squire, Murgeroch, and whatever is in it, Marisette unties Conall without another question and looks at him differently afterwards. Crowbill sends word that Randall is a known man of the Lullwater and he would like him back. Or Emmeralda, if she is alive and herself, is carried down to look at them and shakes her head. Marisette is right in the end either way; the slow way means hours in the dark first.

### What It Makes

This is the first time the four characters are in one room: two women who saw it, two men who were blamed for it, and a madame who now owes the twins an apology. Play the moment the ropes come off. It is the party's founding image.

Satisfied, Marisette looks at the face in front of her for a long moment. If the coat has been brought from the house, she turns the collar over and reads the crest. **She names the di Teufels.** She does not know which di Teufel, and she knows exactly what it means that two strangers in her city wear their face.

From her questions, Conall has just heard what was done upstairs at the Sylph's Embrace. Demon-craft worked on a woman under a Purewater roof is the King's Assay's business, and he is the only assayer in the city. He walks out of the cellar and goes to read the room.

Advance *Find who carved Emmeralda* by 2.

## Conall Reads the Room

<!-- beat: conall-reads-the-room -->

The moment Conall learns what was done at the Sylph's Embrace, he investigates it. It is not curiosity. Seating a spirit in an unwilling person is a hanging matter under the Assay's charter. If he hears about it sooner, on the night, from the street or in a note from Temerach, bring this scene forward.

**The door.** Constantine does not let strangers into that room. The warrant opens it: a thumb-ring of assay silver, a steel punch, and a man who says *the Crown's Assay* in a voice that expects to be obeyed. She has never seen an assayer, though she has heard of them. She lets him in and stands in the doorway to watch.

**What is in the room** is whatever Santo left and nobody has taken:

- **The chalk seat** under the rug, still live. Detect Magic finds it. Read Magic, or the Assay's training, reads what it was *for*: a seating, not a summoning. Somebody tried to put something into her, and whether the seat is spent or broken tells him whether it took.
- **The spent scroll,** nine lines in an unregistered hand, which is an offence before anybody asks whose. Conall can say accurately that whoever wrote it has never been on the Register and is very, very good.
- **The barbed knife** behind the armoire, if the working failed. The Assay knows the mark of Asmodeus on its pommel.
- **The coat,** if Marisette has not already taken it.
- **Emmeralda,** cut open and tainted if it failed; and if it took and she is still in the house, bright and wrong and asking for the window to be opened. Conall's Detect Magic shows him what is sitting in her.

**What it is worth.** The Assay's doctrine is *establish, record, refer*, and Conall is good at it. A written finding, struck with his own punch, is evidence a council cannot wave away and the basis of a capital charge. It is the first lawful lever anybody has had on the di Teufels all week, and it reaches a jurisdiction the standing order does not touch.

**And the price.** A finding that names a great lord, laid by a junior assayer who cannot make it stand, is a finding he answers for himself. Temerach will want to know what he has written before he seals it. The doctrine says send it to Caldreth, where it will arrive after everything has happened. His brother, whom he has had for about four hours, says do something now.

**The race.** Santo wants that room back, and his men come by water between the noon rest and the evening trade. In the natural course Conall gets there first, straight from the catacombs, and whatever he takes is safe from Santo. If the party dawdles, the boat arrives while they are still on the stairs, and the scene becomes a fight in a doorway over a coat.

## A Boat Taken off the Water-Stair

<!-- beat: a-boat-taken-off-the-water-stair -->

Four Knights come down a water-stair on the Pearl and step into a working skiff that already has a fare in it. The fare gets out. The boatman, a Shoals man named Perrin Cale, forty, who has rowed this stair for twenty years, stays with his boat, because it is his livelihood and he has decided, wrongly, that he can. They let him row. The two boatmen taken before him, numbers 24 and 25 in the watch's referral book, have not come back. Nobody has found a body, and the Dredgers have decided they were rowed out through the Mouth and left there.

**Onscreen, this is the easiest intervention in the scenario and the most instructive.** Four armoured men in a small boat on deep water, in a city where every local can swim and none of them can. A party that thinks about the water rather than steel wins this without a fight, and makes three hundred dockworkers extremely interested in them.

**Offscreen,** roll for Perrin Cale: he comes back at midnight with a broken hand, or he does not come back at all. Either way Marda Blackwater hears about it before breakfast.

Advance *Hold the streets* by 1 and *Make them pay for one of ours* by 1.

## Santo Comes Back for Her

<!-- beat: santo-comes-back-for-her -->

**This scene happens only if the working took and Santo failed to get Emmeralda out on the night.** If he already has her, she is on the barge and this is cancelled. If the working failed, he does not want a girl with a wound the length of her body: cancel this and go straight to the tantrum.

She is still in Constantine's house, and she still wants to go with him. That is the horror of it: the house has to keep her in while she is trying to get out, and the thing inside her is patient and charming and keeps asking for the window to be opened.

So he comes back by water: a boatman hired off the Shoals, two men who ask no questions, and a covered skiff at the water-stair behind the house between the noon rest and the evening trade. He does not have to carry her. He only has to be there when she reaches the door. He also wants his coat and his scroll, and sends one of the men upstairs for them if the house is quiet enough. If the room has been searched, the man finds bare boards, and Santo goes after whoever has them.

**Onscreen,** this is a fight, a chase over water, or a negotiation the party wins by simply being present and awake. The hard part is Emmeralda, who is on his side. **Offscreen,** if nobody is watching that stair, he gets her, and from dusk she is aboard the DragonBarge, willing, and nobody's prisoner but her own. Gardwen will find her there on the first night of the Tourney. If he fails, the tantrum follows by dusk.

Advance *Have her* by 2 if he takes her, and *Clean up after the young master* by 2 if it goes loudly wrong in public.

## Sinnit Tells the Pearl It Was Randall

<!-- beat: sinnit-tells-the-pearl-it-was-randall -->

By mid-morning the whole Pearl knows Marisette's people took somebody in the night for what was done at the Sylph's Embrace, and Sinnit makes sure it knows who. He tells it on every stair and in every wine-shop between Pearl Quay and the Shoals: *Randall.* Randall, raised in the Promise of Heaven, who turned on the women who raised him. Randall, whom Hesper herself came for. Sinnit was in the Lullwater last night with a man wearing that face, and he has a bruise to show for it.

**It does not matter that Marisette cleared him.** The clearing happened in a cellar in front of four people. The accusation happens in the open, forty times, in a voice that carries. The city now believes, falsely, that the man who carved Emmeralda was Randall the Pearl thief.

What it costs Randall, on the one ground where he is rich:

- The Promise of Heaven's door opens to him a little more slowly, and Asphodel's face holds a question she is ashamed to ask.
- A fence he has used for six years is out when he calls.
- Somebody spits on the stair behind him.
- Ila, at the Sylph's Embrace, looks at him and runs.

None of it is permanent, and all of it is this week, which is the week he needs it. Marisette can end it with one sentence on the Quay, and will if somebody asks her, at a price. Emmeralda, if she is alive and herself, ends it by being seen with him. Or Randall can go and find Sinnit, which is what Sinnit wants.

**Sinnit has also seen the real Randall today,** walking the Pearl free and unbruised, and understands there are two of that face. Advance *Make Randall say it* by 1 for that, and 1 for the rumour.

## Santo's Tantrum on the Pearl Street

<!-- beat: santo-s-tantrum-on-the-pearl-street -->

**This scene happens if Santo does not have her,** because the working failed or the boat did. If he has her safe aboard the barge he has no reason to make a scene; skip this, and Constantine takes her loss to Marisette instead.

Santo comes back to the Pearl at dusk with his right hand bandaged and nobody with him, because he has run out of people to send. He stands in front of the Sylph's Embrace and demands her. Then he demands his coat. Then he says "I'll burn your house with your girls inside it," in front of the evening crowd.

**And then he does something about it.** A cantrip his father let him learn: a gout of fire into the doorway, the hangings and the painted shutters. It is not big, and it does not need to be. On a street of timber and lamp-oil it is the worst thing anybody has done on the Pearl in twenty years, and people are running with buckets before he has finished.

**Blau arrives inside a minute.** He has been looking for the boy since noon. He takes him by the collar and by the burnt wrist and walks him off the Pearl without a word, through thirty witnesses who now know exactly what that face is called. The name follows them down the street: *di Teufel. The son.*

**This is the scene that makes Santo known.** Until now he was a face; now the face has a name, a burnt hand and a public threat on it, and anybody who was in the catacombs this morning can put the three together. Constantine puts out her doorway, counts her girls and goes straight to Marisette.

Advance *Clean up after the young master* by 2. Blau now has to decide how much of this reaches the Baron.

## The Pearl Closes to the Dragon Knights

<!-- beat: the-houses-close-to-the-di-teufels -->

Constantine comes to the Mother of Pearl with smoke still in her hair, or, if Santo got the girl away clean, with an empty room and a coat with a crest in it, and tells Marisette what a di Teufel has done under one of her roofs.

Marisette does not close the Pearl. That would put four hundred of her own people out of work to punish twenty-five men. **She closes it to them.** Every house on the island, at once, to anybody in di Teufel colours or in the Baron's pay: no rooms, no drink, no music and no girls, with a doorman on every stair who knows the dragon when he sees it. By morning a Knight who walks onto the Pearl walks into a street where every door is shut in his face and every woman on it is watching him go. Crowbill's people enforce it, for a fee Marisette pays without blinking.

It costs the di Teufels nothing material and a great deal of face: twenty-five bored men with no Pearl in tournament week. The whole city reads it as the Pearl taking a side. It costs the Pearl almost nothing, which is why it can hold. The Baron does nothing to Marisette directly; he leans on the Prince's councillors instead, and gives Blau a sharp word about the boy. The Knights take it out on the street.

Advance *Find who carved Emmeralda* by 1 and *Hold the streets* by 1.

## Temerach Tells Conall Whose Son He Is

<!-- beat: temerach-tells-conall-whose-son-he-is -->

In a stable, out of earshot of anybody, Temerach finally tells Conall the truth she has held for years. She has not been waiting for courage. She has been waiting for the moment when the truth is more useful to her told than kept: the moment he would find it out anyway, from somebody she does not control. Today he woke in a chair in the catacombs next to a thief wearing his face, who had been called *my lord* by a guard at the Baron's stirrup.

His mother was an elven mage the Baron seduced and broke. She fled to this city and died here. Temerach has known since before she recruited him.

**Play it as a handler briefing an asset, and let the warmth leak through anyway.** She tells him in about six sentences. She does not apologise. She tells him what it means for the next two days and what she needs him to do about it, and then, once, she says something that is not a task.

If Conall is not a character, this still happens, and the party learns it secondhand from a Conall who is suddenly a different man. If the players work it out on their own first, bring the scene forward: there is nothing worse than a Non-Player Character withholding a fact the table already has.

## The Stairs Stop

<!-- beat: the-stairs-stop -->

**This happens if nobody has helped Marda Blackwater by dawn on the eve of the Tourney:** if Emmit Rill has not been paid, the boatmen have not been accounted for, and the referral book has not been put in front of somebody who outranks the council. If any of those has happened, cancel the scene and let her hold her people.

At dawn the water-stairs are empty. There is no cartage, no provisioning, no boats for hire, no ice, no fish and no lamp oil. And there is no competitors' gear moved to the Tournament Ground, which turns a labour dispute into a sacred crisis. Marda stands at the head of the Fishmarket Stair and is entirely reasonable about it, which is what makes her formidable. Her terms are small: wages for Emmit Rill, an accounting of the two boatmen, and one Knight answering for something in front of a magistrate. Any one of the three, and the stairs move again.

**What it does to everybody:**

- **The Baron** cannot have this. A stopped port on the eve of the Tourney draws the Prince's personal attention onto his men two days before he needs a quiet room, and Tat Atarer tells him so bluntly. This is the pressure point where Hanzo can be made to give something up: a Knight handed to a magistrate, or a purse for Rill.
- **The council** leans on the watch to clear the stairs, and the watch has to decide whether it clubs three hundred of its own neighbours on the orders of the men who wrote the standing order. Sergeant Abel Weir will not.
- **The Order of the Lake Lady** cares because the Tourney is sacred and the festival is being strangled.
- **The characters** can broker it, break it or arm it. A crowd of three hundred at the Tournament Ground is something the Baron's plan does not survive, and neither, possibly, does anybody standing in front of it.

Do not resolve it for them. Put the empty stair in front of the players and stop. If nobody brokers it, the stairs open again at dusk on the first day of the Tourney, on the Prince's personal promise to hear Marda after the closing, and the anger goes back into the Lullwater to wait.

## Maro Quist Reads It to the End

<!-- beat: quist-reads-it-to-the-end -->

**An opportunity, not an event:** this scene happens when the characters take what they have found to the oldest councillor in the city, at Palazzo Quist in the Merchant's Quarter.

Maro Quist is seventy-one, upright, and always wears his chain. He does not interrupt and cannot be interrupted. He reads the whole thing, actually reads it, in front of them, at his own pace, while they stand there. Then he repeats their position back to them more accurately than they put it, and tells them exactly why it is not his.

The watch is under a written standing order, and Quist carried the chamber for it on the merits. He is not corrupt, not frightened and not persuadable, because he is right: a baron's son with a knife is weather, and you do not sue the weather. He also belongs to a dining society, the Ember Society, that is a recruiting front for the Movement, and thinks its members are antiquarians.

**If he reads it twice, he has changed his mind,** and the room should know it. What moves him is not the crime but the hand: a scroll in the Baron's own writing, or an Assay finding with Conall's punch on it, or Ivo Calder's original notes. Quist turned is the best ally in the city government. He cannot touch the Baron, but he can withdraw the standing order, and that changes every street in Purewater.

## The Presentation of the Entrants

<!-- beat: the-presentation-of-the-entrants -->

**The unmasking, and it comes the day before the sand.**

By long form, every entrant in every event is presented to Prince Emeric on the eve of the Tourney: names read, colours shown, and each competitor bare-headed for the space of a bow. It is ceremony, it is tedious, and the whole city half-watches it from the stands. The Baron cannot avoid it without forfeiting the entry.

So the champion is walked out in harness with the rest. For perhaps four seconds the helm is off, and a seventeen-year-old who has been opened every night for a decade stands in daylight in front of several hundred people.

**If Gardwen is there, she sees her brother.** Ten years, and he is thirty yards away in somebody else's colours.

**And Blau sees her see him.** He stands behind the champion where he always stands, watching the crowd rather than the Prince, because watching the crowd is his whole job this week, and one woman in it has stopped breathing. He does not know who she is. He knows what that face means on anybody looking at this boy. By dusk he has a description of her, and from then on the watch on the DragonBarge is doubled and changes at no fixed hour. Advance *Keep the champion unseen* by 1. Gardwen's break-in on the first night of the Tourney is harder for it, and should feel it.

If Gardwen is not there, somebody else may recognise the boy anyway: Sana, from the temple; Orrin, from the ship; or anybody who notices a boy too thin for the armour he is in.

This is the last moment before the bout when anything can be arranged calmly. Play the recognition, and do not resolve what she does about it. If nobody stops her, she goes aboard that ship to talk to him.

## Alderic Vantt Jokes About His Cellar

<!-- beat: alderic-vantt-jokes-about-his-cellar -->

The Baron dines tonight with his host, Councillor Alderic Vantt, at the Vantt Estate. Vantt is fifty-two, warm, well fed and genuinely good company: drink, chairs, somebody's coat taken, your name remembered aloud. He moves a conversation the way a man closes a door, so smoothly you only notice you are in the corridor. He is the channel the Baron's money runs through, and he has not said out loud, even at home, how frightened he is.

**The tell is two self-deprecating jokes in a row.** That is the seam, and it is the only one he has. A character who notices it (Insight, Hard) can push there, and he will talk about his debts before he talks about anything else.

**Watching the house is worth as much as anything said inside it:** who arrives, in what order, and who leaves early. The Baron arrives last and leaves first. Tat Atarer does not come at all.

## Sinnit Sells the Face

<!-- beat: sinnit-sells-the-face -->

**This happens if *Make Randall say it* has reached 3 and nobody has turned Sinnit.** If Randall has apologised to him, cancel it: this is the clearest thing the apology buys.

Sinnit has worked it out slowly, the way he works everything out. A man in the Lullwater with Randall's face who did not know Randall's story. Randall himself walking the Pearl free the next day. The young lord on the Pearl street with a bandaged hand, shouting fire, wearing the same face as both of them. That makes three, and a man who knows there are three of something has something to sell.

He goes east to the Vantt camp at night in a borrowed boat and asks for the albino. He is kept waiting an hour in the wet, and then Blau comes out and listens without a word while Sinnit tells it, badly, twice, with the grievance in it: a thief called Randall off the Pearl, and another one, a gentleman with a ring, both with his lordship's face. Blau pays him, more than he expected, and asks one question: where the thief sleeps.

**This wakes *A di Teufel who is not his*,** at once and for good. Blau goes to the barge that night. By dusk on the first day of the Tourney there is a price on the face, and from then on the twins cannot move openly, in the same two days that one of them is meant to be on the Baron's ship in the dark.

What Sinnit wanted was Randall hurt, and he will get that. What he did not think about is what the Baron does with sons he did not know he had. When he finds out, it is the first thing that has ever frightened him more than being laughed at.

Advance *Make Randall say it* by 1 and activate *A di Teufel who is not his*.

## A Courier from the Movement

<!-- beat: a-courier-from-the-movement -->

A quiet boat comes alongside the DragonBarge at night with a sealed packet, and the Baron is reminded that he is not the top of this pyramid. The courier carries instructions about the champion and a line of credit that explains where the tournament money came from.

**This is offscreen unless the characters are watching the ship or intercepting messages,** and catching it is their only chance this week to learn the shape of what stands behind the Baron. The packet names nobody who is in Purewater, and it makes one thing plain: killing the Baron does not end this. The Movement is patient. A failure here costs it a season, not a life's work.
