**What he wants.** Santo has watched his father command devils and has
never once been trusted with the work. He has stolen a soul-knife marked with
the sigil of Asmodeus and intends to bind a pleasure demon into a living host
himself -- proof, laid at his father's feet, that the blood runs true in him too.

**How he pursues it.** Badly. He is cruel, impatient, and half-trained: he
picks a victim who cannot fight back rather than one the ritual suits, works
in a rented room rather than a warded circle, and panics when it goes wrong.
He is a nobleman in a city that indulges noblemen, and he expects that to hold.

**What changes his mind.** Nothing his father would recognise as reason. Fear
works: exposure, a knife at his own throat, or Hanzo learning what he has done
before he can present it as a success. Take the soul-knife and he does not
abandon the plan, he becomes frantic to recover it -- which is far more
dangerous than leaving it in his hand.

**If he dies,** the clock does not stop. Hanzo loses his only legitimate heir
and the campaign gains a succession problem: see *A di Teufel who is not his*.
