**What he wants.** One girl. Santo sees [[Emmeralda]] dance on his first
evening in the city and decides he is taking her home. Not for the night --
he can buy the night, and does. *Kept.* On his father's barge, in his own
rooms, his, and glad about it.

There is no grand design here and no demon army. There is a nineteen-year-old
with a title, an appetite, and a stolen knife, who has never been told no by
anyone who mattered.

**How he pursues it.** In the order a spoiled man does these things.

1. **He buys her evening**, anonymously, like anyone. He does not say his
   name; nobody on the Pearl knows the heir's face, and he likes it that way.
2. **He makes her willing.** He has watched his father seat a spirit in a
   living body and be obeyed by it ever since. He has one stolen scroll, a
   barbed knife, and no training whatsoever. What he means to put in her is a
   lust spirit -- something small and hungry that will make her want what he
   wants.
3. **The dice decide.** If the working takes *and* he gets her out of the
   house, **he keeps her**: she is on the barge by midnight, willing. If it
   takes and he is stopped on the stair, he comes back by boat on `d-2`. If it
   fails, he opens her the length of her body with the knife and runs.
4. **Thwarted, he loses his temper in public** -- fire on the Pearl street --
   and the city learns his name.

**What changes his mind.** Nothing his father would recognise as reason. Fear
works: exposure, a blade at his own throat, or Hanzo learning what he has done
before he can dress it as a success. Taking the knife off him does not make him
abandon her; it makes him frantic, which is far more dangerous than leaving it
in his hand.

**What this is for.** It is the campaign's front door. A wounded girl on the
Pearl, a house that wants justice, a face that belongs to two other men in this
city, and a rescue with an actual clock on it -- none of which the party has to
know is connected to the Tourney at all. It connects on its own.

**If he dies,** the clock does not stop. Hanzo loses his only legitimate heir
and the campaign gains a succession problem: see *A di Teufel who is not his*.
