# The Baron's Crown: the Movement, the Ember Society and the Bones

Design for the restarted Purewater campaign. Agreed in conversation with Gully on
2026-11-26. **Nothing here is canon until the edits below are approved and made.**

## Intent

Purewater restarts at `d-3/dawn`. The four characters played in earlier runs become
people in the world. The Awake the Dragon Movement, the Ember Society and the Baron
are rebuilt as one political conspiracy with the Baron at its head. The aim is a
campaign whose shape a GM can see whole: one hinge in the Tourney, two worlds after it,
and a second adventure (the Bones) that matters in both.

**Goals**
- The Baron is the Movement's leader, and his aim is political: depose the King and
  rule Thornmere as a man.
- Every faction, plot and played character has a place in that conspiracy, or a
  reason to stand against it.
- The Tourney decides which of two worlds follows, and both are playable.
- Thuban's rescue and the Bones adventure close the Gardwen thread properly.

**Non-goals**
- No change to the `setting/` source texts, and no change to any existing save's
  events. The only writes to the s1 save are the adventure and saga records that close
  the old storylines (see *Closing the old storylines*).
- Kag is unchanged.
- No new mechanics for running the old adventures beyond the engine's existing agendas,
  beats and storylines (see *Settled late*).

**Anti-goals**
- The Baron as a dark lord seeking his own transformation into a monster.
- A Movement so large it explains everything. Compartments hold: Vane does not know
  who is at the top, and Venn has never seen the gentleman's face.
- Gabriel as a broken victim to be carried.

## The Baron

**Hanzo di Teufel is a pure narcissist.** He believes in his own greatness and in
power, and in nothing else. The Dragon Kings, the Movement's faith and the fire he
binds are instruments, and so is everyone who serves him.

Proposed core text for his sheet and the possession scheme:

> Baron Hanzo di Teufel believes in one thing: himself. The Dragon Kings, the
> Movement's faith and the fire he binds are instruments, and so is everyone who
> serves him. He means to depose the King and rule Thornmere because he cannot
> imagine anyone else fit to. He lost the Tourney, and he remembers every face that
> watched.

(The last sentence applies only in the world where he loses; see *The hinge*.)

**How it plays**
- He holds the believers in contempt, and he cannot do without them. If they see
  that contempt, they turn on him.
- He is flattered, not briefed. Bad news reaches him late or never.
- He is sure he can master a Dragon King's knight. That is hubris, not a plan.
- He cannot let a humiliation stand.
- He moves for vanity, not for bribes or threats.

**The family**, revised: the di Teufels have wanted for four generations *to rule as
the Dragon Kings ruled*. The first found the texts, the second found Thuban's bones
and carried them to Emberhold, the third worked out the binding school, and the
fourth made it work. Hanzo is the first of them with no belief left, only appetite.

## Santo and the borrowed face

The twins are identical to each other and to Santo. **There is no tell.** Every risk
is behaviour.

**What the face buys:** the DragonBarge by day, with no invitation problem; Santo's
forward cabins and his tracings from the locked case, and with them sight of the
missing scroll; the Vantt dinner on `d-1`; and, on the locket night, a man already
aboard who can open a stern window for Nus and Randall.

**What it does not buy:** aft at night past the Dragon Circle; the champion's door,
where Orrin Sculle sleeps; or Blau, without work.

**Conditions:** only one Santo can be aboard at a time, so the real one must be
somewhere known. The Baron is the most dangerous audience: a twin who stands straight
under his contempt is wrong in a way he notices (Willpower).

**Blau** (Insight 60, Perception 72): Deceit against his Insight, opposed. Hard if
unprepared; Standard with Santo's clothes and rings and his habits learned from
somebody who knows him (Marisette, Emmeralda, the women of the Pearl, or Santo
himself, if held). A failed roll makes Blau suspicious, not certain: he tests, with a
question only Santo could answer or a hand on the collar to haul him home. Failing
the test is exposure. Each new scene with Blau is a new roll.

**How Santo ends:**
- *Exposed to his father* through the scroll: written off, "a father who has stopped
  forgiving him".
- *Dead:* Blau turns to vengeance, and the twins are the only di Teufel blood walking.
- *Unmasked as a stranger before the Baron:* "A di Teufel who is not his" wakes at
  once. The Baron sees a son better than Santo. He wants that son as a tool first, and
  kills the claim second.

## Gabriel and Thuban

**Thuban raised Gabriel.** For ten years below the barge's waterline the knight kept
the boy company, took the fighting and the rites so that Gabriel need not be present,
and taught him in the hours between. The relationship is paternal. Gabriel is not
mentally ill, and he was not held entirely the whole time: his "moments of clarity"
are the times he surfaced when the Baron was not watching, and hid it.

**What he learned:**
- **The code of the Sixth Wing**, above all that law outranks orders.
- **The old court tongue**, spoken and written. He can read the first di Teufel's texts.
- **The sword in his head:** every form, in a body that has never trained.
- **Patience:** ten years of letting the Baron see nothing.

**The cost:** laying Thuban to rest gives Gardwen her brother and takes Gabriel's
father. He goes north anyway, because it is what Thuban wants.

**What frees Thuban** is already canon: somebody must stand where his company stood
and say it was wrong, and then a burial rite works in a moment. *Unseat* alone will
not do it.

|---|---|---|
| The Quiet Room / the Burning | Seated in the Prince | Comes out of the room himself, his father taken. Wants to go after him |
| The Breakout | Breaks out in Gabriel's body, beside Gardwen | With him: two in one body |

## The truth behind the dragons

GM canon. The player-visible history (`the-dragon-kings-and-the-lake-lady.md`) stays as
written, because it is what everybody believes.

- **The Dragon Kings were men**: heraldry, legend and demonology. They never rode
  dragons, and no dragon skeleton has ever been found.
- **Real dragons exist but are extremely rare**, and an appearance is a major event. A
  real one (seed, not this week) might come out of the Hoarfells wanting its kind's
  name back, and set the record straight in front of the Movement's believers.
- **Each King was a man wearing a Crowned demon.** The bound soldiers carried lesser
  demons, and went mad over time, so command was held by unbound knights.
- **Thuban carried no demon.** He was a good enough soldier without one: an unbound
  knight-commander of the Sixth Wing. His company drowned him for refusing to burn the
  valley.
- **Today's Ash Lords** each carry a Wanting, and lose themselves over time.
- **The believers are wrong twice:** about the dragons, and about the Baron, who knows
  it was always demons and does not care.

### The demons

Demons are spirits from outside the world, older than it and hostile to it. Seated in
flesh they burn the host's life as fuel; that heat is where the fire costume came from,
and why the Drowned Trade's victims burn from inside.

| Rank | What it is | Seated, it gives |
|---|---|---|
| **The Wanting** | nameless; one hunger; does not speak beyond it | one gift; over months or years it eats the host's self, which is madness |
| **The Named** | a Wanting that has fed through many hosts and grown a name; speaks, bargains, lies, remembers | several gifts and a will of its own; worth an anchor; knowing its name makes binding it one grade easier |
| **The Crowned** | the greatest; each has a following of lesser demons | rule: the man becomes its face |

**Damnation.** Whoever dies with a demon seated in them does not go to rest: they go
into that demon's Crowned, which keeps them. That is the real price of being an Ash Lord.

**The six Crowned**, one to a King and a Wing:

| Crowned | Domain |
|---|---|
| **Aun, the Dragon** | elemental fire: fire that is alive and has intent. The dragon of legend was Aun's image on its King's banner; **the whole myth starts here.** The Sixth Wing served Aun, which is why the order was to burn the valley |
| **Imbra, the Never-Sated** | passions and drives, satiation and lust. The thing in Emmeralda is one of its Wanting |
| **The Pale Tallyman** | pain, suffering, death and war. The Ash Lords' rage Wanting are his |
| **Ishet, the Broken Choir** | madness and incoherence |
| **Vell of the Second Face** | lies, deceit and subterfuge; its servants are the hardest to detect |
| **Corrow, Who Wears the Crowd** | power over others: enslavement, subjugation, the end of the individual |

**Where they went.** The wild elves tore the Crowned out of the Kings at Drakesfall and
**drowned them in the Lake**: Thuban's death at the scale of an army, and why the age
ended "at the Lake". The Lady's water still holds them. One may be what is in the Old
Water beneath the Forges.

**Kade's ember-bone** is a relic of Aun: a finger of the Sixth Wing's King, still
holding a little of Aun's fire.

**Why the Baron chose a ghost.** A ghost obeys its anchors. A Crowned in the Prince
would eat him and rule in its own name, and a narcissist allows nothing above himself.
His ancestors wanted to become Kings by wearing a Crowned; Hanzo refuses, and that is
the difference between him and four generations. GM note: his idea of rule sits close
to Corrow's domain, everyone subjugated, but with himself on top. Corrow would erase him
as well. He does not see it.

### The Baron and the Named of Corrow

When Hanzo was about twelve, his father, who worked out the binding school, seated a
**Named of Corrow** in him: a test of the rite, and an heir who could move men. Hanzo
was opened as a child, as he later opened Gabriel. He thinks of it as his making.
For over a decade it taught him how people are made to move as one; his Influence and
his warmth are partly learned from it.

At about twenty-five, the elven mage he later seduced saw it with Spirit Sight and
took it out of him, and **it went back where it came from.** Without it he was exactly
the same man, and she was the only person who ever saw that. The narcissism was always
his; the demon only fed it. He broke her for seeing him. Spirit Sight still shows an old
closed seat on him, like a healed scar.

## Elves, and the di Teufels' racism

**Two kinds of elf.** *Standard elves* are common, familiar and playable: Temerach,
Emmeralda, Ila, Gardwen and the borderland elves. *Wild elves* of the Deep Glades are
like RuneQuest's aldryami: made of plants, with almost no contact outside the wood,
alien, and powerful shamans. They broke the Dragon Kings at Drakesfall, the only power
able to pull demons out at the scale of an army. **That is what the Movement fears.**

**Anti-elf racism is a defining feature of di Teufel rule.** The persecution falls on
standard elves, who are within reach, and the propaganda blurs the two kinds on
purpose. It is also strategy: the Wild Line came out of the wood through the
borderland elves, and thirty years of raids on the border cut that bridge.

- **To the Baron** it is useful and personal: land (everything north and west of
  Emberhold), a scapegoat, the counter-knowledge broken, and the elven mage who got
  away from him.
- **On the March:** elves outlawed and hunted; the raids are policy.
- **In Purewater this week:** the Knights single out elves; Hrill's scrapes.
- **If the Baron wins:** a royal edict in the bound Prince's name against elves and
  against the Swords of the Lake.
- **The twins are half-elven**, which is why their claim must be extinguished.
- **Gabriel was chosen on purpose:** elves hold a seated spirit better, which is also
  why they make the best shamans.
- Show it through acts, laws and who gets stopped on a bridge, not speeches.

**Contact with the wild elves** has no envoy. It goes through the bridge (a borderland
elf like Gleinanion who knows the way in) or through Spirit Walk. Wild elves and their
spirits are the likeliest snaring spirits around the Wildwood; going into the Deep
Glades is a scenario of its own.

## Magic: demonology and spirits

Classic Fantasy Imperative has no demonology. The engine's rules graph already
carries it as **powers** on the Mythras superpowers chassis, not spells
(`magic/powers/how-powers-work-here`):

- **The Binding** (`magic/powers/the-binding`): Spirit Sight and Speak with the Bound
  free; then Open the Channel (2 PP), drawing the seat (not magic), Seat the Bound
  (4 PP, opposed Willpower against an unwilling, waking host), Anchor (2 PP each),
  Reinforce the Seat (1 PP every morning) and Draw Forth (4 PP). Hanzo has all of it;
  Santo has the core power and Open the Channel.
- **The Wild Line** (`magic/powers/the-wild-line`): Gardwen's counter-set: Sanctuary
  (2 PP) and Unseat (4 PP, a Turn, opposed Willpower; the spirit comes out loose).
- **The Rites of the Lady**: the Order has no possession magic, by doctrine.

**CFI divine magic against spirits** (already in the graph): Spiritshield (Cleric 0:
a spirit must beat the caster's Piety with its Willpower to enter), Protection from
Evil (Cleric 1: possession automatically fails), Detect Evil (Cleric 1: shows demons),
Dispel Magic (ends spells; does nothing to powers or spirits) and **Exorcism**
(Cleric 3: drives out possession up to half the spell's Intensity; a spirit that wins
the duel may possess the loser). Turn Undead is not in the graph.

### Gaps Emmeralda's case exposes, and the fixes

Agreed: fixes 1 to 5. **Fix 6 (the slipping seat) is rejected.** What answered
Santo's channel in Emmeralda is **a passion spirit**: a Wanting of Imbra.

Santo has the core power, Open the Channel, one stolen scroll of Seat the Bound and
Willpower 69. Emmeralda has Willpower 58.

1. **Scrolls of a power.** A scroll of one step lets somebody with the core power
   perform that step once, one grade harder. Consumed whatever the result. Santo rolls
   Hard Willpower (46) against her 58.
2. **What answers an open channel.** An untrained binder rolls on a short table: from
   a lesser demon of appetite down to taint and nothing. Only a trained binder with an
   anchor gets what he called.
3. **Spirit statistics.** Lesser demons: POW, Willpower and a few manifestations (her
   canon surge: STR 16 and claws 1d3 for one action). The unquiet dead: the same, but
   with nowhere to be sent back to.
4. **A seat's Intensity**, for Exorcism: the binder's POW divided by 3, plus 1 per
   anchor. Santo's seat in Emmeralda is weak; Hanzo's seat of Thuban is beyond any
   priest in the city.
5. **Emmeralda's sheet:** "a powerful Dispel Magic" becomes **Exorcism**, which matches
   the rule that powers cannot be dispelled, and "a lesser pleasure demon" becomes a
   passion spirit, a Wanting of Imbra.
6. ~~The slipping seat~~ (rejected: no clock on Emmeralda's seat).

**Demons and the dead differ:** a demon driven out goes back where it came from, or is
loose in the room. The unquiet dead have nowhere to go: driven out, Thuban is loose and
still held by his bones, which is why only the rite at Emberhold ends him.

### Spirit Walk (new power)

Held by both traditions: **the Wild Line walks as a guest, the Binding as a hunter**,
and every spirit knows which. Walkers in play: Gardwen, Hanzo, the Wildwood elves.

- **The trance:** a few minutes and 2 PP. The body is helpless, deaf and blind, and
  should be watched, ideally under Sanctuary. Returning is free and at will.
- **The spirit world** is the same place seen as spirits see it: the Lady's waters
  shine, the DragonBarge is a wound of heat, seated spirits show through their hosts.
  Distance follows meaning, not miles. Thuban is always at the water where he drowned.
- **Finding** a spirit is Perception, helped by Spirit Sight. **Talking** is ordinary
  social play: Influence, Customs, Deceit. A friendly spirit always wants something.
  Friendly spirits hide from hunters.
- **Hostile spirits** name **one aim**, shown before the roll, and one opposed
  Willpower contest settles it:

| Aim | Spirit wins | Spirit criticals |
|---|---|---|
| **Sever** | Thrown back to the body with a level of Fatigue; no walking again that day | It also knows the walker by name and can find them |
| **Wound** | 1d6 to a hit location of its choice, ignoring armour, appearing on the body as burns or drowning | Also leaves a mark Spirit Sight can see, and the spirit can use it later |

  **The walker wins:** they choose: escape, an advantage on the next roll, or one
  question answered. A critical gives two.
- **Instead of standing:** *leave* before the roll (always allowed; the spirit knows
  they ran); *bargain* first, with an opposed social roll; or *shelter* under
  Sanctuary, which works there too.
- **The walker cannot harm a spirit.** Driving one out stays the work of Unseat,
  Exorcism and Draw Forth.
- **Snare is a rare trait, not an aim any spirit has.** A snaring spirit rules a place
  and takes the walker; the body stays in trance. It is a scenario seed, never a random
  encounter: getting somebody back is an adventure of its own. Candidates: whatever is
  in the Old Water beneath the Forges, something of the Mermaid's Court, a greater demon
  a binder called on purpose. Thuban does not snare: he is a prisoner, not a jailer.
  **The snared walker can break free:** the walker makes an opposed
  Willpower roll against the captor once per scene of the rescue story. Help from
  outside improves the walker's grade: a voice they love at the body, a rescuer
  walking in after them, or the captor's anchor broken in the waking world. A
  snared walker's body takes no Fatigue.

**Uses this week:** a walker can enter Emmeralda and find her beside the demon; Gardwen
or Gabriel can reach Thuban at his water before the Tourney; the Wildwood elves are
worth finding because they still walk; and hunting is how the di Teufels found Thuban.

The binding and wild-line rules live in the engine's rules graph
(`skills/mythras-gm/rules`), shared by every campaign. Changing them needs separate
approval.

## The next adventure: the Bones

Emberhold, four days north by Ashwick and the Scorched Hills: a keep of black stone
and a single tall tower above a burned valley.

**The valley is Thuban's.** The Sixth Wing drowned him in its water, then burned it
anyway. The di Teufels built their seat over the place their power begins. The bones,
the place where his company stood, and the texts holding the rite are all there.

**Stakes, by world:**
- *The Baron won:* laying Thuban to rest frees the Prince, and the coup falls with
  him. A counter-coup with the kingdom on it. The Baron is at court in Caldreth;
  Emberhold is held by a castellan and Ash Lords.
- *The Baron lost:* a race. He rides home behind them with everything he has left,
  wanting the locket.

**Acts:**
1. **The road north.** Ashwick and Sergeant Lysa Bell's post; Vane's oath companies;
   the Scorched Hills.
2. **Emberhold.** The keep, the tower, the box of bones, the first di Teufel's library.
3. **The water.** Standing where the Sixth Wing stood, saying it was wrong, and the
   rite, read and spoken by Gabriel. The Baron's people arrive first or last.

**Allies:** Temerach, who knows the border; Thuban, after the Breakout; Gabriel; the
Wildwood elves, who hold the counter-knowledge. Drakesfall is a day's ride from the keep.

## Closing the old storylines, and carrying them forward

The engine's saga and adventure structures (`STORYLINES.md`, *Sagas and adventures*)
are applied to the three played storylines in two places.

**1. Closing them in the s1 save** (`myth-campaign-purewater-s1`, before it is
archived):
- **Backfill each finished adventure** with `add-adventure --status concluded
  --started D --ended D --outcome ...`, from the journal and the scenario lore the run
  already holds (Hrill's *The Road Beyond the Banners*, *The Long Shore* and *The
  Unbroken Crown* preparation; Nimras's jobs and *The Midnight Exchange* arc; Halla's
  *Drowned Trade*). The builder assigns each one to its storyline by the lore's `about`
  field and the journal, and checks the dates against `list-adventures`.
- **Close the adventure in play** on each storyline: `concluded` where its question
  was answered, otherwise `abandoned`, with an outcome that states where it stood
  (Halla at `d14`, the Palazzo guardroom; Nimras at `d9` with Marek's offer unanswered;
  Hrill at `d44` with Bell's letter to Ward).
- **Set a saga line** on each, so the archived run reads as a finished story.

**2. Carrying them into the new game**, where the three are NPCs:
- **A longer thread for each:** a saga-style line held as the NPC's long agenda, with
  adventure-sized steps under it. Each step has a question and an end, and concludes
  when the question is answered. No step is widened into an unplanned sequel.

3. **A new package version alongside the old.** Clean history, but doubles the
   maintenance and forks the seed.

## Settled late

- **The old adventures run both ways.** The villains' operations run on the world
  clock, and the old protagonists are offscreen opposition: Halla, now an NPC,
  investigates the drownings himself; Hrill is on the road where Vane organises. The
  dice decide how they fare. A player can arrive mid-thread.
- **The partial win:** the Baron can try Draw Forth in the room without the locket, at
  Formidable, using the bones as his anchor. *Failure:* Thuban comes loose in the stone
  room with the Prince, and the Breakout starts there. *Success:* the Prince is seated
  with no leash; Thuban answers only to the bones, and the Baron must ride for Emberhold.
- **Orrin Sculle has noticed Gabriel surfacing, and kept quiet.** He will never help
  openly, but might sleep less soundly across the door on the right night.
- **Sister Anwen Coll** (she/her): gave the survey of the sealed springs. Wants the
  archivist's post she was passed over for; suspects her map is how Kade found the Warm
  Pool.
- **Master Tobias Wrenn** (he/him): the Prince's clerk who put Emeric's name on the
  order silencing the watch. Wants to be indispensable to the Prince; answers to Lady
  Marr through the Ember House; drafts what the Prince signs.
- **King Ostmar** (already canon at Caldreth, `lore/law/the-kings-justice.md`) is
  **decisive and strong**, and his nephew has been a disappointment. When Emeric comes
  home wearing Thuban, courteous, steady and formidable, Ostmar has at last the heir he
  wanted. He loves his nephew only once his nephew is somebody else. Thuban's courtesy
  is real, and the King is right about it: unleashed, Thuban would make a good heir.
- **From bound Prince to the Baron's own crown: the regency route**, one adventure per
  step, in the world where the Baron wins:
  1. **Favour.** Ostmar names the Prince heir and gives him command (the March, or the
     royal army). The Baron rides that trust as the Prince's oldest friend.
  2. **The court.** Lady Marr's people fill the Prince's household; Tobias Wrenn drafts
     what he signs. The elven edict goes out under the King's own seal at the Prince's
     urging. Vane's oath companies and the Drowned Trade's Ash Lords stand ready.
  3. **The King dies.** A strong King cannot be deposed, so he is killed, in a way that
     looks natural, by a servant of **Vell of the Second Face**. The Prince is crowned
     and makes the Baron Lord Protector.
  4. **The Prince is used up**, and the Baron is crowned by the council his Ember House
     made.
- **What it does to the Bones** in that world: laying Thuban to rest takes away the
  heir the King loves. Ostmar will fight the people freeing his nephew, and the players
  must make a strong King believe that the son he is proud of is a dead man on a leash.
  With the partial win (Prince seated, no leash), Thuban answers only to his bones and
  may for a while simply serve Ostmar well.
