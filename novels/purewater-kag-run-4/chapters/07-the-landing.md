# Chapter 7 — The Landing

There were four thousand people on the slope at the Long Butts by noon and the wind was still coming across the range instead of along it, and that was all anybody talked about.

Kag did not hide. She got in among the nearest knot of people, who turned out to be taking bets, and complained about the wind, and inside a minute somebody had handed her a slate to hold because her hands were higher up than theirs.

The wide grey man on the stone below the black box read the crowd in slow sweeps, left to right, the way you read a page. His sweep came across and went over the top of her and kept going.

It put her forty feet off the box with her ear on the wrong side of a canvas screen.

---

The black box had six chairs and five of them were full. The Baron was on the left in a plain coat and was not watching the shooting. Santo was on the right, forward in the seat, elbows on the rail, watching it like a boy at a fair.

Out on the grass it came down to three.

The household's archer was good and shot into the crosswind the way a man shoots when he has been told what the shot is worth. The elf in plain harness did not appear to allow for the wind at all. She put three in the middle at two hundred paces and the noise that went up off the slope was the loudest thing to happen in Purewater in a fortnight.

Then ninety seconds in front of the Governor's box with four thousand people waiting for it.

Temerach Nebulo took the wreath, said the eleven words the form wanted, and walked off the grass.

The slope did not know what to do with that and went on cheering.

Behind the canvas, while it was still going, Kag heard the grey man say a short thing to somebody, and the somebody said, "He'll not like it," and the grey man said:

"He doesn't leave the ship tonight. Tell the boatmen it comes from his father."

---

The horn went for the close of the day's shooting and four thousand people stood up and started to think about the water.

It happened the way Kag had said it would at dawn.

They came off the slope and the landing took about forty of them at a time. The boats were not ready and were never going to be ready. The household came down onto the stone with the rest of the city and stood in it. The Baron with two knights at his shoulder. Santo three feet from a fishmonger's wife. All of them waiting on Purewater boatmen who were in no hurry at all.

Ila went in from the bollard side with a basket of somebody's laundry on her hip, which she had not had an hour before.

She was good. She came up on his blind side and the bundle went from her hand to his without either of them looking down at it, and she was four people away before he had closed his fingers on it.

The wide grey man on the step above watched the whole of it.

He did not shout and he did not reach for Santo. He came down two steps into the crowd at walking pace and put his hand round the top of the girl's arm the way you take hold of a gate, and turned her to face him.

---

There were eleven people between Kag and them and most of them were trying to get to the same nine steps.

She got her shoulder into the back of the press and drove, and a landing full of people who have been standing still for twenty minutes does not move. It compacts. Two men went sideways into a fish crate. Somebody's hat went in the water. The whole mass shunted a foot and a half and stopped against itself.

Blau did not look up at the shove.

Kag went again, and the second time she got it right, because a boat had come alongside and the front of the crowd had started to move and she put her weight into the back of it at the moment it wanted to go anyway.

Forty people went forward at once. A woman two along from Blau lost the stone and went into the water between the hull and the steps with a shriek and a gout of green, and three men grabbed for her, and the whole landing turned into that instead.

Blau let go of the girl's arm to take the woman's wrist and hauled her out one-handed onto the stone like a man landing a net. He set her on her feet. He looked around for the child.

The child was not there any more.

He had her face, though. And he had had her for about eight seconds first.

Out on the water the household's boat was pulling for High Isle. Santo sat in the stern with one hand flat inside his coat, looking at the wake.
