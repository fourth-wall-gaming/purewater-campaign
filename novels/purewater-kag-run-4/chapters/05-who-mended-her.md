# Chapter 5 — Who Mended Her

PELL CAME UP THE OUTSIDE STAIR THREE AT A TIME AT SEVEN IN THE MORNING and had plainly been running a while, and the reason he had been running a while — the reason he had a stitch and could not get the words out and had to put his hands on his knees on her landing — was that the Lullwater from the north stairs is a mile of fondamenta and bridges, and he had no boat fare on him, because Kag had not thought to give him one.

She noticed that. She noticed it properly, the way you notice a thing you are going to fix.

"Kag — Kag—"

"Breathe."

"Hessa says come. She says now and she says the *Pearl*." Another breath. "There's a girl cut. Last night. One of the houses."

---

Here is what Kag did not do, and it is the single reason any of the rest of this happened.

She did not go.

She stood on her own landing at seven in the morning with the whole city already running toward the Pearl, and she stopped, and thought for a moment, and said the thing out loud that had been bothering her since *cut*.

"Who'd want to carve up a girl from the Pearl? Makes no sense."

Then:

"Pell. Go and find out how she's not dead. What saved her."

He did not ask why. That was the thing about him — eleven years old and he had never once asked her why, and one day she was going to have to teach him to, and she knew it, and she had not started.

"How's she not dead," he repeated, getting it right. "Who fixed her."

"That's it. Not who cut her. **Who mended her.** And how long it took."

He was off before she had finished, and she shouted after him and he stopped on the second step.

She gave him a fare. Two, in fact — one out and one back, which nobody had given him the day before and which was precisely why he had arrived with a stitch.

"Boats, Pell. Not legs."

He looked at the coppers in his hand for a second longer than a boy looks at money, and then he was gone.

---

She went the long way to Hessa because Crowbill had told her to stay off the Fishmarket bridge and the Oyster and the Guild landing, and she discovered that going the long way in that city takes forty minutes and three fares and that she was going to be doing it for two days.

By the time she got there it was full light and the water was talking.

Hessa had the back room open and there were already two boys in it eating, and one of them was Jeb, who should have been on the Guild landing selling cockles.

"Before you start," Hessa said, "he's here because the landing's shut."

She turned a page.

"They've put men on it. Not Marisette's — the *Baron's*. Six of them, off the barge, since about four this morning, and nobody's getting near it."

---

She did not stop working while she told the rest of it. She never did.

"Sylph's Embrace. One of Constantine's girls — Emmeralda, the elf, the one everyone goes to see." She squared a stack. "Cut from here to here. One line, all down the front. Sometime after three."

A beat.

"She's alive. Nobody can tell me how, and everybody's got a story about it."

"Marisette shut the whole island before it was light and she's had people on the quay since. And she's taken a fella."

She stopped, briefly, which from Hessa was a held breath.

"They're saying it was Randall."

She let that sit, because she knew Kag would want a second with it.

"Pearl boy. Raised at the Promise of Heaven, ran errands on that quay since he was smaller than Jeb, everybody knows his face. And three separate women out of two houses will swear he was in last week, drinking, and one of them says she called out to him and he laughed at her."

She finally looked up.

"So that's what the water's saying. And it's saying it *sadly*, which is worse. Nobody's angry. They're all saying *that poor boy, what happened to him*."

---

And Kag did the sums on the way it had been told, because that is what she did with everything, including grief.

A house on the Pearl goes over at three in the morning. Marisette locks the island down and has a man in a bag before first light.

And at four o'clock, on the other side of the city — a different island, a different water, nothing whatever to do with any of it — six of the Baron's men walk two local boys off the Guild landing and put them in a boat going east.

Same hour.

Nobody in that room had put those two things next to each other. Nobody in the city had, because the city had spent the morning on the Pearl, where the story was, and the story was a beautiful boy with a lovely face who everybody was sad about.

"Why the sudden switch in the Baron's boating arrangements?" Kag said.

Hessa put the pen down, which she had not done all morning.

"I can't tell you it's connected. I can tell you I don't like it either, and I'd want to know why a man who's been letting local lads sleep on his boats for a day decides at four in the morning that he'd rather do it himself."

She thought about it.

"Could be nothing. Could be somebody counted something and didn't like the answer. Could be they always meant to swap the crews and it landed on the same night by luck." A shrug. "Things do. That's the trap in this work and you know it better than most."

Then the practical half, because there was always a practical half.

"East is Vantt land. Four hundred of them, the horses, the whole tail of it, and it is *not in the city* — no canals, no roofs, no forty people who know your face. You'd be walking across open ground at a camp." She looked at Kag steadily. "You. Across open ground."

"But," she said, and picked the pen back up, "you've got a reason to be there that stands up, which is more than anybody else in Purewater has got. Two men who took Crowbill's water and the Baron's coin in the same week have been carried off east, and somebody has got to find out whether they're working or whether they're in a hole."

She tore a strip off the page and held it out.

"That's collections. That's literally your job, and he told you to do it."
