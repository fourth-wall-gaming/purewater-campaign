# Chapter 2 — Who Mended Her

The girl was carved at the Sylph's Embrace in the small hours and by seven in the morning it was the only thing anybody on the water would talk about.

Kag got it four times before eight and it was different every time. A man had come in off a boat. A man had come down from the Vantt. There had been three of them. There had been one. The girl was dead. The girl was not dead. The girl was an elf and the finest performer on the Pearl and a man had opened her up the length of her body with a knife and gone out of a first-floor window.

The Watch had been and the Watch had written it down and the Watch had gone away again.

Everybody was asking who did it.

Kag sat on the ice-house step with a bowl and thought about that for a while and then she sent for Pell.

---

Pell was eleven and he ran messages for a copper and he had feet like a man of forty. He came at a run with his shirt half off his shoulder and stood in front of her breathing.

"You want me to find out who cut her," Pell said.

"No."

He waited.

"Everybody on this water is asking who cut her," Kag said. "Forty people are asking it and they'll all get told the same three lies by dinner. I don't want that."

"What, then."

"Go to the Pearl. Go in the tradesmen's door at Constantine's and ask the cook, and if the cook won't have you ask whoever's washing. And what you ask them is this. Who mended her."

Pell looked at her.

"Say it back."

"Who mended her."

"Not who cut her."

"Who mended her."

"That's the whole of it. Don't ask anything else. Don't tell them who sent you unless they ask twice, and if they ask twice tell them, because it's a house and they'll find out anyway."

"Why that one."

"Because a girl cut like that bleeds out before dawn," Kag said. "And it's eight in the morning and she's alive. So somebody in that house did something in the dark that a surgeon couldn't do, and whoever that is is the only person in this city who was in the room."

Pell was already going.

"Eat something first," Kag said, and he came back for the bowl and took it with him.

---

He was back at eleven and he had it.

"There's an elf-girl," he said. "She come in Monday off the road with an old woman and the both of them took work for the week, the old one on the door and the elf on the water-stair. And when the screaming started the elf went up, and the cook says she was up there an hour with her hands on her and the girl stopped dying."

"What's her name."

"Gardwen. She's got a wolf."

"She's got a what."

"A wolf. It lies on the landing and it doesn't let anybody past and Constantine's let it stay." Pell was pleased with this part. "And the cook says the elf hasn't left the room since. Not to eat, not for anything. Three days."

"Three days is two days."

"Two days, then."

Kag put the book on her knee and wrote *Gardwen — elf — wolf — water-stair — was in the room.*

"And the old woman."

"Magda. She's on the front door and she was on it that night." Pell wiped his nose. "Cook says the man went out the window rather than come past her, and the cook says that's the most sense he showed all night."

---

She went up to the Pearl at noon and did not go in.

She stood on the quay opposite with a bowl of something and looked at the green shutters for the better part of an hour. The island was quiet. It was quiet at noon anyway, because the Pearl slept in the day, but it was the other kind of quiet as well, the kind where there are people behind every shutter and none of them are moving.

Two of Marisette's men were on the step at Constantine's and they were not pretending to be anything else.

A girl came out at one o'clock with a basket of linen and went down the water-stair and came back up without the basket, and she was about thirteen and she was an elf, and she sat down on the step where she could see the front door and stayed there.

Kag watched her sit for twenty minutes. The girl did not read and did not eat and did not talk to the men on the step. She watched the door.

Then Kag finished the bowl and went back to the boats, because she had a camp to count and it was a long way east and she wanted the tide.

She did not go in and ask the elf-girl anything that day, and she thought about that afterwards more than once.
