# Source Material — Purewater -- Kag (run 4)

## Dramatis Personae

### Conall Bjornlasch <!-- id: myth-char-9d8a0632e5ee -->

Warranted investigator of the King's Assay -- the Crown's arcane inspectorate; twin brother of Randall and secretly a son of Baron Hanzo. A razor-sharp investigator (Insight 88) who is also a capable fighter.

A magical investigator and arcane caster warranted by the King's Assay -- the
Crown's chartered inspectorate for licensing and policing arcane practice -- with a soldier's martial skill (all weapons, all shields). Sharp-eyed and
analytical -- Insight 88.

He rides into Purewater beside Temerach Nebulo for the Lake Lady's Tourney, an
errand she has declined to explain. He knows the city not at all, has never heard of
a brother, and has no idea whose son he is.

**GM:** he is Randall's identical twin and a biological son of Baron Hanzo. Temerach
has known for years and brought him here deliberately -- see the gm-secret lore. He
learns none of this until play delivers it.

### Gardwen <!-- id: myth-char-65e70a6489b1 -->

Feral-raised elf druid and lone survivor of a Dragon Knight raid; a rank-1 divine caster of nature and pure-water magic who walks with a befriended she-wolf, searching for her long-lost brother.

A feral-raised elf druid and the sole survivor of a Dragon Knight raid that killed her family roughly ten years ago. Gardwen channels the magic of nature and pure water and travels with a befriended she-wolf, Ruhi. She is searching for her brother, taken during the raid as a child -- now, unknown to her, the possessed warrior serving Baron Hanzo di Teufel. Dark hair, blue eyes; soft-spoken but fierce in defense of kin and the wild. She journeys with the old warrior-protector Magda (an emissary of the death goddess Hel) toward Zorastor, following the Dragon Knights.

**The old ways gave her more than healing.** The grove that remade itself around eight murdered
people did not teach her herbs alone. She carries the **woodland set** of the binding school --
Spirit Sight, Speak with the Bound, **Unseat**, Sanctuary -- learned in a place thick with people
who died badly, where such things are not theory.

There is no attack spell in it and there never was. **Unseat is a burial rite:** you put your hands
on somebody who is carrying a passenger and you refuse to let go until it can leave. It cannot be
forced on a spirit that is holding on -- and a spirit held by torment counts as holding on until
somebody addresses the torment, which is what Speak with the Bound is for.

She has never used any of it on a person. She has used it twice on animals and once on a place.

### Magda <!-- id: myth-char-4074534baf31 -->

Aged barbarian berserker and sworn servant of the death-goddess Hel; Blessed by Hel (she does not age), she guards Gardwen and the lost twins and fights with berserk fury despite her years.

An old barbarian warrior-woman and sworn servant of Hel, goddess of death, called from a farmer's life by prophetic dreams. Though aged, she fights with berserker fury and is Blessed by Hel -- she does not age naturally. She follows Hel's directive to protect Gardwen and the lost twins, swearing 'by Hel herself' to see the quest through. Curmudgeonly and blunt, with a deep distrust and fear of arcane magic.

### Randall <!-- id: myth-char-14113336e4c1 -->

Street thief of Purewater, raised in a brothel on the Pearl; identical twin of Conall and secretly a son of Baron Hanzo. A nimble, silver-tongued rogue (Deceit 75, Stealth 72, Sleight 71).

A street thief from Purewater, raised in a brothel on the Pearl and never knowing his parents. Quick, charming and light-fingered, Randall works the canals and alleys with lockpicks, disguises, a grappling hook and a fistful of throwing daggers. He is the identical twin of Conall and, unknown to him, a biological son of Baron Hanzo; his uncanny resemblance to the Baron's son Santo is both an opportunity and a danger.

### Abel Weir <!-- id: myth-char-69161cb76e05 -->

Sergeant of the City Watch, forty-one, twenty-two years in blue-grey. Keeps the referral book at the Isle post -- four inches of complaints against the Baron's people that he has been ordered to record and not act on. He is very tired.

Twenty-two years of drunks, cutpurses and canal-right disputes, and Abel Weir has
never once had to decide whether he was a coward. The standing order made that a
daily question and he has been answering it wrong since it came down, in writing,
in a neat hand, in a book.

He is not a hero and he will not draw on a Dragon Knight in the street. What he
will do is *keep the record* -- names, dates, witnesses, the barrow-man's broken
jaw, the two boatmen who did not come back -- and he has begun to understand that
the record is worth something to somebody with the standing to use it.

**Use him** as the party's lawful door. He cannot accuse a lord and he cannot lay a hand on a Knight -- a sergeant who tried would be ruined, and he knows the arithmetic exactly. He can produce
a written, dated, witnessed account of every offence committed in this city in the
last eight days, to the right person, and he badly wants to. He needs it to be
someone else's decision.

**He will recognise a professional.** Anyone who has held a warrant anywhere -- an
Order investigator, a watch captain out of Anminster, a Sword of the Lake -- gets
the book opened for them faster than any amount of Influence.

**What he is afraid of:** not the Knights. Being the sergeant whose name is on the
disobedience when it all goes wrong, with a wife and two daughters on Isle post
pay.

### Alderic Vantt <!-- id: myth-char-b348522179d7 -->

Merchant prince and councillor, fifty-two: borderland transit, grain and timber. The Baron's host -- the Dragon Knights are camped on his water-meadow -- and the man who drafted the standing order and put it in front of the Prince.

**He is bought, and he is not a cultist.** Two winters ago Alderic Vantt lost
three hulls and a bonded warehouse in the same month and was four thousand short
of ruin. Baron Hanzo covered it privately, took no interest, asked for nothing,
and mentioned it never. Vantt has been waiting two years to find out what it cost
him, and this is the week he is finding out.

What has been asked of him so far is small, which is the point. Host the company
-- an honour. Draft a sensible instruction about the watch and the Baron's people
-- prudent, during a festival. Support the temple's alarm being stood down --
technical, and the Watercrafters agreed it was a false reading. Each one alone is
defensible. He has not been asked to do anything a decent man could not explain.

He also has twenty-five armed men on his grass and no way to ask them to leave.

**In a room** he is warm, funny, and extremely good company, and he steers a
conversation the way a man closes a door -- so smoothly you do not notice you are
now in the corridor. He does not lie well under direct pressure and never has to,
because nobody has ever put him under any.

**How to break him.** Not with the debt -- he will call that a private matter
between gentlemen and he will be technically right. Break him with **fear of the
Baron**, which is real and which he has never once admitted out loud, or with the
prospect of the camp being blamed on him. Show him a Knight's crime that will
have *his* name attached and he will move faster than any honest man in the city.

**He does not know about Cailan.** He does not know what the Baron wants with the
Prince. He has never heard of Thuban Eta. If the party tells him, he will be
sick, and then he will be useful.

### Asphodel <!-- id: myth-char-8040d7456ee5 -->

A courtesan at the Promise of Heaven, the house where Randall grew up. Warm, quick, and trusted by him 'as brother and sister' -- his closest confidante on the Pearl Quay and a ready source of information from the working girls.

Asphodel works at the Promise of Heaven, the Pearl house where Randall was raised among its women. The two have known each other for years and trust each other like siblings; she was the one who got word to him that he'd been blamed for Emmeralda's carving, and never believed it for a moment. No fighter and no thief -- her gifts are people: she reads them, charms them, and hears everything that passes through the house. It is Asphodel who gathers intelligence on the Baron's manor from the girls sent up to entertain his soldiers (calling in the older, canny Mariarta to brief Randall directly). Affectionate, shrewd, and quietly brave, she helps Randall because she loves him, not for coin.

### Blau <!-- id: myth-char-1d17728fc0ed -->

Baron Hanzo's albino lieutenant -- known to the ranks as Commander Vask. A thin, red-eyed, burn-scarred killer with unnatural strength who runs the Dragon Knights' day-to-day command and handles the Baron's 'dirty work': a cold, efficient assassin and intimidator.

Blau is the Baron's lieutenant and enforcer -- the albino the rank-and-file know as Commander Vask, who handles both the Dragon Knights' day-to-day command and the dirty work. An albino with red eyes, a raspy voice, and burn scars across much of his thin frame (which belies an unnatural, wiry strength), he is cold, calculating and cruel: a quiet, efficient killer who keeps the order running with ruthless discipline. Publicly he is 'utterly loyal' to Baron Hanzo. (His secret) he studies the Baron's operation and techniques closely, planning to one day break away and serve no one but himself.

### Cailan <!-- id: myth-char-0d9f630b6182 -->

Gardwen's possessed brother (birth name Gabriel) -- the Baron's tournament champion, host to the ancient dragon-knight spirit Thuban Eta. Dragon-scale armor fused to his skin; eyes flicker reptilian when possessed; fights with uncanny ancient skill and minor fire.

Kidnapped as a child a decade ago, Gabriel was subjected to Baron Hanzo's possession ritual and bound to Thuban Eta, an ancient dragon-knight spirit. Now called Cailan, he is the Baron's unbeaten tournament champion: unnatural precision with bow and spear, ancient fighting techniques drawn from Thuban's memories, and minor fire abilities channeled through the binding locket. Years of shared consciousness have fractured his mind -- moments of clarity surface between possessions, and Thuban himself sometimes fights the Baron's will. Freeing him is the emotional core of his sister Gardwen's quest.

### Cassian Bree <!-- id: myth-char-7cbe2046b495 -->

Secretary of the Ember Society, forty-four: courteous, damp-handed, immaculate minutes, boring on purpose. The only confirmed member of the Awake the Dragon Movement in Purewater -- and everything he does is legal.

**He is the funnel, and he is not a fanatic.** Cassian Bree came to Purewater
eight years ago, took over the secretaryship of a dying antiquarian dining club
from a man who was glad to be rid of it, and has run it beautifully ever since.
Subscriptions taken, room booked, addresses printed and bound, minutes kept in a
hand you could read across a room.

What he does for the Movement is **sorting**. He watches sixty men eat dinner
eleven times a year and decides which of them get invited to a private reading,
and which of the readers can eventually be asked for one thing. He is very good at
it. He has been wrong twice in eight years and both times the man simply declined
and went on coming to dinners.

**Under investigation he is the worst kind of suspect: helpful.** He will produce
the subscription list before you ask for it, apologise for the accounts, lend you
the bound volumes, and give you the names of the last four speakers. There is no
contraband. There is no ritual room. He does not lie, because he has arranged a
life in which he does not need to.

**What he actually holds:** a locked correspondence file with four letters in it,
and a memory nobody can subpoena. The letters are the only physical evidence that
the Society is anything at all, and he will burn them the moment a party's
interest stops looking like antiquarianism.

**He is the contact for the courier** who comes on `d-1/night`.

**Under real pressure** -- a knife, a spell, a magistrate with the letters in hand
-- he gives up the Movement without much of a fight, because he is a clerk and not
a martyr. What he gives up is thin: a name in another city, a bank, and the fact
that Baron Hanzo is *not his superior but his charge*. He has been reporting on
Hanzo to the Movement for six years. **Hanzo does not know that.**

### Constantine <!-- id: myth-char-7ba27e566acc -->

Proprietor of The Sylph's Embrace on the Pearl -- shrewd, protective of her workers, fiercely independent. Operates under Marisette's authority. After Santo's attack on Emmeralda in her house, she is hiring protection and wants justice.

Constantine runs The Sylph's Embrace, a high-end pleasure house on the Pearl, under the overall authority of the madame Marisette. Sharp, businesslike and fiercely protective of her workers, she prizes loyalty and codes of conduct. Her establishment was the scene of Santo di Teufel's brutal ritual attack on the courtesan Emmeralda, and in the aftermath she is quietly hiring protection and seeking anyone who can deliver justice. No warrior, but no fool either: she keeps a stiletto and a loaded hand crossbow close, and reads a room well.

### Corvin <!-- id: myth-char-72c818662c56 -->

Sword of the Lake, twenty-two, earnest and brave and not remotely subtle. Rows well. Currently in a brown coat that is too short in the arm and sulking about it.

### Crowbill <!-- id: myth-char-2357f0fb2f3c -->

Leader of Purewater's criminal underworld -- canal smuggling, gambling, protection. Wiry and forgettable but for a small crow tattoo on his right hand. A master of streets and shadows (Streetwise 88, Stealth 82, Conceal 78) who keeps to a code: no killing locals, no harming children, no touching the Lake Lady's sites.

Crowbill runs Purewater's criminal underworld -- canal smuggling, gambling dens, protection rackets -- and does it so quietly that nothing about him stands out except a small crow tattoo on the back of his right hand. He keeps to established codes: no murder of locals, no harm to children, no disruption of the Lake Lady's sacred sites. His people signal with crow marks, he knows hidden boat paths through the canals and keeps safe houses across the city. He was happy enough to profit from the Baron's arrival at first, but the Dragon Knights' brutality is wrecking the equilibrium his business depends on -- and that is steadily turning a wary criminal into a possible ally.

### Emmeralda <!-- id: myth-char-7af8076fff25 -->

Elven courtesan of The Sylph's Embrace -- poised, magnetic, and the finest performer on the Pearl (CHA 18, Seduction 88, Dance 85).

Emmeralda is an elven courtesan at The Sylph's Embrace and the most
sought-after performer on the Pearl: a poised, magnetic presence (Dance 85, Sing 80,
Seduction 88) with a following among people who can afford her.

At the campaign's opening she is entirely well, and has an evening booked with a
young nobleman who has paid generously and asked for privacy.

**GM -- what she becomes.** She is Santo's intended vessel. If the binding happens
and she survives it, a lesser pleasure demon stays partly lodged in her: strange
behaviours, bestial voices, and asking after 'Lord Santo.' That makes her the
clearest living proof of what he did -- and, if she recovers enough to stand and
speak, a witness who can **swear over still water**, which in this realm is worth
more than any quantity of parchment -- and -- because an interrupted binding is a
unique case -- the one subject from which a counter-ritual might be devised. Whether
she lives at all depends on who reaches her in the first minutes. None of this is
true yet.

### Guildmaster Torval Bluehand <!-- id: myth-char-c8821b918bc7 -->

Guildmaster of the Watercrafters -- middle-aged, practical innovator whose hands are permanently blue-tinged from decades of watercraft enchantment. A Magic-User specialized in water artifice (Craft 85, Arcane Casting 60, Mechanisms 80) rather than battle magic; his guild's enchantments keep the canal-city running.

Torval Bluehand leads the Guild of Watercrafters, and his trade has marked him: decades of working watercraft enchantments have stained his hands a permanent blue. He is practical and forward-thinking, valuing innovation over tradition -- the kind of artificer-mage who enchants pumps, locks, gondolas and aqueducts rather than hurling fire. His arcane skill is real but applied: Craft and Mechanisms in the 80s, water-aspected Arcane Casting around 60. The Guild is allied with the Order of the Lake Lady and quietly essential to Purewater itself, since the whole canal-city depends on the watercraft his people keep enchanted and running.

### Hanzo di Teufel <!-- id: myth-char-f1bad9d5757b -->

Baron Hanzo di Teufel -- the campaign's master villain: a charismatic borderland noble and fire-wielding arcane sorcerer/demonologist who commands the Dragon Knights, runs possession rituals, and secretly seeks to become a Dragon King.

Baron Hanzo di Teufel is a rising political power in the borderlands and the true 'power behind the throne' -- urbane, magnetic and utterly ruthless (Deceit 93, Influence 92, Willpower 94). A master arcane sorcerer steeped in fire magic and demonology, he binds spirits into living flesh and is engineering a possession scheme to win the Lake Lady's Tourney; his ultimate aim is dragon transformation -- to rise as a Dragon King. He commands the Dragon Knights ('Dragonshits' to the locals) and arrives on the steam-wreathed DragonBarge. He is the father, by an elven mage, of the twins Randall and Conall, and of his cruel heir Santo.

**What he can actually do (rules).** Hanzo is a magician of the ordinary framework who happens to
hold a school that is taught nowhere: the complete **Binding School** — Spirit Sight, Speak with
the Bound, Open the Channel, Seat the Bound, Reinforce the Seat, Anchor, Draw Forth. Arcane Casting
88. That is the entire scheme on one sheet, and every step of what was done to Cailan is a spell he
demonstrably knows.

He is **not** a battle-mage and never has been. He has never personally fought anybody; that is
what Blau and Tat Atarer are for. His other spells are all social or defensive -- Charm Being,
Suggestion, Hold Person, Sleep, Hypnotism -- and they are how three men on a prince's council came
to advise him to silence a temple's fouling alarm.

**His vulnerability is the anchors.** He holds two for Thuban Eta: the bones in the border tower
(primary) and the locket he wears (working). Take the locket and the daily Reinforce the Seat gets
hard. Take both and the binding starts failing without anybody doing anything.

### Harbormaster Velen Deepkeel <!-- id: myth-char-7fa11d04c18c -->

Stern, weathered Harbormaster with forty years at the docks -- meticulous, traditional, publicly incorruptible. A consummate seaman and administrator (Seamanship 85, Navigation 82, Maritime Law 80) who controls the harbor's lawful face while quietly tolerating Crowbill's smuggling.

Harbormaster Velen Deepkeel rose through forty years of service to command the Harbor Masters, the body that governs Purewater's docks and shipping. Weathered and stern, he is meticulous and traditional, resistant to change or corruption -- at least in his public persona. In private the arrangement is murkier: he officially opposes Crowbill's smuggling while secretly facilitating it for the right price. He keeps strong ties to the Governor, the council and the wealthy merchants who live by shipping, holds the Dredgers in open contempt, and watches the Baron warily, weighing the threat against the opportunity. A capable old sailor under the ledgers and lawbooks, he still knows the cutlass.

### Hesketh Pyle <!-- id: myth-char-ed563128eef8 -->

Councillor, fifty-eight: salt, glass and a good name going soft. Votes as he is told and drinks more than he did last year. The crack in the council.

**He is frightened, and he is the way in.**

Two years ago Hesketh Pyle's son Corin killed a man in a duel in the borderlands
-- a bad duel, over nothing, with a witness who said it was not a duel at all.
Baron Hanzo's people made it go away inside a fortnight, and the deposition that
would have hanged the boy exists, somewhere, in a hand Pyle has seen once.

Since then he has voted the way he is asked to vote. He is not paid. He would
rather be paid; it would feel cleaner.

**In a room** he is quiet, agrees quickly, and looks at the table. He seconds
Vantt's motions before anyone can discuss them, which is his job, and he is aware
that everybody has noticed.

**He is drinking.** Not spectacularly. Enough that he can be found in the evening
at a wine-shop off the Merchant's Quarter where councillors do not go, and enough
that he will talk to a sympathetic stranger for longer than he should.

**What he wants**, and has never said aloud: for somebody else to make it stop, so
that it is not his decision and not his son's ruin. He will not volunteer
anything. He will confirm almost anything -- if he is given a reason to believe
that the thing is already known and that his son is not the price.

**Insight on him reads as guilt, and it is the wrong guilt.** He is not a
conspirator. He is a father who made a trade and has been paying it in instalments
for two years.

**If the party protects Corin**, Hesketh Pyle will stand up in council and say
what he was made to do, in public, and that single act unravels the standing order
faster than any evidence.

### Hesper <!-- id: myth-char-85b7a393679b -->

To Purewater, Hesper is the madame Marisette's cold, dangerous mage-enforcer -- quiet, watchful, able to drop a strong man with a single spoken word. The truth is the opposite of what the mask suggests: Hesper is a deep-cover agent of the Quiet Hand, a hidden fellowship that works unseen for the good of the world, and the menace is a costume worn over a conscience. Master arcane caster (Arcane Casting 100, Willpower 95).

**Hesper is neither a man nor a woman, and uses they/them.** Not a disguise and not a secret -- simply what Hesper is. Nobody in Purewater knows it as gossip because nobody has ever had the nerve to ask, and Hesper has never once given them a reason to.

Hesper moves through Purewater like cold air through a door, and everyone who feels it assumes the worst -- which is the point. For years Hesper has played the part of Marisette's arcane enforcer, the perfect cover for a listening post at the heart of the Pearl's secrets. In truth Hesper is a 'finger' of the Quiet Hand, the secret fellowship that stops great evils before they ripen by knowing of them first and acting unseen, and has been watching Baron Hanzo since before he ever reached the city. The seizing of Conall and Randall into the catacombs looked like cruelty; it was the Hand taking control of a dangerous night, learning the truth of the soul-knife, and keeping it -- and the twins -- out of worse hands. Hesper kills rarely and reluctantly, never for convenience, and will not be moved by gold or threat; behind the predator's stillness is an old, patient kindness that gives nothing away.

### High Priestess Nerissa <!-- id: myth-char-7c1b95f3d697 -->

High Priestess of the Temple of the Lake Lady -- middle-aged, silver-streaked hair, blue-green robes that ripple like water. A powerful divine caster (Piety 92, Willpower 85) who senses water corruption, blesses sacred waters, and speaks with water spirits. Temple law forbids her from intervening directly, so she works through the PCs.

Nerissa is High Priestess at the Temple of the Lake Lady, a serene, formidable woman with silver-streaked dark hair and blue-green robes that seem to ripple like water as she moves. She senses corruption within sacred waters, performs water-based blessing rituals, and communicates with the Lake Lady's water spirits. Since the Baron's arrival she has been tracking a steady rise in that corruption -- but temple law forbids her from intervening directly unless the sacred sites themselves are threatened. So she becomes a patron: supplying spiritual support, ritual knowledge, and the water-magic countermeasures the players will need, while relying on them to act where she cannot.

### Ila <!-- id: myth-char-27691473e570 -->

A young elven girl (about thirteen) at the Sylph's Embrace, devoted to Emmeralda. An innocent of the Pearl, and a thread on the party's conscience.

Ila is one of the youngest at the Sylph's Embrace -- an elven girl of
perhaps thirteen, more ward than worker, who has attached herself to the courtesan
Emmeralda and follows her about the house like a duckling.

No fighter and no schemer: a bright, frightened, brave child in a dangerous quarter.

**GM.** She is the small, immediate measure of what the di Teufels cost people who
have no part in any of it. If Emmeralda is hurt, Ila is the one who comes running
for help and the one who will not leave her side afterwards -- and whoever helps
will have made a friend for life on the Pearl.

### Ivo Calder <!-- id: myth-char-ce2c5475d725 -->

Tide Master of the Watercrafters Guild, fifty, assessor by appointment to the city -- and the man who signed the Lake Lady's fouling alarm off as a false reading six weeks ago. He has not slept properly since.

**One of the Four, and he does not know what he is part of.**

Ivo Calder is a genuinely distinguished water-diviner: thirty years of readings,
an appointment as the city's assessor, and a reputation for being unbribable that
is entirely deserved. Nobody bribed him.

He was invited to a private reading of the [[The Ember Society]] four years ago
and it was the best evening of his intellectual life -- real pre-uprising texts,
handled, in front of him. He has been to eleven since. Six weeks ago a man he
respects asked him, once, over port, for one thing: that he take the temple's
fouling reading himself and give the guild an honest assessment, since the Order
was plainly excitable and the guild's own interest in the Baron's hull deserved
better than a panic.

He took the reading. **It was not false.** The corruption bleeding off the
DragonBarge is real, measurable, and spreading north toward the Lake.

He signed it false anyway, and he has spent six weeks constructing the reason: the
guild wants to *study* that hull, a panic would have it towed, the reading was at
the edge of the instrument's range, the festival, the tariffs, the Order's
excitability. He can say the whole argument aloud and it sounds well.

**He is a confession waiting for a competent interviewer.** Insight on him reads
as a man carrying something. He will not volunteer it and he will defend the
assessment vigorously -- until somebody puts the original reading, or a second
independent reading, in front of him. Then he folds completely and at once,
because he has been waiting six weeks for someone to make him.

**What his recantation does.** It reopens the Lake Lady's fouling alarm. That puts
the Order of the Lake Lady officially onto the corruption three days before the
Tourney, with standing to act, and it is the single most damaging thing the party
can do to the Baron by lawful means. It also destroys Calder, who knows it.

**He cannot tell them who asked him.** Not from loyalty -- he genuinely does not
know what he would be naming. He can describe an upper room, a good dinner, and a
courteous secretary who takes the minutes.

### Kerrin (brown coat) <!-- id: myth-char-3e2842edbc35 -->

One of Crowbill's watchers. Dockside, unremarkable, brown coat. Professional -- follows, does not close.

Put on Conall the afternoon a well-dressed stranger spent the day asking the working canals where a man finds criminals, mercenaries and untaxed cargo. Crowbill wants to know whether that is a mark, a rival, or the Watch before anything happens to him.

### Lilura Deepcurrent <!-- id: myth-char-ab3dca40a27a -->

Deputy to High Priestess Nerissa and her right hand in the Order of the Lake Lady -- about thirty, dark, usually wet to the knee because she is the one who actually goes into the channel to check it. Rumoured merfolk ancestry on her mother's side; she holds her breath unnaturally long and does not discuss it. A genuine divine caster in her own right, and the person who decides who reaches the High Priestess and who does not.

Lilura Deepcurrent runs the practical half of the Order so that Nerissa can run the sacred half. She keeps the gate, keeps the rota, keeps the correspondence, and keeps the High Priestess upright through the parts of the job that would otherwise eat her. Fiercely loyal and not remotely deferential -- she is the only person in Purewater who argues with Nerissa to her face and the only one Nerissa listens to when she does.

She is a true cleric of the Lake Lady, not an administrator with a title: her Piety is real and so is her reading of people. Where Nerissa senses the water, Lilura senses the room.

### Marda Blackwater <!-- id: myth-char-620657421b72 -->

Broad-shouldered leader of The Dredgers, risen from the dredge-boats during a labor dispute five years ago. A powerful working-class champion (Brawn 75, Oratory 78, Influence 75) who rallies dock labor against the merchant princes and -- above all -- against the Baron's Dragon Knights.

Marda Blackwater leads The Dredgers, Purewater's organization of dock workers and canal laborers. A broad-shouldered woman in her forties, she began as a dredger herself and rose to prominence during a bitter labor dispute five years ago; she is practical, passionate, and utterly committed to improving conditions for the city's working class. She is no trained soldier but a formidable physical presence and a galvanizing speaker (Oratory 78), able to turn out the docks at a word. She'll work with the progressive wing of the Order of the Lake Lady and with ethical merchants, keeps a wary distance from Crowbill, and reserves real hatred for the merchant princes, the Governor, and the Baron's Dragon Knights -- to her, the purest face of aristocratic oppression.

### Mariarta <!-- id: myth-char-9a77a4db0663 -->

An older, canny courtesan of the Promise of Heaven with 'the calm intelligence of someone who can survive almost any situation involving men.' Sent up to entertain the Baron's soldiers, she brings back the hard intelligence on his guarded quarters -- Randall's most valuable inside source on the Dragon Knights' manor.

Mariarta (the novelization's Moriata) is one of the older women of the Promise of Heaven, calm and shrewd, long practiced at reading men and outlasting trouble. When the Baron's soldiers sent for company, she was among the girls who went up to the fortified manor -- and she came back with exactly what Randall needed: the Baron never left his quarters, no one was permitted near them, the room sat second-storey on the eastern side, and the guards were too disciplined to trifle with. She'll keep gathering for him, and ask only that he find something in it for the girls. No fighter -- her weapons are patience, charm, and a clear eye.

### Marisette <!-- id: myth-char-af01a2536d03 -->

Madame of The Mother of Pearl and the near-absolute authority over Purewater's Pearl -- the proprietors, Constantine among them, answer to her. A consummate social power broker who keeps the pleasure district running, keeps her accounts in obligations, and resents the disorder the Baron and Santo have brought to it. Her oldest rule: the Pearl sells a night, never a person.

Marisette is the madame who holds sway over the whole Pearl: the individual house-proprietors, Constantine included, operate under her overall authority. She is no fighter, but in her own arena she is dangerous -- a master of people, money and information who keeps the pleasure district's delicate order intact and skims its profits. Santo di Teufel's ritual attack on Emmeralda in one of her houses, and the Dragon Knights' growing menace, are precisely the kind of disorder she cannot abide, which can make her a useful, self-interested ally to anyone working against the Baron.

### Maro Quist <!-- id: myth-char-0c38efa94d32 -->

Councillor, seventy-one, thirty years on the council: incorruptible, formidable, and completely wrong. He voted for both instructions on the merits and will defend them to your face.

**He is the honest one, and he is the problem.**

Maro Quist cannot be bought and has never tried to be. He is the best-read man in
the chamber, he drafts beautifully, and his word carries the council on any
question he cares about. He voted for the standing order because you do not police
a great lord's retinue during a sacred festival unless you want a diplomatic
incident, and he voted to stand down the temple's fouling alarm because the
Watercrafters' own assessor called it a false reading and the city does not need a
water panic in Tourney week.

Both positions are defensible. Both are wrong. Nobody lied to him -- he was
simply given true facts in the right order by people who knew what he would
conclude, which is the only way anyone has ever moved Maro Quist.

**He is the red herring, deliberately.** Quist subscribes to the **Ember
Society**: eleven dinners a year, a printed address, borderland-facing gentlemen
of means, and a great deal of learned melancholy about the greatness of the age
that ended at the Lake. He has attended three. He thinks they are antiquarians.

**They are a recruiting front for [[The Awake the Dragon Movement]]** (see [[The Ember Society]]), and a party
that finds the subscription list will find Quist's name on it and conclude they
have found their cultist. They have found a seventy-one-year-old who likes being
read to about the fall of the Dragon Kings and who has never been asked for
anything, because the Society asks nothing of most of its members and everything
of about four of them.

**How to move him.** Evidence. Not passion, not pressure, and absolutely not
accusation -- accuse Maro Quist of treason and he will destroy you in open council
in about four minutes, and he will be right to. Bring him **[[Abel Weir]]'s
referral book**, or the spent scroll in the Baron's hand, or a Knight's crime with
witnesses, and he will read it properly, and then he will change his position in
public and take the council with him, because that is what he has always done.

**He is the single most valuable ally in the city government and the party will
almost certainly waste him.**

### Prince Emeric <!-- id: myth-char-3f62e7193ce7 -->

The young Governor of Purewater -- the King's nephew, perhaps two-and-twenty: clever, idealistic, genuinely decent, and entirely unsuited to govern a city like this. He wants to reform Purewater; the factions humor him, manage him, and run rings around his good intentions.

Prince Emeric was sent to Purewater by his uncle, King Halvern, to govern the city -- a prestigious first posting for a promising young royal, and (his relatives assumed) a safe one. It was a misjudgment. Emeric is bookish and high-minded, schooled in law, history, and the philosophy of just rule, and he arrived burning to clean up 'Pisswater': to curb the smuggling, protect the working poor, and restore the dignity of the Lake Lady's Tourney. But he has no instinct for the knife-in-the-dark politics of a city run by madames, brokers, crime lords, and corrupt guilds. He trusts the wrong people, signs the documents put in front of him, and mistakes flattery for agreement. The Harbor Masters and merchant princes steer him; Marisette and Ravella read him like a broadsheet; and Baron Hanzo cultivates him charmingly as a 'visiting dignitary,' the better to operate under royal cover. For the party, Emeric is opportunity and hazard both: an honest man at the top who could open real doors -- if they can reach him past his handlers, and if telling him the truth doesn't simply get him killed.

### Ravella <!-- id: myth-char-00e095f2e09f -->

Half-merfolk proprietor of The Siren's Call -- pleasure house and information market in one. Subtle waterfolk tells (webbed fingers, water-reflecting eyes, Swim 90). Ravella runs The Siren's Call, where high-end pleasure and the city's information trade intersect. ### Sana <!-- id: myth-char-6864bfae6390 -->

### Santo di Teufel <!-- id: myth-char-6ed423cbc351 -->

Baron Hanzo's cruel son and heir -- a sadistic young fire-mage who has decided he wants Emmeralda of the Sylph's Embrace, and who carries a stolen ritual knife marked with the sigil of Asmodeus because he cannot imagine being told no.

Santo di Teufel is Baron Hanzo's cruel, entitled heir, trained in his father's
dark arts and trusted with none of them. A junior fire-mage with a taste for cruelty
(Lust 80, Arrogance & Entitlement 75), charming and predatory in equal measure
(Seduction 81, Deceit 66).

He arrives in Purewater with the column, spends one evening on the Pearl, and sees
**Emmeralda** dance. That is the whole of his plot. He wants her -- not for a night,
which he can buy, but *kept*: on the barge, his, pleased to be there. He is nineteen
and he has never in his life been refused something he could point at.

He is not building anything and he is not hunting anyone else. There is one girl and
there is a stolen barbed knife bearing the sigil of Asmodeus that his father does not
know he has taken.

**GM:** his agenda is *Have her*. He tries to buy her contract first, because that is
what a di Teufel does; when the house refuses him he decides to make her willing
instead, which is a thing he has watched his father do and has never once been taught.
Left alone he botches it, opens her the length of her body, and runs leaving the knife
behind -- the evidence that ties the di Teufels to the possession scheme. Everything
after that depends on who was in the house, and on the fact that **he still wants her**
and now needs her off the Pearl before she can say his name.

**What he can actually do (rules).** Almost nothing. Santo taught himself by *tracing*
out of a locked case: he has **Open the Channel** and **Spirit Sight** and that is the
whole of his binding capability. He cannot **Seat the Bound**. He has no **Anchor** and
no **Draw Forth** and cannot **Speak with the Bound** -- he has never once heard the
voice of the thing he is trying to move.

What he has is **one scroll**, lifted from his father's case: a single use of Seat the
Bound, and a scroll is consumed on use whatever the result. One attempt, on one girl,
and no second chance -- which is exactly why the night at the Sylph's Embrace goes the
way it goes, and why afterwards he is frantic rather than methodical. He has spent the
only real thing he had and he still does not have her.

**His father does not know the scroll is gone.**

### Temerach Nebulo <!-- id: myth-char-b73546ad8c81 -->

Elf knight of the Swords of the Lake, called 'the Lakelady's Javelin'; a deadly archer and peerless martial fighter who has spent four years trying to get Baron Hanzo called to answer -- and has not yet dared make the accusation.

An elven knight of the elite order of the Swords of the Lake, known as 'the
Lakelady's Javelin' for her uncanny skill with the bow. Disciplined, sharp and
lethally precise. A consummate warrior in full plate, she fights with longsword,
rapier and kite shield -- but it is her marksmanship that has made her name.

**A rare outsider who rose to greatness among human knights**, admitted on merit by
an order that did not care where she came from. It is why she recognises the same
thing in [[Conall]] on sight, and why neither of them can be bought with anything
this city has to offer.

**What she is actually doing here, which is not detective work.** There is no
arresting a baron. A lord answers to the King, and King Halvern is at Sharna, far
away and content, surrounded by men who have been Hanzo's friends for thirty years.
Temerach has petitioned the Crown twice; both petitions were received courteously
and died in that warmth.

So she works in the forms her order actually has. **She has standing** -- a sworn
knight of a recognised order may call a lord to answer, and that is the one real
weapon she carries. She has used the **Rite of Account** three times in two years,
requiring the Lady's clergy to put a question to the goddess on her behalf, and the
answers have come back poorer each time as the pools foul. **She asks men to swear
over still water**, and has learned most of what she knows from who refused.

And she spent four years earning an **archery** entry to this Tourney, because a
competitor is presented to the Crown's representative -- which buys her a quarter
of an hour in front of a prince. That is what the entry is for.

**Why she has not accused him.** Because a lord called to answer may refuse the
matter and offer trial by arms, or his champion's, and Hanzo has a champion nobody
has seen fight. She is too good a soldier to open with a move whose answer she
cannot predict. She is very probably the one blade who could beat that champion,
and she cannot enter the Single Combat without giving up the room.

**She has long known whose son Conall is** and has not told him. She brought him
here deliberately. Both of those facts are going to cost her his trust, and she has
already decided to pay it.

### Thuban Eta <!-- id: myth-char-1a276445679f -->

The ancient dragon-knight spirit bound inside Cailan. A betrayed tactical genius from the Dragon Kings era, held in thrall by Baron Hanzo's ritual through his bones and the binding locket. Indomitable will (Willpower 95), peerless tactics, honors the old codes -- a potential ally if freed.

Thuban Eta was a dragon-knight and war-leader of the Dragon Kings era, betrayed by treacherous comrades and slain. He is a reluctant prisoner: he still feels the torment of his violent death and betrayal, yet honors the old codes, and occasionally wrenches against the Baron's will -- those struggles surface as Cailan's lucid moments. His own being is defined by towering INT, POW and Willpower (the will the Baron must keep crushing); physically he acts through his host. His remains and the binding locket are the leash. Free him, and the betrayed knight may repay the debt.

**Statted as a spirit, not a man.** Thuban Eta has no body and therefore no hit locations of his
own and no spells. What he has is what he *lends*, and what he *resists*.

- **Lends the host:** his combat style, whole. Cailan is seventeen, thin, and has never trained;
  the thing on the sand fights like a knight-companion of the dragon wars because it is one. The
  boy is not present for it, which is the nearest thing to a mercy in his life.
- **Resists with Willpower.** Seat the Bound and Draw Forth are resisted by *him*, not by the
  vessel. A proud spirit resists hardest; a betrayed one hardest of all, which is precisely why
  the Baron needed two anchors and a daily rite to hold him at all.
- **Is reachable.** Speak with the Bound gets past Cailan and addresses him directly, and he will
  answer, because nobody has spoken to him in a hundred years.

**The binding does not rest on iron. It rests on the night he died** -- drowned by his own company
in his mail with his hands tied, for refusing to fire a valley. He cannot leave that water because
leaving it means accepting that it happened and that nobody ever came, and he has had a thousand
years of finding that unbearable.

Which is the actual lock, and why **Unseat alone will not free him.** He counts as holding on. The
torment has to be addressed first -- by somebody standing where his company stood and saying it was
wrong -- and then a burial rite will work in a moment.

## Locations

### Caravan Square <!-- id: myth-loc-b07cc79ed591 -->

The landward gate of Purewater: a larger outcrop at the tip of the thin western promontory, where the borderland road meets the city. The Gate of Triumph, the caravan staging-yard, and the first crush of arrivals before the canals begin.

Purewater's only **land approach**. From the western mainland a thin **promontory**
reaches eastward and swells, at its tip, into a larger outcrop: **Caravan Square**,
where the long borderland road finally meets the city and the **Gate of Triumph**
opens onto the first bridges and quays. Here caravans unload, travellers and
competitors for the tourney throng in, beasts are penned and goods are staged before
everything must take to boats. It is the southern jaw of the Mouth, looking across
the landward channel to [[High Isle]]'s south-west point and the [[The Merchant's Quarter]] beyond.

It was in the crush of Caravan Square that [[Randall]] first marked the disguised
[[Baron Hanzo di Teufel]] coming into the city -- and was taken for "Lord Santo" by
the Baron's guard.

### High Isle <!-- id: myth-loc-9635620c72db -->

The great triangular island in the Mouth, splitting the Lake's outflow. The Grand Canal bisects and branches across it, dividing the quarters: Merchant's (SW), the official & noble quarter (NE), Forge & industry (E), and the long residential northern edge sloping rich-to-poor toward the Lullwater.

The heart of Purewater: a large, irregular, roughly **triangular** island wedged in
the Mouth, splitting the sacred Lake's outflow around it. Its three points anchor
the city -- the **south-west** point reaching toward [[Caravan Square]] across the
landward channel, the **east** point toward [[The Shoals]] across the seaward
channel, and the **north-west** point facing the open Lake.

The **Grand Canal** does not ring the isle so much as **bisect and branch across
it**, a web of broad cut channels that carve the island into natural quarters and
carry barge-traffic to every door:

- **The [[The Merchant's Quarter]]** -- the **south-west** corner, by the landward
  channel and the caravan trade: counting-houses, guild halls, the Watercrafters,
  the great market plaza, and the palazzi of the merchant princes who sit on the
  city council.
- **The official & noble quarter** -- the **north-east**, the prestigious frontage
  facing the Lake and [[Temple Isle]]: the **Governor's Palazzo** (seat of
  [[Prince Emeric]]), the **Hall of Justice**, the **Barracks**, and **Palace
  Square**, ringed by the houses of the nobility and the city's wealthiest (though,
  in truth, few of the realm's *truly* powerful keep more than a token residence
  here).
- **The [[The Forge Quarter]] & the industrial yards** -- the **east** point:
  forges, foundries, tanneries and boatyards trailing smoke, the gritty working
  belt that spills naturally eastward into [[The Shoals]].
- **The residential northern edge** -- broad avenues run **east-west** across the
  isle, from the Merchant's Quarter to the forges. Along the **northern edge**,
  moving south-east, the houses slope steadily from **rich to middling** until they
  reach a canal-mouth opening to the Lake -- and **across that canal lies [[The Lullwater]]**, the crowded poor-and-criminal quarter.

The Baron's **[[The DragonBarge]]** lies at a private pier on the isle's **eastern,
seaward side**, steam rising where its hull meets the water. The **Tournament
Grounds** -- archery ranges firing across open water, the combat lists, and the
start of the Harbor Run -- occupy the open ground between Palace Square and the
quays, the stage for the campaign's climax.

### Lost Isle <!-- id: myth-loc-e6d5283d7d50 -->

A half-drowned ruin among the Northern Isles -- collapsed and weed-choked buildings rising from the Lake, deserted and shunned. The Mermaid's Court keeps hidden chambers somewhere beneath these waters.

One of [[The Northern Isles]]: a half-sunken district of drowned, abandoned buildings
and ancient ruins where the Lake has flowed undisturbed for generations, weed-choked
and silent. Animists hold that powerful spirits have taken up residence here, which
makes it both perilous and prized by spirit-binders -- and somewhere beneath these
waters lie the hidden chambers of [[The Mermaid's Court]]. Mysterious, dangerous,
and largely unexplored; the boatmen give it a wide berth.

### Purewater <!-- id: myth-loc-ba64180cd96f -->

A canal city built across the Mouth -- the narrow strait where the sacred Lake (north) drains to the open Sea (south). Two land-jaws and the great triangular High Isle split the holy outflow; a scatter of lake-islands lies to the north. Locals call it 'Pisswater'.

Purewater is built across **the Mouth**: the narrow choke-point where the sacred
**Lake** to the north pours its holy waters south into the **open Sea**. It is the
gateway between inland lake-trade and the ocean routes -- rich, strategic, and
rotten. Locals call it **"Pisswater"** for the pollution and the corruption alike.

**The shape of the place** (north = the Lake; south = the Sea; west = the mainland):

- From the **western** mainland, a thin **promontory** reaches eastward and ends in
  a larger outcrop -- **[[Caravan Square]]** and the **Gate of Triumph**, the city's
  only land approach. This is the southern jaw of the Mouth.
- A second **peninsula**, set a little to the north and east, reaches in to form the
  seaward jaw. On it sit **[[The Shoals]]** -- the working docks, fish-markets and
  smugglers' jetties facing the open sea.
- Between the two jaws sits **[[High Isle]]**, a great irregular triangular island
  that splits the Lake's outflow. Its south-west point reaches toward Caravan Square
  across a calmer **landward channel**; its east point reaches toward the Shoals
  across the **seaward channel** (the true Mouth); its north-west point faces the
  open Lake. The waters part around the isle and rejoin to the south.
- North of High Isle, scattered across the Lake's calmer waters, lie **[[The Northern Isles]]** -- among them the bright pleasure-island of **[[The Pearl]]**, the
  half-drowned ruins of **[[Lost Isle]]**, quiet court-family retreats, and a few
  shunned, haunted, or magically claimed islets. Off the north-east edge, alone and
  unbridged, sits little **[[Temple Isle]]** with its temple and Sacred Confluence.

Gondolas and barges are the streets; bridges stitch the islands; mist and steam,
salt and canal-stink fill the air. Under it all runs the campaign's elemental
tension -- the Lake Lady's pure waters against the fire-corruption bleeding from the
Baron's **[[The DragonBarge]]**, moored on High Isle's seaward side. Beneath
everything run **[[The Catacombs]]**.

### Temple Isle <!-- id: myth-loc-6741d3a06d5c -->

A small island alone off the north-east edge, unbridged to High Isle and reached only by boat. Dominated by the temple of the Lake Lady and the Sacred Confluence pool -- to be drawn as detailed, planned sacred architecture.

A small island set apart off the **north-east** edge of the city, out in the Lake's
clean waters -- **deliberately unbridged** to [[High Isle]] and reached only by the
temple's own ferries, so that the profane city cannot simply walk to the holy
ground. It is given over almost entirely to the **temple of the Lake Lady** and the
**Sacred Confluence**: a great round sacred pool, fed where the purest lake-water
wells up, where divine magic runs strongest and the goddess is said to manifest at
high ceremony.

Here [[High Priestess Nerissa]] tends the waters and tracks the corruption creeping
north from the DragonBarge; here the [[The Order of the Lake Lady]] keeps its rites.

> **For the mapmaker:** unlike the dense house-by-house districts, Temple Isle should
> be rendered as **detailed, deliberate sacred architecture** -- a planned
> ground-plan of the temple precinct: the colonnaded approach, the temple itself, the
> ring of lesser shrines, and the geometry of the Confluence pool and its channels
> and basins. A formal architectural layout, not a tangle of rooftops.

### The Anminster Road <!-- id: myth-loc-d96831851b8e -->

The last stretch of the inland road, an hour out from the ford

Hedgerow and cart-ruts on the approach from Anminster. Wide enough for two carts, which means an armed column travelling at speed puts everyone else in the verge. Conall and Temerach ride this stretch as the Dragon Knights overtake them.

### The Catacombs <!-- id: myth-loc-928e65fd3c1e -->

Ancient tunnels and water-pools beneath Purewater, older than the city -- smuggling routes, secret meeting places, and the site of clandestine 'justice'.

A network of tunnels, caverns, and centuries-old water-pools beneath Purewater, older than the city above. Smugglers use them as hidden routes and the underworld uses them for meetings -- and for darker work; to be "taken to the catacombs" is to be in mortal trouble. It was here that [[Marisette]]'s people dragged [[Randall]] and [[Conall]], bound to chairs and a hairsbreadth from having their throats cut, before the truth of Santo's crime came out and the four strangers' alliance was forged.

The untouched pools have developed strange magical properties, and some believe they connect to primordial water sources predating Purewater itself -- making the catacombs of interest to animists, the [[The Mermaid's Court]], and anyone seeking the city's oldest secrets.

### The DragonBarge <!-- id: myth-loc-2e100e5e9ff2 -->

Baron Hanzo's dragon-prowed vessel at a private pier on High Isle's seaward side. Twenty aboard and no more -- the Baron, his household, six of the Dragon Circle, and one boy kept below the waterline. The company itself camps ashore on the Vantt estate.

Baron Hanzo's personal vessel: a large ship adorned with dragon motifs that
**steams continuously where its hull meets the water**, fire magic warring with the
sacred channel and slowly corrupting it. It lies at a private pier on **[[High Isle]]'s eastern, seaward side**, among the smoke of the **[[The Forge Quarter]]**
and within easy reach of the open sea through the Mouth -- the corruption it sheds
is felt worst in these eastern yards and creeps north toward the Lake from here.

**Twenty people aboard, and that is the whole of it.** The barge is not a barracks
and it will not hold one. The company -- the Knights, the horses, the farriers,
the armourers, the servants, the tourney gear -- is camped ashore on **[[The Vantt Estate]]**, east of the city, and comes aboard only when sent for.

What that means in play: **a stranger on this deck is noticed inside a minute.**
There is nowhere to be lost in a crowd. Everyone aboard knows every other face,
and the ship is quiet enough at night to hear a man walk.

**Who is aboard**

| | |
|---|---|
| **The Baron** | aft, in his own quarters, with the working cabin beside them |

*The laundress cannot get a grey residue out of the same linen every morning and
has stopped mentioning it.*

**Aft of the companionway**: the Baron's quarters, his apparatus, the working
cabin, the locked case behind the border rolls -- and the daily rite at first
light, which is the one fixed point in the ship's day. Cailan is exercised on the
after deck before dawn, in harness, with the deck cleared.

**Getting aboard.** Not a stealth problem and not a crowd to hide in -- an
*invitation* problem. Provisioning comes out from shore daily and is taken at the
forward gangway by the cook, who does not let carriers past it. A tailor, a
horse-dealer, a petitioner, a woman brought out for an evening and put ashore in
the morning: all of these come aboard, all of them are escorted, and none of them
goes aft.

**Which is why the estate is the softer target.** Two hundred people, tents, a
kitchen, hired local labour, and men who talk. See [[The Vantt Estate]].

**Deck by deck:** [[The DragonBarge: deck plan]].

### The Forge Quarter <!-- id: myth-loc-bce4d87d2d7b -->

High Isle's eastern point: forges, foundries, tanneries and boatyards (many Dwarven-run), trailing smoke. The gritty industrial belt that spills east into the Shoals; the DragonBarge is moored on its seaward edge.

The eastern point of [[High Isle]] -- the city's industrial belt. Forges, foundries,

### The Lists <!-- id: myth-loc-0ee0f1819367 -->

The tourney ground on Temple Isle: raked sand, tiered timber stands on three sides, the Governor's box hung with lake-green cloth, and beyond the stands the water and the temple steps.

Raked sand inside a rope, tiered timber going up on three sides, and the

### The Long Butts <!-- id: myth-loc-59ecece4e11b -->

Two hundred paces of marked grass on the north shore between the water and the stands, where the open archery is shot. No house colours required, no fee, anyone may enter.

Two hundred paces of flat grass marked out between the water and the stands,

### The Lullwater <!-- id: myth-loc-e65aeb455425 -->

Purewater's poor-and-criminal quarter, across the canal east of High Isle and sloping toward the Shoals: a low, crowded tangle of the narrowest canals and crooked tenements. Its pretty, calm-sounding name hides where the underworld and the working poor live cheek by jowl. Home of the Moist Oyster and the Dredgers' Hall.

Across the canal from [[High Isle]]'s residential north-east edge, sloping
south-east toward the docks of [[The Shoals]], lies the **Lullwater** -- a low,
crowded, water-logged quarter of the **narrowest, most crooked canals** in the city
and tenements leaning over them until they almost touch overhead. Its name sounds
gentle -- a lull, a calm, still water -- and that is the joke of it: the Lullwater
is where Purewater's **underworld and its working poor live cheek by jowl**, thieves
and dredgers and smugglers and dockhands all crammed into the same drowned maze.

It is easy to get lost here, and dangerous to *look* lost -- [[Conall]] learns as
much when he is cornered within an hour of wandering in. [[Crowbill]]'s people move
the canals by hidden boat-paths; the honest [[The Dredgers]] keep their **Dredgers'
Hall** and safe houses here; and the storyteller [[Thorne]] holds court most
evenings at the **[[The Moist Oyster]]** tavern. The quarter's nearness to the
smuggling jetties of the Shoals is no accident -- contraband flows from one straight
into the other.

### The Merchant's Quarter <!-- id: myth-loc-a5ed3c8aeb43 -->

The south-west quarter of [[High Isle]], where the landward channel and the road
from [[Caravan Square]] bring the city's trade ashore. Here stand the guild halls

from the city council. Wealthy, busy, and politically slippery -- this is where the
city's money is made, weighed, and quietly steered. (Formerly described as a
separate "Merchant's Isle"; in the city's true layout it is a quarter of High Isle,

### The Moist Oyster <!-- id: myth-loc-8d11960bd730 -->

A rough tavern in the Lullwater, sign of a half-open oyster -- cheap drink, loose tongues, knife fights, and the storyteller Thorne holding court most evenings.

A rough tavern deep in [[The Lullwater]], its suggestive sign a half-open oyster
shell. Cheap drink, loose tongues, and the occasional knife fight; an underground
market of rumour and whispered deals. (It echoes the old Zorastor "Vulgar Unicorn,"
down to its bawdy sign.)

Most evenings the storyteller [[Thorne]] holds court here, collecting tales and
gossip from new arrivals and dispensing his own -- a frail-seeming old man with
too-vibrant blue eyes, a blade hidden in his cane, and, secretly, the deep
historical knowledge of a former Order of the Lake initiate. It is the natural place
to gather rumour about the tournament, the Baron, and the underworld -- if you can
keep your own secrets from the wily old man, which the party conspicuously fails to
do.

### The Mother of Pearl <!-- id: myth-loc-be87010ca4e1 -->

Marisette's own house and the seat of her authority over the Pearl -- the oldest and grandest establishment on the Quay, and the least advertised. Where her one unmoved rule was made.

[[Marisette]]'s house, and through it the seat of her authority over the whole
[[The Pearl|Pearl]]. It is named for the lustrous inside of the shell and for
what she is to the island. Where [[The Sylph's Embrace]] and [[The Siren's Call]]
hang their lamps out over the water, the Mother of Pearl has a plain water-stair,
a doorman who already knows who you are, and no sign of any kind. It is the
oldest house on the Quay and the only one that has never needed to advertise.

You do not go there until you are worth meeting. She receives; she does not
travel.

**The rule was made here.** A night is for sale; a person is not. It predates
every proprietor now working, every house on the Quay enforces it, and no amount
of money has ever moved it — which is the reason there is anyone left working on
the Pearl at all.

### The Northern Isles <!-- id: myth-loc-07766c964f19 -->

The scatter of irregular islets across the Lake's calm waters north of High Isle: the bright Pearl, the drowned ruins of Lost Isle, quiet court-family retreats, and a few shunned, haunted, or magically claimed islands.

North of [[High Isle]], where the Lake widens into its calmer, cleaner reaches, lies
a **scatter of small irregular islands** -- the gentler, stranger half of Purewater,
away from the crush of the Mouth.

- **[[The Pearl]]** -- the bright one: pleasure-houses and high nightlife.
- **[[Lost Isle]]** -- ruins, some of them half-drowned, weed-choked and deserted;
  the hidden chambers of [[The Mermaid's Court]] lie somewhere beneath the Northern
  Isles.
- **Court retreats** -- several islets are owned by court families as private
  island-villas and garden-retreats. (Worth remembering: comparatively **few of the
  realm's truly powerful** actually live in Purewater; many of these "noble"
  retreats are minor houses, faded names, or shuttered most of the year.)
- **The shunned isles** -- and some are simply avoided. One is said to be **haunted**;
  another was long ago **claimed by some water-power or magic** and is no longer
  quite of the mortal city; a third sank within living memory and now shows only
  rooftops and a drowned bell at low water. Boatmen know which channels not to take
  after dark.

The Northern Isles reward exploration: a place for retreats and trysts and quiet
deals, for ruins and water-spirits, and for mysteries the bustling city below the
Mouth has half-forgotten.

### The Pearl <!-- id: myth-loc-0b5c4109bdd7 -->

The bright pleasure-island among the Northern Isles -- high-end nightlife and the great houses of the courtesan trade (the Sylph's Embrace, the Siren's Call, the Promise of Heaven) along the lamp-strung Pearl Quay, under Marisette's authority.

The brightest of [[The Northern Isles]]: a small island north of [[High Isle]],
given over to pleasure and the high end of Purewater's nightlife. Its houses string
their lamps like pearls along the waterfront **Pearl Quay** -- the [[The Sylph's Embrace]], the grander [[The Siren's Call]], [[The Mother of Pearl]], the Lily Garden,
and the Promise of Heaven (where [[Randall]] was raised among its women). Legitimate
entertainment and quiet crime braid together here, all under the near-absolute
authority of the madame [[Marisette]].

Set apart on its own island, lamplit and lovely over dark water, the Pearl is where
the city's powerful come to be indiscreet -- which makes it, after the [[The Currents]], the richest seam of secrets in Purewater. It is also the scene of the
crime that opens the campaign: [[Santo di Teufel]]'s botched binding ritual on the
courtesan [[Emmeralda]] at the Sylph's Embrace, three nights before the tourney.

*(Formerly "the Street of Red Lanterns" / "the Pearl Quarter"; now its own island.)*

### The Shoals <!-- id: myth-loc-6201c037fbd2 -->

The seaward jaw of the Mouth: a peninsula reaching in from the north-east to form the eastern side of the strait, facing the open sea. The working docks, fish-markets, smugglers' jetties, and the Harbor Masters' tower; tied to the Lullwater by trade both honest and not.

The **seaward jaw of the Mouth**: a peninsula reaching in from the north-east to
form the eastern side of the strait, where the Lake's outflow finally meets the
**open sea**. This is Purewater's working waterfront -- fish-markets and wharves,

traffic, and the half-hidden **smugglers' jetties** that govern the rest.

The Shoals face the sea and take its weather; ancestor-spirits of drowned sailors
are honoured here. The quarter draws much of its life from the neighbouring
[[The Lullwater]] just inland and west, and the two are bound by trade both honest
and not. The [[The Dredgers]] are strong on these docks; the [[The Harbor Masters]]
officially rule them while quietly tolerating the contraband that keeps the whole
waterfront fed.

### The Siren's Call <!-- id: myth-loc-c3e12bafbb03 -->

Ravella's establishment where the canals of High Isle meet the poorer districts -- part pleasure house, part information market, neutral ground for every faction.

[[Ravella]]'s establishment, sited where High Isle's canals meet the poorer districts: part high-end pleasure house, part information market, and one of the few patches of neutral ground in Purewater. Here the half-merfolk broker holds court and runs her network of gondoliers, washerwomen, and dockhands -- everyone who touches water daily -- trading in secrets as much as pleasure. Whispered deals between rival factions happen under its roof; it is the city's great clearinghouse of information, and a likely doorway to the merfolk paths beneath Purewater.

### The Sylph's Embrace <!-- id: myth-loc-5f2b58f4bd7c -->

Constantine's high-end pleasure house on the Pearl -- elegant, thick-walled, and the site of Santo's ritual attack on Emmeralda.

An elegant, high-end pleasure house run by [[Constantine]], its walls thick enough to muffle the night's business. [[Magda]] took work here protecting the girls, and brought [[Gardwen]] in to stay.

Two nights after their arrival it became a crime scene: a patron resembling [[Randall]] carved a long, curling ritual wound the length of the courtesan [[Emmeralda]]'s body before fleeing, dropping his soul-knife behind an armoire. Gardwen's druidic healing saved the girl from death -- and from complete possession by the pleasure demon Santo was trying to bind. The house now keeps extra security and a wary eye on the street; Constantine wants justice, and the demon still stirs in Emmeralda's flesh.

### The Vantt Estate <!-- id: myth-loc-47286c1b4a58 -->

Councillor Alderic Vantt's country house and water-meadows east of the city, where the Dragon Knights are camped for the Tourney -- twenty-five Knights, their horses, servants and gear, on a Purewater merchant prince's own land.

Two miles east of the walls, where the ground rises out of the marsh: a good stone
house, stables for forty, orchards, and eight acres of water-meadow running down to
the channel. It belongs to **[[Alderic Vantt]]**, merchant prince, councillor, and
the Baron's host.

**The Dragon Knights are camped in the meadow.** Twenty-five of them, their horses,
the farriers and armourers, the servants, the tourney gear and the pavilions, from
eight days before the Tourney until whenever they choose to leave. [[Tat Atarer]]
runs the camp; the Baron himself sleeps aboard [[The DragonBarge]] and comes out
perhaps once a day.

**Why this matters more than it looks.** A foreign lord's armed company is quartered
on the private land of a sitting member of the Governor's council -- which means it
is not the city's business, no permission was required, and no one had to be asked.
It is also *why* Vantt wanted the standing order. There are twenty-five armed men on
his grass and he would like the week to pass without an incident that has his name
on it.

**What a party finds here**

- **Local labour, daily.** The camp eats. Bread, beer, fodder, water, laundry,
  and a farrier's boy from the village who is in and out of the horse lines all
  day and is not searched by anybody.
- **Men who talk.** Twenty-five bored mercenaries three days from a tournament,
  drinking Vantt's beer. Far looser than the six aboard the barge.
- **The gear.** Tourney harness, the champion's spare kit, the litter that carried
  something covered from the borderlands, and whatever came off it.
- **Vantt's household**, who did not ask for this and are frightened of it: a
  steward, a housekeeper, grooms, and a daughter of nineteen who has been sent to
  her aunt's in the city for the duration and is furious about it.
- **Tat Atarer**, findable, approachable, and increasingly unhappy about what a
  camp with nothing to do is doing to his company's discipline.

**It is not a fortress.** It is a rich man's farm with tents on it. There are no
walls, four gates, a lane, a river frontage, and a great deal of dark.

## Factions

### Crowbill's Underworld <!-- id: myth-faction-8ac6e57a9a87 -->

Purewater's criminal underground -- canal smuggling, gambling, protection -- run to a code by the unremarkable crime lord Crowbill. Signaled by crow marks. The occupation is wrecking the equilibrium his business depends on.

Purewater's criminal underground: canal smuggling, gambling dens, and protection rackets, run by [[Crowbill]] -- a wiry, forgettable man whose only distinctive mark is a small crow tattoo on the back of his right hand. His people signal with crow symbols, and he keeps hidden boat-paths through the canals and safe houses across the city. He works out of [[The Catacombs]] and is very hard to find on purpose.

**The code.** No murder of locals. No harm to children. No touching the Lake Lady's sacred sites. *(In the older Zorastor telling this was Jubal's Talons and the thief Randall's home turf; in Purewater the role is Crowbill's, given an explicit ethic.)* The code is not sentiment — it is why the city tolerates him, why the watch works around him rather than against him, and why he has lasted.

**Stated aim:** profit and quiet. **Real aim:** to keep Purewater *level* -- a city where everyone's business is predictable -- because a level table is worth more to him than any single score.

## Where they stand, Tourney week

**He was content to profit from the Baron's arrival and he is not any more.** The Dragon Knights are breaking the spirit of all three of his rules and he cannot enforce a code against armoured men without starting a war he loses. Every day the occupation goes unanswered costs him standing with people who pay him precisely because he keeps things level.

He is also the only power in the city with an intelligence network that is *already* watching the di Teufels -- [[Kerrin (brown coat)]] and others, on anyone interesting who comes through the gate, from the hour they arrive.

This is what makes him gettable. He does not need converting; he needs a *route*. Someone else has to be the one who acts, and he would like to know who they are before he decides.

## The door

**Whoever is already following you.** Crowbill's watchers are put on strangers who ask the working canals about criminals, mercenaries or untaxed cargo, and the fastest way in is to notice one and buy him a drink instead of losing him.

Otherwise: [[Nus]], [[Randall]], [[Mariarta]], or a crow chalked by a doorway. You do not find Crowbill. He fetches you, at dawn, off a ledge, and it is not a negotiation.

**He asks for nothing at the first meeting and offers nothing.** He is deciding what you are. Straight dealing works on him far better than leverage: tell him you cannot promise a thing and that you will come and say so to his face if it ever comes to that, and he will take it.

## What they can do for you, and the price

| | |
|---|---|
| **Hidden boat-paths** | move through the canals unseen, including past the harbour watch |
| **The network** | he found a specific boatman in one guess; there is very little he cannot locate in a day |
| **Safe houses** | across the city, including the Catacombs |
| **Theft to order** | [[Nus]] can lift almost anything off almost anyone -- including a locket off a Baron |
| **Enforcement** | he will guarantee Marisette's boycott for a price, and he honours guarantees |

**The price is the code.** Break it in his city -- hurt a local, hurt a child, foul a sacred site -- and no fee buys you back.

## Friction

Old paying relationship with [[The Pleasure Houses of the Pearl]]. An uneasy secret arrangement with the [[The Harbor Masters]], who publicly parade strict regulation while waving his cargo through. Complicated with [[The Dredgers]] -- he employs their people and undercuts their leverage. **Increasingly and dangerously opposed to the Baron**, whose men are ruining the equilibrium and whose heir is the exact kind of noble the code exists to survive.

## Hooks

- The party is tailed from their first afternoon in the city. What they do about it sets the whole relationship.
- Crowbill wants something from a PC and will not say what -- an honest refusal is worth more to him than a false yes.
- *Nus lifts the binding locket* on `d-1/day` is this faction's hand reaching into the main plot, and it gets Nus killed if the party is not careful.
- A Knight kills a local. Crowbill now has to act or visibly not act, and both cost him.
- Someone offers him the Baron's business, at a price that would set him up for life and end him in this city.

**Agendas on the clock:** *Keep the table level* ([[Crowbill]], 0/6) and *One big score, and a name* ([[Nus]], 0/4) -- the second of which is a boy who idolises the wrong things and dies in *The purge: a body on display* if it runs.

### The Awake the Dragon Movement <!-- id: myth-faction-53ff3ca60436 -->

A shadowy movement to revive the ancient draconic order and transform men into Dragon Kings. Baron Hanzo's true cause; an existential threat to water magic.

Beyond Baron Hanzo himself lies the cause he serves: a movement to revive the magic of the ancient Dragon Kings and -- ultimately -- to transform chosen men into dragons. The Baron's possession ritual, binding the ancient dragon-knight spirit [[Thuban Eta]] into a living vessel, is an early proof of this art: an attempt to recreate the draconic transformations of an age the realm thought destroyed in the old uprising.

How wide the movement runs is unknown -- it may have members across the realm, in other cities suffering their own corruptions. To the water-faiths and the [[The Mermaid's Court]] it is an existential threat: the return of an ancient order of fire and dominion against the older, pre-human powers of water. The Baron's victory at the Lake Lady's Tourney is meant to be the movement's first foothold in Purewater. **(GM:** see the GM-secrets lore for how the movement connects to the Baron's tower, Thuban's bones, and the wider campaign.)

**How it recruits, and the only face it has in Purewater.** Not with robes. Through
[[The Ember Society]] -- a respectable thirty-year-old antiquarian dining club it
hollowed out eight years ago, whose printed addresses contain no dragons and no
magic, only a beautifully argued melancholy about an age that was greater than
this one. Sixty subscribers; four who have ever been asked for anything; and none
of the four told why. Its secretary, [[Cassian Bree]], is the only confirmed
Movement member in the city -- a courteous clerk who keeps immaculate minutes and
has been quietly reporting on Baron Hanzo to his superiors for six years. **Hanzo
does not know that.** He is not their leader; he is their most promising
experiment, and they are funding him.

**This faction is deliberately thin and should stay that way.** It is the horizon
behind the story: even a total victory over Hanzo leaves the Movement intact,
holding Thuban's bones and looking for the next candidate.

### The City Watch <!-- id: myth-faction-9d005ad4ce8f -->

Purewater's ordinary police: about ninety men and women in blue-grey, competent at drunks, thieves and canal disputes -- and under standing orders not to provoke the Baron's people until the Tourney is over.

Ninety-odd watchmen in blue-grey, under **Watch-Captain Ottilie Sarn**, run out of
posts on each of the big isles. They are not soldiers. Their ordinary work is
drunks, cutpurses, canal-right disputes, dead men fished out of the Lullwater, and
keeping the Pearl from catching fire. They are reasonably honest by the standards
of a port and thoroughly outmatched by anybody in armour.

**The standing order.** Seven days before the Tourney -- the morning after the
first complaints about the Baron's advance detachment came in -- the watch
received a written instruction from the Governor's council: *the Baron's people are guests of the
city and of the Crown's representative; complaints against them are to be recorded
and referred, and not acted upon, until the Tourney is concluded.* Recorded and
referred. Not acted upon.

It came down over Prince Emeric's signature. He signed it because three of his
councillors -- [[Alderic Vantt]], [[Hesketh Pyle]] and [[Maro Quist]] -- told him
it would keep the peace during the festival, and he is two-and-twenty and badly
advised. **The same three later have him sign the instruction that silences the
temple's fouling alarm.** They are not a cult cell and they did not act for the
same reason: see *[[The Governor's council]]*. A party that pulls on the
watch's order is pulling on the same thread as the party that investigates the
Lake's corruption, and neither of them knows it yet.

**What this does to the watch.** It humiliates them. Sergeants have started
routing patrols away from the Merchant's Quarter rather than be seen not acting.
The referral book at the Isle post is four inches thick and it is only a week old. Some of them have decided
that recording is at least something and are doing it obsessively; others have
started drinking off duty in places where dragonshits do not go.

**There is no one crack in it.** The order does not fail because a single
sergeant disobeys -- it fails, or holds, at ninety desks at once. Every post
keeps its own book and every sergeant has a private view about it, and a party
that wants the watch has to give the whole institution a reason rather than
recruit one sympathetic man.

**Stated aim:** keep the peace through the festival. **Real aim,** among the ones
who still care: get the book in front of **somebody with standing to use it** --
the Prince in person, the Lady's clergy, a Sword of the Lake, a warranted assayer
-- without any watchman having to be the one who disobeyed.

A sergeant cannot accuse a baron. He would be ruined for trying, and every one
of them has done that sum. What the watch can do is keep an unimpeachable record
and act the moment somebody hands them a jurisdiction the standing order does not
cover -- the King's Assay, maritime law, the Order's sacred ground. They are not
waiting for proof. They are waiting for cover. See *[[The King's justice, and how a lord is called to answer]]*.

### The Currents <!-- id: myth-faction-68601e1d1bdc -->

A leaderless network of spies and informants trading in secrets, not contraband. Marked by chalk water-signs; the hidden 'Source' directs it.

An intelligence network and loose guild of independent operators who trade in secrets and favors rather than goods. They keep no headquarters, meeting at sites marked by chalk drawings of flowing water and identifying one another by coded talk of tides and currents.

**Ranks:** Droplets, Streams, Rivers, Confluences, Deltas, and the inner Watershed. There is no single head; the nearest thing is **"The Source,"** a hidden consortium of three to five unknown individuals.

**Stated aim:** keep information flowing and prevent any one faction dominating. **Real aim:** to be the true power behind Purewater, playing factions against each other to a balance that serves their unknown masters -- whom some believe sit in the capital or abroad. They deal with everyone -- [[Ravella]], [[Crowbill]], temple, and government alike -- and ally closely with no one. Their rivals are other spy networks, including Ravella's personal agents and the Baron's.

### The Dragon Knights <!-- id: myth-faction-c75709d1f531 -->

Baron Hanzo's 20-30 sworn warriors -- brutal mercenaries in dragon heraldry, enhanced with fire demonology. In Purewater for the Tourney and behaving like an army of occupation, because they have been told the watch will not touch them.

Ostensibly a noble martial order, the Dragon Knights are in truth Baron Hanzo di Teufel's instrument of conquest: hardened raiders and murderers who have terrorized the borderlands for thirty years. They wear armor emblazoned with dragons and many carry fire-touched weapons. Roughly twenty to thirty strong in Purewater once the column is in -- a dozen came ahead, eight days early, to open the estate and provision the barge. Well-disciplined and thoroughly violent. The city calls them **the dragonshits**, and does so quietly.

**They are in the streets, daily, and that is the point.** They are camped on [[The Vantt Estate]] two miles east of the city -- a sitting councillor's own water-meadow, which is why nobody had to grant them permission -- but they are not kept there. They walk the Merchant's Quarter and the Forge Quarter in threes and fours, they drink on the Pearl, they take the middle of a bridge and let the locals go round, and they eat and drink on the promise that *the Baron will settle it* -- which he does, late, and short. Boats are commandeered off the water-stairs. A dockhand who was slow got a broken jaw six days before the Tourney and has not worked since. Nobody has been killed yet.

**And the watch has been told to leave them alone.** See *[[The dragonshits in the streets]]*. That single instruction is what turns twenty-five thugs into an occupation: every Purewater citizen has now worked out, separately and correctly, that there is no one to complain to.

**Command.** The mercenary captain [[Tat Atarer]] is the leader of record -- a professional disciplinarian who fights for pay, not ideology ("the Baron pays for our swords, not our opinions"). He does not like what the standing order is doing to his company's discipline and says so, once, to the wrong person. Day-to-day operations and the Baron's dirty work fall to his albino lieutenant [[Blau]], known to the ranks as Commander Vask: burn-scarred, raspy-voiced, utterly loyal in public while secretly studying the Baron's techniques to one day break away.

**Ranks** (per the order's own conceit): Ember Recruits, Flame Soldiers, Smoke Lieutenants, Charred Captains, Ash Lords (elites who have undergone partial fire-binding), and the Dragon Circle (the Baron's inner guard, who serve aboard [[The DragonBarge]] and are not seen in the streets at all).

**Stated aim:** keep order in the Baron's territories and honor the order through tournament display. **Real aim:** use the Lake Lady's Tourney as cover to plant a foothold in Purewater, disrupt its water magic, and extend Baron Hanzo's reach. Their worst enemies are those who survived their past raids -- [[Gardwen]] foremost among them. The Baron himself keeps to [[The DragonBarge]] with six of the Dragon Circle; everyone else is on the grass at Vantt's.

**How a GM should run them.** Not as an encounter table. As *weather* -- present in most street scenes, mostly not fighting, always making the city smaller. A party that never draws on them still spends the whole campaign watching people step off pavements. And every incident is a lit match near [[The Dredgers]], who work the water-stairs the Knights keep commandeering.

### The Dredgers <!-- id: myth-faction-64500ba063b3 -->

Labor guild and mutual-aid society of Purewater's working poor -- canal-clearers, dockers, fishers. Black armbands, white wave. Led by Marda Blackwater, and three hundred strong in a week when the Baron's men are taking their boats.

Born as an association of canal dredgers who clear silt and refuse from the waterways, now a broad movement of laborers, fishers, and the working poor of [[The Lullwater]] and [[The Shoals]]. They keep a guildhall in the Lullwater and safe houses across the poor districts, and wear plain black armbands marked with a white wave.

**Ranks:** Hands, Voices, Channels, Harbors, and an executive committee of Pilots.

**Leadership.** [[Marda Blackwater]] -- a broad-shouldered woman in her forties who rose from the dredge-boats during a labor dispute five years ago; practical, fiery, and devoted to the working class. Brawn 75, Oratory 78, Influence 75, and the only person in Purewater who can put three hundred bodies anywhere she likes.

**Stated aim:** fair wages, safe conditions, respect for essential work. **Real aim:** to remake Purewater's power structure through worker solidarity and a seat in its governance -- the radical wing wants to overthrow the order entirely.

## Where they stand, Tourney week

**They are the powder.** Everything the occupation does, it does to them first. The Knights commandeer boats off the water-stairs, and the stairs are Dredger work. They take food off stalls the Dredgers' families keep. A dockhand named **Emmit Rill** had his jaw broken with a mailed hand six days before the Tourney and has not worked since -- complaint 31 in [[Abel Weir]]'s referral book. **Two boatmen rowed out by Knights have not come back**, and no body has been found, which is somehow worse.

Marda has petitioned the Harbor Masters and got sympathy and a referral. She has petitioned the council in writing and been ignored. She does not want a riot and she is going to get one anyway, because she has three hundred angry people, a watch under written orders not to help, and the whole city's Tourney provisioning passing through her members' hands.

**If nobody helps her get redress the lawful way, the stairs stop on `d-1/dawn`** -- no cartage, no fish, no ice, no lamp oil, and no competitors' gear moved to Temple Isle. A stopped port during a sacred festival is a crisis, and it is the pressure point at which even the Baron has to give something up.

## The door

**Marda herself**, and she is the most approachable major figure in the city -- she will talk to anyone who takes the dockhands seriously, in the guildhall, in daylight, without ceremony. She is also the easiest to *help*: get Rill's wages paid, get the two boatmen accounted for, or get somebody official to hear the complaint, and she will hold her people another day. Every single time.

Below her, any Hand on the Shoals stair will talk to a stranger who buys the beer and does not talk down.

## What they can do for you, and the price

| | |
|---|---|
| **Eyes on every quay** | who landed, what was in it, which pier it went to. Nothing moves on this water unseen |
| **Boats and boatmen** | including men who will row somewhere they should not, at night |
| **Three hundred bodies** | a crowd, a picket, a wall of people -- the one thing the Baron's plan cannot survive on the Tourney ground |
| **Hands and backs** | anything needing lifting, digging, dragging or hauling out of water |
| **A bed in the Lullwater** | where nobody official goes |

**The price is solidarity, and it is checked.** Marda gives nothing to people who treat her members as scenery. Help once, publicly, and the whole guild knows your face by evening.

## Friction

Bitterly hostile to the [[The Dragon Knights]] as the purest face of aristocratic oppression, and now with names and injuries attached. Open contempt from the [[The Harbor Masters]], returned with interest. Complicated toward [[Crowbill]] -- he employs their people and undercuts their leverage, and his code is the only law on the water that has ever been enforced. Cautiously allied with the progressive wing of the [[The Order of the Lake Lady]], who need labour for the Tourney and dislike what is being done to the poor.

## Hooks

- A party that intervenes in *A boat taken off the water-stair* has three hundred allies by nightfall.
- Marda asks the party to carry her petition somewhere it will actually be read.
- The radical wing wants to burn the Vantt estate camp and is not asking Marda's permission.
- Emmit Rill's family cannot make rent. Small, cheap, and it buys more than gold.
- When the stairs stop, the council leans on the watch to clear them -- and [[Abel Weir]] will not.

**Agendas on the clock:** *Make them pay for one of ours* ([[Marda Blackwater]], 1/4), which fires *The stairs stop* if it reaches 4.

### The Harbor Masters <!-- id: myth-faction-947ad753327b -->

The regulatory guild of Purewater's docks and shipping -- tariffs, berths, maritime law. Dark blue uniforms, a harbor tower HQ, a publicly incorruptible Harbormaster, and increasingly corrupt upper ranks. They berthed the DragonBarge.

The body that controls maritime traffic in and out of Purewater: docks, berths, tariffs, and the enforcement of maritime law. They work from a harbor tower and dockside offices, in dark blue uniforms badged by rank.

**Ranks:** Dockhands, Pilots, Harbormen, Tide Lords, Port Captains, and the seven-master Anchor Council.

**Leadership.** [[Harbormaster Velen Deepkeel]] -- stern, weathered, forty years risen through the ranks; meticulous and traditional, publicly incorruptible, and genuinely so. Seamanship 85, Navigation 82, Maritime Law 80. The rot is beneath him, not in him, and it is the thing he would least like to look at directly.

**Stated aim:** safe, efficient harbors and the city's standing as a trade hub. **Real aim:** to keep their near-monopoly on maritime commerce and its wealth; the upper ranks are increasingly corrupt, taking bribes to wave through smuggling while parading strict regulation.

## Where they stand, Tourney week

**They have the one document nobody has asked for.** The DragonBarge lies at a private pier on [[High Isle]]'s seaward side, and a private pier still requires a berth registration, a tariff assessment, a manifest, and a pilot to bring a hull that size through the Mouth. **All of that is on file in the harbour tower**, including what the Baron declared he was carrying and what the pilot who brought her in actually saw on her deck.

The paperwork is the softest evidence in the campaign and the least guarded, because it is *administration*.

They are also feeling the occupation from an angle nobody else is: the Knights are commandeering boats, which is a maritime-law matter, which is *theirs* -- and the standing order has removed their appetite for enforcing it. Deepkeel has recorded the incidents and referred them, exactly as instructed, and it is eating him.

## The door

**A Harbourman at a dockside office**, for anything routine -- and routine is where the value is. A berth registration is a public record; the manifest is not, but a clerk can be persuaded, bribed, or shown an Assay warrant.

**[[Harbormaster Velen Deepkeel]]** himself is reachable, formal, and immovable on procedure. He will not be bribed, will not bend a rule, and *will* act on a properly evidenced complaint under maritime law -- which is a much shorter road than the criminal law the watch has been forbidden to use. Nobody has thought of this. It is one of the best unpulled levers in the city.

## What they can do for you, and the price

| | |
|---|---|
| **The barge's papers** | berth registration, tariff assessment, declared manifest, and the name of the pilot who brought her in |
| **The pilot** | a man who stood on that deck and looked around while conning her through the Mouth |
| **Maritime law** | a jurisdiction the standing order does not cover -- boats, berths, cargo, and the water itself |
| **Berths and passage** | a hull, a slip, or a quiet departure |
| **Corruption, if you want it** | the upper ranks will wave things through for money, and Deepkeel must not find out |

**The price** depends entirely which door you use. Deepkeel's price is procedure and evidence. His subordinates' price is cash, and using them puts you on the wrong side of the man you actually need.

## Friction

Uneasy secret arrangement with [[Crowbill]] -- officially opposed, quietly facilitating. Open contempt for [[The Dredgers]], warmly returned. Strong ties to the Governor, the council, and the shipping merchants, [[Alderic Vantt]] among them. A wary, opportunistic eye on the Baron, whose berth fees are enormous and whose men are becoming a maritime nuisance.

## Hooks

- Somebody finally asks to see the DragonBarge's manifest, and the declared cargo does not include a covered litter.
- The pilot who brought her through the Mouth remembers the after deck being cleared and a man in harness being walked on it.
- Deepkeel is shown a boat-commandeering complaint he cannot refer away without breaking his own law. Watch him choose.
- A Port Captain takes the Baron's money and a party can prove it -- worth more as leverage than as a scandal.

**Agendas on the clock:** none of their own. They are a lever, not an actor -- which is precisely why nobody is watching them.

### The King's Assay <!-- id: myth-faction-6558cbba5de4 -->

The Crown's chartered body for licensing, inspecting and recording arcane practice in Mystamyr -- founded after the uprising to put magic on a register. Warrants, hallmarks, and a personal steel punch. Conall is one of theirs.

Formally *the Assay of Mystamyr*; universally **the Assay**. Not a college, not a
cult, and emphatically not a guild of adventuring wizards -- a **chartered
inspectorate**, established by the Crown in the years after the uprising for the
plainest possible reason: the realm had just finished a war against men who ruled
by magic, and it never intended to be surprised by that again.

**What they actually do.** License practitioners. Inspect and hallmark enchanted
goods so a buyer knows what he is holding. Keep **the Register** -- who may work,
what they may work, and what they have made. Investigate unlicensed practice, and
lay the proscribed kind before the Crown.

They are, in consequence, about as popular as any body that can close your
workshop.

**Ranks:** Prentice, Marker, Assayer, Master Assayer, and a governing **Table** of
seven Masters who keep the Register at Sharna.

**The marks of the office** -- and there is nothing on the body, no sigil, no
brand:

- **The warrant.** Folded, sealed, renewed each year. It is the authority, and
  producing it changes a conversation.
- **The punch.** A small steel die bearing the assayer's own mark, struck into
  everything they pass. Each punch is unique and recorded in the Register. **An
  assayer who loses their punch is in very serious trouble**, because a punch in
  the wrong hands licenses anything.
- **The thumb-ring**, assay silver, struck with the bearer's own mark. Everyday
  identification, and hard to forge precisely because the mark is on file.

## Who they take

**Outsiders, on purpose, and for entirely unsentimental reasons.**

An inspectorate is only worth its charter if it cannot be leaned on, and a man
with a great family behind him can always be leaned on -- through the family. So
the Assay recruits the people nobody can apply pressure to: foundlings, foreigners,
the second sons of nobody, a militiaman's foster boy pulled out of a ditch at
nineteen and taught to read. It is the most nakedly meritocratic institution in
Mystamyr and it did not get that way out of principle. It got that way because a
Register kept by well-connected men is worth nothing.

The result is a body of competent, unplaceable people with no natural constituency
and no social protection, holding a warrant that outranks local convenience. The
city's comfortable arrangements do not include them, which is the whole point, and
which is also why nobody in Purewater is glad to see one.

**It is not connected to [[The Swords of the Lake]], and there is no arrangement
between them.** A Crown inspectorate and a goddess's knights have separate
charters, separate loyalties, and no institutional business with each other at
all. [[Temerach Nebulo]] did not come to Conall through the Assay; **Old Johz
delivered Conall to *her***, personally, and then left -- and neither body was
consulted.

But an elf who rose to greatness among human knights and a ditch-boy who rose
among Crown assayers **recognise each other on sight**. Both were let in by an
institution that judged them on what they could do and not on who they came from.
Both hold an oath that answers somewhere other than this city. Neither of them
belongs here, and neither of them can be bought with anything Purewater has to
offer. That kinship is real, it is entirely personal, and it is not a treaty
between orders -- which is exactly why it will hold when treaties would not.

**Stated aim:** the responsible stewardship of arcane knowledge. **Real aim:** the
same, honestly -- plus an institution's ordinary appetite for jurisdiction,
precedence, and being the body that other bodies must consult.

## Where they stand, Tourney week

**Their man in Purewater is here by accident and holds no instructions.**
[[Conall]] came as [[Temerach Nebulo]]'s companion, on an errand she has not
explained. He carries the training, the methods, the warrant and the authority by
association, and nobody sent him.

The Assay itself is *elsewhere* -- a Table at Sharna, a correspondence, and a
distance measured in days. That is deliberate. It is a resource the party reaches
for and cannot command, and the delay is the point: writing to the Assay is a real
action with a real cost in time, and the answer arrives after the thing has
happened.

**And this is squarely within their charter.** Seating a spirit in an unwilling
person is a hanging matter, and a warranted assayer is one of the few people in
Mystamyr with **standing to call a lord to answer for it** -- not to arrest him,
which nobody can do, but to lay a finding before the Crown and put his own name
behind it. A borderland baron holding a spirit inside a living child for ten
years, working from nine folded sheets in an unregistered hand, is the exact thing
they were chartered to find.

**And a warrant is only worth the man holding it.** The Table sits at Sharna among
courtiers and can be leaned on like anything else made of people. An assayer who
lays a finding against a great lord and cannot make it stand answers for it
himself.

## The door

**Old Johz** -- the Master Assayer who recognised [[Conall]] as the Baron's son
when he was a boy in Anminster, healed him years later in Sharna, trained him
*"so that power didn't fall into the wrong hands"*, and then delivered him to
Temerach and abruptly departed. Nobody has said where he went, and an assayer
walking away from the Register without a word is itself irregular. His absence is
a live question.

**For anyone else:** the Assay keeps correspondents in most cities of size, and a
warrant opens doors. It is worth far more with [[Abel Weir]], [[Maro Quist]] and
[[Harbormaster Velen Deepkeel]] than with any criminal in Purewater -- and rather
less than nothing with [[Crowbill]], who has spent his life avoiding exactly this
kind of paper.

## What they can do for you, and the price

| | |
|---|---|
| **Warranted authority** | a jurisdiction that is neither the city's nor the army's, and that the standing order does not touch |
| **A written finding** | Assay findings are evidence a council cannot wave away, and the basis of a capital charge |
| **Counsel on the possession** | the likeliest expertise on what was done to [[Cailan]] and to [[Emmeralda]] -- by letter, slowly |
| **The Register** | who is licensed, what they made, and whose mark is on it. Nine folded sheets in an unregistered hand are already an offence |
| **Identification** | Read Magic, Detect Magic, and the vocabulary to know what you are looking at |

**The price is the doctrine: *establish, record, refer.*** Assayers investigate
and write it down; they do not act, and they certainly do not improvise. Every
genuinely decisive thing the party does this week will be something the Assay
would have advised against, and Conall will have to decide -- more than once --
whether he is an investigator or a man with a sword and a brother.

## Friction

Structurally resented by [[The Watercrafters Guild]]: a guild that *makes*
enchanted goods and a body that decides whether they may be sold is a quarrel with
no end, and the guild has been fighting Assay hallmarking for two hundred years.
Respected by [[The Order of the Lake Lady]] as a fellow institution, with the usual
condescension in both directions between a water-faith and a Crown inspectorate.
Useful to and used by the [[The City Watch]] and [[The Harbor Masters]], who like
anyone with a jurisdiction they can refer things to. Regarded by [[The Quiet Hand]]
as well-meaning, honest, and far too slow to be of use.

**A note on Hesper.** Because the Assay is the most visible arcane authority in
these parts, the sorcerer [[Hesper]] -- Marisette's enforcer in the Pearl -- is
sometimes loosely assumed to hold a warrant. Hesper does not, and the assumption
suits Hesper fine. *(GM: Hesper's true allegiance lies elsewhere -- see [[The Quiet Hand]].)*

## Hooks

- Conall writes to the Table. What he puts in the letter and what he leaves out is
  a real decision, with an answer that arrives late.
- Where did Old Johz go, and why immediately after handing Conall to Temerach?
- A warrant produced at the right moment gets the referral book opened, the guild's
  assessment reviewed, or a councillor to grant an interview.
- **Somebody tries to steal an assayer's punch.** With it you can license anything,
  and the Register will back you until the fraud is found.
- The doctrine says establish and report. The week says act now. Play the cost of
  both, and make him choose in front of people.

**Agendas on the clock:** none. The Assay is deliberately off the board --
reachable, slow, and not a lever anyone can pull twice.

### The Mermaid's Court <!-- id: myth-faction-1a0317907b94 -->

A secretive society of pure-water practitioners bound to merfolk, preserving water magic older than the city. Hidden under Lost Isle; led by the enigmatic 'Pearl'.

A hidden society of water-magic practitioners -- humans, half-merfolk, and the water-touched -- preserving the oldest forms of pure water magic, from chambers beneath [[Lost Isle]] and the canals. They leave little trace, known only by mother-of-pearl tokens and faint blue-green markings.

**Ranks:** Surface Swimmers, Tide Dancers, Depth Callers, Wave Shapers, Abyss Touched (transformed elites who can breathe water for hours), and a seven-member Deep Council. Their leader is known only as **"The Nacre"** (the mother-of-pearl) -- merfolk princess, ancient elemental in human shape, or something else; few have met them. *(Named so as not to collide with the pleasure-island of [[The Pearl]].)*

**Stated aim:** protect the purest water magic and the bond with merfolk. **Real aim:** to reclaim Purewater, over generations if need be, and cleanse what they see as a spiritual as well as physical corruption. They are fundamentally opposed to the Baron's fire magic but favor patient sabotage over open war. [[Ravella]], the half-merfolk broker, may have ties to them.

### The Order of the Lake Lady <!-- id: myth-faction-b0ed23843b44 -->

The water-faith of Purewater: priesthood of the lake goddess, guardians of pure water magic, and owners of the Tourney. Robed in blue-green with silver wave patterns. Their fouling alarm has been silenced by written instruction.

Both a religious cult and a civic power, the Order maintains the temples and sacred pools of Purewater (chiefly on [[Temple Isle]]) and performs the rituals that keep the increasingly polluted waters pure. Members wear blue-green robes worked with silver waves.

**Ranks:** Water Initiates, Reflectionists, Tide Wardens, Wave Speakers (those with proven water-affinity), Deep Visionaries (who commune with the Lake Lady), and The High Tide -- the ruling council of five Deep Visionaries.

**Leadership.** [[Lilura Deepcurrent]] has led the Order for three decades: elderly, piercing-eyed, rumored to carry merfolk ancestry. Beneath her, [[High Priestess Nerissa]] runs the Temple of the Lake Lady day to day -- she can sense water corruption, bless sacred waters, and speak with water spirits, but **temple law forbids her from intervening directly unless the sacred sites are threatened.** She therefore needs agents.

**And the Swords are not hers to send.** [[The Swords of the Lake]] are sworn to the goddess, not to the priesthood; Nerissa cannot command one, and the forms give her no graceful way to ask. See *[[The Order and the Swords: one goddess, two houses]]*. That double constraint -- gagged by an instruction, and unable to call on the one armed body that shares her goddess -- is the engine of the party's whole relationship with the Order.

**Stated aim:** preserve the sacred waters and the Lake Lady's blessings. **Real aim:** hold their political power as the waters fail; some higher clergy fear the goddess's power is waning and quietly study the corruption to understand it.

## Where they stand, Tourney week

**The Tourney is theirs.** It is a sacred festival, older than the Governor's office, and the Order runs it. That means the Baron's entire scheme is being staged inside their liturgy, on their island, under their authority — which is why the campaign's climax is desecration and not merely politics.

**And they have been gagged.** Six weeks ago the fouling alarm registered the corruption bleeding off the Baron's hull. The assessment went to the Watercrafters, [[Ivo Calder]] signed it off as a false reading, and the Governor's council carried an instruction standing the alarm down for the duration of the festival. Nerissa knows the reading was right. She has an instruction over the Prince's signature telling her it was not, and temple law telling her she may not act unless the sacred sites are threatened.

She is a powerful priestess, boxed in on two sides, watching the water go wrong and holding a festival anyway. **She is desperate for agents and will not say so in those words.**

## The door

**[[High Priestess Nerissa]]**, and she is actively looking for the party rather than the other way round. She will receive strangers who show any sign of caring about the water, and the temple is public.

**[[Sana]]**, her niece — nineteen, six weeks at the temple, notices everything and says it at the wrong moment — is the cheaper way in and the one who will tell you what the priestesses will not.

[[Lilura Deepcurrent]] is reachable only once the party matters, and reaching her is itself a victory: the Order's head can overrule an instruction the way Nerissa cannot.

## What they can do for you, and the price

| | |
|---|---|
| **Healing** | the best divine healing in the city, freely given to the hurt |
| **Sanctuary** | temple ground, which even the Baron will not violate openly |
| **Water-reading** | Nerissa can sense corruption and say where it comes from -- point her at a hull and she will tell you |
| **Speak with water spirits** | what the Lullwater saw is a question they can ask |
| **Standing** | the Order's public word carries weight the council cannot easily contradict |
| **The Tourney itself** | they can delay a bout, close a ground, or refuse a rite |

**The price is discretion and the sacred.** Bring violence onto temple ground or use them as a weapon in a factional fight and the door shuts.

## Friction

Kin to [[The Swords of the Lake]], and neither house commands the other. Allied with [[The Watercrafters Guild]] -- which is now *awkward*, since a Watercrafter's signature is what silenced them. On arranged terms with [[Ravella]]. Cautiously allied with the progressive wing of [[The Dredgers]]. Their chief enemy is the Baron's fire demonology, and since his arrival the corruption -- and their alarm -- has steadily risen.

## Hooks

- **Get [[Ivo Calder]] to recant.** It reopens the alarm and puts the Order officially onto the Baron three days before the Tourney. This is the single most valuable lawful act in the campaign.
- Nerissa asks the party to do something she is forbidden to do, and is careful about how she asks.
- Emmeralda, cut and demon-marked, is brought to the temple: divine healing against a demonic wound, and a priestess who now has personal cause.
- When the stairs stop, the Order's festival is being strangled and they have to pick a side.
- Somebody proposes stopping the Single Combat on sacred grounds. Nerissa needs a *reason* she can write down.

**Agendas on the clock:** *Keep the Lady's water clean* ([[High Priestess Nerissa]], 0/6).

### The Pleasure Houses of the Pearl <!-- id: myth-faction-8e135eb27ed4 -->

The courtesan network of the Pearl, under the madame Marisette's near-absolute authority. Constantine's Sylph's Embrace and Ravella's Siren's Call among them. The richest information market in Purewater, and the campaign's front door.

The entertainment trade of Purewater's pleasure district is no loose collection of houses but a network under the near-absolute authority of the madame [[Marisette]], who rules the business of pleasure from [[The Mother of Pearl]]. Individual proprietors answer to her: [[Constantine]] runs [[The Sylph's Embrace]]; the half-merfolk broker [[Ravella]] runs the grander, information-soaked [[The Siren's Call]]. The lesser houses -- the Lily Garden, the Promise of Heaven (where [[Randall]] was raised) -- line the Quay below them.

**The rule that matters.** The Pearl does not sell women off the islands. A night is for sale; a person is not. It is Marisette's oldest rule, it predates Constantine's house, and it is the reason there is anyone left working on the Pearl at all. Every proprietor enforces it and no amount of money has ever moved it.

**Stated aim:** run a profitable, orderly trade. **Real aim:** to remain the only power in Purewater that answers to nobody -- not the council, not the Order, not the Baron -- by being indispensable to all of them and knowing what all of them do at night.

## Where they stand, Tourney week

Tourney week is the richest fortnight of the year and this one has gone wrong before it started. Twenty-five Dragon Knights are drinking on the Pearl at night with no watch worth the name, and the houses are absorbing it because complaining is bad for trade. Two girls have been hurt already in ways that got settled quietly with money.

**Then a di Teufel offers to buy a woman outright**, is refused in Constantine's own hall, and comes back that night with a knife. See *An offer for the girl* and *Santo carves Emmeralda*.

**When that happens** -- and it is a beat, not a certainty; the party may be standing in it -- Marisette's response is total and immediate: **all services rescinded for the entire di Teufel retinue, enforced city-wide**, and she will pay [[Crowbill]] a princely sum to guarantee it. That is a courtesan network declaring economic war on a baron in a city where his men outnumber the watch. It is also the single loudest thing that happens before the Tourney, and it puts the Pearl in the Baron's way for the rest of the campaign.

## The door

**[[Constantine]]** is the one a party meets first: proprietor of the Sylph's Embrace, shrewd, protective, and not intimidated in her own house by anybody. After the attack she is hiring protection and wants justice, and she does not much care which of those a stranger is offering.

**[[Ravella]]** is the one worth cultivating: the Siren's Call is a pleasure house and an information market in one, and she brokers both. Light innate water magic, Insight 85, and a quiet second business protecting the city's hidden waterfolk.

**[[Marisette]]** you do not meet until you are worth meeting.

## What they can do for you, and the price

| | |
|---|---|
| **What soldiers say at night** | the best intelligence on the Dragon Knights in the city -- see [[Mariarta]], who is sent up to entertain them |
| **A name, a habit, a debt** | any notable in Purewater, from Ravella, for a favour or a fee |
| **Shelter on the Pearl** | quiet lodging nobody official will search |
| **An introduction** | to merchants, councillors, and the Baron's own officers |
| **The city-wide boycott** | the network's one real weapon, and Marisette spends it once |

**The price** is never money alone. Marisette trades in obligations and keeps perfect accounts. **[[Hesper]]** -- her mage-enforcer, and secretly far more than that -- is what collects.

## Friction

Cordial with [[Crowbill]] (a paying relationship, mutual and old). Arranged terms with the [[The Order of the Lake Lady]], who disapprove and need them. Contemptuous of the [[The Harbor Masters]]' public piety. **Hostile to the di Teufels the moment Emmeralda is cut**, and until then merely wary of a baron whose son is spending freely and badly.

## Hooks

- A party lodging on the Pearl is *in the house* when the screaming starts -- the campaign's best opening.
- Constantine hires the party as protection: honest work, and it puts them exactly where Santo returns.
- [[Ila]], thirteen and devoted to Emmeralda, will not be kept out of it.
- Marisette offers to buy something the party has -- the knife, a name, silence -- and her price is a favour unspecified.
- A Knight goes too far with one of Ravella's people and the Pearl has to decide whether it settles this quietly again.

**Agendas on the clock:** *Find who carved Emmeralda* ([[Marisette]], 0/6), which is the Pearl hunting a man with a face two PCs share.

### The Quiet Hand <!-- id: myth-faction-bb46b4299d0e -->

A secret, centuries-old fellowship that steers the world toward the good from the shadows -- by knowing first and acting unseen. Espionage as mercy. Hesper is their agent in Purewater. (GM: a benevolent hidden patron.)

> *"Save the world a tragedy at a time -- and take no credit for it."*
> -- the first article of the Empty Hand

**GM note: the Quiet Hand is a secret.** The players will not find its name in
any archive; most of Purewater has never heard of it. It is recorded here as a
hidden patron and a long lever on the campaign.

## What it is

The Quiet Hand is a fellowship of spies, scholars, healers, diplomats, and a few
mages who share one conviction: that the great evils of the world are best stopped
not by armies or open sorcery, but by **knowing of them first and acting before
they ripen** -- a planted word, an intercepted letter, a witness brought to the
right magistrate, a frightened heir reached the night before the coup. They hold
that history turns on small, almost invisible moments -- they call each one a
**hinge** -- and that whoever sees the hinge first and leans on it gently can bend
the future without ever taking a throne.

They are not the [[The Currents]]. The Currents sell secrets to whoever pays and
keep the city's powers in a profitable balance; the Hand takes no fee, serves no
faction, and spends its secrets to *prevent* harm. Where the Currents are a market,
the Hand is a conscience -- and a far older, far quieter thing.

## Origin (GM)

The Hand was founded generations ago by survivors of a catastrophe they could have
turned aside had they only known in time and dared to act unseen -- by their own
telling, the ruin left in the long fall of the **Dragon Kings**. They swore to
become the thing that strangles the next such evil in its cradle. This makes the
**[[The Awake the Dragon Movement]]** their oldest and most personal nightmare: the
one catastrophe they exist, above all, to prevent from happening twice.

## How it works -- the metaphor of the hand

The order is built so that no captured member can betray more than a fingertip:

- A single agent is a **finger**.
- A few fingers who work together, knowing only one another, are a **hand** (a cell).
- A handler who runs a hand is a **wrist**.
- The leadership -- unknown even to most wrists -- is the **Palm**.

Members carry no insignia and keep no hall. They know one another by **counter-sign**
(one speaks half of an old couplet; the other completes it) and a brief gesture --
an open, empty palm shown for a heartbeat, the sign of the Empty Hand. There is no
roster; there is no banner to capture.

## Method and creed

They prize the bloodless solution above all: the forged writ, the bought hesitation,
the timely rescue, the rumor that empties a square before the soldiers arrive, the
healer who reaches the right bedside. They **kill rarely and reluctantly, never for
convenience**, and they will not seize power for themselves -- power is precisely
the corruption they guard against, so they always act *through* others and then
vanish. Their successes are invisible by design: the war that never started, the
plague quietly contained, the tyrant who inexplicably lost his nerve. The measure of
their work is **absence**.

Mages among them are a prized minority -- valued less for force than for what they
can *learn* and *carry*: farsight and scrying, sendings, disguise, wards, the
occasional decisive word. Every member swears the **Oath of the Empty Hand**: take
no throne, title, land, credit, or monument; act never for yourself; spare whom you
can; carry the light, and pass it on.

## In Purewater

The Hand has one known finger in the city: **[[Hesper]]**, who for years has worn
the mask of [[Marisette]]'s cold, dangerous "enforcer." It is perfect cover -- a
listening post at the very heart of the Pearl's secrets, beside a woman who
hears everything. Hesper has watched [[Baron Hanzo di Teufel]] since before he ever
reached Purewater, and the seizing of [[Conall]] and [[Randall]] into the catacombs
was the Hand controlling a volatile night and getting at the truth -- and keeping
the soul-knife out of worse hands -- not the cruelty it appeared to be.

For the party, this means a **secret patron**. If they prove themselves against the
Baron, a door will quietly open, a guard will be looking the wrong way, the thing
they need will already be where they need it. They may, in time, learn whose hand
arranged their luck. Or they may never know -- which is exactly how the Hand prefers
it.

### The Swords of the Lake <!-- id: myth-faction-6195fe674783 -->

An austere knightly order sworn to the Lake Lady and the protection of the realm's water magic. Temerach Nebulo is their most famous blade -- and the only armed authority in Purewater that does not answer to the Governor's council.

A close-knit, austere order of knights sworn **to the Lake Lady herself and not to her priesthood** -- a code of honor beyond the city watch or the King's army, held to account by the goddess directly. They take no tithes and keep no temples. *[[The Order of the Lake Lady]] cannot command a Sword and never could*: see *[[The Order and the Swords: one goddess, two houses]]*, which is the reason Temerach can act in a week when the clergy may not. They hold battle honors from the ancient uprising against the Dragon Kings and live disciplined lives of service. They operate across Mystamyr to protect the realm and, above all, the old water magic.

**Ranks** are earned and worn plainly; the white belt is the mark of a full Sword, and [[Corvin]] would tell you at length how much he wants one.

**Leadership** in Purewater is effectively [[Temerach Nebulo]], "the Lakelady's Javelin" -- an elven knight of legendary skill with bow and spear, a rare outsider who rose to greatness among them. She has entered the Tourney's **archery** competition, not for glory but for the court access it buys, and she has been tracking the Baron and the [[The Awake the Dragon Movement]] for years. She has also long known whose son [[Conall]] is, and has not told him.

**No connection to [[The King's Assay]].** Separate charters, separate loyalties, and no arrangement between the orders -- Old Johz delivered [[Conall]] to Temerach personally, and neither institution was consulted. What the two of them share is not a treaty but a kind: an elf who rose among human knights and a ditch-boy who rose among Crown assayers, both admitted on merit by bodies that did not care who they came from, both holding an oath that answers somewhere other than this city. Neither can be bought with anything Purewater has.

**Stated aim:** safety of the realm. **Real aim:** expose and stop the Baron's revival of draconic power before it can take root. They have resources, agents, and dead-drops throughout Purewater, and move through quiet, unknown intermediaries.

## Where they stand, Tourney week

**They are the loophole.** The Governor's council has ordered the watch not to act against the Baron's people. The Swords do not answer to the Governor's council. They answer to their order and to the goddess, which makes Temerach the only armed person in Purewater who could intervene against a Dragon Knight in the street and not be disobeying anything.

She knows it. Everyone who has thought about it for two minutes knows it. **And she will not spend it lightly**, because she is one blade, the Baron has twenty-five, and the thing she is actually here for is the room the archery entry buys her.

**The entry is a genuine constraint, not colour.** Four years of work bought her a competitor's access to the Tourney and the court around it. She cannot also enter the Single Combat -- and she is very probably the one blade in the city who could beat the Baron's champion on the sand. She has to choose, and both choices cost.

## The door

**[[Murgeroch]]**, her squire and go-between: the steady young fighter who carries her messages, screens her meetings, and tends her gear. Usually the first of Temerach's people anyone deals with.

**[[Corvin]]** -- twenty-two, earnest, brave and not remotely subtle -- is the one who will actually help you, immediately, without checking, which is both his best and worst quality.

**Temerach herself** is direct, unsentimental, and formidable. She will tell a PC a hard truth to their face when she judges it time, and not before.

## What they can do for you, and the price

| | |
|---|---|
| **Lawful violence** | the one blade in the city that can act against the Knights without breaking an order |
| **Court access** | a competitor's entry reaches the Governor's enclosure and the people around the Prince |
| **The order's memory** | battle honours from the uprising; they know what a dragon-knight was and how one fought |
| **Dead-drops and intermediaries** | a communication network that is not Crowbill's and not the Currents' |
| **Standing** | a Sword's word is believed by exactly the people the party most needs to be believed by |

**The price is the code**, and it is strict. They do not lie, they do not murder, and they do not use people. A party that wants Temerach's sword must be able to stand having its methods looked at.

## Friction

Kin to the [[The Order of the Lake Lady]] and formally its equal -- bread, water and a bed at any temple, the Rite of Account on request, and no authority in either direction. Four hundred years of courteous disagreement about whether a priesthood that accepted civic power can still act when it matters. Respectful of the [[The City Watch]] and privately appalled at what has been done to it. Contemptuous of the [[The Harbor Masters]]. Implacably opposed to the [[The Dragon Knights]], whose past raids the order has been documenting for thirty years -- and one of whose survivors, [[Gardwen]], is the reason this is personal for more than one person in the story.

## Hooks

- Temerach chooses: keep the archery entry and the court access, or enter the Single Combat and try to beat the champion on the sand. Let the party influence it.
- She tells Conall whose son he is on `d-2/night`, and everything afterwards is different.
- Corvin does something brave and stupid on the party's behalf and needs pulling out of it.
- A Knight assaults someone in front of Temerach. Everyone in the street understands that she is the only one who can.
- The order's records on the raids include the one that took Gardwen's brother.

**Agendas on the clock:** *Shield the boy, unmask the house* ([[Temerach Nebulo]], 1/6).

### The Watercrafters Guild <!-- id: myth-faction-97a4578a9c5d -->

Boat-builders, canal engineers, water-diviners and makers of water-enhanced goods -- the artisans whose enchantments keep the canal-city working. Indigo sashes. One of their Tide Masters signed the Baron's corruption off as a false reading.

The guild of boat-builders, canal engineers, water-diviners, and makers of water-enhanced goods -- the artisans whose enchantments keep the canal-city functioning. They wear indigo sashes, rank marked in silver, blue, or mother-of-pearl.

**Ranks:** Apprentices, Journeymen, Master Crafters, Tide Masters, and a governing council of seven.

**Leadership.** [[Guildmaster Torval Bluehand]] -- middle-aged, practical, forward-thinking, his hands stained permanently blue by decades of watercraft enchantment. He prizes innovation over tradition, which is exactly the trait the Baron has been able to use.

**Stated aim:** advance the craft and protect guild members. **Real aim:** monopolize water technology and magic in Purewater; they secretly research how to harness the power released where lake and sea waters mix.

## Where they stand, Tourney week

**They are fascinated by the wrong thing.** The guild has been studying [[The DragonBarge]] since it moored -- a hull that steams continuously where it meets the water, fire magic held in sustained contact with a sacred channel, which as an engineering problem is the most interesting object any of them has ever seen. Torval Bluehand wants to understand it. He does not want it towed away, and he has said so in guild council.

That appetite is why the fouling assessment went the way it did. The Order's alarm was referred to the guild; **[[Ivo Calder]], Tide Master and the city's appointed assessor, took the reading himself, found it entirely real, and signed it false.** He was asked to, once, politely, by a man he respects at a private dinner of [[The Ember Society]] -- and the reason he has been able to live with it for six weeks is that the guild's own institutional wish was pulling the same way.

**The guild does not know it has been used.** Torval Bluehand believes his assessor's signature. If Calder recants, the guild is publicly humiliated and Bluehand becomes an extremely motivated enemy of the man who arranged it.

## The door

**Any Journeyman on a canal job**, for ordinary business -- repairs, a hired diviner, a water-enchanted tool.

**[[Ivo Calder]]** for the plot: fifty, distinguished, unbribable, and carrying something. He kept his original working notes, which show the fouling as real and bearing north-north-east from the Baron's pier, and he has moved them twice. He will defend the assessment vigorously and fold completely the moment somebody puts an independent second reading in front of him.

**Torval Bluehand** if the party has something technical worth his time -- and nothing gets his attention faster than an offer of access to that hull.

## What they can do for you, and the price

| | |
|---|---|
| **A second reading** | an independent water-divination of the channel: the one piece of evidence that breaks Calder |
| **Water-enchanted gear** | breathing, buoyancy, sealed cases, tools that work wet |
| **Canal engineering** | sluices, levels, and where the water goes -- including under buildings |
| **Boats** | built, hired, or quietly modified |
| **Expertise on the hull** | what is actually happening where that ship touches the water, from the only people studying it |

**The price is craft and monopoly.** They trade knowledge for knowledge, guard their methods jealously, and will charge a guild rate for everything else.

## Friction

Allied with the [[The Order of the Lake Lady]] -- an alliance now sitting on a fault line, since a Watercrafter's signature is what gagged the Order's alarm. Rivals of the dwarven crafters of [[The Forge Quarter]]. Two hundred years of quarrel with [[The King's Assay]], who decide whether guild work may be sold and stamp it accordingly. Professionally contemptuous of the [[The Harbor Masters]], who regulate water they do not understand.

## Hooks

- The party asks for a second reading and does not realise they have just aimed a weapon at the Baron.
- Torval Bluehand offers the party money and gear to get him aboard that ship, or to bring him back measurements.
- Calder is followed, or warned, or found in the water, depending on how loudly the party asks.
- A guild apprentice has noticed something about the barge's steam nobody senior wants written down.

**Agendas on the clock:** none of their own -- but the guild is the hinge of *Keep the Lady's water clean* ([[High Priestess Nerissa]]), because their signature is what closed it.

### Magic in Purewater: Water and Fire <!-- id: myth-lore-5d776e238b62 -->

The CFI magic of the city framed around its central conflict -- pure water magic and the Lake Lady's divine power against the Baron's corrupting fire demonology.

Purewater sits at a nexus where sacred lake meets sea, and its magic is organized around a single great conflict: **water against fire**.

**Pure water magic** -- the oldest power, tied to the merfolk, the Lake Lady, and the blessed waters. Healing and protective; stronger near pure water; able to sense corruption and disturbance. [[Gardwen]]'s druidic gift is explicitly of this kind, as is the divine power of [[High Priestess Nerissa]] and the [[The Order of the Lake Lady]]. It is increasingly rare and suppressed as the waters foul.

**Corrupt water magic** -- the grey zone, where practitioners blend natural magic with exploitation and profit over purity. [[Ravella]]'s domain shades into this.

**Fire / dragon demonology** -- the Baron's art: unnatural, ritual-precise, and corrupting. It binds spirits and demons into flesh, creates steam and heat where it meets water, and spreads its corruption gradually through the channels (most visibly from [[The DragonBarge]]). It is ancient and forbidden, the magic of the Dragon Kings reborn.

**In Classic Fantasy (CFI) terms.** This campaign is run under Classic Fantasy Imperative: the protagonists are Cleric, Fighter, Magic-User, and Rogue. The Lake Lady's water-priests are **Clerics** (divine/Theism, Piety and Exhort, water-aspected miracles -- blessing, healing, cleansing, sensing corruption). Arcane practitioners like [[Conall]] and the assayers of [[The King's Assay]] are **Magic-Users** (Arcane Casting -- illusion, invisibility, alteration of appearance, divination/find, counterspell). The Baron and his son work fire **demonology** as forbidden Magic-User/ritual art. Light innate gifts (Ravella's water-sense, a fisherman's cantrips) read as **Folk Magic**. Mages cannot cast effectively in armor.

**Manifestations of the conflict:** steam where fire meets water; channels near the DragonBarge running unnatural colors; pure waters recoiling from corrupted areas; water spirits withdrawing; merfolk paths closing; cold spots where fire magic has been worked; and water-magic practitioners troubled by visions. The Tournament becomes the symbolic battleground for which magic will hold the city.

*(In the original Mythras telling these traditions are Animism, Folk Magic, Divine, Sorcery, and Mysticism, each with its own faction practitioners -- the Mermaid's Court spirit-binders, the Watercrafters' water-sorcery, the Currents' scrying, the Dragonknights' Ash Lords. The CFI port maps these onto the four classes; the flavor of each faction's craft is preserved in their entries.)*

### The King's justice, and how a lord is called to answer <!-- id: myth-lore-da8d92e42e4f -->

There is no arresting a baron. Justice in Mystamyr flows from the King through people, is claimed in old forms -- oath over still water, the Rite of Account, petition, and trial by arms -- and is corrupted the way people are corrupted.

**Nobody in this story is going to arrest Baron Hanzo di Teufel.** There is no
prosecutor, no impartial bench that binds a lord, and no body of officers who may
lay hands on him. Understanding what *does* exist is the difference between a
campaign that works and one that keeps reaching for a constable.

## Justice is a person

It flows from **King Halvern** at Sharna, and nowhere else. In a city it flows
through the King's representative -- here, **[[Prince Emeric]]**, who may act in
the King's name today, in this city, on his own word.

Everything else -- the watch, the guilds, the Assay, the port -- is
administration. Administration can record, refuse, impound, close a workshop and
turn a man away from a berth. **It cannot touch a baron.**

## Standing: who may accuse a lord

Not anyone. A dockhand's word against a baron is not a small case, it is *no*
case. To call a lord to answer you must be:

- **another lord**, or the Crown's own representative;
- **a knight of a recognised order**, sworn and in good standing -- which is
  precisely what [[Temerach Nebulo]] is, and the whole reason she matters;
- **a Crown officer under warrant**, such as an assayer of [[The King's Assay]],
  within their charter;
- **a priest of a recognised faith**, within hers -- the Lady's clergy may speak
  to what is done to the water;
- **a guild master**, within his guild's jurisdiction and no further.

This is why the party's real game is never *finding proof*. It is **finding
somebody with standing who is willing to spend themselves**, and giving them
enough that they will.

## Accusation is a wager

To accuse a great lord and fail is to answer for it: ruin, exile, or the rope.
[[Abel Weir]] knows this, which is why a sergeant keeps a book instead of acting.
[[Alderic Vantt]] knows it, which is why a bought man stays bought. [[Maro Quist]]
knows it and would do it anyway if he were shown enough -- and would be destroyed
if he were wrong.

**Nobody makes this wager on a rumour.** That is what all the documents are
actually for. The referral book, the manifest, the assessment, the spent scroll:
none of them convicts anyone. Each of them is a thing you put in front of a person
with standing so that they will risk their name.

## The forms by which a lord is called to answer

**The oath over still water.** Nothing sworn over still water may be broken; the
Lady sees it, and the realm has built four hundred years of law on top of that
fact. A statement sworn over a temple pool is worth more than any quantity of
parchment, because everyone present knows what happens to oathbreakers. A witness
who *will not* swear has told you something, loudly, in front of everybody.

**The Rite of Account.** A knight of [[The Swords of the Lake]] may require the
Lady's clergy to put one question to the goddess on their behalf. **This is how a
knight of the Lake investigates** -- not by collecting depositions but by asking.
The answer comes as reflection and is seldom plain, and it is nonetheless an
answer that a court of lords will hear.

**Petition to the Crown.** Carried to Sharna by someone the King will receive.
Weeks of travel, and then it must survive the men around him.

**The King's representative.** Emeric, here, now. He can hear a matter and act on
it in the King's name the same afternoon. **He is also the shortest road and the
one everybody has forgotten**, because he is young and managed. *(And he is the
man the Baron intends to take. The only door to royal justice in Purewater is the
target.)*

**Trial by arms.** A lord called to answer by someone of standing may refuse the
matter and offer his body instead -- **or his champion's.** This is not a
loophole; it is the ordinary and honourable resolution, under the goddess's eye,
and its verdict is final.

**A lord's own court.** In his own lands a baron *is* the justice. Thirty years of
atrocity in the borderlands produced no case because there was nobody there to
bring one to.

## Why it is so easily corrupted

Because the channel is **people**, and people have debts, ambitions, and children.

The King is far away and content to be. Baron Hanzo has spent thirty years making
sure of that -- deniability in the borderlands, charm at court, and friends around
Halvern who will see that a petition arrives at the wrong hour and is answered
kindly and filed. He is magnificent at exactly this (Deceit 93, Influence, and
spells that make men agreeable), and he has never once needed to bribe a judge,
because there is no judge to bribe. He only needs to be *liked* by the right four
men, and to make anyone who might accuse him think about what happens if they are
wrong.

Written law is not the weak point. **The people carrying it are the whole
mechanism, and they are all reachable.**

## And the goddess is not answering

The Lady's gift is **prophecy-in-reflection**, and it has been failing for two
years. The pools are fouled; the answers come back poorer and stranger.

This is not colour. **The corruption bleeding off the Baron's hull is jamming the
one method by which the Lady's people investigate anything** -- and the fouling
alarm that would have said so has been silenced by an instruction over the
Prince's signature. Whether Hanzo planned that or merely benefits from it is a
question worth leaving open.

So the Rite of Account is available, sacred, and *unreliable*. And it must be
performed by the priesthood, which is gagged. See *[[The Order and the Swords: one goddess, two houses]]*.

## What this means at the table

- Stop looking for a constable. Start asking **who has standing, and what would
  make them spend it.**
- Every document is a lever on a person, never a verdict.
- The fastest lawful road in the city runs through a twenty-two-year-old who signs
  what is put in front of him -- and getting to him past his handlers is a real
  objective.
- **Any successful accusation may end in trial by arms**, and the Baron has a
  champion nobody has seen fight. The lawful route does not avoid the sword. It
  leads to it, with the Crown's blessing and a crowd watching.

### The Gods of Mystamyr <!-- id: myth-lore-2be9cd803cc1 -->

What is actually worshipped in Purewater and how: the Lake Lady's establishment water-faith, the older pre-human water beneath it, the woodland old ways, the soldier's god, the northern death-goddess, the trade and craft cults -- and where demonology sits, which is outside all of it and against the law.

## How religion works here

Purewater is not a devout city; it is a **transactional** one. People give to the
god whose business they are in, at the season it matters, and expect a return. A
boatman pours a little water back before a long pull. A stallholder in the
Merchant's Quarter keeps a craft-god's token by the scale. Nobody thinks these
are exclusive commitments, and only two traditions in the city ask for exclusivity
at all.

Worship is mostly **public, wet, and civic**: processions on the water, offerings
into it, the reflecting pools, and a calendar of festivals of which the Lake
Lady's Tourney is by far the largest.

---

## The Lake Lady (Nimue) — the establishment faith

Purewater's patron: goddess of the sacred waters, of purification and abundance,
healing and prophecy-in-reflection, and protection against corruption. Hers is the
city's official religion, its landlord, and its festival calendar.

**What she asks:** that water be kept clean, that the drowned be named, that
guests be given water before questions, and that nothing sworn over still water be
broken.

**Two houses serve her, and they do not command each other** -- the priesthood
([[The Order of the Lake Lady]]) and the knights ([[The Swords of the Lake]]).
This distinction matters enormously in play: see *[[The Order and the Swords: one goddess, two houses]]*.

**As the city's waters foul, her power is felt to wane.** Healing works less well
than it did. The reflecting pools give poorer answers. Whether this is the
goddess weakening or the medium fouling is the argument dividing her own clergy,
and some of the higher clergy are quietly studying the corruption rather than
merely praying about it.

## The older water — the Mermaid's Court

Beneath the Lady's faith, and considerably older than it, are the **pre-human
water powers**: not gods with temples but presences with places, honoured by the
merfolk and by [[The Mermaid's Court]] in hidden chambers under the Northern
Isles. [[Ravella]] and the city's concealed waterfolk keep these observances
quietly.

**The Order finds this awkward** and always has. The water-faith arrived, put
temples on the pools, and named the goddess -- and something was already in the
water when it got here. Officially the Court is a superstition. Unofficially
Lilura Deepcurrent is rumoured to carry merfolk ancestry and nobody in the Order
raises the subject.

## The woodland old ways

Not a church, not a priesthood, and not written down: the observances of the
forest peoples of the borderlands, carried by whoever is left to carry them.
Burial rites, hospitality rites, the naming of springs, and the old obligation to
lay the unquiet dead to rest properly rather than bind, banish or use them.

**This is [[Gardwen]]'s tradition** -- what found her in the grove after the raid
and what her woodland magic comes out of. *Unseat* is a burial rite before it is
anything else, which is exactly why it will not move a spirit that is holding on
because of what was done to it until somebody deals with the wrong first.

The city regards it as peasant superstition. The city is wrong.

## Vacarus — the soldier's god

God of war, invoked constantly in oaths and exclamations (*"Vacarus knows"*) by
people with no religious feeling whatsoever. He has a square in the old city and
no great temple here. Soldiers, watchmen and duellists keep his day; so, nominally
and without much sincerity, do the Dragon Knights, whose dragon heraldry is the
thing they actually revere.

## Hel — the northern death-goddess

Grim and terrible, worshipped in the north and barely at all in Purewater. It is
Hel who sends [[Magda]] -- through jarring, violent nightmares ending always in
Magda's own death -- to protect the four companions and *"lay the dragon warrior
to rest."* One does not serve the Goddess of Death without dread, and Magda curses
her patroness even as she obeys.

There is no temple to Hel in this city and Magda would not use one.

## The working cults

- **Sea-gods of the Shoals** -- small, numerous, bargained with rather than
  worshipped. Boatmen and fishers. The two boatmen who did not come back will get
  observances whether or not bodies are ever found.
- **Craft-gods of the Forge Quarter** -- the dwarven smiths' own, kept properly
  and privately, with a formality the rest of the city finds comic.
- **Nilo Valis** -- patron spirit of thieves and quick improvisation, invoked with
  affection and no ceremony at all. [[Nus]] would tell you Nilo Valis is real and
  has personally intervened on his behalf.

## And then there is demonology

**It is not a religion and it is not worship.** Nobody in this story prays to
Asmodeus. Demonology is a *technical tradition* -- a body of practice for opening,
seating, anchoring and drawing out spirits -- and it uses devil-sigils the way a
surveyor uses a benchmark, because those names hold and can be written down.

**Asmodeus**'s runic sigil is emblazoned on the demonologist's **soul-knife**,
the instrument [[Santo di Teufel]] means to use on [[Emmeralda]]. His mark on the
blade is the unmistakable signature of the fire-demonology at the campaign's
heart, and the sight of it makes even Hel's warrior recoil.

**The position under the King's law:** arcane practice is lawful and licensed --
that is what [[The King's Assay]], the Crown's arcane inspectorate, exists to
oversee. **Seating a spirit in an unwilling person is a hanging matter**, and has
been since the uprising, for the obvious historical reason.

**Which does not mean anybody can arrest a baron for it.** A lord answers to the
King and to nobody else, and the King is at Sharna. A spent scroll of *Seat the
Bound* in Baron Hanzo di Teufel's own hand convicts no one by itself -- what it
does is give a person with **standing** something worth risking their name on: a
sworn knight, a warranted assayer, the Lady's clergy speaking to what was done to
the water, or the Crown's own representative in this city. That is the whole of
the lawful road, and it is a real one. See *[[The King's justice, and how a lord is called to answer]]*.

### The Order and the Swords: one goddess, two houses <!-- id: myth-lore-bdd6db5777c8 -->

The Order of the Lake Lady is a priesthood; the Swords of the Lake are a knightly order sworn to the goddess herself. Neither commands the other, which is why Nerissa cannot simply send knights -- and why Temerach can act when no one else may.

Two institutions serve the Lake Lady. **They are not the same body, and neither
can give the other an order.** This is the single most load-bearing fact in the
campaign's political thread, so it is worth being exact about.

## The division

**[[The Order of the Lake Lady]] is the priesthood.** Temples, sacred pools,
rites, tithes, the calendar, and the Lake Lady's Tourney. It is a civic power with
property and politics, and it is bound by **temple law**: the clergy may not
intervene directly in the affairs of the city *unless the sacred sites themselves
are threatened.* [[High Priestess Nerissa]] is not being timid. She is bound, and
she has been bound for her whole career.

**[[The Swords of the Lake]] are sworn to the goddess, not to her clergy.** A
realm-wide martial order, austere, propertyless by rule, holding battle honours
from the uprising against the Dragon Kings. They take no tithes, keep no temples,
and answer to their own chapter and to the goddess who holds them to account
directly.

**So:**

- A High Priestess **cannot command a Sword**, or discipline one, or forbid one
  anything.
- A Sword **cannot command clergy**, or claim temple revenues, or speak for the
  Order.
- Neither can excommunicate the other. Only the goddess is held to do that, and
  she does it in her own time and in her own way -- which both houses will tell
  you, at length, means something different.

## Why the split exists

At the uprising, the water-faith did two things at once: it fought, and it kept
the pools. Afterwards the question of **who held the goddess's authority** was
never settled so much as divided. The priesthood took the waters and the
calendar. The knights took the war and the road.

The sore point is what came after. Generations ago the Order accepted **civic
power** -- temples on Temple Isle, a seat at the city's table, the running of a
tournament that brings in a third of its annual revenue. The Swords regard that as
the compromise that produced temple law in the first place: a priesthood that
cannot act because it has too much to lose.

The Order's reply, delivered with great courtesy for four hundred years, is that
the Swords have the luxury of purity because they have never had to govern
anything, feed anyone, or keep a city's water clean through a bad decade.

**Both positions are correct.** They are not enemies and they do not feud. It is
closer to two branches of one family who love each other and cannot spend a week
in the same house.

## The courtesies

These are formal, ancient, and playable:

- **Bread, water and a bed.** Any Sword may claim hospitality at any temple of the
  Lady, and the use of the pool. It is never refused.
- **The Rite of Account.** A Sword may ask the clergy to put one question to the
  goddess on their behalf. The priesthood performs it; the answer, such as it is,
  belongs to the Sword.
- **The road.** Clergy travelling may claim a Sword's protection, and a Sword may
  not decline.
- **No orders, either way.** A request between the houses is made in the open, in
  plain words, and may be refused without insult. Neither house has ever developed
  a polite way of *asking a favour*, because the forms assume equals who need
  nothing.

## Tourney week, and the deadlock

This is where the arrangement bites.

The Order's fouling alarm has been silenced by an instruction over the Prince's
signature, on the strength of [[Ivo Calder]]'s false assessment. Nerissa knows the
reading was right, and temple law says she may not act unless the sacred sites are
threatened -- and a corrupt channel is not yet, technically, a threatened site.

**[[Temerach Nebulo]] is bound by none of that.** The Governor's council has
ordered the watch to stand down; the Swords do not answer to the Governor's
council. She is the only armed authority in Purewater who can move against the
Baron's people without disobeying anyone.

So the Order has the knowledge and cannot act. The Sword can act and does not have
the standing to *declare* anything about the water. Each could solve the other's
problem in an afternoon.

**And neither will ask**, because the forms give them no way to ask that does not
read as one house claiming precedence over the other -- and because Nerissa is
proud, and Temerach is here on an errand of her own that she has not explained to
anybody.

**This is a party-shaped hole.** Somebody who belongs to neither house can carry
the words between them, and it costs nothing but the walk. It is one of the
cheapest and largest wins available in the whole campaign, and it is invisible
unless a player thinks to look at the two of them and ask why they are not already
working together.

### The Dragon Kings and the Lake Lady <!-- id: myth-lore-9f43bfd1dad1 -->

The ancient draconic order, the uprising that destroyed it, and the older water-powers that resisted it -- the deep history the Baron is trying to revive.

In an age before the present order, the **Dragon Kings** ruled -- a draconic power of fire and dominion, served by an order of dragon-knights. They were thrown down in a great **uprising**, in which the [[The Swords of the Lake]] won their oldest battle honors. The realm of Mystamyr has remembered the Dragon Kings as a destroyed evil ever since; their heraldry is an unwelcome thing to see flying on a border lord's banners.

Older still are the **pre-human water-powers** -- the merfolk, the spirits of lake and sea, and the goddess the city calls the **Lake Lady**. These older powers resisted the draconic order then, and the water-faiths hold that they resist it now. The campaign's deep conflict is precisely this: the Baron's [[The Awake the Dragon Movement]] is an attempt to *revive* the Dragon Kings' transformations -- and his possession of the ancient dragon-knight spirit [[Thuban Eta]] is a first, monstrous proof that the old draconic magic can be made to work again.

This is the history the storyteller [[Thorne]] secretly carries, and why he -- a former Order of the Lake initiate -- can recognize the Baron's rituals for what they truly are.

### The Realm of Mystamyr <!-- id: myth-lore-2a496b9bd0ec -->

The wider world: the kingdom of Mystamyr under King Halvern, its capital Sharna, the lawless borderlands, and Purewater's place as its corrupt water-gate.

Purewater is a city of the realm of **Mystamyr**, a kingdom ruled by **King Halvern** from the capital, **Sharna**. It is an old land that remembers the **Dragon Kings** and the uprising that threw them down -- a memory that makes the Baron's dragon heraldry, openly flaunted, both strange and ominous to those who notice.

Beyond the settled heart of the kingdom lie the **borderlands**, a lawless march of forests, swamps ("kritch"), and scattered villages where raids, pogroms, and reprisals have run unchecked for thirty years. It is here that [[Baron Hanzo di Teufel]] rules as a sorcerous tyrant -- enslaving, murdering, exterminating elves, and committing atrocities always with enough deniability that King Halvern, far away in Sharna, suspects nothing. He is, in [[Temerach Nebulo]]'s judgment, the greatest existing threat to Mystamyr and the King's reign.

Purewater itself sits at the kingdom's watery edge, the gate between inland lake and open sea. It is governed in the King's name by his young nephew, **[[Prince Emeric]]** -- clever, idealistic, and hopelessly unsuited to a city like this one, where every faction runs circles around his good intentions. The smaller towns of the march -- **Anminster**, where [[Conall]] was raised by a militiaman's family; the woodland grove where [[Gardwen]]'s people were massacred -- feed travelers and tragedy alike toward the city and its tournament.

*(Setting note: this is the Purewater conversion of the original campaign, which set the same story in the southern city of Zorastor in the world of Greymoor. Names, gods, and a few institutions were relocated; the plot, characters, and themes are preserved.)*

### The dragonshits in the streets <!-- id: myth-lore-d32693978d84 -->

What the Baron's occupation actually feels like at street level in the days before the Tourney -- and the standing order that tells the watch to record complaints rather than act on them.

**An advance detachment** -- a dozen Knights under a Smoke Lieutenant -- came in
eight days before the Tourney to open the estate, provision the barge and hold the
Baron's moorings. That is the week the city has already had. **The column arrives
on `d-3`** with the Baron, Blau, Tat Atarer and the rest, and doubles them.

So the party does not walk into a city bracing for an occupation. They walk into
one that has been living with a small one for a week and is watching it treble in
an afternoon. This is the texture under every outdoor scene until somebody changes
it.

## What it looks like

- **Threes and fours, everywhere.** The Merchant's Quarter, the Forge Quarter, the
  bridges, the Pearl after dark. They take the middle of a bridge and the traffic
  goes round them.
- **They do not pay.** Food, drink, a bottle off a stall, a night on the Pearl:
  *the Baron will settle*. He settles late and light, and the stallholders have

- **Six days ago**: a dockhand named Emmit Rill was slow moving a barrow and had
  his jaw broken with a mailed hand in front of forty people. He is still not
  working. The complaint is number 31 in the referral book.
- **Nobody is dead yet.** Everybody expects somebody to be. That expectation is
  the mood of the whole city.

## The standing order

Seven days ago -- the morning after the first complaints about the advance
detachment reached the Isle post -- the **[[The City Watch]]** received a written
instruction from the Governor's council: the Baron's people are guests of the
Crown's representative; complaints against them are to be **recorded and referred,
and not acted upon**, until the Tourney is concluded.

It went out over **[[Prince Emeric]]**'s signature. Three of his councillors --
**[[Alderic Vantt]]**, who drafted it, **[[Hesketh Pyle]]**, who seconded it, and
**[[Maro Quist]]**, who carried the chamber for it -- told him it would keep the
peace through the festival. He is two-and-twenty and badly advised, and it is the
second time this month those same three have got his name onto something.

They are not cultists and they did not do it for the same reason. See *[[The Governor's council]]*.

The watch is not corrupt. The watch has been *ordered*, in writing, and every
sergeant in the city knows exactly how that will read afterwards.

## What it does to the board

- **The Dredgers** work the water-stairs the Knights keep commandeering. [[Marda Blackwater]] has three hundred dock labourers, a grievance, and no legal route.
  She is the powder in this.
- **[[Crowbill]]**'s code is *no killing locals, no harming children, no touching
  the Lady's sites*. The Knights are breaking the spirit of all three and he
  cannot be seen to enforce a code against armoured men without starting a war he
  loses. It is costing him standing daily.
- **The Pearl** is taking the worst of the nights and is the least able to
  complain, because complaining is bad for trade.
- **[[The Swords of the Lake]]** answer to their order and not to the council,
  which makes [[Temerach Nebulo]] the only armed person in the city who could act
  and not be disobeying anything. She knows it. She is also holding an archery
  entry she has spent four years earning.

## For the GM

Put this in the background of ordinary scenes and let the players decide when it
stops being background. A single incident witnessed early -- a broken jaw, a
commandeered boat, a sergeant writing in a book and doing nothing -- teaches the
city's rules faster than any amount of exposition, and it makes the party's first
act of interference feel enormous, because it is.

### The Lake Lady's Tourney <!-- id: myth-lore-74c3c2484575 -->

Purewater's annual sacred tournament -- archery, single combat, and the unique Harbor Run -- and the stage the Baron means to hijack with his possessed champion.

The **Lake Lady's Tourney** is Purewater's most prestigious event: an annual sacred tournament honoring the goddess, older than the current political order, drawing competitors from across the realm. Because it is sacred, the Baron's plan to manipulate it is not mere politics but **desecration**.

**The events:**
- **Archery (the main event)** -- traditional targets, plus moving targets on boats between the islands and difficult cross-channel shots. Prizes carry court access and noble titles. This is the competition [[Temerach Nebulo]] has entered.
- **Single Combat** -- a duelling tournament of one-on-one matches, and the only martial event. Guild apprenticeships and contracts go to the placed fighters; but the **champion** receives the old rite: by a form older than the Governor's office, the victor is presented **alone and unarmed to the Crown's representative**, immediately after the bout, behind closed doors. It is ceremony, not politics, which is why nobody has ever thought to guard it. **This is where the Baron means to field his possessed champion, [[Cailan]] -- and the rite is the reason why.**
- **The Harbor Run** -- an obstacle course unique to Purewater: swimming, climbing, and boat-hopping through the canals and over rooftops, testing agility, strength, and local knowledge. Cash and local fame for the winner -- and a covert recruiting ground for criminal and military interests.

**The presentation of the entrants.** On the eve of the Tourney (`d-1`) every competitor in every event is presented to the Crown's representative by long form: names read, colours shown, and each entrant **bare-headed for the space of a bow**. It is ceremony, it is tedious, and it cannot be avoided without forfeiting the entry. It is also the only occasion before the sand on which the Baron's masked champion must show his face in daylight.

**Timing:** arrivals and preparations begin three days before the main event; registration and the sizing-up of competitors two days before; the presentation of entrants on the eve; then the events proper. The tourney's opening is the campaign's hinge -- the first public viewing of the Baron's champion, and the moment [[Gardwen]] recognizes her stolen brother.

### The City and Its Islands <!-- id: myth-lore-070ff6cde860 -->

Purewater as a strait-city: the Mouth where the Lake drains to the Sea, the two land-jaws (Caravan Square and the Shoals), the great triangular High Isle and its quarters, and the scatter of Northern Isles.

## The shape of Purewater

Purewater is not a ring of districts around a centre -- it is a city built across
**the Mouth**, the narrow strait where the sacred **Lake** (to the **north**) drains
south into the **open Sea**. Hold that picture and the rest follows.

**The two jaws of the Mouth.**
- From the **western** mainland, a thin **promontory** reaches east to a larger
  outcrop: **[[Caravan Square]]** and the Gate of Triumph -- the only way into the
  city by land. This is the southern jaw.
- A second **peninsula**, set a little north and east, forms the seaward jaw and
  carries **[[The Shoals]]** -- the docks, fish-markets and smugglers' jetties that
  face the open sea.

**[[High Isle]]**, the heart, is a great irregular **triangular** island wedged
between the jaws, splitting the Lake's outflow. Its **south-west** point reaches
toward Caravan Square (the calmer landward channel); its **east** point toward the
Shoals (the seaward channel, the true Mouth); its **north-west** point faces the open
Lake. The **Grand Canal bisects and branches across it**, dividing its quarters:

- **[[The Merchant's Quarter]]** (south-west) -- trade, guilds, the Watercrafters,
  the merchant princes.
- **The official & noble quarter** (north-east) -- the Governor's Palazzo
  ([[Prince Emeric]]), Hall of Justice, Barracks, Palace Square, and the wealthy.
- **[[The Forge Quarter]]** & industry (east) -- forges and yards spilling into the
  Shoals; the Baron's **[[The DragonBarge]]** at its seaward pier.
- **The residential north edge** -- sloping rich-to-middling south-east toward the
  canal-mouth, beyond which lies **[[The Lullwater]]**, the poor-and-criminal quarter
  (the old "Maze" and "Warren" combined; its calm name a deliberate mask).

**[[The Northern Isles]]**, scattered across the Lake's calmer waters north of High
Isle: **[[The Pearl]]** (pleasure and nightlife), **[[Lost Isle]]** (drowned ruins;
the [[The Mermaid's Court]] beneath), quiet **court-family retreats**, and a few
**shunned or haunted** islets. Off the north-east edge, alone and unbridged, sits
little **[[Temple Isle]]** with its temple and the **Sacred Confluence**.

Boats are the streets; bridges stitch it together; **[[The Catacombs]]** run beneath
it all. The set-pieces the geography invites: canal chases through the Lullwater, the
Harbor Run across the Mouth, and the steam-wreathed corruption spreading from the
DragonBarge on the seaward channel.
