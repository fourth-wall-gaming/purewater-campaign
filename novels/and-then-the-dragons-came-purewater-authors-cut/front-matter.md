Purewater is built on water and has never pretended otherwise. There is no
ground in it worth the name. There are buildings, and there is water, and
between the two there are ledges a yard wide with no rail and a drop into
traffic that is poled and rowed and shouting at you. Children learn the ledges
before they learn their letters. Visitors learn them by falling in.

The city drinks from a channel that comes down out of the lake and runs through
the temple on Temple Isle before it runs anywhere else, and the priestesses of
the Lake Lady will tell you that the water *is* the goddess, and they do not
mean it as a figure of speech, and if you push them on it they will look at you
with the particular patience of people who have had this conversation with
merchants before.

Once there were Dragon Kings. Everybody knows they are gone. What is left of
them is scale-pattern armour on a few border lords, a name on a barony out past
the marshes, and a small number of extremely wealthy people who would like the
old power back and are prepared to be patient and expensive about getting it.

The Lake Lady's Tourney comes once a year — archery, single combat, and a
scramble through the canals for whoever can swim and climb and doesn't mind
being laughed at. It is older than the Governor's office and everyone knows
that too. For three days the city fills up with strangers and nobody looks
closely at anybody, which is very good for trade and has historically been very
bad for one or two individual people.

This is an account of three of those days, and a fourth that nobody had
planned for.
