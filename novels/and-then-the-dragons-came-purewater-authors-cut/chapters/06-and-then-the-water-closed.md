# Chapter 6 — And Then the Water Closed

GOING DOWN THREE FLIGHTS IN THE DARK AT SPEED AT SEVENTY IS NOT A HEROIC
act and Magda has never once pretended otherwise. Nothing dramatic happened.
She did not fall. She simply took the stairs at the pace a body takes them at
the end of a long life, one hand hauling on the rail, with the lamp on the
second landing gone out and the turns tight and every second of it audible to
her as *not fast enough*.

She met Ila coming up. The girl hit her rather than stopped — two fistfuls of
skirt, barefoot, white to the lips.

"She won't *open* it—" hauling, trying to turn her round, back up the way she
had come. "The door's shut and she won't open it and Constantine's got the bar
out and Emmeralda *won't answer*—"

Gardwen went past both of them toward that door without breaking stride.

And Magda stood there for exactly as long as it took to notice that the
canal-side stair kept going *down* — past the boot room, to the water door and
the slipway — and that not one person in that house was going that way. Every
soul under that roof was going up. Down there in the dark it was quiet, and the
water was still moving from whatever had gone into it.

She took the girl's hands off her skirt.

She loaded as she went. Stone in the pouch, cord round the knuckles, staff
cocked back along the forearm.

---

The water door was unbarred and standing half open and swinging. Whoever came
through it had left it that way.

Four stone steps going down into black water, green and slick below the third.
A skiff moored to the ring at the bottom, riding oddly — the painter still tied
but the boat shoved out and drifted back, rocking harder than the canal
warranted.

The water directly off the slipway was still working. Not ripples. The deep,
slow turnover of something that went in heavy and recently.

One lamp on the far bank, forty feet off, its light lying flat on the surface.
Under that surface, nothing at all.

There was a shape on the second step down, right at the waterline, that was not
stone. She got a hand on it and hauled it up: a cloak, heavy with canal, dark,
good cloth, expensive, the hem still carrying road-dust under the wet. It had
caught on the mooring ring. It had not come off a man who was being careful.

Then she looked out across the water and found something to shoot at.

Thirty feet out and moving left, upstream: the fast ugly chop of somebody
swimming badly and in a hurry. A head. The pale smear of a face turning back
toward the house. Then just the shoulders working.

He was making for the far ledge where the lamp was, where there was a set of
iron rungs up the wall. Forty feet of open water between him and them, and he
had covered maybe a third of it.

He had not seen her. She was a shape in a dark doorway and he was looking at
the upper windows, where all the noise was.

---

Here is the whole of the reasoning, and it took about a second and a half.

She did not know his name. She did not know he was a baron's heir, or that the
house would be turned upside down for him inside a day, or that a man with no
colour in his face at all would spend three days walking this city with her
description in his mouth. She knew that somebody had gone into a room in a
house where she was a guest and done something to a woman in it that made a
wolf refuse a doorway, and that he was now thirty feet away and getting further,
and that she had a sling.

Backlit against the far lamp. Dark water. A moving target.

The staff came over and the stone went out flat across the water with that low
buzzing note they make.

She did not hear it hit. She *saw* it — the head snapped sideways, hard, wrong,
the way a head does when it has stopped being steered from inside. The working
shoulders stopped working.

He went under without a sound. No thrashing, no cry. The chop where he had been
smoothed out into ordinary canal, and the wake he had been dragging caught up
with the place he was not any more, and there was one hand above the surface for
a moment, open, not grasping at anything.

And then that was gone too.

Forty feet away the lamp on the far bank went on being a lamp. The iron rungs he
had been making for went on being empty.

The water closed and kept moving.

Behind her, up in the house, a woman began screaming and this one did not stop.

---

She went at the cloak there and then, on the slipway, in the dark, with feet
already coming down the boot-room stair — and did not finish. Outer pockets
first, because that is where a hurrying man puts things: a few coins, a folded
scrap gone to pulp, a lump of resin or wax. Then her hands found weight.
Something small and hard sewn in behind the lining at the breast, with no seam
to it on the outside, needing a knife and a minute and a light.

She had none of the three. Not yet.

Later, two doors down from a room she could not stay in, with a lamp off a
linen-closet bracket and the cloak opened flat on the floor and the stitching
arguing with the thumb-edge of her belt knife, she took the thing apart
properly.

A ring. Heavy gold, a man's, big enough to want a knuckle, with a beast cut
into the face — long, coiled round on itself, its tail through its own jaws.
Good work. Old work. Sewn into a cloak by somebody who did not want it on his
hand that night.

A twist of oiled silk, still dry inside. A few long hairs bound at one end with
fine copper wire, and a flake of something brown and hard that was not wax.

The hairs were dark red.

Emmeralda's are black to the root.

Eleven border coins, heavier and older and off a different mint, unchanged
since he arrived. A tailor's mark stitched inside the collar where the wearer
never looks — a little tower with three windows, embroidered rather than
stamped, which is a mark for somebody whose custom is worth the extra work. A
pulped letter dried flat against the hot lamp glass, of which two things
survived: part of a line in a fine clerk's hand, *—ee days before the Lady's
Tourney, at the hou—*, and the bottom half of a broken seal with the same beast
on it.

And in the left cuff, folded twice and stitched flat so it would not rattle, a
second twist of oiled silk.

Empty. Used.

There were three more like the first, wrapped in the same bundle. All full. All
hair.

None of it black.

Whatever he had come to that house to do, he had the makings for four more.
