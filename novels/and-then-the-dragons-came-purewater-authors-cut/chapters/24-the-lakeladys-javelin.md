# Chapter 24 — The Lakelady's Javelin

TEMERACH NEBULO HAS BEEN TRYING TO PUT HANZO DI TEUFEL IN FRONT OF A
magistrate for four years and has, in that time, accumulated letters, witnesses
who recant, and two dead informants.

That is the whole of her career at present. She is aware of how it sounds.

She was sitting on an upturned crate in the competitors' enclosure at the Lake
Lady's Tourney with her bow across her knees, doing nothing at all — which,
people are always surprised to discover, is the entirety of her preparation. She
is entirely still and she is not thinking about anything. It works.

She had not looked at the Governor's box once, which was remarkable, because
everybody else on that field had.

She did not know that a ship had gone down in the Mouth. She did not know that
Blau was dead, or that a seventeen-year-old boy was in a cell under the temple
she could see from where she was sitting, or that her Order's High Priestess had
spent the night on a drowned island cutting thousand-year-old wire off a dead
knight's wrists.

The enclosure is sealed from the first horn. No messages in, no messages out.
Nobody through the rope but judges and the Lady's own. It is not a Governor's
rule; it is a temple rule and it is four hundred years old and it exists
precisely so that nobody can nobble a competitor with news from outside.

Nerissa wrote it. Or her predecessors did. She enforces it.

So there had been a young Sword standing at the cordon since first light with a
folded note in his hand and the face of a man failing, and Temerach had not seen
him, because she was not looking at anything.

---

The woman who came and stood in front of her was in a novice's vestment about a
foot too long, and was seventy years old, and had canal on the hem, and moved
like somebody who had fought yesterday and slept badly.

Temerach looked up.

She said four sentences. They were: we've rescued Thuban and the boy. Di Teufel
was possessing people with demons all over the place. I just need the Baron's
locket to complete the rite and lay the haunt to rest.

That is three. The fourth was *he's in a cell under the colonnade*, and it came
later.

Temerach does not react the way people react. There was no start and no *what*.
She took it in and filed it, and then she asked the two questions that mattered
to her, in order.

"Is Conall alive?"

"Who?"

And that stopped her. Properly stopped her — the first thing all day that had.

"Conall Bjornlasch." She was watching the old woman's face now and getting
nothing, because there was nothing there to get. "Dark. Twenty-two. Rides with
me. He's been in this city three days."

"Never heard the name in my life, lass."

Something happened behind Temerach's composure that she did not intend and could
not have described.

"...Then you don't know," she said slowly. "None of you know. You've spent three
days pulling this apart and nobody has mentioned him once.

"That is either very good news or the worst news I've had this week, and I don't
yet know which."

Then she put it aside — visibly, the way she does everything — and looked past
her across the ground at the canopied box and the man in dragon-green.

It was the first time all day she had looked at him. Her face did not change at
all.

"Later. The locket first. Tell me what it looks like, and how close you need to
be."

---

She thought about the plan for perhaps four seconds, which for her is a long
deliberation.

"Not in the box."

She said it as settled fact.

"Six thousand people are watching that canopy. If he dies in it, or is *taken*
in it, then by tonight the story is that the temple and the Governor murdered a
foreign lord at the Lady's own Tourney, and the three men sitting beside Emeric
will make sure it's the only story. I have watched that happen to better cases
than this one.

"So we don't go to him. **We make him leave.**

"The Single Combat is called at the second hour. His champion is in a cell under
that temple. He does not know it yet. So at the second hour a herald calls for
the di Teufel man, and calls again, and *nobody comes* — in front of every Guild
in this city.

"He will have about a minute to decide whether he is a man whose champion is
late or a man who has been robbed. And he is not stupid. He'll know.

"And then he leaves that box. He has to. He'll go for his boat, and the only
water-stair from the Governor's enclosure is the one behind the colonnade, which
is twelve feet wide with a wall on one side.

"That's where he can be taken, and it is out of sight of the ground, and
everything that happens there happens in front of temple witnesses instead of
the whole city."

She was stringing the bow while she talked. She did not look at what her hands
were doing.

"A magician. Yes, I'd assumed. Which is why he does not get a minute of warning
and does not get his hands free."

Then she looked at the old woman properly, and there was something almost like
humour in it.

"And you're the part I can't do. He will leave when the herald calls. Or he will
leave *sooner*, and on our timing instead of his, if somebody he does not know
walks up to that box and shows him something that tells him it has all already
gone wrong."

A pause.

"You've been carrying that sack since you got here."

---

"It's the quiet one's head," said the old woman. "He was pissing me off. I was
told you wouldn't be OK with that but it'd definitely get the good Baron's
attention."

Temerach's face did not change at the word *head*.

"I am not going to pretend that pleases me." Flat. "But I have seen what that
man's people do to fourteen-year-olds and I am not going to stand on a field and
lecture you about a demonologist's neck.

"Use it if it works."

And then the old woman asked whether Conall was any good with a bow.

"No," Temerach said.

Much too fast.

The old woman waited.

"He's — " She was stringing something that was already strung. "He has a
soldier's training, all weapons. He's competent. He's not an archer."

"That's not what I asked, lass. I asked if he was any good."

"He's *competent*."

---

Here is a thing about Temerach Nebulo which she is aware of and has never been
able to do anything about. She is one of the most dangerous people in this
realm and she is an appalling liar. She has no facility for it whatsoever.

She had just told an old woman she met four minutes earlier two things she had
no intention of telling anybody: that Conall is a great deal better than
competent, and that she would rather chew her own hand off than have him within
a mile of that box.

She heard herself do it, too.

"He is my responsibility," she said, more carefully. "He is twenty-two, he came
to this city because I brought him, and there is a reason I have kept him away
from the di Teufels that I am not going to discuss on a competition field with a
woman I met four minutes ago.

"Ask me again tonight and I may tell you. Ask me now and I'll say no and mean
it."

Then, briskly, closing it:

"The Archery is called in a quarter of an hour. I will be on that line for the
better part of an hour and I cannot leave it without forfeiting — and I want to
win it, because the prize is court access and that is *four years* of my work.

"So you have an hour where I am the most visible person in Purewater and can do
nothing at all.

"Use it. Get me one thing: whether he still has the locket on him. If he's left
it on a ship or in a strongbox we are all wasting the afternoon."

---

The Archery runs in three passes and by the third there were four of them left.

First pass, fixed targets at eighty paces. She hit everything and the crowd did
not notice her, because everybody hits at eighty paces.

Second pass, moving targets on boats poled across the gap between the islands.

That is when six thousand people found out who she was.

The boats go across left to right at a walking pace with the targets standing on
them, and the field is allowed six shafts. The Guild man ahead of her put four
in and the ground was very pleased with four.

Temerach shot six.

She does not track. That is the thing you notice from the rail. Everyone else
swings the bow with the boat. She does not. She puts the bow where the boat is
*going to be*, and waits, and looses, and the target arrives at the arrow.

Six shafts. Six hits. The last two into the same target inside a handspan while
the boat was at its furthest and fastest.

The noise the ground made was not applause. It was the noise people make when
they have been shown something.

Third pass, cross-channel, two hundred and fifty paces over open water with the
wind off the Mouth. She and the Guild archer both came through it, and he is
genuinely good, and on any other day in any other year he wins that and dines
out on it for a decade.

Today he was standing next to somebody who had put two arrows into the same
moving target at range and then apologised to the judges for the grouping.

They called it for Temerach Nebulo, Knight of the Swords of the Lake. A purse, a
silver bracer, and access to the Governor's court.

---

There was one window to warn her and it was a good one, and it did not work.

Nerissa was to present the prize. It is her Tourney and they are her Order's
colours and the High Priestess handing a Sword of the Lake her bracer in front
of the city is the most ordinary thing that happens all afternoon. Four words,
while she does it. *Box coming. He has it.*

She was three steps from the dais with the bracer in her hands when a man in
Governor's livery came down the steps and took it out of them.

Vosk. The chamberlain who had kept Lilura in an anteroom for four hours.

He did it beautifully. A small bow, a murmured word about the Prince wishing to
honour the champion of the Archery *personally*, and it is protocol, and it is
correct, and there is not one thing the High Priestess of the Lake Lady can do
about it in front of six thousand people without making a scene that the three
men beside Emeric would enjoy enormously.

She stood there with empty hands.

Emeric — who is twenty-four and did not understand what had just been done with
him — presented the bracer himself, delightedly, and said something
complimentary about the second pass.

Temerach received it, bowed, and was invited to stay in the canopy.

She had no idea what was coming.

Four feet to her left, a man in dragon-green was being handed a fitted wooden
case by a steward, with a note in it written by a woman who learned to write
late.
