# Chapter 4 — Not My Business

THE KNOCK CAME AT THE HOUR WHEN A BROTHEL STOPS BEING A HOUSE AND STARTS
being a business, and it was the kind of knock a young man gives a door when he
is entirely certain of his welcome. Three, brisk.

Magda was three floors up with a brazier going and a shutter that stuck, and
Gardwen was sitting with her back against the bed-frame cleaning mud off her
boots with a knife, and Ruhi was asleep across the door, and none of them had
any business with whoever that was.

Then a second voice in the hall. Young. Male. Well-bred. Border vowels.

And a name, clear enough through the floor to be certain of it: *Emmeralda.*

Magda stepped over the wolf and got the door shut without letting the latch
speak, and went down as far as the turn above the second landing, where the
overhang puts you in shadow and the hall lamp does not reach.

She has spent most of seventy years moving quietly around animals. She got
where she was going and nobody marked it.

From up there she could see most of the hall. Constantine with her back turned.
The young man facing her, hood back, dark clothes, road-dust still on the hem —
he had not changed since he rode in. Nothing in his hands. Talking quickly.
Constantine nodding in the way she nods.

Then Emmeralda came in from the parlour door, changed since the afternoon —
hair up, something green. She said something. The young man laughed.

Then she went up the near stair, the one that comes past the second landing,
twelve feet from where Magda was standing.

He followed her.

They would pass below her in a few seconds. The shadow was good. It was not a
wall. Staying put was about to become a contested thing, and there was no
particular reason to be found on a stair in a house that was not hers.

She withdrew.

*Not my business.*

And it *wasn't*. That is the thing to hold on to, afterwards, and she did hold
on to it, and it did not help at all. Constantine had run that house for thirty
years. Emmeralda was not a girl. Hel had not walked her three hundred miles to
mind a brothel door, and a woman who came in through the water-gate that
morning without paying does not go and tell a nobleman that she does not care
for his face.

She went back up. Gardwen did not ask, which was a thing Magda filed away about
her. Ruhi resettled across the door and the house carried on being a house —
the stringed instrument, someone laughing on the second landing, water working
at the stone under the window.

She lay on the bed with her boots off and her staff under her hand where it
always is, and looked at the ceiling, and after a while she went under.

---

Somewhere below, an hour later, a young man who was not nearly as good at this
as he believed attempted something he had never been trusted with, and — against
every expectation of his father's, and of his father's men, and very probably
of his own — he got it right.

Then he tried to leave, and made a comprehensive disaster of it.

---

There was no scream. That was the first thing, and it was worse than a scream.

There was a crash — something big going over, a table or a chest, and glass
after it — and then a man's voice, high and completely undone, saying *no no no
no* very fast. Then a door hitting a wall hard enough to shake the frame two
floors up.

Running feet on the canal-side stair. A slip. A worse noise: a body hitting a
landing full length, and something metal skittering away across boards.

More running. A shutter thrown.

And a splash in the canal below that was far too big to be a man getting neatly
into a boat.

Magda came up out of sleep on animal reflex because Ruhi was already standing.
Then the house began shouting at itself all at once — Constantine bellowing
with no words in it, a woman crying on the second floor, somebody hammering on
a door.

Gardwen was on her feet with the boot-knife held low and reversed, looking at
Magda, waiting to follow her lead.

The metal thing that skittered across the boards had not gone into the water.

And from the room the two of them had gone into: nothing at all.
