# Chapter 5 — Shrieks of the Sylph

CONSTANTINE HAD RUN THE SYLPH'S EMBRACE FOR THIRTY YEARS AND HAD BURIED
two of her women and married off four more and had never once, in all that
time, had to stand in a doorway of her own house and refuse to go into a room.

There were twenty-two people under her roof that night. Nineteen upstairs and
three in the kitchen, and she could have told you every name and what each of
them was owed and who was sending money home to whom.

She had been in the hall when he came. She had done the warm professional thing
she does. She had thought, in passing, *this one is very bright tonight*, and
had not made anything of it, because a young man with border money going up her
stairs with his heart going is not a phenomenon, it is a Tuesday.

Now she was standing in a doorway with a bar in her hand and her people crowded
in the passage behind her, and the smell that was coming out of that room was
scorched hair and something sweetish underneath it, like fat gone over in a
pan, and something else beneath *that* with no name she had for it, and the
back of her throat kept closing on its own.

The wolf would not go through the door. That was the detail that finished her.
The animal was braced in the doorway with its legs locked and its head low,
snarling at a room with nothing in it.

Chalk on her floorboards. A worked figure, a big one, with corners on it,
scuffed through at the foot where somebody standing inside it had gone
backwards out in a hurry.

The shutter was off its latch and swinging and there was cold canal air coming
in.

And Emmeralda was sitting up in the bed.

She was opened from the hollow of her throat to her hip. One long deliberate
cut. Not a slash and not a stab — somebody had *drawn* it, slowly, with
something barbed, and the edges of it were wrong, and it was still bleeding,
and it was not being held closed by anything at all.

She had her head tilted slightly. She was looking at the door. She was smiling
at Constantine.

"You needn't crowd," she said. Her own voice, perfectly level, pleasant. "It
doesn't hurt at all."

---

The druid girl had got there first and had been on the bed with both hands in
it to the wrists, head down, saying something low and fast in no language
anybody knew, and every hair on Constantine's arms had gone up.

She had watched the flesh come together under the girl's palms. She wants that
on record. She *saw it close*.

And then she saw it open again from the inside, deliberately, the way a hand
pulls a curtain back.

The girl came out into the passage afterwards with blood to both elbows that
was not hers and her hands hanging at her sides, and stood against the wall,
and said, "I closed it. Magda, I *closed it*. Both hands, everything I had, and
it went in and it *took* — and then it opened again. On its own. Not tore.
*Opened*. Like a mouth."

Inside the room, quite clearly, in a warm and perfectly ordinary voice,
Emmeralda was telling Constantine that he was fussing and that she would like
her green shawl, the one with the birds on it, because it was cold with the
shutter open.

Constantine has been called by every name a woman in her trade gets called and
has answered to most of them cheerfully. Nobody in that house had ever called
her *he* in her life. She did not correct it. She stood in the doorway with the
bar in her hand and she did not correct it, because the thing in the bed was
using Emmeralda's mouth to be charming at her, and there did not seem to be a
polite way to raise the matter.

---

Then the old woman came up the stairs.

She had gone *down*, apparently, while everyone else in the house was going up,
and she came back with a soaking cloak over her arm and the entirely settled
expression of a woman who has been outside dealing with something.

She did not come straight in, either. She got as far as the doorway, and looked
at the bed for about four seconds, and then went away again down the passage
with the cloak, and Constantine — who had a great deal else to be looking at —
registered it and did nothing about it.

She was gone perhaps a quarter of an hour. Constantine has since worked out
roughly what she was doing in that quarter of an hour and has decided not to
ask.

She went into the room. She sat on the bed when she was asked to. She let the
thing pat the coverlet at her with a bloody hand.

And then, in the voice of a woman asking after a neighbour's roof, she asked it
who the young gentleman had been, so that they would know how to greet him on
his return.

It brightened at being consulted about hospitality.

"Santo," it said. "Just Santo — he didn't give me the rest, they never do, they
think it's clever." A little conspiratorial lean, and the wound gaped with the
movement. "He'll want the green room, I should think. He liked the birds on my
shawl."

And then, thoughtfully, arranging the coverlet over the ruin of itself,
house-proud:

"He said he'd come back for the others. Four more, he said, and then his father
would *have* to see. He was quite emotional about it." A small indulgent laugh.
"Men and their fathers, Magda."

Constantine got both hands flat on the doorframe and held herself up with them.

"*Santo*," she said. "He said *Santo*."

Because there is exactly one Santo who comes into this city in the week of the
Lady's Tourney with border vowels and a cloak that cost more than her front
door, and he is the son and heir of Baron Hanzo di Teufel, who rode in through
Caravan Square that same morning with twenty-odd Dragon Knights at his back and
who was at that moment being toasted by half the men on the Governor's council.

Twenty-two people under her roof.

A priest meant a report, and a report meant the Watch, and the Watch that week
meant the Governor, and the Governor was drinking with that man's father.

She got them into her counting room and put her back against the door and said,
"What do we do?" — and understood, as she said it, that she was asking a
pig-farmer from the north whom she had not seen in twenty years, and that she
was going to do whatever the answer was.
