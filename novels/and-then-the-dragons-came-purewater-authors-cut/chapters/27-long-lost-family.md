# Chapter 27 — Long Lost Family

CONALL BJORNLASCH HAD BEEN IN A SMALL ROOM OFF THE TEMPLE'S SOUTH CLOISTER
for an hour being told nothing, and he was not pleased about it.

He is quick. That is the trouble with him and always has been. He had already
worked out that something enormous had happened in this city in the last three
days and that he had been kept a mile away from all of it on purpose, and he had
got as far as being angry and no further.

Then the door opened and Temerach came in with an old woman.

He did the whole of her in one pass, because that is what he does: the vestment
she had no right to, the canal on the hem, the staff, the fact that she was
moving like somebody who had fought yesterday and slept badly.

"You're not clergy."

"No."

"Temerach, what is this."

---

She did not sit down. She stood with her hands at her sides like a woman giving
a report to a superior officer, which is the only register she has, and it
turned out to be exactly the right one.

"Conall. I am going to tell you four things and then you may do whatever you
like to me.

"**One.** Baron Hanzo di Teufel is your father. You were born of an elven mage
he seduced and ruined, twenty-two years ago, and she fled to this city and died
here.

"**Two.** You have a brother. A twin. He is somewhere in Purewater and he does
not know about you either.

"**Three.** I have known both of those since before I took you in at nineteen.
Every day since, I have chosen not to tell you.

"**Four.** He was arrested this afternoon, by me, on the water-stair, and he
will be questioned tonight — and he will say all of this out loud in front of a
magistrate to buy himself an hour. So you were going to hear it today whatever I
did. I wanted it to be from me.

"That is not the same as it being *right*."

And then she stopped. Completely. No softening, no *I'm sorry*, nothing.

---

He did not move for a long moment.

And because he is quick, he did not get to have the easy reaction. He went
straight past *you're lying* and straight past *this can't be*, and landed on
all the small things at once, and you could watch each one arrive.

*That's why you found me at nineteen.*

*That's why we came to Purewater.*

*That's why you entered the Archery.*

He put one hand flat on the table.

"How long have I been the reason?" he said. "For — any of it. All of it. The
Order, the training, the—"

His voice went.

He looked up.

And he was not looking at Temerach. He was looking at the stranger. The one with
no stake. The only person in that room who had nothing whatsoever to do with any
of it.

"Is she telling the truth?"

---

The old woman pulled the sash off over her head and dropped the whole vestment
on the chair, and stood there in her own filthy shirt and ringmail, which
answered his question about who was talking before she opened her mouth.

"Yeah. Course she is."

That was all. She did not dress it and she did not explain it and she did not
tell him it would be all right.

"Go find your brother."

He stared at her.

"Where?"

"Don't know. It's your city, not mine." A shrug. "But he's got your face, so ask
around. Someone'll have seen it."

---

Then, conversationally, to Temerach, while Conall was sitting down very
suddenly:

"He's better looking than Santo."

Temerach made a small strangled noise.

"Huh," said the old woman. "Does that make them the shitbird's heirs, then?
What's that make you, lad — a Baronet?"

And Temerach answered it, because she has spent four years in law books.

"No. And yes. And that is the most dangerous thing in this room."

She finally sat.

"Bastards do not inherit. Not in the border law and not here — that's clean. But
Santo di Teufel was the only legitimate issue, and Santo is dead, and if Hanzo
is attainted for desecration and demon-craft then the barony and the tower and
everything in it goes back to the Crown to dispose of.

"And when a Crown disposes of a border holding, it looks for **blood**. Not
because bastards have a right. Because it's tidier to hand a wild border march
to somebody the local men will follow than to install a stranger and pay for a
garrison for twenty years."

She looked at Conall.

"So no, you are not his heir. But there are men in three counties who will hear
*the di Teufel bastard* and start doing arithmetic — and there is a border tower
full of a hundred years of that family's work sitting empty tonight, and there
is a movement of people who wanted a Dragon King badly enough to fund all of
this, and **they have just lost their candidate**.

"You and your brother are going to be *extremely* interesting to some very bad
people by the end of the month. Both of you. And neither of you has any
protection at all except the Order and me."

Conall said, hoarsely: "And him. He's alive. He knows I exist now?"

"He will by tonight."

---

While that was happening on Temple Isle, the High Priestess of the Lake Lady was
standing on the temple steps at the close of the Tourney in front of the
Governor, every Guild in Purewater, and six thousand people who had come for the
Single Combat and got something considerably better.

She named the desecration. She named the arrest, by warrant, of Baron Hanzo di
Teufel. She named the fouling of the sacred channel, and the fact that she rang
the alarm and was told to stop — and she looked directly at Prince Emeric's
chamberlain when she said that part, and Vosk went the colour of paper.

And then she said the sentence she owed.

***"The canal folk kept the Lady's water clean."***

She said it slowly. She let it sit. And when the ground did not quite know what
to do with it, she said it again, louder, and added:

*"Say it back to me."*

And six thousand people did.

Somewhere out on the water, a very forgettable man with a crow inked on the back
of his hand heard an entire city say it, and nobody has ever found out what his
face did.

---

The Moist Oyster, that evening, because Thorne is owed the true version once and
because it is the only room in Purewater where a Guildmaster, a thief, a
priestess and a wolf can all be in the same place and nobody will comment.

Constantine sent food she could not afford to send. Marisette sent wine nobody
will ever be able to afford to drink again. Ravella came in through the
water-door with four of hers, which stopped the room for a moment.

Ila was there because she had threatened Constantine with a very specific kind
of hell, and she had attached herself to Bird — who was sitting in the warmest
corner in three cloaks, eating steadily, saying almost nothing, with Gardwen on
one side and a wolf across his feet.

Nerissa did not come. She sent Lilura, and a note, and a cask.

Hesper came late, from the school, and said the two shallow girls were out and
sleeping, and did not say anything at all about the third.

And Thorne was in his corner with his chalk, watching the door.

---

At about the ninth bell, Asphodel came in with a young man behind her.

Dark. Twenty-two. Well-dressed in a way that was not his money.

He was halfway through saying something to her when he saw the man standing up
at the far table — the one with his own face on him — and the sentence just
stopped.

The whole room stopped with it.

Conall and Randall looked at each other across the Moist Oyster for the first
time in twenty-two years.

Neither of them said anything.

And Thorne, very quietly, from his corner, to nobody at all:

"*Oh*, that's good."
