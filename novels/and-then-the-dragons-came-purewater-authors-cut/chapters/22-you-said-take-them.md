# Chapter 22 — You Said Take Them

RAVELLA ASKED FOR ONE THING BEFORE ANY OF IT AND MAGDA HAD SAID YES
without much thinking about it, in a room at the Siren's Call, the way you agree
to a price when your mind is on the goods.

Two dead men laid down properly, for one cold ship.

The Court had kept its half before she had done anything at all. When the temple
boat came in on the south side of Lost Isle an hour before midnight, there were
two shapes lying side by side on the stone under a sheet at the top of the
cleared stair: Ellick Sarn, and the one she had put an axe through, brought up
out of the drowned street by people who live down there.

She walked over to them on a bad leg with the axe still in her hand because she
had forgotten she was holding it, and she knelt down on the wet stone, which
hurt, and she pulled the sheet back off their faces, because she has never in
her life said words over a covered face.

Behind her everybody stopped moving. Nerissa took her lamp two steps back. Under
the stair the water went very still and it was not the wind that did it.

She said it.

*"Mother of the darkness. Take these drowned back to their rest. It's time they
went home."*

And nothing happened.

The water stayed still. The lamps went on burning. The two men on the stone went
on being two men on the stone and Ellick Sarn's face did not change in any way
at all.

She waited. She gave it a good while, because she has stood at enough gravesides
to know that the words sometimes take a minute.

They did not.

Somewhere down that stair, under the black water, something was *listening* and
had not been given what it needed.

---

Ravella came and crouched on the other side of the bodies and looked at her
across them, and she was not angry, which was worse.

"It's not the words," she said quietly. "The words were right."

"Then what?"

"You said *take them*." She laid two fingers on Ellick's forehead. "You're
asking her to come and fetch. She won't. She never does — that's the entire
nature of your goddess, isn't it? She takes the ones nobody sings about, and she
takes them *when somebody finally brings them*."

She looked up.

"Your storyteller told you this and you were listening to the other half of it.
Nobody ever came back for Thuban. Nobody has come back for *these two* either.
Ellick's wife thinks he ran off, and that one's people don't know he's dead."

She stood.

"You have to do the part that costs you. Not ask her to take them. **Give them
to her.** Say whose they are and say what was done to them and say that somebody
came back — and you have to mean the last one, because you're the somebody."

Nerissa, from behind, very quietly: "That is exactly how it works, Magda. In
every faith I know."

And Hesper, who had been listening while he unpacked his bag, and who had gone
rather pale:

"And if that's the shape of it for two drowned boatmen who died last week..."

He did not finish it. He did not need to.

---

She muttered *I'm not a fucking priest* at the stone.

And then she did it anyway, kneeling on wet stone with a bad leg, in the voice
you would use across a yard.

*"Oi. Listen up then.*

*"Brothers, workers, toilers, men. You lived your lives as we all do — with
imperfect plans, a little hope, a little selfishness, likely with much love and
sadness when you died. In this place. In the water. The water that takes all
things.*

*"But then these bastards brought you back. To haul, and work, and toil. Because
it helped them.*

*"So fuck 'em.*

*"Come with me, brothers. Rage with me at the loss and the death — and then go
back to the dark deep sea. Go to your rest. Go now, my brothers. Go."*

She was, at this point, shouting at a hole in the ground.

---

The water at the foot of the stair did not part, and it did not glow, and it did
not do any of the things a story would have it do.

It went *warm*.

That was all. It came up the steps past her knees like a draught from an open
door in a cold house, and every lamp on that island bent toward it at once, and
Nerissa made a small sound behind her.

And the two men on the stone let go.

There was no light. There was a *slackening* — she has seen it four hundred
times in byres and beds and she would know it anywhere. Whatever was still
holding the shape of Ellick Sarn's face stopped holding it, and what was left
was just a boatman who died, and looked it, and looked *finished*.

The warmth went down the stair and took something with it.

Then the water was only water.

---

Nobody said anything for a while.

Ravella had both hands flat on the stone and her head down, and she stayed like
that a long time. When she got up her face was wet and it was not canal water.

"They're through. The Court has them."

She looked at Magda and for once there was no broker anywhere in it.

"You said whose they were. Nobody has said whose anybody was down here in four
hundred years."

And Hesper, staring:

"Magda. That was two men who died *last week*, and it took everything you had."

Out on the black water, north-east, one of Ravella's people whistled once. Low,
two notes.

A small boat. One man. Something heavy amidships.

"Shut up, old man," said Magda. "Give me a drink so I can get ready for the
next."
