# Chapter 11 — The Tourney

The dawn casting found three.

Nerissa went into the channel at the school's water-stair to her knees and
Gardwen took her hand and put the other flat on the wall of the building, and it
took about four seconds.

"There," she said. Then, immediately: "*No.* Wait—"

Her grip tightened hard enough to make the priestess wince.

"There's three."

Three wrongnesses in a refectory where forty girls were being counted and
treating it as an outrage. Bettan, fourteen, cheerful. And two more who had gone
quiet for a week sometime in the last month and come back lovely and started
asking everyone about their families.

Nobody had noticed. Why would they. A homesick child getting happier is the
thing you want to see.

"They weren't collecting vessels," Gardwen said, grey. "They've been *filling*
them. Here. For a month. Four hundred yards from that stair."

Nerissa came up out of the water very slowly.

"Then how many have they done?"

---

Magda slept four hours in a cell under the temple and gave the mail and the axe
back on the steps.

"I said keep it."

"Aye. And it's about four stone and I'm seventy. That's a young woman's kit."

Nerissa carried the axe in personally, which is not a thing a High Priestess does
for a piece of armoury.

---

The field was on the north ground of Temple Isle with water on three sides and
six thousand people on it. Bunting. Bad wine. Fried fish, which did a great deal
to improve Magda's mood.

In the Governor's box: Prince Emeric, twenty-four, flanked and managed by three
older men. Two Guildmasters. And Baron Hanzo di Teufel in dragon-green with a
cup in his hand, being toasted, waiting for the Single Combat to be called.

On a chain under the green, something small.

A serving girl at four feet over the better part of an hour brought back the
shape of it: a flattened gold oval about the size of a thumbnail, worn under the
shirt, the chain showing at the collar. And every quarter hour his hand went
flat to his chest over it, briefly, the way a man checks a purse — and after he
did it, he looked round.

She was caught on the fourth pour. He smiled at her and said, pleasantly, in
front of two Guildmasters and a Prince: *you've very good hands, girl. Who do
you serve?*

"He wasn't angry," she said afterwards, with her hands not steady. "That's
what's wrong with it. He was *pleased*."

---

Temerach Nebulo shot in the Archery and did not look at the Governor's box once,
which was remarkable, because everyone else did.

Magda got to her inside the competitors' rope wearing a novice's vestment that
Nerissa pinned at the throat with her own hands and her mouth in a line, and
would not meet her eye while doing it.

"Don't," she said, stepping back.

"Wasn't going to say a word."

"You were *radiating*."

The Javelin was sitting on an upturned crate with her bow across her knees doing
nothing at all, which turned out to be the whole of her preparation. Magda gave
her four sentences.

She did not react the way people react. She filed it, and asked one question.

"Is Conall alive?"

"Who?"

That stopped her properly — the first thing all day that had.

"Conall Bjornlasch. Dark. Twenty-two. Rides with me. He's been in this city three
days."

"Never heard the name in my life, lass."

"...Then you don't know. None of you know. You've spent three days pulling this
apart and nobody has mentioned him once." She put it aside, visibly, the way she
did everything, and looked past Magda at the canopy and the man in dragon-green.
"Later. The locket first. Tell me what it looks like, and how close you need to
be."

---

She won the Archery on the second pass.

The boats went across the gap between the islands at a walking pace with the
targets standing on them and the field was allowed six shafts. The Guild man
ahead of her put in four and the crowd was very pleased with four.

She did not track. That was the thing you noticed from the rail — everyone else
swung the bow with the boat, and she put the bow where the boat was going to be
and waited, and the target arrived at the arrow.

Six shafts. Six hits. The last two into the same target inside a handspan while
the boat was at its furthest and fastest.

The noise the ground made was not applause. It was the noise people make when
they have been shown something.

The prize was a purse, a silver bracer, and access to the Governor's court.

Nerissa was three steps from the dais with the bracer in her hands when a man in
Governor's livery came down and took it out of them. The chamberlain who had
kept Lilura in an anteroom for four hours. He did it beautifully — a small bow,
a murmured word about the Prince wishing to honour the champion personally — and
it was protocol, and correct, and there was nothing the High Priestess of the
Lake Lady could do about it in front of six thousand people.

She stood there with empty hands.

So the warning never reached the Javelin. But the box did.

---

A boy in a chandler's apron carried a fitted wooden case up the enclosure steps,
handed it to a steward, said *for the Baron di Teufel, paid delivery*, and was
gone before anyone thought to ask anything.

The note inside was written by a woman who had learned late.

> YOUR SHIP IS ON THE BOTTOM. YOUR MAN IS DEAD.
> I HAVE YOUR CHAMPION AND HIS BONES.
> — THE PIG WOMAN

He slid the lid. He looked into the box for about two seconds. He slid the lid
back.

He said something to the Guildmaster beside him and the Guildmaster laughed.

Then he set the case down, stood, made a small apologetic gesture to the Prince
— *forgive me, a moment* — and stepped out of the back of the canopy with his
cup still in his hand.

He did not hurry. He did not look round the ground once.

He was very, very good.

---

The water-stair behind the enclosure was twelve feet wide with a wall on one
side and thirty-odd steps down to a landing where three boats were tied.

Magda got there first, because she was closer and he was being unhurried and
correct.

He got three steps down before he registered an old woman in a novice's
vestment standing against the wall with a loaded staff sling.

"Ah," he said. And then, with what sounded like genuine warmth: "*The pig
woman.*"

He had not put the cup down.

"You've cost me a great deal in three days and I would very much like to know
how you did the ship. I'll pay for that answer, and I pay well. Or we can do the
other thing, and you should know before you choose that you are standing on wet
stone at the bottom of a walled passage, which is a dreadful place to fight a
man like me."

Behind him in the arch, unhurried, blocking it, Temerach Nebulo already had an
arrow on the string.

---

Magda spat on the step between them.

"Hel's waiting for you, Baron. Time you went down."

He threw himself sideways and was quick for fifty and the stone found him
anyway. It took him high in the chest and turned him completely round and the
cup went out of his hand and shattered, and he came up on one elbow on wet stone
with his court silk torn open.

He was not warm any more.

His hand came up — palm out, fingers spread — and the air in that passage went
dry.

"My lord Baron," Temerach said, from the top of the stair. "Put the hand down."
The bow was drawn to the ear and held. "You are under arrest by warrant of the
Order of the Lake Lady for the profanation of the Tourney and the sacred
channel. There are two Guildmasters and a Prince forty feet above us. I have
been trying to say those words to you for four years."

The hand did not go down.

Magda went down the three steps with the staff held short in both hands, the way
a woman goes at a rat in a grain store, and brought the end of it onto his
wrist.

There was a crack that was mostly the staff and partly not.

The hand shut. Whatever had been gathering in that passage came apart all at
once with a sound like a held breath let go, and the temperature came back, and
somewhere above them a gull started complaining again.

He cradled the arm against his chest and looked up at her with his hair come
loose and fifty years of never once being touched undone on a service stair by
an old woman with a stick.

"You *hag*—"

His good hand moved toward his own collar.

She slapped it out of the air like a wasp off a pie, took a fistful of green
silk and collar and chain together, and put her face four inches from his.

She did not pull straight away. She held him there and let him get a proper look
at what had been coming up the stair for him, which was not a knight and not a
magistrate. It was an old woman who kept pigs, sent by a goddess he had never
once thought about, wearing a vestment she had no right to, with fried fish on
her breath.

Then she pulled.

The chain broke across the back of his neck and took skin, and it came away in
her fist: gold, warm, the warmth of a thing that has been alive against a chest
for a hundred years, and heavier than it had any business being.

Inside it, something moved.

Above them, six thousand people were being told that the di Teufel champion
withdrew through injury, and there was a certain amount of booing.
