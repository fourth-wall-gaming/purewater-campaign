# Chapter 1 — The Ford

The mist did not lift. It was the same temperature as the air and it stood
waist-high over the river and the queue stood in it.

Magda had walked three hundred miles. She had a quarterstaff and a staff sling
and a purse with very little in it, and she was seventy years old and did not
look it, which was the goddess's doing and not hers.

For eleven nights she had dreamed the same three things. A young woman with a
wolf. A burning house. A knight in dragon-scale who was dead and had not been
told. Hel did not explain. Hel pointed.

The woman from the dream was thirty feet up the queue.

She was younger than Magda had thought. Her cloak had been mended by someone
with no gift for it. She was not looking at the city like the others. She was
looking north up the road where the last of the dragon-scale was going away at
a trot, and there was a grey wolf sitting against her leg, and nobody in the
queue stood within eight feet of her.

Magda went up the outside of the line on the wolf's side, which is the wrong
side.

At twelve feet the wolf stood up. It did not growl. A carter behind her said
*oh* very quietly and found something to do with his hands.

She put her hand into the fur at the base of its neck where the ruff is
thickest and a hand goes in to the wrist. The animal locked. Its head came
round and its jaw was four inches from her forearm and its breath was hot on
the inside of her wrist.

She scratched. Down, once, the way you work out a burr.

The animal came apart in the shoulders first and then the back.

"—*don't*," the woman said. She was three seconds too late and her hand had
stopped halfway out of her cloak.

Magda kept scratching. She nodded once, the way you nod at a neighbour over a
fence, and looked the woman in the eye.

"Who are you?"

---

"Gardwen."

"Magda."

"You'll want to be somewhere else, mother. People who stand near me have a poor
time of it."

"I've been dreaming of you, girl."

The queue moved six feet and stopped. Neither of them moved with it.

"Your goddess," Gardwen said. "Which one."

"Hel. Death." Magda watched her take it. "Not death in battle. The other kind.
Everywhere else. In the bed, in the byre, in the bad winter. The ones nobody
sings about."

Gardwen looked at the mud for a while.

"That's an honest god."

The wolf sat down against Magda's leg and not against hers, and she noticed
that too.

"Does she show you a boy?"

"No. Not a boy." Magda turned her free hand over, looking for the words. "A
knight. Dragon-scale. Never seen anyone look that way before and I've seen a
great many people look a great many ways. He's dead, girl. Standing up and
walking and dead, and nobody's told him."

"Dragon Knights." Gardwen's voice had gone flat. "It's Dragon Knights you dream
of."

She did not say the rest.

---

Magda knelt.

The mud took her knee with a sound like a boot coming out of a bog and it went
straight through the wool. Somewhere behind her one of the tumblers said
something and another one laughed and was hit.

She held the staff out crosswise in both hands, the way her grandmother had
done it to a man who died the following spring.

"I'm to fight for you, girl. Protect you and yours, however you'd ask it. Die
for you, if it comes to that."

Gardwen did not take the staff.

She got a hand under Magda's arm and hauled, and her grip was stronger than it
looked and not steady at all.

"Get up. The ground's freezing, you'll ruin your knees, get up—"

She got her upright and did not let go straight away.

"Ten years," she said, very low. "Ten years and not one person. Not one. And a
pig-farmer kneels in the *mud* at a *ford*—"

She stopped. She put her face back on and it did not sit right.

"I don't know what to do with you."

Then she offered her forearm, wrist to wrist, the woodland way.

"My brother was taken by men in that armour. Ten years ago. He's the only thing
I have left to look for, and I'm three days behind that column, and if your god
has business with a dead man in dragon-scale then Hel and I want the same road
for a while."

Magda took the arm.

Behind them the ford had opened and the queue had started to move properly, and
the crowd went round the two of them like water round a stone.
