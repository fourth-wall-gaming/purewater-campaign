# Chapter 8 — The Name

His name was Ellick Sarn and he had to think about it.

"That's — yes. That's right, I'm fairly sure. I poled the Forge Quarter run.
Timber mostly, sometimes charcoal, and a woman called Tam who I think was—"

He stopped and frowned at the sky.

"There was something about Tam. It's gone."

He sat trussed in the bottom of the boat, agreeable as a man taking a seat at an
inn, and told them how it happened. The quiet one had paid him eleven marks to
take him out to the drowned streets four times and say nothing. He had said
nothing. He wanted that noted. He had kept his end.

"And then on the fourth time he said, would I like to not be cold any more."

He said it perfectly evenly.

"I've been cold on that water for thirty-one years. Every winter. Hands gone the
colour of the sky. And he said it like he was offering me a coat. And I said
yes. That's the part everyone wants to skip past. Nobody did anything to me that
I didn't stand still for."

"What went in you?"

"Something out of the stairs." A shrug that moved the whole trussed body oddly.
"It's not unkind. It doesn't want anything much. It likes the digging. It's got
a lot more of me than it had at Candlemas. That's the trade — it warms you and
it wears you, and you don't notice the wearing because the part that would
notice is the part it's wearing."

Then, conversationally:

"You'll be wanting to know about the one on the ship. That's a different thing
entirely. That one's *furnished*."

---

He told them that too.

"Mine's content. It got a boatman who was cold and it's warm now and it likes
lifting stone. There's no fight in it. That's why I'm still able to sit here and
talk to you about Tam and nearly remember her.

"That one *fights*.

"I was aboard twice, carrying. You can hear it through the deck. Not screaming
— worse than that, it's *arguing*. Goes on for hours. Two voices out of one
throat having a row, and one of them's a boy and one of them isn't, and the one
that isn't sounds like a man who's been told to do something he thinks is
dishonourable."

He shook his head slowly.

"That's what the morning cut's for. It's not just the wear. It's to keep it
tired. You bleed a man to keep him from arguing."

He worked his jaw, hunting.

"They've a name for it. Old-sounding. Thoo— Thuban. Thuban something. Eta,
maybe. He's a knight out of the dragon wars and he's been in one thing or
another for a very long while, and he doesn't want to be, and he never has."

He looked round the boat at all of them.

"You keep saying rescue the boy. I'd think about that. There's two in there, and
only one of them's been asking to get out."

---

Thorne kept the corner of the Moist Oyster and a stub of chalk he turned over in
his fingers.

At the name he put the chalk down.

"Say that again."

"Thuban Eta."

"Thuban Eta was real. That's not a story name. That's a *muster roll* name."

He had been Order of the Lake for thirty-one years before he wasn't.

"The Dragon Kings had knight-companies and the Eta were one of them — the
letter's a company mark, not a family. And Thuban was the one they teach you
about in your second year, because of how he died.

"He wouldn't burn a town.

"That's the whole of it. The company was ordered to fire a valley and he refused
and argued it in front of the others, and about a third of them came round to
him. So the ones who hadn't took him out on the water at night to talk it over,
and they put him over the side in his mail with his hands tied.

"Drowned. By his own company. For refusing an order that even they thought was
filthy — that's the part that damns them. They knew he was right and they killed
him for making them know it."

He looked up.

"And that is exactly the kind of dead man you can bind. You don't hold a shade
with iron, mother. You hold it with the thing it can't put down. You keep him in
the moment of it — tied hands and cold water and his own men's faces looking
down at him. He'll pull against it for a hundred years and never once get out,
and every time he pulls you can use the pulling."

"That's what's in my brother," Gardwen said.

"That's what's in your brother. And it has been for ten years, and it has hated
every hour, because a man who wouldn't fire a valley is not going to enjoy being
made into a champion."

He pushed the chalk away.

"Your goddess said *lay him to rest*. Everyone at this table has been thinking
that means a door and a ritual. It might not. You don't lay that man down with a
stair. You lay him down by undoing the betrayal."

---

Magda said the obvious thing. A thousand years. Who could possibly answer for
it. The kings and their armies were laid to rest and every child knows it.

"You're right," Thorne agreed. "There's no company left and no descendants worth
the name. If it wants the actual men, it's impossible and we may as well go
home."

He held up a finger.

"But that's not how a debt like that works. It's been a thousand years — Thuban
knows they're dust. What holds him isn't a grudge against nine specific
bastards. It's that **nobody ever came back for him.** He went over the side and
the water closed and that was the end, and no company, no order, no god, no
sister has stood over that water since and said *this was wrong, and he was
ours, and we're taking him home*."

Gardwen spoke slowly, in the voice she used with her hands in the water.

"There's a thing we do in the wood, for a beast that's died badly. In a snare,
or under a fall, where it couldn't get free. You don't pray it away. You go and
you *sit* with it, and you say out loud that it wasn't right, and then you carry
it. That's the whole rite. Somebody has to actually pick the thing up."

She looked at Magda.

"It doesn't need the ones who did it. It needs somebody willing to stand where
they stood and answer for it. Not because it's theirs. Because they're the one
who turned up."

And Thorne said, quietly, laying a card down:

"And who exactly do we know that serves the god of the ones nobody sings about?"

He looked at her.

"Not the battle-dead, you said. The other kind. In the bed, in the byre, in the
bad winter. A knight drowned in the dark by his own friends for refusing to burn
a village, and left out of every song since, in every land, for a thousand
years."

A pause.

"Mother — whose *is* he, if he isn't hers?"

Magda sat with that for a while.

"Fuck me," she said. "Hel. The old lady has her ways."

She put her cup down.

"All right. I'll do it."
