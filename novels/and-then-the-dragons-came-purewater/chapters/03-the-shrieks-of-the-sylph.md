# Chapter 3 — The Shrieks of the Sylph

The young man came at dusk and knocked three times, confident.

Magda went down as far as the turn of the stair and looked over the rail. The
fourth step from the top had a voice and she had forgotten it, and it spoke
under her, but she got her look first.

Twenty-two or three. Dark clothes cut border-fashion with the road-dust still
on the hem. Handsome in the way that gets told to a boy early. He was too
bright. There was sweat at his hairline in a cold stone hall and he laughed a
half-beat before the joke finished, twice.

His cloak hung badly on the left. Something under it at the hip. Not a sword —
the hang was wrong, too short and too far forward. Something with a guard on
it.

His right hand went to that place four times while she watched, and each time
it came away, and he did not know he was doing it.

Beside her the wolf's hackles came up along her whole spine.

Then the step creaked and he looked up, fast, before the smile was back — and
for a quarter of a second what was on his face was not fear of being caught. It
was fury. The flat outraged fury of something interrupted.

Then the smile arrived.

"Ah — forgive me," he said, and bowed, and it was a real bow. "I didn't know
the house had guests up. I've been rude."

"My old friend from the north, my lord," Constantine said. "Deaf as a post and
twice as stubborn."

"I would never." He held it two beats longer than a man looking at furniture.

Then he turned back and his hand went to his hip a fifth time.

"Now. Emmeralda. She's expecting me — I've paid for the whole evening, and I'd
take it very kindly if we weren't disturbed."

---

Magda went back up.

"Young lord downstairs," she said. "Paid for the evening. It's a bawdy-house,
lass. That's the trade."

"You don't like him."

"I don't like anybody."

Gardwen took the floor by the brazier and was asleep in ninety seconds. Magda
lay on the bed with her boots off and her staff under her hand and looked at
the ceiling and thought about a hang that was wrong for a sword.

The music stopped downstairs. The house went quiet by degrees. She went under.

---

She came up because the wolf was standing.

Ruhi was at the door with her ears flat and every hair up and she had made no
sound at all. That was what had woken her. Not noise. The absence of it.

Then it came. It was higher than a scream and had no shape and went on past the
length of one breath.

Then it stopped, mid-note.

And in the ringing after it a young man's well-bred border voice was saying
something fast and low and rhythmic that did not stop when the screaming did.

---

Afterwards nobody could say how long it was.

There was a crash below — a chest going over, and glass — and a man's voice
saying *no no no no* very fast, and a door hitting a wall hard enough to shake
the frame two floors up. Running on the canal-side stair. A slip. A body hitting
a landing full length and something metal skittering away across boards.

A shutter thrown. A splash in the canal far too big for a man getting neatly
into a boat.

Then voices all over the house.

And from the room they had gone into, nothing.

---

Magda went down. Ila caught her on the second landing with two fistfuls of her
skirt and a face white to the lips, saying *she won't open it*, and Gardwen went
past both of them without breaking stride.

Magda went down instead of up.

The water door stood half open, swinging. She loaded as she went — stone in the
pouch, cord round the knuckles, staff cocked back along her forearm.

The slipway was four steps into black water. A skiff rode hard on its painter.
The water off the steps was still turning over, slow and deep, the way water
does when something has gone into it heavy.

Something pale was on the second step at the waterline. She hauled it up. A
cloak, sodden, good cloth, road-dust under the wet.

Thirty feet out and moving upstream there was a wake — the ugly chop of a man
swimming badly and in a hurry. She got a head, and the pale smear of a face
turning back toward the house, and then the shoulders working.

He was making for the iron rungs under the far lamp. He had covered a third of
it. He had not seen her.

The range was nothing.

---

She did not hear it hit. She saw it. The head snapped sideways, hard, wrong, the
way a head goes when it has stopped being steered from inside.

He went under without a sound. There was one hand above the surface for a
moment, open, not grasping at anything.

Then that was gone too.

The chop smoothed out. The lamp on the far bank went on being a lamp.

Upstairs a woman began to scream and did not stop, and it was not Emmeralda's
voice.
