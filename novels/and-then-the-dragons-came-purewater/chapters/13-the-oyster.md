# Chapter 13 — The Oyster

Nerissa proclaimed it from the temple steps at the close of the Tourney, in
front of the Governor and every Guild in Purewater and six thousand people who
had come for the Single Combat and got something considerably better.

She named the desecration. She named the arrest. She named the fouling of the
sacred channel and the fact that she had rung the alarm and been told to stop,
and she looked directly at the Prince's chamberlain while she said that part.

Then she said the sentence she owed.

**"The canal folk kept the Lady's water clean."**

She said it slowly and let it sit. When the ground did not know what to do with
it she said it again, louder, and added *say it back to me* — and six thousand
people did.

Somewhere on the water a very forgettable man with a crow inked on his hand
heard the whole city say it.

---

Temerach told Conall in a small room off the south cloister, standing with her
hands at her sides like a woman giving a report, because it was the only
register she had and it turned out to be the right one.

Four things, and then he could do whatever he liked to her. That the Baron was
his father. That he had a twin brother somewhere in the city. That she had known
both since before she took him in at nineteen and had chosen every day not to
tell him. And that the man was arrested and would say it all out loud in front
of a magistrate tonight to buy himself an hour, so he was going to hear it
today whatever she did.

"I wanted it to be from me. That is not the same as it being right."

He was quick, so he did not get the easy reaction. He went straight past *you're
lying* and landed on all the small things at once.

*That's why you found me at nineteen. That's why we came to Purewater.*

He looked at the stranger in the room, the one with no stake.

"Is she telling the truth?"

Magda pulled the vestment off over her head and dropped it on a chair, which
answered the question of who was talking before she opened her mouth.

"Yeah. Course she is."

She did not dress it and did not tell him it would be all right.

"Go find your brother."

---

Marisette found him inside the hour, because Marisette had known the boy since
he was four.

They picked the Moist Oyster because Thorne was owed the true version, once, and
because it was the only room in Purewater where a Guildmaster, a thief, a
priestess and a wolf could all be in the same place and nobody would comment.

Constantine sent food she could not afford to send. Marisette sent wine nobody
would be able to afford again. Ravella came in through the water-door with four
of hers, which stopped the room for a moment. Ila was there because she had
threatened Constantine with a very specific kind of hell, and had attached
herself to Bird — who sat in the warmest corner in three cloaks, eating
steadily, saying almost nothing, with Gardwen on one side and a wolf across his
feet.

Nerissa did not come. She sent Lilura, and a note, and a cask.

Hesper came late from the school and said the two shallow girls were out and
sleeping, and did not say anything about the third.

And Thorne sat in his corner with his chalk, watching the door.

At about the ninth bell Asphodel came in with a young man behind her. Dark.
Twenty-two. Well-dressed in a way that was not his money.

He was halfway through saying something to her when he saw the man standing up
at the far table with his own face on him, and the sentence stopped.

The whole room stopped with it.

Neither of them said anything.

And Thorne said, very quietly, to nobody at all:

"*Oh*, that's good."
