# Chapter 4 — The Scullery

The demon sat up in the bed and asked for its shawl.

Emmeralda was opened from the hollow of her throat to her hip. One cut, drawn
slow with something barbed, and the edges were wrong and it was still bleeding
and nothing was holding it closed. The chalk figure on the boards had a scuff
through it at the foot where a man had stepped backwards out of it in a hurry.

She had her head tilted and she was smiling at the door.

"You needn't crowd," she said. "It doesn't hurt at all."

Gardwen had closed it with both hands and everything she had. The flesh had
knit. Then it had opened again from the inside, deliberately, like a hand
pulling back a curtain.

"I think it *liked* it," she said afterwards in the passage. "I think it wanted
to see what I'd do."

---

Magda went in and sat on the coverlet where the thing patted it.

"You feel ok, lass? What's all this though?"

It looked down at the wound the way you look at a stain on a good tablecloth,
and touched the edge with two fingers, and the flesh moved under them.

"That's the way in. He wasn't very neat about it, but he got there in the end,
bless him — he was *so* nervous, Magda. Shaking. I had to be kind to him."

The fingers came away red. It did not notice.

"He's coming back, you know. He said so. He was very definite about it." It
plucked at the coverlet, tidying. "So we should have the room straight. And
someone really ought to sweep up that chalk."

"Who is the young gentleman? So we know how tae greet him. On his return."

"Santo. Just Santo — he didn't give me the rest, they never do, they think it's
clever." A confiding lean, and the wound gaped with it. "He said he'd come back
for the others. Four more, he said, and then his father would *have* to see. He
was quite emotional about it."

Behind her Constantine had both hands flat on the doorframe and had gone the
colour of tallow.

"*Santo*," she said. "He said *Santo*."

---

The ring was sewn into the lining of the cloak. Heavy gold, a man's, with a
beast cut into the face: long, coiled round on itself, the tail through its own
jaws.

"That's their arms," Constantine said, and did not touch it. "The worm eating
itself. It's on the litter, it's on the banners, it's on every second cup in
this city this week."

There were eleven border coins, unchanged since he arrived. A tailor's mark
stitched inside the collar where the wearer never looks — a little tower with
three windows, embroidered rather than stamped. A letter gone to pulp in the
canal, dried against a lamp glass until two things survived: *—ee days before
the Lady's Tourney, at the hou—*, and the bottom half of a seal with the same
beast on it.

And five twists of oiled silk. Four of them full. Each held a hank of long dark
red hair bound at one end with fine copper wire.

Emmeralda's hair was black to the root.

---

Constantine's counting room, with the door shut.

"Santo di Teufel is the son and heir of Baron Hanzo di Teufel, who rode into
this city this morning with twenty-odd Dragon Knights at his back, and who is at
this moment the guest of half the men on the Governor's council."

She dragged a hand down her face.

"There is a dead di Teufel in the canal behind my house and a demon sitting up
in my green room asking for her shawl, and there are nineteen people under this
roof tonight."

"Twenty-two," Gardwen said from the corner. "There are three in the kitchen."

"Twenty-two." Constantine closed her eyes. "What do we do?"

---

They needed the body and the body was under nine feet of black water with the
ebb two hours off.

Gardwen went in on a line and came up thrashing eighteen inches from the
gunwale, not swimming, climbing at the water like it was a wall, and put the
skiff over on its beam ends. Magda got a fist in the line and the other in the
back of her shirt and hauled her aft where the freeboard was lowest and brought
her in over the transom like a netted fish. She lay in six inches of bilge and
coughed up canal and could not speak for a while.

"That," she said eventually, "is not a river."

---

So it was the girl.

Ila came down the slipway barefoot in her shift with somebody's shawl over it
and her face blotched from crying and lit up with purpose, and was in the boat
before anyone offered a hand.

"What am I looking for?"

Magda told her the truth, all of it, in about four sentences, and then said
*Gardwen, fuck, help me explain, I'm no good at this.*

Gardwen came forward on her knees so she was down at the child's level.

"When I was your age, men came to my house at night and killed my family. Every
one of them. And I hid, because I was small and there was nothing else I could
do, and I have spent ten years being the person who hid." She put the line into
Ila's hands. "Nobody's asking you to hide. There's a job, and you're the only
one in this boat who can do it, and it's a *filthy* job. You'll touch him. It'll
be over in a minute and you will not like any part of it, and then Emmeralda's
house stays standing."

Ila's fingers closed on the line.

"Two tugs if I've got him. Three if I need to come up."

She went in without a splash. The line paid out smooth and long and stopped.

Ten. Twenty.

Two tugs. Firm. Then two more, and the line went dead-weight heavy.

She surfaced with her arms round it and her face at the sky.

"His face is *up*," she said.

---

Afterwards Magda took the wet shawl off her and put her own cloak on her
instead, which swamped her entirely and smelled of three weeks of road and pig,
and rubbed her arms hard enough to hurt.

"You went into black water on the word of an old woman you met this morning,
and you found what you were sent for, and you came back up and you said the
right thing first. His face is up. That's the whole job."

"I touched his—"

"Aye. You did. That'll come back on you tonight and probably tomorrow and then
less after that." She pulled the cloak closed under the girl's chin and held it
there. "It doesn't mean anything's wrong with you. It means you did a hard
thing. There's a difference, and nobody tells you, so I'm telling you."

The girl folded forward against her and cried for about a minute with her fists
in Magda's shirt.

---

The scullery had stone to waist height, a slate table, a drain in the corner and
a shuttered window onto the canal. Constantine lit two lamps, set out the saw
and the cleaver and the bone-tub, said *I'll be in the counting room*, and went.

She did not come back.

They worked. It was not fast and it was not hard. You find the joint and you go
through the joint and you do not fight the bone. Gardwen held what needed
holding and turned what needed turning and did not flinch once.

The drain did its work. The canal took the rest.

"We killed one of the enemy tonight, girl. That's a good start."

Gardwen stopped with her hands on the table.

"He's the right age to be one of their sons. Not one of the men." She turned a
piece over. "Ten years ago he'd have been twelve. Sat at somebody's table while
it happened. But he grew up into exactly what they wanted him to grow into, and
he did *that* to Emmeralda with four more women's hair in his cloak, and I'd
have put the stone through his head myself."

She wiped her wrist across her forehead and left a mark there she did not know
about.

"It's a start. It's not the one I came for."

Four barrels. Constantine's own, with her own house-mark burned in the ends.

She walked them down to the hog-yard herself at the fifth bell, because it was
not the sort of thing you send a boy with.
