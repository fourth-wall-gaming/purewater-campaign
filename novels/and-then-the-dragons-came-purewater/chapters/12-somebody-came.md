# Chapter 12 — Somebody Came

The tide was right. Nerissa looked at the channel coming down the temple stair
and said *it's thin*, in the voice of a woman reporting a fact she wished she
could not feel.

It took forty minutes to get everything to Lost Isle. The boy, carried, awake
and frightened, with Gardwen's hand in his the whole way. Ruhi in the bottom of
the boat. Hesper with a box inside a box. Nerissa, who had left her own Tourney
running behind her without a backward glance. Twelve Swords. Ravella, and four
of hers in the water alongside, which no boatman in that city had ever had
happen to them.

The drowned street. The swept stair. The bones on the stone with the wire cut
off the wrists and a borrowed axe laid across them.

Hesper opened the outer box and then, with a great deal of care, the inner one.

The thing inside it moved.

---

They put the boy down at the top of the stair and Gardwen held him, and Thuban
Eta came up into his face for what everyone there understood was the last time.

He looked at the bones. Then at the locket. Then at Magda.

"You came back," he said.

"Aye."

"Then I am ready, and I have been ready for a thousand years, and I would like
you to be quick — because he is very tired and I have taken enough of his life."

---

She did it exactly as she had done it before, because it had been right before.

She said the name first, out loud, so everyone on that rock had to hear it.
**Thuban Eta.**

She told the tale in a market-day voice — the valley he would not burn, the
argument he won in front of men who killed him for it, the boat in the dark, the
wire on the wrists, the water. She named the wrong so it was in the air where it
could be heard: *this was murder, and it was cowardice, and no one ever answered
for it.*

Then she went two steps down into the black water in a vestment she had no right
to, up to her knees, and stood exactly where nine men had stood over a bound
knight a thousand years before and done nothing.

She did not look away.

"You went in here and nobody came. I came. You're not theirs and you're not his
and you were never Valhalla's, because they cheated you of the death that buys
it.

"You're hers. You've always been hers. And she takes the ones nobody sings about
— but only when somebody finally brings them.

"Mother of the darkness. This is Thuban Eta and I have brought him. He's yours.
Take him home."

---

The locket opened on the stone by itself. Not dramatically. It came apart the
way a shell does when whatever was holding it shut has stopped bothering.

And the water went warm.

It came up past her knees like a draught from an open door in a cold house, and
every lamp on that island bent toward it at once, and under the surface
something enormous and patient turned over and paid attention.

On the stone the boy arched once against Gardwen's arms and made a sound that
was two voices and then one voice and then no voice at all.

Something went down that stair.

She did not see it. She felt it go past her in the water, unhurried, the way a
big fish goes past your legs in a river. And there was one moment, exactly one,
of being *looked at* by something that was grateful and was not going to say so,
because it was a soldier.

Then it was gone. The bones settled very slightly, the way bones do.

The water was just water.

---

The boy was limp in his sister's arms with his eyes open. Breathing.

"...it's quiet," he said.

---

Magda came up out of the water and her legs did not work properly and she did
not much care. She got to them on her knees on the wet stone and put her arms
round both of them at once and held on.

Then she came apart.

It was the ugly kind, from the chest, the kind that hurts your throat. A great
old woman in a stolen vestment on a rock in a drowned street with her face in a
druid's hair and one hand knotted in a boy's shirt.

Gardwen had hold of her arm with her free hand and did not let go of it. The boy
did not understand who she was at all. He put his head against her anyway,
because she was warm and there was nothing left in him to do anything else with.
Ruhi shoved her whole head under Magda's elbow and stayed there.

Nerissa turned her back — not away from her; she turned and faced the others and
made them stop looking, which was the kindest thing she did all week.

Hesper sat down heavily on the step with the empty locket in his hands and
stared at nothing.

Ravella crouched at the edge of the stair and said something down into the water
that was not in any language anyone there knew, and something answered, and this
time everybody heard it.

---

It took a while.

When Magda got her breath the light had moved on the water and the boy was
asleep against Gardwen with his mouth open, and Gardwen had not moved a muscle
in case it woke him.

She looked at Magda over the top of his head. Her face was wrecked and about ten
years younger.

"Magda. I don't know his name." A pause. "He was seven. We called him Bird."

"Call him Bird then. It'd be a lot better than anything those bastards called
him." Magda wiped her face with the back of her wrist. "And he'll pick his own
when he's ready. That's how it works. You get given one, and then one day you
take one. Nobody's given him anything since he was seven."

Gardwen nodded and did not trust herself to say anything, and put her cheek back
down on the top of his head.
