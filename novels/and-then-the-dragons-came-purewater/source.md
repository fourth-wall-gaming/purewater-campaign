# Source Material — And Then the Dragons Came: Purewater

## Dramatis Personae

### Conall Bjornlasch <!-- id: myth-char-06f87a379bbf -->

Arcane-casting magical investigator of the Order of the Blue Star; twin brother of Randall and secretly a son of Baron Hanzo. A razor-sharp investigator (Insight 88) who is also a capable fighter.

A magical investigator and arcane caster trained by the Order of the
Blue Star, with a soldier's martial skill (all weapons, all shields). Sharp-eyed and
analytical -- Insight 88.

He rides into Purewater beside Temerach Nebulo for the Lake Lady's Tourney, an
errand she has declined to explain. He knows the city not at all, has never heard of
a brother, and has no idea whose son he is.

**GM:** he is Randall's identical twin and a biological son of Baron Hanzo. Temerach
has known for years and brought him here deliberately -- see the gm-secret lore. He
learns none of this until play delivers it.

### Gardwen <!-- id: myth-char-5c39daf64e36 -->

Feral-raised elf druid and lone survivor of a Dragon Knight raid; a rank-1 divine caster of nature and pure-water magic who walks with a befriended she-wolf, searching for her long-lost brother.

A feral-raised elf druid and the sole survivor of a Dragon Knight raid that killed her family roughly ten years ago. Gardwen channels the magic of nature and pure water and travels with a befriended she-wolf, Ruhi. She is searching for her brother, taken during the raid as a child -- now, unknown to her, the possessed warrior serving Baron Hanzo di Teufel. Dark hair, blue eyes; soft-spoken but fierce in defense of kin and the wild. She journeys with the old warrior-protector Magda (an emissary of the death goddess Hel) toward Zorastor, following the Dragon Knights.

### Magda <!-- id: myth-char-d8b643d61817 -->

Aged barbarian berserker and sworn servant of the death-goddess Hel; Blessed by Hel (she does not age), she guards Gardwen and the lost twins and fights with berserk fury despite her years.

An old barbarian warrior-woman and sworn servant of Hel, goddess of death, called from a farmer's life by prophetic dreams. Though aged, she fights with berserker fury and is Blessed by Hel -- she does not age naturally. She follows Hel's directive to protect Gardwen and the lost twins, swearing 'by Hel herself' to see the quest through. Curmudgeonly and blunt, with a deep distrust and fear of arcane magic.

### Randall <!-- id: myth-char-5aa2aad55f3b -->

Street thief of Purewater, raised in a brothel on the Pearl; identical twin of Conall and secretly a son of Baron Hanzo. A nimble, silver-tongued rogue (Deceit 75, Stealth 72, Sleight 71).

A street thief from Purewater, raised in a brothel on the Pearl and never knowing his parents. Quick, charming and light-fingered, Randall works the canals and alleys with lockpicks, disguises, a grappling hook and a fistful of throwing daggers. He is the identical twin of Conall and, unknown to him, a biological son of Baron Hanzo; his uncanny resemblance to the Baron's son Santo is both an opportunity and a danger.

### Asphodel <!-- id: myth-char-4b75598941c7 -->

A courtesan at the Promise of Heaven, the house where Randall grew up. Warm, quick, and trusted by him 'as brother and sister' -- his closest confidante on the Pearl Quay and a ready source of information from the working girls.

Asphodel works at the Promise of Heaven, the Pearl house where Randall was raised among its women. The two have known each other for years and trust each other like siblings; she was the one who got word to him that he'd been blamed for Emmeralda's carving, and never believed it for a moment. No fighter and no thief -- her gifts are people: she reads them, charms them, and hears everything that passes through the house. It is Asphodel who gathers intelligence on the Baron's manor from the girls sent up to entertain his soldiers (calling in the older, canny Mariarta to brief Randall directly). Affectionate, shrewd, and quietly brave, she helps Randall because she loves him, not for coin.

### Blau <!-- id: myth-char-dbb5fdad535d -->

Baron Hanzo's albino lieutenant -- known to the ranks as Commander Vask. A thin, red-eyed, burn-scarred killer with unnatural strength who runs the Dragon Knights' day-to-day command and handles the Baron's 'dirty work': a cold, efficient assassin and intimidator.

Blau is the Baron's lieutenant and enforcer -- the albino the rank-and-file know as Commander Vask, who handles both the Dragon Knights' day-to-day command and the dirty work. An albino with red eyes, a raspy voice, and burn scars across much of his thin frame (which belies an unnatural, wiry strength), he is cold, calculating and cruel: a quiet, efficient killer who keeps the order running with ruthless discipline. Publicly he is 'utterly loyal' to Baron Hanzo. (His secret) he studies the Baron's operation and techniques closely, planning to one day break away and serve no one but himself.

### Cailan <!-- id: myth-char-cac22cc2cbf6 -->

Gardwen's possessed brother (birth name Gabriel) -- the Baron's tournament champion, host to the ancient dragon-knight spirit Thuban Eta. Dragon-scale armor fused to his skin; eyes flicker reptilian when possessed; fights with uncanny ancient skill and minor fire.

Kidnapped as a child a decade ago, Gabriel was subjected to Baron Hanzo's possession ritual and bound to Thuban Eta, an ancient dragon-knight spirit. Now called Cailan, he is the Baron's unbeaten tournament champion: unnatural precision with bow and spear, ancient fighting techniques drawn from Thuban's memories, and minor fire abilities channeled through the binding locket. Years of shared consciousness have fractured his mind -- moments of clarity surface between possessions, and Thuban himself sometimes fights the Baron's will. Freeing him is the emotional core of his sister Gardwen's quest.

### Constantine <!-- id: myth-char-49ee20e7a554 -->

Proprietor of The Sylph's Embrace on the Pearl -- shrewd, protective of her workers, fiercely independent. Operates under Marisette's authority. After Santo's attack on Emmeralda in her house, she is hiring protection and wants justice.

Constantine runs The Sylph's Embrace, a high-end pleasure house on the Pearl, under the overall authority of the madame Marisette. Sharp, businesslike and fiercely protective of her workers, she prizes loyalty and codes of conduct. Her establishment was the scene of Santo di Teufel's brutal ritual attack on the courtesan Emmeralda, and in the aftermath she is quietly hiring protection and seeking anyone who can deliver justice. No warrior, but no fool either: she keeps a stiletto and a loaded hand crossbow close, and reads a room well.

### Crowbill <!-- id: myth-char-e303e68fea08 -->

Leader of Purewater's criminal underworld -- canal smuggling, gambling, protection. Wiry and forgettable but for a small crow tattoo on his right hand. A master of streets and shadows (Streetwise 88, Stealth 82, Conceal 78) who keeps to a code: no killing locals, no harming children, no touching the Lake Lady's sites.

Crowbill runs Purewater's criminal underworld -- canal smuggling, gambling dens, protection rackets -- and does it so quietly that nothing about him stands out except a small crow tattoo on the back of his right hand. He keeps to established codes: no murder of locals, no harm to children, no disruption of the Lake Lady's sacred sites. His people signal with crow marks, he knows hidden boat paths through the canals and keeps safe houses across the city. He was happy enough to profit from the Baron's arrival at first, but the Dragon Knights' brutality is wrecking the equilibrium his business depends on -- and that is steadily turning a wary criminal into a possible ally.

### Emmeralda <!-- id: myth-char-b713577f61eb -->

Elven courtesan of The Sylph's Embrace -- poised, magnetic, and the finest performer on the Pearl (CHA 18, Seduction 88, Dance 85).

Emmeralda is an elven courtesan at The Sylph's Embrace and the most
sought-after performer on the Pearl: a poised, magnetic presence (Dance 85, Sing 80,
Seduction 88) with a following among people who can afford her.

At the campaign's opening she is entirely well, and has an evening booked with a
young nobleman who has paid generously and asked for privacy.

**GM -- what she becomes.** She is Santo's intended vessel. If the binding happens
and she survives it, a lesser pleasure demon stays partly lodged in her: strange
behaviours, bestial voices, and asking after 'Lord Santo.' That makes her the
clearest living evidence against him, and -- because an interrupted binding is a
unique case -- the one subject from which a counter-ritual might be devised. Whether
she lives at all depends on who reaches her in the first minutes. None of this is
true yet.

### Guildmaster Torval Bluehand <!-- id: myth-char-46bd3d314355 -->

Guildmaster of the Watercrafters -- middle-aged, practical innovator whose hands are permanently blue-tinged from decades of watercraft enchantment. A Magic-User specialized in water artifice (Craft 85, Arcane Casting 60, Mechanisms 80) rather than battle magic; his guild's enchantments keep the canal-city running.

Torval Bluehand leads the Guild of Watercrafters, and his trade has marked him: decades of working watercraft enchantments have stained his hands a permanent blue. He is practical and forward-thinking, valuing innovation over tradition -- the kind of artificer-mage who enchants pumps, locks, gondolas and aqueducts rather than hurling fire. His arcane skill is real but applied: Craft and Mechanisms in the 80s, water-aspected Arcane Casting around 60. The Guild is allied with the Order of the Lake Lady and quietly essential to Purewater itself, since the whole canal-city depends on the watercraft his people keep enchanted and running.

### Hanzo di Teufel <!-- id: myth-char-cee57a7f5de2 -->

Baron Hanzo di Teufel -- the campaign's master villain: a charismatic borderland noble and fire-wielding arcane sorcerer/demonologist who commands the Dragon Knights, runs possession rituals, and secretly seeks to become a Dragon King.

Baron Hanzo di Teufel is a rising political power in the borderlands and the true 'power behind the throne' -- urbane, magnetic and utterly ruthless (Deceit 93, Influence 92, Willpower 94). A master arcane sorcerer steeped in fire magic and demonology, he binds spirits into living flesh and is engineering a possession scheme to win the Lake Lady's Tourney; his ultimate aim is dragon transformation -- to rise as a Dragon King. He commands the Dragon Knights ('Dragonshits' to the locals) and arrives on the steam-wreathed DragonBarge. He is the father, by an elven mage, of the twins Randall and Conall, and of his cruel heir Santo.

### Harbormaster Velen Deepkeel <!-- id: myth-char-d648a23d9c77 -->

Stern, weathered Harbormaster with forty years at the docks -- meticulous, traditional, publicly incorruptible. A consummate seaman and administrator (Seamanship 85, Navigation 82, Maritime Law 80) who controls the harbor's lawful face while quietly tolerating Crowbill's smuggling.

Harbormaster Velen Deepkeel rose through forty years of service to command the Harbor Masters, the body that governs Purewater's docks and shipping. Weathered and stern, he is meticulous and traditional, resistant to change or corruption -- at least in his public persona. In private the arrangement is murkier: he officially opposes Crowbill's smuggling while secretly facilitating it for the right price. He keeps strong ties to the Governor, the council and the wealthy merchants who live by shipping, holds the Dredgers in open contempt, and watches the Baron warily, weighing the threat against the opportunity. A capable old sailor under the ledgers and lawbooks, he still knows the cutlass.

### Hesper <!-- id: myth-char-156265245397 -->

To Purewater, Hesper is the madame Marisette's cold, dangerous mage-enforcer -- quiet, watchful, able to drop a strong man with a single spoken word. The truth is the opposite of what the mask suggests: Hesper is a deep-cover agent of the Quiet Hand, a hidden fellowship that works unseen for the good of the world, and the menace is a costume worn over a conscience. Master arcane caster (Arcane Casting 100, Willpower 95).

Hesper moves through Purewater like cold air through a door, and everyone who feels it assumes the worst -- which is the point. For years Hesper has played the part of Marisette's arcane enforcer, the perfect cover for a listening post at the heart of the Pearl's secrets. In truth Hesper is a 'finger' of the Quiet Hand, the secret fellowship that stops great evils before they ripen by knowing of them first and acting unseen, and has been watching Baron Hanzo since before he ever reached the city. The seizing of Conall and Randall into the catacombs looked like cruelty; it was the Hand taking control of a dangerous night, learning the truth of the soul-knife, and keeping it -- and the twins -- out of worse hands. Hesper kills rarely and reluctantly, never for convenience, and will not be moved by gold or threat; behind the predator's stillness is an old, patient kindness that gives nothing away.

### High Priestess Nerissa <!-- id: myth-char-1f1bcc8ed954 -->

High Priestess of the Temple of the Lake Lady -- middle-aged, silver-streaked hair, blue-green robes that ripple like water. A powerful divine caster (Piety 92, Willpower 85) who senses water corruption, blesses sacred waters, and speaks with water spirits. Temple law forbids her from intervening directly, so she works through the PCs.

Nerissa is High Priestess at the Temple of the Lake Lady, a serene, formidable woman with silver-streaked dark hair and blue-green robes that seem to ripple like water as she moves. She senses corruption within sacred waters, performs water-based blessing rituals, and communicates with the Lake Lady's water spirits. Since the Baron's arrival she has been tracking a steady rise in that corruption -- but temple law forbids her from intervening directly unless the sacred sites themselves are threatened. So she becomes a patron: supplying spiritual support, ritual knowledge, and the water-magic countermeasures the players will need, while relying on them to act where she cannot.

### Ila <!-- id: myth-char-2cabe8ce1bf4 -->

A young elven girl (about thirteen) at the Sylph's Embrace, devoted to Emmeralda. An innocent of the Pearl, and a thread on the party's conscience.

Ila is one of the youngest at the Sylph's Embrace -- an elven girl of
perhaps thirteen, more ward than worker, who has attached herself to the courtesan
Emmeralda and follows her about the house like a duckling.

No fighter and no schemer: a bright, frightened, brave child in a dangerous quarter.

**GM.** She is the small, immediate measure of what the di Teufels cost people who
have no part in any of it. If Emmeralda is hurt, Ila is the one who comes running
for help and the one who will not leave her side afterwards -- and whoever helps
will have made a friend for life on the Pearl.

### Lilura Deepcurrent <!-- id: myth-char-0299f408c181 -->

Elderly head of the Order of the Lake Lady for three decades -- piercing blue eyes, rumored merfolk ancestry (she holds her breath unnaturally long). The city's most powerful divine caster (Piety 95, Willpower 92), guiding the Order's water-faith against the Baron's fire demonologists.

Lilura Deepcurrent has led the Order of the Lake Lady for thirty years: an elderly woman with piercing blue eyes whose unnatural breath-holding feeds rumors of merfolk ancestry. She is the senior religious authority of Purewater's water-faith -- cordial with the Governor and council despite tensions over water management, allied with the Watercrafters Guild, on arranged terms with Ravella, and implacably opposed to the Baron's fire demonologists who pollute the sacred waters. Where Nerissa leads the Temple day to day, Lilura sits above the whole Order, the deepest well of divine water-magic and theological authority in the city -- and one of the few figures with the standing to move openly against the Baron.

### Marda Blackwater <!-- id: myth-char-7b078f2349da -->

Broad-shouldered leader of The Dredgers, risen from the dredge-boats during a labor dispute five years ago. A powerful working-class champion (Brawn 75, Oratory 78, Influence 75) who rallies dock labor against the merchant princes and -- above all -- against the Baron's Dragon Knights.

Marda Blackwater leads The Dredgers, Purewater's organization of dock workers and canal laborers. A broad-shouldered woman in her forties, she began as a dredger herself and rose to prominence during a bitter labor dispute five years ago; she is practical, passionate, and utterly committed to improving conditions for the city's working class. She is no trained soldier but a formidable physical presence and a galvanizing speaker (Oratory 78), able to turn out the docks at a word. She'll work with the progressive wing of the Order of the Lake Lady and with ethical merchants, keeps a wary distance from Crowbill, and reserves real hatred for the merchant princes, the Governor, and the Baron's Dragon Knights -- to her, the purest face of aristocratic oppression.

### Mariarta <!-- id: myth-char-e2628befaaee -->

An older, canny courtesan of the Promise of Heaven with 'the calm intelligence of someone who can survive almost any situation involving men.' Sent up to entertain the Baron's soldiers, she brings back the hard intelligence on his guarded quarters -- Randall's most valuable inside source on the Dragon Knights' manor.

Mariarta (the novelization's Moriata) is one of the older women of the Promise of Heaven, calm and shrewd, long practiced at reading men and outlasting trouble. When the Baron's soldiers sent for company, she was among the girls who went up to the fortified manor -- and she came back with exactly what Randall needed: the Baron never left his quarters, no one was permitted near them, the room sat second-storey on the eastern side, and the guards were too disciplined to trifle with. She'll keep gathering for him, and ask only that he find something in it for the girls. No fighter -- her weapons are patience, charm, and a clear eye.

### Marisette <!-- id: myth-char-28320448049e -->

The madame with overall authority over Purewater's Pearl -- the proprietors, Constantine among them, answer to her. A consummate social power broker (Influence 85, Insight 82, Commerce 82) who keeps the pleasure district running and resents the disorder the Baron and Santo have brought to it.

Marisette is the madame who holds sway over the whole Pearl: the individual house-proprietors, Constantine included, operate under her overall authority. She is no fighter, but in her own arena she is dangerous -- a master of people, money and information who keeps the pleasure district's delicate order intact and skims its profits. Santo di Teufel's ritual attack on Emmeralda in one of her houses, and the Dragon Knights' growing menace, are precisely the kind of disorder she cannot abide, which can make her a useful, self-interested ally to anyone working against the Baron.

### Murgeroch <!-- id: myth-char-2e299ed60112 -->

Temerach Nebulo's squire and go-between -- the steady, competent young fighter who carries the knight's messages, screens her meetings, and tends her gear. Usually the first of Temerach's people the party deals with.

Murgeroch is squire to the elven knight Temerach Nebulo. Where Temerach moves cloaked and unseen, Murgeroch is her visible hand: he sets the meeting places, signals the party to follow, minds the horses, and keeps a soldier's eye on the road for trouble. Loyal and disciplined, trained in the way of the Swords of the Lake without yet being one, he is a reliable secondary contact and a capable blade if the knight's business turns violent.

### Nus <!-- id: myth-char-df4b5d854459 -->

A quick-fingered Purewater street thief and loyal friend of Randall -- lookout, fence, and second pair of hands for a job; nimble, streetwise, and easy to underestimate.

Nus runs the same canals and rooftops as Randall and counts him a true friend -- the pair grew up working the alleys of Purewater together. Wiry and quick, with a fence's eye for value and a lookout's nose for trouble, Nus is the one Randall calls when a job needs a second set of hands or a fast way out. Loyal to his mates and slippery with everyone else.

### Prince Emeric <!-- id: myth-char-eb79260a2a9b -->

The young Governor of Purewater -- the King's nephew, perhaps two-and-twenty: clever, idealistic, genuinely decent, and entirely unsuited to govern a city like this. He wants to reform Purewater; the factions humor him, manage him, and run rings around his good intentions.

Prince Emeric was sent to Purewater by his uncle, King Halvern, to govern the city -- a prestigious first posting for a promising young royal, and (his relatives assumed) a safe one. It was a misjudgment. Emeric is bookish and high-minded, schooled in law, history, and the philosophy of just rule, and he arrived burning to clean up 'Pisswater': to curb the smuggling, protect the working poor, and restore the dignity of the Lake Lady's Tourney. But he has no instinct for the knife-in-the-dark politics of a city run by madames, brokers, crime lords, and corrupt guilds. He trusts the wrong people, signs the documents put in front of him, and mistakes flattery for agreement. The Harbor Masters and merchant princes steer him; Marisette and Ravella read him like a broadsheet; and Baron Hanzo cultivates him charmingly as a 'visiting dignitary,' the better to operate under royal cover. For the party, Emeric is opportunity and hazard both: an honest man at the top who could open real doors -- if they can reach him past his handlers, and if telling him the truth doesn't simply get him killed.

### Ravella <!-- id: myth-char-77eeb2d15196 -->

Half-merfolk proprietor of The Siren's Call -- pleasure house and information market in one. Subtle waterfolk tells (webbed fingers, water-reflecting eyes, Swim 90). A master broker (Insight 85, Streetwise 88, Commerce 80) with light innate water magic, secretly protecting the city's hidden waterfolk.

Ravella runs The Siren's Call, where high-end pleasure and the city's information trade intersect. She keeps her half-merfolk heritage mostly secret -- faint scales at the hairline, slightly webbed fingers, eyes that reflect water, and an uncanny ability to hold her breath -- because pure merfolk society ostracizes mixed blood. She has turned that exclusion into power, building an information empire (gondoliers, washerwomen, dockhands) to shield the waterfolk who live hidden among humans. Behind a veneer of respectability she reads people effortlessly (Insight 85), trades secrets (Streetwise 88), and carries a light innate gift of water-themed Folk Magic. She can sense threats to water magic itself, and as the Baron's fire-corruption spreads she is torn between profit and protecting everything the Lake Lady's waters represent.

### Santo di Teufel <!-- id: myth-char-1ebc75c92e6a -->

Baron Hanzo's cruel son and heir -- a sadistic young fire-mage and demonology dabbler, carrying a stolen ritual knife marked with the sigil of Asmodeus.

Santo di Teufel is Baron Hanzo's cruel, entitled heir, trained in his
father's dark arts and trusted with none of them. A junior fire-mage with a taste
for cruelty (Lust 80, Arrogance & Entitlement 75), he is charming and predatory in
equal measure (Seduction 81, Deceit 66).

He arrives in Purewater with the column, and with a barbed ritual knife bearing the
sigil of Asmodeus that his father does not know he has taken. He means to work a
demonic spirit-binding of his own and present it as proof that the blood runs true
in him -- and he is nowhere near skilled enough to do it safely.

**GM:** his agenda is *Bind a demon, and be his father's son*. Left alone he will
choose a victim who cannot fight back, botch the ritual, and flee leaving the knife
behind -- the evidence that ties the di Teufels to the possession scheme. Everything
after that depends on who was in the house.

### Sinnit <!-- id: myth-char-33c0da8d53fe -->

A brutish Purewater canal-rat and rival thief who nurses a hard grudge against Randall -- a brawling enforcer-type with a long memory and a short temper.

Sinnit works the same Purewater underworld as Randall, but where Randall is quick and charming, Sinnit is heavy-handed and mean. A canal-rat brawler and small-time crew muscle, he blames Randall for an old double-cross -- a job gone wrong, a cut he never got, a humiliation he never forgot -- and he has been looking to settle the score ever since. He hits hard, fights dirty, and holds a grudge like a creditor.

### Tat Atarer <!-- id: myth-char-fff1e79e219c -->

Captain of the Dragon Knights -- a hardened, pragmatic mercenary commander who serves Baron Hanzo for pay, not ideology. Master fighter (combat 97%); 'the Baron pays for our swords, not our opinions.'

Tat Atarer is the military commander of Baron Hanzo's Dragon Knights -- a former mercenary captain, professional and disciplined, motivated by money and security rather than any ideological commitment to the Baron's dark schemes. A peerless veteran fighter (combat style 97%, Unarmed 87, resistances at 80), he leads from the front and keeps his men in line. His creed: 'The Baron pays for our swords, not our opinions.'

### Temerach Nebulo <!-- id: myth-char-a34f0237a753 -->

Elf knight of the Swords of the Lake, called 'the Lakelady's Javelin'; a deadly archer and peerless martial fighter tracking Baron Hanzo to expose his crimes.

An elven knight of the elite order of the Swords of the Lake, known as 'the Lakelady's Javelin' for her uncanny skill with the bow. Disciplined, sharp and lethally precise, Temerach has been tracking Baron Hanzo for some time and carries knowledge of his past crimes; she means to expose and stop his threat to Mystamyr. A consummate warrior in full plate, she fights with longsword, rapier and kite shield -- but it is her marksmanship that has made her name.

### Thorne <!-- id: myth-char-e828864471f6 -->

Elderly storyteller of The Moist Oyster -- frail-seeming but with penetrating blue eyes and a cane that hides a blade. Secretly a former Order of the Lake initiate and Dragon-Knight-raid survivor with peerless historical lore (Dragon Kings era 92) and knowledge of possession-ritual weaknesses.

Most evenings Thorne holds court at The Moist Oyster, collecting tales and gossip from new arrivals. He looks frail, but his blue eyes are too vibrant for his age and his cane conceals a blade. He is a survivor: a former member of the Order of the Lake who lived through the early Dragon Knight raids. His value to the players is knowledge -- he recognizes Baron Hanzo's rituals as attempts to recreate ancient draconic transformations, he knows where possession bindings are vulnerable, and he can supply the historical context of the Dragon Kings era that the whole campaign turns on. Behind the storyteller's warmth sits a sharp, wary mind and an extensive web of contacts.

### Thuban Eta <!-- id: myth-char-a46cf24af084 -->

The ancient dragon-knight spirit bound inside Cailan. A betrayed tactical genius from the Dragon Kings era, held in thrall by Baron Hanzo's ritual through his bones and the binding locket. Indomitable will (Willpower 95), peerless tactics, honors the old codes -- a potential ally if freed.

Thuban Eta was a dragon-knight and war-leader of the Dragon Kings era, betrayed by treacherous comrades and slain. Baron Hanzo's ritual dragged his spirit back and bound it into the body of the kidnapped boy Gabriel ('Cailan'). He is a reluctant prisoner: he still feels the torment of his violent death and betrayal, yet honors the old codes, and occasionally wrenches against the Baron's will -- those struggles surface as Cailan's lucid moments. His own being is defined by towering INT, POW and Willpower (the will the Baron must keep crushing); physically he acts through his host. His remains and the binding locket are the leash. Free him, and the betrayed knight may repay the debt.

Ruhi is Gardwen's befriended companion, a she-wolf who pads at the druid's side through canal-town and wildwood alike. Fast (Move 8) and tireless, with a hunter's nose and ear (Track 84, Perception 74), she fights with a pack-savage bite and guards Gardwen with single-minded loyalty.

## Locations

### Caravan Square <!-- id: myth-loc-b46458cc59e8 -->

The landward gate of Purewater: a larger outcrop at the tip of the thin western promontory, where the borderland road meets the city. The Gate of Triumph, the caravan staging-yard, and the first crush of arrivals before the canals begin.

Purewater's only **land approach**. From the western mainland a thin **promontory**
reaches eastward and swells, at its tip, into a larger outcrop: **Caravan Square**,
where the long borderland road finally meets the city and the **Gate of Triumph**
opens onto the first bridges and quays. Here caravans unload, travellers and
competitors for the tourney throng in, beasts are penned and goods are staged before
everything must take to boats. It is the southern jaw of the Mouth, looking across
the landward channel to [[High Isle]]'s south-west point and the [[The Merchant's
Quarter]] beyond.

It was in the crush of Caravan Square that [[Randall]] first marked the disguised
[[Baron Hanzo di Teufel]] coming into the city -- and was taken for "Lord Santo" by
the Baron's guard.

### High Isle <!-- id: myth-loc-85a0d71a0693 -->

The great triangular island in the Mouth, splitting the Lake's outflow. The Grand Canal bisects and branches across it, dividing the quarters: Merchant's (SW), the official & noble quarter (NE), Forge & industry (E), and the long residential northern edge sloping rich-to-poor toward the Lullwater.

The heart of Purewater: a large, irregular, roughly **triangular** island wedged in
the Mouth, splitting the sacred Lake's outflow around it. Its three points anchor
the city -- the **south-west** point reaching toward [[Caravan Square]] across the
landward channel, the **east** point toward [[The Shoals]] across the seaward
channel, and the **north-west** point facing the open Lake.

The **Grand Canal** does not ring the isle so much as **bisect and branch across
it**, a web of broad cut channels that carve the island into natural quarters and
carry barge-traffic to every door:

- **The [[The Merchant's Quarter]]** -- the **south-west** corner, by the landward
  channel and the caravan trade: counting-houses, guild halls, the Watercrafters,
  the great market plaza, and the palazzi of the merchant princes who sit on the
  city council.
- **The official & noble quarter** -- the **north-east**, the prestigious frontage
  facing the Lake and [[Temple Isle]]: the **Governor's Palazzo** (seat of
  [[Prince Emeric]]), the **Hall of Justice**, the **Barracks**, and **Palace
  Square**, ringed by the houses of the nobility and the city's wealthiest (though,
  in truth, few of the realm's *truly* powerful keep more than a token residence
  here).
- **The [[The Forge Quarter]] & the industrial yards** -- the **east** point:
  forges, foundries, tanneries and boatyards trailing smoke, the gritty working
  belt that spills naturally eastward into [[The Shoals]].
- **The residential northern edge** -- broad avenues run **east-west** across the
  isle, from the Merchant's Quarter to the forges. Along the **northern edge**,
  moving south-east, the houses slope steadily from **rich to middling** until they
  reach a canal-mouth opening to the Lake -- and **across that canal lies [[The
  Lullwater]]**, the crowded poor-and-criminal quarter.

The Baron's **[[The DragonBarge]]** lies at a private pier on the isle's **eastern,
seaward side**, steam rising where its hull meets the water. The **Tournament
Grounds** -- archery ranges firing across open water, the combat lists, and the
start of the Harbor Run -- occupy the open ground between Palace Square and the
quays, the stage for the campaign's climax.

### Lost Isle <!-- id: myth-loc-69e2657b23c6 -->

A half-drowned ruin among the Northern Isles -- collapsed and weed-choked buildings rising from the Lake, deserted and shunned. The Mermaid's Court keeps hidden chambers somewhere beneath these waters.

One of [[The Northern Isles]]: a half-sunken district of drowned, abandoned buildings
and ancient ruins where the Lake has flowed undisturbed for generations, weed-choked
and silent. Animists hold that powerful spirits have taken up residence here, which
makes it both perilous and prized by spirit-binders -- and somewhere beneath these
waters lie the hidden chambers of [[The Mermaid's Court]]. Mysterious, dangerous,
and largely unexplored; the boatmen give it a wide berth.

### Purewater <!-- id: myth-loc-93c75b267cb2 -->

A canal city built across the Mouth -- the narrow strait where the sacred Lake (north) drains to the open Sea (south). Two land-jaws and the great triangular High Isle split the holy outflow; a scatter of lake-islands lies to the north. Locals call it 'Pisswater'.

Purewater is built across **the Mouth**: the narrow choke-point where the sacred
**Lake** to the north pours its holy waters south into the **open Sea**. It is the
gateway between inland lake-trade and the ocean routes -- rich, strategic, and
rotten. Locals call it **"Pisswater"** for the pollution and the corruption alike.

**The shape of the place** (north = the Lake; south = the Sea; west = the mainland):

- From the **western** mainland, a thin **promontory** reaches eastward and ends in
  a larger outcrop -- **[[Caravan Square]]** and the **Gate of Triumph**, the city's
  only land approach. This is the southern jaw of the Mouth.
- A second **peninsula**, set a little to the north and east, reaches in to form the
  seaward jaw. On it sit **[[The Shoals]]** -- the working docks, fish-markets and
  smugglers' jetties facing the open sea.
- Between the two jaws sits **[[High Isle]]**, a great irregular triangular island
  that splits the Lake's outflow. Its south-west point reaches toward Caravan Square
  across a calmer **landward channel**; its east point reaches toward the Shoals
  across the **seaward channel** (the true Mouth); its north-west point faces the
  open Lake. The waters part around the isle and rejoin to the south.
- North of High Isle, scattered across the Lake's calmer waters, lie **[[The Northern
  Isles]]** -- among them the bright pleasure-island of **[[The Pearl]]**, the
  half-drowned ruins of **[[Lost Isle]]**, quiet court-family retreats, and a few
  shunned, haunted, or magically claimed islets. Off the north-east edge, alone and
  unbridged, sits little **[[Temple Isle]]** with its temple and Sacred Confluence.

Gondolas and barges are the streets; bridges stitch the islands; mist and steam,
salt and canal-stink fill the air. Under it all runs the campaign's elemental
tension -- the Lake Lady's pure waters against the fire-corruption bleeding from the
Baron's **[[The DragonBarge]]**, moored on High Isle's seaward side. Beneath
everything run **[[The Catacombs]]**.

### Temple Isle <!-- id: myth-loc-6f1056d77024 -->

A small island alone off the north-east edge, unbridged to High Isle and reached only by boat. Dominated by the temple of the Lake Lady and the Sacred Confluence pool -- to be drawn as detailed, planned sacred architecture.

A small island set apart off the **north-east** edge of the city, out in the Lake's
clean waters -- **deliberately unbridged** to [[High Isle]] and reached only by the
temple's own ferries, so that the profane city cannot simply walk to the holy
ground. It is given over almost entirely to the **temple of the Lake Lady** and the
**Sacred Confluence**: a great round sacred pool, fed where the purest lake-water
wells up, where divine magic runs strongest and the goddess is said to manifest at
high ceremony.

Here [[High Priestess Nerissa]] tends the waters and tracks the corruption creeping
north from the DragonBarge; here the [[The Order of the Lake Lady]] keeps its rites.

> **For the mapmaker:** unlike the dense house-by-house districts, Temple Isle should
> be rendered as **detailed, deliberate sacred architecture** -- a planned
> ground-plan of the temple precinct: the colonnaded approach, the temple itself, the
> ring of lesser shrines, and the geometry of the Confluence pool and its channels
> and basins. A formal architectural layout, not a tangle of rooftops.

### The Anminster Road <!-- id: myth-loc-d7201945f52a -->

The last stretch of the inland road, an hour out from the ford

Hedgerow and cart-ruts on the approach from Anminster. Wide enough for two carts, which means an armed column travelling at speed puts everyone else in the verge. Conall and Temerach ride this stretch as the Dragon Knights overtake them.

### The Catacombs <!-- id: myth-loc-3e03305b5abb -->

Ancient tunnels and water-pools beneath Purewater, older than the city -- smuggling routes, secret meeting places, and the site of clandestine 'justice'.

A network of tunnels, caverns, and centuries-old water-pools beneath Purewater, older than the city above. Smugglers use them as hidden routes and the underworld uses them for meetings -- and for darker work; to be "taken to the catacombs" is to be in mortal trouble. It was here that [[Marisette]]'s people dragged [[Randall]] and [[Conall]], bound to chairs and a hairsbreadth from having their throats cut, before the truth of Santo's crime came out and the four strangers' alliance was forged.

The untouched pools have developed strange magical properties, and some believe they connect to primordial water sources predating Purewater itself -- making the catacombs of interest to animists, the [[The Mermaid's Court]], and anyone seeking the city's oldest secrets.

### The DragonBarge <!-- id: myth-loc-0900adc8395d -->

Baron Hanzo's massive dragon-prowed vessel, moored at a private pier on High Isle's eastern, seaward side by the Forge Quarter. It steams where its hull meets the water, corrupting the seaward channel. His floating fortress and demonology workshop.

Baron Hanzo's personal vessel: a massive ship adorned with dragon motifs that
**steams continuously where its hull meets the water**, fire magic warring with the
sacred channel and slowly corrupting it. It lies at a private pier on **[[High
Isle]]'s eastern, seaward side**, among the smoke of the **[[The Forge Quarter]]**
and within easy reach of the open sea through the Mouth -- the corruption it sheds
is felt worst in these eastern yards and creeps north toward the Lake from here.

It is a mobile focus for the Baron's fire demonology and the environmental sign of
his presence. Within: his quarters and magical apparatus, fire-demonology
workspaces, a training area for the possessed champion [[Cailan]], and documents
revealing his political aspirations and the [[The Awake the Dragon Movement]].
Guarded by Dragon Knights and well-defended, but not impregnable -- a key
infiltration target.

### The Ford <!-- id: myth-loc-e68bde0d7264 -->

The river crossing on the north road, an hour outside Purewater

Where the north road meets the water and everyone entering Purewater from inland has to queue. In tourney week the queue is carters, pilgrims, tumblers and pickpockets, backed up behind whatever armed column has decided it goes first. The city is visible from here as a smear of towers and standing water. Magda and Gardwen meet in this queue.

### The Forge Quarter <!-- id: myth-loc-941ec73f1da6 -->

High Isle's eastern point: forges, foundries, tanneries and boatyards (many Dwarven-run), trailing smoke. The gritty industrial belt that spills east into the Shoals; the DragonBarge is moored on its seaward edge.

The eastern point of [[High Isle]] -- the city's industrial belt. Forges, foundries,
boatyards, tanneries and metalworks crowd the seaward channel here, many of them
**Dwarven-run**, their chimneys trailing smoke over the water. The quarter grinds
on day and night and spills naturally eastward into the docks of [[The Shoals]]. It
is also where the Baron's **[[The DragonBarge]]** lies at its private pier, steam
boiling off the channel where fire-magic meets the Lake's water -- the corruption
worst, and the heat most felt, in these eastern yards. (Formerly described as a
separate "Forge Isle"; it is in truth a quarter of High Isle.)

### The Lullwater <!-- id: myth-loc-d09bf7a910cb -->

Purewater's poor-and-criminal quarter, across the canal east of High Isle and sloping toward the Shoals: a low, crowded tangle of the narrowest canals and crooked tenements. Its pretty, calm-sounding name hides where the underworld and the working poor live cheek by jowl. Home of the Moist Oyster and the Dredgers' Hall.

Across the canal from [[High Isle]]'s residential north-east edge, sloping
south-east toward the docks of [[The Shoals]], lies the **Lullwater** -- a low,
crowded, water-logged quarter of the **narrowest, most crooked canals** in the city
and tenements leaning over them until they almost touch overhead. Its name sounds
gentle -- a lull, a calm, still water -- and that is the joke of it: the Lullwater
is where Purewater's **underworld and its working poor live cheek by jowl**, thieves
and dredgers and smugglers and dockhands all crammed into the same drowned maze.

It is easy to get lost here, and dangerous to *look* lost -- [[Conall]] learns as
much when he is cornered within an hour of wandering in. [[Crowbill]]'s people move
the canals by hidden boat-paths; the honest [[The Dredgers]] keep their **Dredgers'
Hall** and safe houses here; and the storyteller [[Thorne]] holds court most
evenings at the **[[The Moist Oyster]]** tavern. The quarter's nearness to the
smuggling jetties of the Shoals is no accident -- contraband flows from one straight
into the other.

> *(This district collapses the older "Maze" and "Warren" into one: the criminal
> tangle and the labouring slum are the same drowned quarter. The lovely name is a
> deliberate, ironic mask.)*

### The Merchant's Quarter <!-- id: myth-loc-477fc558c083 -->

High Isle's south-west corner, by the landward channel and the caravan trade: guild halls, counting-houses, the Watercrafters' Guild, the great market plaza, and the merchant-prince palazzi.

The south-west quarter of [[High Isle]], where the landward channel and the road
from [[Caravan Square]] bring the city's trade ashore. Here stand the guild halls
and counting-houses, the **Watercrafters' Guild**, the great market plaza, and the
palazzi of the **merchant princes** who handle Purewater's day-to-day governance
from the city council. Wealthy, busy, and politically slippery -- this is where the
city's money is made, weighed, and quietly steered. (Formerly described as a
separate "Merchant's Isle"; in the city's true layout it is a quarter of High Isle,
divided from the rest by a branch of the Grand Canal.)

### The Moist Oyster <!-- id: myth-loc-b7741b178ab1 -->

A rough tavern in the Lullwater, sign of a half-open oyster -- cheap drink, loose tongues, knife fights, and the storyteller Thorne holding court most evenings.

A rough tavern deep in [[The Lullwater]], its suggestive sign a half-open oyster
shell. Cheap drink, loose tongues, and the occasional knife fight; an underground
market of rumour and whispered deals. (It echoes the old Zorastor "Vulgar Unicorn,"
down to its bawdy sign.)

Most evenings the storyteller [[Thorne]] holds court here, collecting tales and
gossip from new arrivals and dispensing his own -- a frail-seeming old man with
too-vibrant blue eyes, a blade hidden in his cane, and, secretly, the deep
historical knowledge of a former Order of the Lake initiate. It is the natural place
to gather rumour about the tournament, the Baron, and the underworld -- if you can
keep your own secrets from the wily old man, which the party conspicuously fails to
do.

### The Northern Isles <!-- id: myth-loc-ec677c07203d -->

The scatter of irregular islets across the Lake's calm waters north of High Isle: the bright Pearl, the drowned ruins of Lost Isle, quiet court-family retreats, and a few shunned, haunted, or magically claimed islands.

North of [[High Isle]], where the Lake widens into its calmer, cleaner reaches, lies
a **scatter of small irregular islands** -- the gentler, stranger half of Purewater,
away from the crush of the Mouth.

- **[[The Pearl]]** -- the bright one: pleasure-houses and high nightlife.
- **[[Lost Isle]]** -- ruins, some of them half-drowned, weed-choked and deserted;
  the hidden chambers of [[The Mermaid's Court]] lie somewhere beneath the Northern
  Isles.
- **Court retreats** -- several islets are owned by court families as private
  island-villas and garden-retreats. (Worth remembering: comparatively **few of the
  realm's truly powerful** actually live in Purewater; many of these "noble"
  retreats are minor houses, faded names, or shuttered most of the year.)
- **The shunned isles** -- and some are simply avoided. One is said to be **haunted**;
  another was long ago **claimed by some water-power or magic** and is no longer
  quite of the mortal city; a third sank within living memory and now shows only
  rooftops and a drowned bell at low water. Boatmen know which channels not to take
  after dark.

The Northern Isles reward exploration: a place for retreats and trysts and quiet
deals, for ruins and water-spirits, and for mysteries the bustling city below the
Mouth has half-forgotten.

### The Pearl <!-- id: myth-loc-6062ee281912 -->

The bright pleasure-island among the Northern Isles -- high-end nightlife and the great houses of the courtesan trade (the Sylph's Embrace, the Siren's Call, the Promise of Heaven) along the lamp-strung Pearl Quay, under Marisette's authority.

The brightest of [[The Northern Isles]]: a small island north of [[High Isle]],
given over to pleasure and the high end of Purewater's nightlife. Its houses string
their lamps like pearls along the waterfront **Pearl Quay** -- the [[The Sylph's
Embrace]], the grander [[The Siren's Call]], the Aphrodisia House, the Lily Garden,
and the Promise of Heaven (where [[Randall]] was raised among its women). Legitimate
entertainment and quiet crime braid together here, all under the near-absolute
authority of the madame [[Marisette]].

Set apart on its own island, lamplit and lovely over dark water, the Pearl is where
the city's powerful come to be indiscreet -- which makes it, after the [[The
Currents]], the richest seam of secrets in Purewater. It is also the scene of the
crime that opens the campaign: [[Santo di Teufel]]'s botched binding ritual on the
courtesan [[Emmeralda]] at the Sylph's Embrace, three nights before the tourney.

*(Formerly "the Street of Red Lanterns" / "the Pearl Quarter"; now its own island.)*

### The Shoals <!-- id: myth-loc-e4becf72aa90 -->

The seaward jaw of the Mouth: a peninsula reaching in from the north-east to form the eastern side of the strait, facing the open sea. The working docks, fish-markets, smugglers' jetties, and the Harbor Masters' tower; tied to the Lullwater by trade both honest and not.

The **seaward jaw of the Mouth**: a peninsula reaching in from the north-east to
form the eastern side of the strait, where the Lake's outflow finally meets the
**open sea**. This is Purewater's working waterfront -- fish-markets and wharves,
ship-chandlers and net-lofts, the **Harbor Masters' tower** that governs the lawful
traffic, and the half-hidden **smugglers' jetties** that govern the rest.

The Shoals face the sea and take its weather; ancestor-spirits of drowned sailors
are honoured here. The quarter draws much of its life from the neighbouring
[[The Lullwater]] just inland and west, and the two are bound by trade both honest
and not. The [[The Dredgers]] are strong on these docks; the [[The Harbor Masters]]
officially rule them while quietly tolerating the contraband that keeps the whole
waterfront fed.

### The Siren's Call <!-- id: myth-loc-adbcda5c3828 -->

Ravella's establishment where the canals of High Isle meet the poorer districts -- part pleasure house, part information market, neutral ground for every faction.

[[Ravella]]'s establishment, sited where High Isle's canals meet the poorer districts: part high-end pleasure house, part information market, and one of the few patches of neutral ground in Purewater. Here the half-merfolk broker holds court and runs her network of gondoliers, washerwomen, and dockhands -- everyone who touches water daily -- trading in secrets as much as pleasure. Whispered deals between rival factions happen under its roof; it is the city's great clearinghouse of information, and a likely doorway to the merfolk paths beneath Purewater.

### The Sylph's Embrace <!-- id: myth-loc-d70253b09da8 -->

Constantine's high-end pleasure house on the Pearl -- elegant, thick-walled, and the site of Santo's ritual attack on Emmeralda.

An elegant, high-end pleasure house run by [[Constantine]], its walls thick enough to muffle the night's business. [[Magda]] took work here protecting the girls, and brought [[Gardwen]] in to stay.

Two nights after their arrival it became a crime scene: a patron resembling [[Randall]] carved a long, curling ritual wound the length of the courtesan [[Emmeralda]]'s body before fleeing, dropping his soul-knife behind an armoire. Gardwen's druidic healing saved the girl from death -- and from complete possession by the pleasure demon Santo was trying to bind. The house now keeps extra security and a wary eye on the street; Constantine wants justice, and the demon still stirs in Emmeralda's flesh.

## Factions

### Crowbill's Underworld <!-- id: myth-faction-b13b4eec5c17 -->

Purewater's criminal underground -- canal smuggling, gambling, protection -- run to a code by the unremarkable crime lord Crowbill. Signaled by crow marks.

Purewater's criminal underground: canal smuggling, gambling dens, and protection rackets, run by [[Crowbill]] -- a wiry, forgettable man whose only distinctive mark is a small crow tattoo on the back of his right hand. His people signal with crow symbols, and he keeps hidden boat-paths through the canals and safe houses across the city.

What sets the underworld apart is its **code**: no murder of locals, no harm to children, no disruption of the Lake Lady's sacred sites. (In the older Zorastor telling this was Jubal's Talons and the thief Randall's home turf; in Purewater the role is Crowbill's, given an explicit ethic.) Crowbill was content to profit from the Baron's arrival at first, but the Dragon Knights' brutality is wrecking the equilibrium his business depends on -- which is steadily turning him into a possible ally. [[Randall]], [[Nus]] and [[Mariarta]] move within this world; the courtesan [[Asphodel]], Randall's confidante at the Promise of Heaven, is tied to it only through him.

### The Awake the Dragon Movement <!-- id: myth-faction-c0a9a96aa980 -->

A shadowy movement to revive the ancient draconic order and transform men into Dragon Kings. Baron Hanzo's true cause; an existential threat to water magic.

Beyond Baron Hanzo himself lies the cause he serves: a movement to revive the magic of the ancient Dragon Kings and -- ultimately -- to transform chosen men into dragons. The Baron's possession ritual, binding the ancient dragon-knight spirit [[Thuban Eta]] into a living vessel, is an early proof of this art: an attempt to recreate the draconic transformations of an age the realm thought destroyed in the old uprising.

How wide the movement runs is unknown -- it may have members across the realm, in other cities suffering their own corruptions. To the water-faiths and the [[The Mermaid's Court]] it is an existential threat: the return of an ancient order of fire and dominion against the older, pre-human powers of water. The Baron's victory at the Lake Lady's Tourney is meant to be the movement's first foothold in Purewater. **(GM:** see the GM-secrets lore for how the movement connects to the Baron's tower, Thuban's bones, and the wider campaign.)

### The Currents <!-- id: myth-faction-f54dfb1a03b4 -->

A leaderless network of spies and informants trading in secrets, not contraband. Marked by chalk water-signs; the hidden 'Source' directs it.

An intelligence network and loose guild of independent operators who trade in secrets and favors rather than goods. They keep no headquarters, meeting at sites marked by chalk drawings of flowing water and identifying one another by coded talk of tides and currents.

**Ranks:** Droplets, Streams, Rivers, Confluences, Deltas, and the inner Watershed. There is no single head; the nearest thing is **"The Source,"** a hidden consortium of three to five unknown individuals.

**Stated aim:** keep information flowing and prevent any one faction dominating. **Real aim:** to be the true power behind Purewater, playing factions against each other to a balance that serves their unknown masters -- whom some believe sit in the capital or abroad. They deal with everyone -- [[Ravella]], [[Crowbill]], temple, and government alike -- and ally closely with no one. Their rivals are other spy networks, including Ravella's personal agents and the Baron's.

### The Dragon Knights <!-- id: myth-faction-37a5ba90873e -->

Baron Hanzo's 20-30 sworn warriors -- brutal mercenaries armored in dragon heraldry, enhanced with fire demonology. Locally despised as the 'dragonshits'.

Ostensibly a noble martial order, the Dragon Knights are in truth Baron Hanzo di Teufel's instrument of conquest: hardened raiders and murderers who have terrorized the borderlands for thirty years. They wear armor emblazoned with dragons and many carry fire-touched weapons. Roughly twenty to thirty strong in Purewater, well-disciplined and thoroughly violent.

**Command.** The mercenary captain [[Tat Atarer]] is the leader of record -- a professional disciplinarian who fights for pay, not ideology ("the Baron pays for our swords, not our opinions"). Day-to-day operations and the Baron's dirty work fall to his albino lieutenant [[Blau]], known to the ranks as Commander Vask: burn-scarred, raspy-voiced, utterly loyal in public while secretly studying the Baron's techniques to one day break away.

**Ranks** (per the order's own conceit): Ember Recruits, Flame Soldiers, Smoke Lieutenants, Charred Captains, Ash Lords (elites who have undergone partial fire-binding), and the Dragon Circle (the Baron's inner guard).

**Stated aim:** keep order in the Baron's territories and honor the order through tournament display. **Real aim:** use the Lake Lady's Tourney as cover to plant a foothold in Purewater, disrupt its water magic, and extend Baron Hanzo's reach. Their worst enemies are those who survived their past raids -- [[Gardwen]] foremost among them. They are barracked in a fortified estate east of the city; the Baron himself keeps to the [[The DragonBarge]].

### The Dredgers <!-- id: myth-faction-b6384f17d338 -->

Labor guild and mutual-aid society of Purewater's working poor -- canal-clearers, dockers, fishers. Black armbands, white wave. Led by Marda Blackwater.

Born as an association of canal dredgers who clear silt and refuse from the waterways, now a broad movement of laborers, fishers, and the working poor of [[The Lullwater]] and [[The Shoals]]. They keep a guildhall in the Lullwater and safe houses across the poor districts, and wear plain black armbands marked with a white wave.

**Ranks:** Hands, Voices, Channels, Harbors, and an executive committee of Pilots.

**Leadership.** [[Marda Blackwater]] -- a broad-shouldered woman in her forties who rose from the dredge-boats during a labor dispute five years ago; practical, fiery, and devoted to the working class.

**Stated aim:** fair wages, safe conditions, respect for essential work. **Real aim:** to remake Purewater's power structure through worker solidarity and a seat in its governance -- the radical wing wants to overthrow the order entirely. Cautiously allied with the progressive wing of the [[The Order of the Lake Lady]] and ethical merchants; complicated toward [[Crowbill]]; and bitterly hostile to the Baron's Dragon Knights as the purest face of aristocratic oppression.

### The Harbor Masters <!-- id: myth-faction-3f3c69cd7854 -->

The regulatory guild of Purewater's docks and shipping -- tariffs, berths, maritime law. Dark blue uniforms; a harbor tower HQ. Led by Velen Deepkeel.

The body that controls maritime traffic in and out of Purewater: docks, berths, tariffs, and the enforcement of maritime law. They work from a harbor tower and dockside offices, in dark blue uniforms badged by rank.

**Ranks:** Dockhands, Pilots, Harbormen, Tide Lords, Port Captains, and the seven-master Anchor Council.

**Leadership.** [[Harbormaster Velen Deepkeel]] -- stern, weathered, forty years risen through the ranks; meticulous and traditional, publicly incorruptible.

**Stated aim:** safe, efficient harbors and the city's standing as a trade hub. **Real aim:** to keep their near-monopoly on maritime commerce and its wealth; the upper ranks are increasingly corrupt, taking bribes to wave through smuggling while parading strict regulation. Strong ties to the Governor, council, and shipping merchants; an uneasy secret arrangement with [[Crowbill]]; open contempt for the [[The Dredgers]]; and a wary, opportunistic eye on the Baron.

### The Mermaid's Court <!-- id: myth-faction-f5c4a0122bf5 -->

A secretive society of pure-water practitioners bound to merfolk, preserving water magic older than the city. Hidden under Lost Isle; led by the enigmatic 'Pearl'.

A hidden society of water-magic practitioners -- humans, half-merfolk, and the water-touched -- preserving the oldest forms of pure water magic, from chambers beneath [[Lost Isle]] and the canals. They leave little trace, known only by mother-of-pearl tokens and faint blue-green markings.

**Ranks:** Surface Swimmers, Tide Dancers, Depth Callers, Wave Shapers, Abyss Touched (transformed elites who can breathe water for hours), and a seven-member Deep Council. Their leader is known only as **"The Nacre"** (the mother-of-pearl) -- merfolk princess, ancient elemental in human shape, or something else; few have met them. *(Named so as not to collide with the pleasure-island of [[The Pearl]].)*

**Stated aim:** protect the purest water magic and the bond with merfolk. **Real aim:** to reclaim Purewater, over generations if need be, and cleanse what they see as a spiritual as well as physical corruption. They are fundamentally opposed to the Baron's fire magic but favor patient sabotage over open war. [[Ravella]], the half-merfolk broker, may have ties to them.

### The Order of the Blue Star <!-- id: myth-faction-97541b57307e -->

An institutional order of mages marked by a blue star at the brow. Trained Conall (through Old Johz) in arcane magic and investigation; takes a careful interest in dangerous power.

A magical order of arcane practitioners -- a true institution, with a hall, ranks, and a doctrine -- recognizable by the blue star that blazes at a master's forehead. They represent institutional magical authority and take a long, careful interest in dangerous power falling into the wrong hands.

It was the Blue Star wizard **Old Johz** who recognized [[Conall]] as the Baron's son when he was a boy in Anminster, healed him years later in Sharna, and trained him in arcane magic -- "so that power didn't fall into the wrong hands" -- before delivering him to [[Temerach Nebulo]] and abruptly departing. The order may intervene directly against the Baron, and is a likely source of counsel on the possession ritual and the demon afflicting [[Emmeralda]].

**A note on Hesper.** Because the Blue Star is the most visible mage-order in these parts, the sorcerer [[Hesper]] -- Marisette's enforcer in the Pearl -- is sometimes loosely assumed to be one of its wizards. Hesper is **not** of the Blue Star, and the assumption suits Hesper fine. *(GM: Hesper's true allegiance lies elsewhere -- see [[The Quiet Hand]].)*

### The Order of the Lake Lady <!-- id: myth-faction-2b6cfc142c63 -->

The water-faith of Purewater: priesthood of the lake goddess, guardians of pure water magic, political power over the sacred waters. Robed in blue-green with silver wave patterns.

Both a religious cult and a civic power, the Order maintains the temples and sacred pools of Purewater (chiefly on [[Temple Isle]]) and performs the rituals that keep the increasingly polluted waters pure. Members wear blue-green robes worked with silver waves.

**Ranks:** Water Initiates, Reflectionists, Tide Wardens, Wave Speakers (those with proven water-affinity), Deep Visionaries (who commune with the Lake Lady), and The High Tide -- the ruling council of five Deep Visionaries.

**Leadership.** [[Lilura Deepcurrent]] has led the Order for three decades: elderly, piercing-eyed, rumored to carry merfolk ancestry. Beneath her, [[High Priestess Nerissa]] runs the Temple of the Lake Lady day to day -- she can sense water corruption, bless sacred waters, and speak with water spirits, but temple law forbids her from intervening directly unless the sacred sites are threatened. She therefore needs agents.

**Stated aim:** preserve the sacred waters and the Lake Lady's blessings. **Real aim:** hold their political power as the waters fail; some higher clergy fear the goddess's power is waning and quietly study the corruption to understand it. Allied with the [[The Watercrafters Guild]] and on arranged terms with [[Ravella]]; their chief enemy is the Baron's fire demonology. Since his arrival the corruption -- and their alarm -- has steadily risen.

### The Pleasure Houses of the Pearl <!-- id: myth-faction-c22ef5f70cb3 -->

The courtesan network of the Pearl, under the madame Marisette's near-absolute authority. Constantine's Sylph's Embrace and Ravella's Siren's Call among them.

The entertainment trade of Purewater's pleasure district is no loose collection of houses but a network under the near-absolute authority of the madame [[Marisette]], who rules the business of pleasure from the Aphrodisia House. Individual proprietors answer to her: [[Constantine]] runs [[The Sylph's Embrace]]; the half-merfolk broker [[Ravella]] runs the grander, information-soaked [[The Siren's Call]]. Houses like the Lily Garden, the Promise of Heaven (where [[Randall]] was raised), and the Aphrodisia House line the Pearl.

The network is fiercely protective of its own. When [[Santo di Teufel]] carved up the courtesan [[Emmeralda]] in the Sylph's Embrace, Marisette's response was total: all services rescinded for the entire di Teufel retinue, enforced city-wide -- worth paying the underworld a princely sum to guarantee. The houses are also among Purewater's richest sources of information, their workers privy to soldiers' secrets and nobles' weaknesses alike.

### The Quiet Hand <!-- id: myth-faction-56e45a44c14b -->

A secret, centuries-old fellowship that steers the world toward the good from the shadows -- by knowing first and acting unseen. Espionage as mercy. Hesper is their agent in Purewater. (GM: a benevolent hidden patron.)

> *"Save the world a tragedy at a time -- and take no credit for it."*
> -- the first article of the Empty Hand

**GM note: the Quiet Hand is a secret.** The players will not find its name in
any archive; most of Purewater has never heard of it. It is recorded here as a
hidden patron and a long lever on the campaign.

## What it is

The Quiet Hand is a fellowship of spies, scholars, healers, diplomats, and a few
mages who share one conviction: that the great evils of the world are best stopped
not by armies or open sorcery, but by **knowing of them first and acting before
they ripen** -- a planted word, an intercepted letter, a witness brought to the
right magistrate, a frightened heir reached the night before the coup. They hold
that history turns on small, almost invisible moments -- they call each one a
**hinge** -- and that whoever sees the hinge first and leans on it gently can bend
the future without ever taking a throne.

They are not the [[The Currents]]. The Currents sell secrets to whoever pays and
keep the city's powers in a profitable balance; the Hand takes no fee, serves no
faction, and spends its secrets to *prevent* harm. Where the Currents are a market,
the Hand is a conscience -- and a far older, far quieter thing.

## Origin (GM)

The Hand was founded generations ago by survivors of a catastrophe they could have
turned aside had they only known in time and dared to act unseen -- by their own
telling, the ruin left in the long fall of the **Dragon Kings**. They swore to
become the thing that strangles the next such evil in its cradle. This makes the
**[[The Awake the Dragon Movement]]** their oldest and most personal nightmare: the
one catastrophe they exist, above all, to prevent from happening twice.

## How it works -- the metaphor of the hand

The order is built so that no captured member can betray more than a fingertip:

- A single agent is a **finger**.
- A few fingers who work together, knowing only one another, are a **hand** (a cell).
- A handler who runs a hand is a **wrist**.
- The leadership -- unknown even to most wrists -- is the **Palm**.

Members carry no insignia and keep no hall. They know one another by **counter-sign**
(one speaks half of an old couplet; the other completes it) and a brief gesture --
an open, empty palm shown for a heartbeat, the sign of the Empty Hand. There is no
roster; there is no banner to capture.

## Method and creed

They prize the bloodless solution above all: the forged writ, the bought hesitation,
the timely rescue, the rumor that empties a square before the soldiers arrive, the
healer who reaches the right bedside. They **kill rarely and reluctantly, never for
convenience**, and they will not seize power for themselves -- power is precisely
the corruption they guard against, so they always act *through* others and then
vanish. Their successes are invisible by design: the war that never started, the
plague quietly contained, the tyrant who inexplicably lost his nerve. The measure of
their work is **absence**.

Mages among them are a prized minority -- valued less for force than for what they
can *learn* and *carry*: farsight and scrying, sendings, disguise, wards, the
occasional decisive word. Every member swears the **Oath of the Empty Hand**: take
no throne, title, land, credit, or monument; act never for yourself; spare whom you
can; carry the light, and pass it on.

## In Purewater

The Hand has one known finger in the city: **[[Hesper]]**, who for years has worn
the mask of [[Marisette]]'s cold, dangerous "enforcer." It is perfect cover -- a
listening post at the very heart of the Pearl's secrets, beside a woman who
hears everything. Hesper has watched [[Baron Hanzo di Teufel]] since before he ever
reached Purewater, and the seizing of [[Conall]] and [[Randall]] into the catacombs
was the Hand controlling a volatile night and getting at the truth -- and keeping
the soul-knife out of worse hands -- not the cruelty it appeared to be.

For the party, this means a **secret patron**. If they prove themselves against the
Baron, a door will quietly open, a guard will be looking the wrong way, the thing
they need will already be where they need it. They may, in time, learn whose hand
arranged their luck. Or they may never know -- which is exactly how the Hand prefers
it.

An elite knightly order sworn to the Lake Lady (Nimue) and the protection of the realm and its water magic. Temerach Nebulo is their most famous blade.

A close-knit, austere order of knights sworn to a code of honor beyond the city watch or the King's army, and held to account by the goddess herself. They hold battle honors from the ancient uprising against the Dragon Kings and live disciplined lives of service. They operate across Mystamyr to protect the realm and, above all, the old water magic.

She has entered the Lake Lady's Tourney's archery competition not for glory but to track the Baron and the "Awake the Dragon" movement, and to shield [[Conall]], whose parentage she has long known.

**Stated aim:** safety of the realm. **Real aim:** expose and stop the Baron's revival of draconic power before it can take root. They have resources, agents, and dead-drops throughout Purewater, and move through quiet, unknown intermediaries.

### The Watercrafters Guild <!-- id: myth-faction-072453a4a6c9 -->

## Lore (player-visible)

### Magic in Purewater: Water and Fire <!-- id: myth-lore-045804b8f541 -->

The CFI magic of the city framed around its central conflict -- pure water magic and the Lake Lady's divine power against the Baron's corrupting fire demonology.

Purewater sits at a nexus where sacred lake meets sea, and its magic is organized around a single great conflict: **water against fire**.

**Pure water magic** -- the oldest power, tied to the merfolk, the Lake Lady, and the blessed waters. Healing and protective; stronger near pure water; able to sense corruption and disturbance. [[Gardwen]]'s druidic gift is explicitly of this kind, as is the divine power of [[High Priestess Nerissa]] and the [[The Order of the Lake Lady]]. It is increasingly rare and suppressed as the waters foul.

**Corrupt water magic** -- the grey zone, where practitioners blend natural magic with exploitation and profit over purity. [[Ravella]]'s domain shades into this.

**Fire / dragon demonology** -- the Baron's art: unnatural, ritual-precise, and corrupting. It binds spirits and demons into flesh, creates steam and heat where it meets water, and spreads its corruption gradually through the channels (most visibly from [[The DragonBarge]]). It is ancient and forbidden, the magic of the Dragon Kings reborn.

**In Classic Fantasy (CFI) terms.** This campaign is run under Classic Fantasy Imperative: the protagonists are Cleric, Fighter, Magic-User, and Rogue. The Lake Lady's water-priests are **Clerics** (divine/Theism, Piety and Exhort, water-aspected miracles -- blessing, healing, cleansing, sensing corruption). Arcane practitioners like [[Conall]] and the [[The Order of the Blue Star]] are **Magic-Users** (Arcane Casting -- illusion, invisibility, alteration of appearance, divination/find, counterspell). The Baron and his son work fire **demonology** as forbidden Magic-User/ritual art. Light innate gifts (Ravella's water-sense, a fisherman's cantrips) read as **Folk Magic**. Mages cannot cast effectively in armor.

**Manifestations of the conflict:** steam where fire meets water; channels near the DragonBarge running unnatural colors; pure waters recoiling from corrupted areas; water spirits withdrawing; merfolk paths closing; cold spots where fire magic has been worked; and water-magic practitioners troubled by visions. The Tournament becomes the symbolic battleground for which magic will hold the city.

*(In the original Mythras telling these traditions are Animism, Folk Magic, Divine, Sorcery, and Mysticism, each with its own faction practitioners -- the Mermaid's Court spirit-binders, the Watercrafters' water-sorcery, the Currents' scrying, the Dragonknights' Ash Lords. The CFI port maps these onto the four classes; the flavor of each faction's craft is preserved in their entries.)*

### The Gods of Mystamyr <!-- id: myth-lore-5f0681b87e74 -->

The Lake Lady (Nimue), patron of Purewater's waters; Hel, the northern death-goddess who sends Magda; Vacarus, god of war; and Asmodeus, the lord devil of the soul-knife.

**The Lake Lady (Nimue).** Purewater's patron: goddess of the sacred waters, of purification and abundance, healing and prophecy-in-reflection, and protection against corruption. Her [[The Order of the Lake Lady]] tends the temples and sacred pools; her knights, the [[The Swords of the Lake]], are held to account by the goddess herself. As the city's waters foul, her power is felt to wane.

**Hel.** The death-goddess of the Northern barbarians, grim and terrible. It is Hel who sends [[Magda]] -- through jarring, violent nightmares ending always in Magda's own death -- to protect the four companions and "lay the dragon warrior to rest." One does not serve the Goddess of Death without dread, and Magda curses her patroness even as she obeys.

**Vacarus.** The god of war, invoked by the city's people in oaths and exclamations ("Vacarus knows"); the central square of the old city bore his name in Magda's recurring vision.

**Asmodeus.** The lord devil whose runic sigil is emblazoned on the demonologist's **soul-knife** -- the instrument [[Santo di Teufel]] used to carve binding marks into [[Emmeralda]]. His mark on the blade is the unmistakable signature of the fire-demonology at the campaign's heart, and the sight of it makes even Hel's warrior recoil.

Lesser invocations heard on the street include **Nilo Valis**, patron spirit of thieves and quick improvisation, and the sea-gods of the Shoals and craft-gods of Merchant's Quarter.

### The Dragon Kings and the Lake Lady <!-- id: myth-lore-f7c4f9a0286e -->

The ancient draconic order, the uprising that destroyed it, and the older water-powers that resisted it -- the deep history the Baron is trying to revive.

In an age before the present order, the **Dragon Kings** ruled -- a draconic power of fire and dominion, served by an order of dragon-knights. They were thrown down in a great **uprising**, in which the [[The Swords of the Lake]] won their oldest battle honors. The realm of Mystamyr has remembered the Dragon Kings as a destroyed evil ever since; their heraldry is an unwelcome thing to see flying on a border lord's banners.

Older still are the **pre-human water-powers** -- the merfolk, the spirits of lake and sea, and the goddess the city calls the **Lake Lady**. These older powers resisted the draconic order then, and the water-faiths hold that they resist it now. The campaign's deep conflict is precisely this: the Baron's [[The Awake the Dragon Movement]] is an attempt to *revive* the Dragon Kings' transformations -- and his possession of the ancient dragon-knight spirit [[Thuban Eta]] is a first, monstrous proof that the old draconic magic can be made to work again.

This is the history the storyteller [[Thorne]] secretly carries, and why he -- a former Order of the Lake initiate -- can recognize the Baron's rituals for what they truly are.

### The Realm of Mystamyr <!-- id: myth-lore-4f25bed70921 -->

The wider world: the kingdom of Mystamyr under King Halvern, its capital Sharna, the lawless borderlands, and Purewater's place as its corrupt water-gate.

Purewater is a city of the realm of **Mystamyr**, a kingdom ruled by **King Halvern** from the capital, **Sharna**. It is an old land that remembers the **Dragon Kings** and the uprising that threw them down -- a memory that makes the Baron's dragon heraldry, openly flaunted, both strange and ominous to those who notice.

Beyond the settled heart of the kingdom lie the **borderlands**, a lawless march of forests, swamps ("kritch"), and scattered villages where raids, pogroms, and reprisals have run unchecked for thirty years. It is here that [[Baron Hanzo di Teufel]] rules as a sorcerous tyrant -- enslaving, murdering, exterminating elves, and committing atrocities always with enough deniability that King Halvern, far away in Sharna, suspects nothing. He is, in [[Temerach Nebulo]]'s judgment, the greatest existing threat to Mystamyr and the King's reign.

Purewater itself sits at the kingdom's watery edge, the gate between inland lake and open sea. It is governed in the King's name by his young nephew, **[[Prince Emeric]]** -- clever, idealistic, and hopelessly unsuited to a city like this one, where every faction runs circles around his good intentions. The smaller towns of the march -- **Anminster**, where [[Conall]] was raised by a militiaman's family; the woodland grove where [[Gardwen]]'s people were massacred -- feed travelers and tragedy alike toward the city and its tournament.

### The Lake Lady's Tourney <!-- id: myth-lore-74a1179fcabc -->

Purewater's annual sacred tournament -- archery, single combat, and the unique Harbor Run -- and the stage the Baron means to hijack with his possessed champion.

The **Lake Lady's Tourney** is Purewater's most prestigious event: an annual sacred tournament honoring the goddess, older than the current political order, drawing competitors from across the realm. Because it is sacred, the Baron's plan to manipulate it is not mere politics but **desecration**.

**The events:**
- **Archery (the main event)** -- traditional targets, plus moving targets on boats between the islands and difficult cross-channel shots. Prizes carry court access and noble titles. This is the competition [[Temerach Nebulo]] has entered.
- **Single Combat** -- a dueling tournament of one-on-one matches. Prizes of guild apprenticeships and contracts. **This is where the Baron means to field his possessed champion, [[Cailan]].**
- **The Harbor Run** -- an obstacle course unique to Purewater: swimming, climbing, and boat-hopping through the canals and over rooftops, testing agility, strength, and local knowledge. Cash and local fame for the winner -- and a covert recruiting ground for criminal and military interests.

**Timing:** arrivals and preparations begin three days before the main event; registration and the sizing-up of competitors two days before; then the events proper. The tourney's opening is the campaign's hinge -- the first public viewing of the Baron's champion, and the moment [[Gardwen]] recognizes her stolen brother.

### The City and Its Islands <!-- id: myth-lore-502f5d9703aa -->

Purewater as a strait-city: the Mouth where the Lake drains to the Sea, the two land-jaws (Caravan Square and the Shoals), the great triangular High Isle and its quarters, and the scatter of Northern Isles.

## The shape of Purewater

Purewater is not a ring of districts around a centre -- it is a city built across
**the Mouth**, the narrow strait where the sacred **Lake** (to the **north**) drains
south into the **open Sea**. Hold that picture and the rest follows.

**The two jaws of the Mouth.**
- From the **western** mainland, a thin **promontory** reaches east to a larger
  outcrop: **[[Caravan Square]]** and the Gate of Triumph -- the only way into the
  city by land. This is the southern jaw.
- A second **peninsula**, set a little north and east, forms the seaward jaw and
  carries **[[The Shoals]]** -- the docks, fish-markets and smugglers' jetties that
  face the open sea.

**[[High Isle]]**, the heart, is a great irregular **triangular** island wedged
between the jaws, splitting the Lake's outflow. Its **south-west** point reaches
toward Caravan Square (the calmer landward channel); its **east** point toward the
Shoals (the seaward channel, the true Mouth); its **north-west** point faces the open
Lake. The **Grand Canal bisects and branches across it**, dividing its quarters:

- **[[The Merchant's Quarter]]** (south-west) -- trade, guilds, the Watercrafters,
  the merchant princes.
- **The official & noble quarter** (north-east) -- the Governor's Palazzo
  ([[Prince Emeric]]), Hall of Justice, Barracks, Palace Square, and the wealthy.
- **[[The Forge Quarter]]** & industry (east) -- forges and yards spilling into the
  Shoals; the Baron's **[[The DragonBarge]]** at its seaward pier.
- **The residential north edge** -- sloping rich-to-middling south-east toward the
  canal-mouth, beyond which lies **[[The Lullwater]]**, the poor-and-criminal quarter
  (the old "Maze" and "Warren" combined; its calm name a deliberate mask).

**[[The Northern Isles]]**, scattered across the Lake's calmer waters north of High
Isle: **[[The Pearl]]** (pleasure and nightlife), **[[Lost Isle]]** (drowned ruins;
the [[The Mermaid's Court]] beneath), quiet **court-family retreats**, and a few
**shunned or haunted** islets. Off the north-east edge, alone and unbridged, sits
little **[[Temple Isle]]** with its temple and the **Sacred Confluence**.

Boats are the streets; bridges stitch it together; **[[The Catacombs]]** run beneath
it all. The set-pieces the geography invites: canal chases through the Lullwater, the
Harbor Run across the Mouth, and the steam-wreathed corruption spreading from the
DragonBarge on the seaward channel.
